### 1. Deus Ex producer Warren Spector says he is retiring from game development (People)

Spector admitted there are still three games he would like to make, but argued the business has changed and “it’s just not as much fun for me anymore,” adding that a new generation deserves the spotlight. His credits run from Origin’s Ultima work through Looking Glass titles such as Thief and System Shock to Ion Storm’s Deus Ex. He steps away as chief creative officer of OtherSide Entertainment; this year’s Thick as Thieves underperformed, and the studio had laid people off.

## II. Regulation & Gambling

### 5. Labor and the Coalition strike a deal; Australian gambling-ad bill passes the House 96–9 (Regulation)

**Summary:** On August 18 the Albanese government and the Coalition agreed on gambling-advertising and inducement reforms. The bill passed the House of Representatives 96 votes to 9 and still needs the Senate; SBS said it is expected to clear this week. Communications Minister Anika Wells called the package “the most significant reforms to online gambling in Australia’s history.” Amendments include extending the live-sport ad blackout from five minutes to 15 minutes before a match, moving the three-ads-per-hour broadcast cap to start at 5 a.m., banning ads in children’s programming, barring direct marketing for 14 days after a new betting signup and for 90 days after someone leaves the BetStop self-exclusion register, banning commissions tied to customer losses for staff or affiliates, and a wagering-funded online ad opt-out register to be built under the communications regulator. The Guardian reported the register could take up to 12 months and is unlikely to be ready by 1 January 2027; the laws would be reviewed after three years. The late MP Peta Murphy’s 2023 report recommended a total ban on inducements and wagering ads; the final text meets neither. Coalition MPs Andrew Wallace and Pat Conaghan crossed the floor. SBS quoted Wallace saying Australians lose about A$36 billion a year on gambling.

### 6. South Korea’s media regulator votes to permanently block prediction market Polymarket (Regulation)

**Summary:** The Herald Business reported on August 18 that the Korea Communications Standards Commission’s telecommunications review subcommittee voted that day to issue a permanent access-blocking order against Polymarket, an overseas blockchain prediction market. The commission said winner-takes-all wagers on events users cannot control — elections, rates, sports, crypto prices — promote a gambling instinct, and that operators open markets, set rules, pool and distribute funds through virtual-asset deposit and settlement, and take fees on share trades. Polymarket argued it is a non-custodial peer-to-peer smart-contract system, not a “host,” and that it neither collects funds nor issues sports-promotion tickets, so it falls outside Korea’s network law and criminal or sports-promotion gambling definitions. The commission said technical features such as decentralization or the presence of Korean-language service cannot evade domestic law, and that a block is needed to protect local users. The same report noted France, Australia, and Germany have already blocked the platform as illegal online gambling.

**Links:**

- [The Herald Business — KCSC blocks Polymarket](https://mbiz.heraldcorp.com/article/10844227)

**Commentary:** Regulators are scoring the cash that follows wins and losses, not whether settlement happens at a casino cage or on-chain.

### 7. Thailand’s digital minister says a game-industry bill may cover board games and trading cards (Regulation)

**Summary:** The Thaiger reported on August 18 that Digital Economy and Society Minister Chaichanok Chidchob has assigned a team to revise the draft Promotion of the Game Industry Act. He wants concrete progress inside 2026 but set no deadline for a finished draft, and admitted he had not yet followed up. He said the original text leaned too far toward control, missed current business models, and left the definition of “game” thin — asking whether it should cover only video games or also board games, trading cards, and related trades so Thai operators get fairer rules. Another priority is intellectual-property language so Thailand can build domestic IP from local culture and characters; he cited Japan and China turning cartoons into games. The Digital Economy Promotion Agency has been drafting the bill for more than a year as a central framework for the industry.
