## I. M&A and Studios

### 1. Alibaba agrees to sell Lingxi Games to Trustar Capital (M&A)

**Summary:** On August 17, Lingxi Games CEO Zhou Bingshu told staff in an internal letter that Alibaba Group and Trustar Capital (Citic Capital’s private-equity arm) have a definitive deal: Alibaba will transfer its entire Lingxi stake, and Trustar will become the new shareholder. Zhou said management will stay. The memo did not disclose price, closing date, or regulatory conditions. The South China Morning Post, Reuters, and The Wall Street Journal all reviewed it. Bloomberg had previously reported a valuation that could exceed $1.5 billion; Reuters, citing a person familiar with the matter, said Alibaba expects to receive more than $2 billion. Chinese outlets often cite “over $1.5 billion, about 10.1 billion yuan.” Neither side confirmed a figure. Lingxi is best known for Three Kingdoms: Strategy Edition, developed with Koei Tecmo. Zhou framed the sale as Alibaba “sharpening its strategic focus” on e-commerce, cloud, and AI.

- [National Business Daily — Full memo and bidding recap](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-08-17/4543763.html)

**Commentary:** This is not a games company buying a games company; it is a platform handing a still-profitable non-core studio to private equity so cash and attention can move to AI and cloud.

### 2. Mattel launches Mattel Game Studios, folding ~300 staff in L.A. and Hangzhou (Studio)

**Summary:** Toy maker Mattel created Mattel Game Studios under Mattel Digital Studios to develop, publish, and operate games based on its brands, covering the existing mobile slate, new free-to-play titles, and original experiences on UGC platforms such as Roblox and Fortnite. The unit unites almost 300 games workers in Los Angeles and Hangzhou. The move follows Mattel’s earlier buyout of joint-venture studio Mattel163 from NetEase at a $318 million valuation; Mattel163 said on Facebook it will now sit inside the new studio. Since 2018, UNO!, UNO Wonder, Phase 10, and Skip-Bo have generated more than 550 million downloads and about 20 million monthly active players. Global head of Mattel Digital Studios Marcus Liassides said the company wants to “publish more original gaming experiences inspired by our iconic brands.” UNO Wild is in soft launch and a new Hot Wheels title is in development. The first self-published mobile game, Skeletor: Until Next Time!, shipped in June.

## III. Regulation

### 7. Australia’s Labor and Coalition strike a tentative deal on a one-stop gambling-ad opt-out register (Regulation)

**Summary:** ABC News reported on August 17 that the federal government and the Coalition have a tentative deal to pass gambling reforms: the Australian Communications and Media Authority would run a single register so people can opt out of wagering ads across online platforms, replacing a “triple lock” that would have required a manual opt-out on every account. Amendments would also curb inducements aimed at vulnerable gamblers and spending-based commissions for VIP account managers. About 15 amendments were briefed to a Labor backbench committee and the Coalition shadow ministry that evening, with caucus and party-room votes due August 18 before the changes go to parliament. Labor wants the bills through this week; the Greens and independent senators still want a full ad ban. Wagering and tech sources immediately questioned how a central register would cover every platform and who would pay for it.

**Links:**

- [ABC News — Opt-out register in exchange for Coalition support](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-17/labor-gets-coalition-support-for-gambling-laws-after-amendments/107047766)

**Commentary:** A promised ad crackdown has been rewritten as a do-not-call list for betting spots: politically passable, with the hard work kicked to platforms and users.
