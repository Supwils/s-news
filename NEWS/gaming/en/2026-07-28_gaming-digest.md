## II. Platforms & Services

### 3. Xbox CTO apologizes for ~20-hour outage, blames licensing dependency, opens full review (Platform)

**Summary:** Around July 28, Xbox CTO Scott Van Vliet detailed on X the outage that ran from late July 26 into July 27: a licensing service outside Xbox but shared inside Microsoft began failing, breaking sign-in, full-library entitlement checks, and launches of owned games—including some disc/entitlement paths. Eurogamer, PCMag, IGN, and Beebom followed.

### 8. South Korea publishes first overseas publisher compliance roster: 81 firms, one in pre-penalty review (Regulation)

**Summary:** Per TechTimes and Gamemeca on July 28, South Korea’s Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and the Game Rating and Administration Committee released the first official compliance roster under the foreign-publisher domestic-representative mandate. Eighty-one companies without a Korean place of business that meet thresholds such as roughly ₩1 trillion prior-year global revenue or about 1,000 average new daily installs in Korea must appoint a local agent—names spanning Nintendo, Roblox, miHoYo, and NetEase. Eighty have complied; one is in a pre-penalty opinion process facing administrative fines of up to about ₩20 million, which can be reimposed. About 24 companies below the thresholds appointed agents voluntarily; pending bills would toughen gacha-disclosure penalties and could allow distribution suspensions.

**Links:**

## V. Regulation & Deals

### 10. Saudi-led $55bn EA buyout clears EU merger review; foreign-subsidy ruling due around July 30 (M&A)

**Summary:** The European Commission has approved under the EU Merger Regulation the roughly $55 billion acquisition of Electronic Arts led by Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund with Silver Lake and Affinity Partners, finding limited competition concerns in the relevant markets. The deal still faces review under the EU Foreign Subsidies Regulation, with a decision window widely cited around July 30, plus U.S. CFIUS and other national-security reviews; reporting also cites a contractual outside date around September 28, 2026. GamesIndustry.biz, PC Gamer, and Engadget note that closing would mark one of history’s largest leveraged buyouts and could load EA with more than $20 billion in deal-related debt.

**Links:**

- [GamesIndustry.biz — EU Commission approves $55bn EA buyout](https://www.gamesindustry.biz/eu-commission-approves-55bn-ea-buyout-says-it-would-not-raise-competition-concerns)
- [PC Gamer — Saudi-led EA takeover cleared by European Commission](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/saudi-arabias-takeover-of-ea-gets-approval-from-european-commission/)

**Commentary:** Antitrust is nearly clear; the hard gates are still subsidy and national security—the industry’s biggest LBO is stuck at Brussels’ and Washington’s second checkpoints.
