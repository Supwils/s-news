## II. Regulation & Deals

### 5. EU antitrust clears Saudi-led ~$55B Electronic Arts take-private; foreign-subsidy review still due by Jul 30 (M&A)

**Summary:** The European Commission approved under EU merger rules the acquisition of sole control of Electronic Arts by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, concluding the deal would not raise competition concerns. Coverage centers on a Jul 23 decision covering PC, console, and mobile publishing plus esports commercialization. The ~$55 billion take-private—led by PIF with Silver Lake and Affinity Partners—is widely described as the largest leveraged buyout on record. A separate EU Foreign Subsidies Regulation review remains outstanding with a decision deadline around Jul 30, and other clearances including in the United States are still required. GamesIndustry.biz, PC Gamer, and Engadget continued coverage into the weekend window.

### 6. Paramount agrees to pause ~$111B Warner Bros. Discovery deal until after state AG antitrust trial (Entertainment M&A)

**Summary:** Paramount Skydance reached a stipulation with a coalition of state attorneys general not to close its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery until five days after a merits ruling or Jun 1, 2027, whichever comes first. Deal value is reported around $110–111 billion. Twelve states led by California had already secured a temporary restraining order, arguing the merger would harm competition in cable and theatrical markets. The pact cancels an Aug 3 injunction hearing; if closing slips past Sep 30, Paramount may owe Warner Bros. shareholders a roughly $7 million-per-day ticking fee. Variety, USA Today, and Ars Technica reported on Jul 24–25.
