## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. EU Approves €63 Billion French Offshore Wind Aid: 11.1 GW, 47.8 TWh/Year (Policy · EU)

**Summary:** On July 13, 2026, the European Commission approved a French state aid scheme worth up to €63 billion (~$72 billion) to support the construction and operation of 11 offshore wind farms in the North Sea, Atlantic, and Mediterranean, with combined capacity of up to 11.1 GW and expected annual output of 47.8 TWh—about 10.6% of France's yearly electricity consumption. Aid will run for 25 years through two-way contracts for difference awarded via competitive bidding, with safeguards against compensation during negative price periods. The measure was cleared under the Clean Industrial Deal State Aid Framework (CISAF) adopted on June 25, 2025, and replaces an August 2025 authorization for three earlier projects. Executive Vice-President Teresa Ribera said the decision "clears the way for France's offshore wind support scheme."

### 2. China's 15th Five-Year Carbon Peak Action Plan Gains Traction: 25% Non-Fossil Share by 2030 (Policy · China)

**Summary:** On July 14, 2026, Sina Finance cited expert analysis of the State Council's newly issued 15th Five-Year Carbon Peak Action Plan, which frames 2026–2030 as the critical period for peaking emissions. The plan targets a 17% reduction in CO₂ emissions per unit of GDP versus 2025 and a 25% share of non-fossil energy in consumption by 2030. "Accelerating energy structure optimization" is the top priority, covering non-fossil development, renewable integration, coal substitution, and oil and gas demand restructuring. Industrial measures include zero-carbon parks, factories, and green data centers, plus six major programs on inter-provincial power exchange, coal substitution, sectoral efficiency, zero-carbon transport corridors, and carbon-accounting capacity. Analysts expect follow-on regulations and local implementation plans to turn headline targets into auditable project lists.

### 3. Canada Publishes Pathways CCS MOU: 6 Million Tonnes/Year CO₂ by 2035 (Policy · Canada)

**Summary:** CBC and BNN Bloomberg reported on July 13, 2026 that Alberta, Ottawa, and five oil sands producers signed a trilateral MOU on July 2 to advance the Pathways carbon capture and storage project, linked to a proposed West Coast oil pipeline. The staged plan targets shared transport and storage infrastructure in service by January 1, 2032, with the first-phase goal of capturing, transporting, and storing about 6 million tonnes of CO₂ per year completed by January 1, 2035, and a cumulative net reduction pathway of 16 million tonnes by 2045. Ottawa will extend carbon capture investment tax credits to 2035 and advance operating-cost financing frameworks; Alberta will finalize capture incentives and apply a 120-day approval timeline for qualified projects. Binding definitive agreements are targeted by November 15, 2026, or the MOU expires.

### 4. COP31 President Kurum: Global Electrification Target of 35% by 2035; Hormuz Crisis Raises Urgency (Policy · International)

**Summary:** In a July 10, 2026 Carbon Brief interview, COP31 president-designate and Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum said the share of final energy demand met by electricity should rise from roughly 20% today to 35% by 2035 ("35 by 35"), grounded in IEA and IRENA analysis to keep the 1.5°C goal within reach. The EU, UK, Canada, and the Brazilian COP30 and Ethiopian COP32 presidencies have welcomed the target and confirmed it will be central to November's Antalya COP31 talks. The initiative sits in the presidency's voluntary Action Agenda, parallel to formal negotiations; Turkey itself plans 120 GW of renewable capacity by 2035.

## II. Green Power & Storage

### 6. Philippines MTerra Solar-Storage Project Feeds Luzon Grid: Initial 750 MW Cleared for Testing (Green Power · Asia-Pacific)

**Summary:** BusinessWorld reported on July 14, 2026 that the Philippines Department of Energy said MTerra Solar—the country's largest integrated solar-and-battery project—has received National Grid Corporation of the Philippines approval for an initial 750 MW to enter testing and commissioning and supply the Luzon grid. Located across Nueva Ecija and Bulacan, the ~₱200 billion project comprises 3,500 MWp solar and 4,500 MWh storage, developed by MGEN Renewable Energy, an affiliate of Meralco. The project first energized 250 MW in March; developers plan to add 600 MW this month to reach 1,350 MW online, with phase two due next year. At full build-out it could power about 2.4 million households and provide 850 MW of mid-merit capacity to Meralco under a power supply agreement.

### 7. China Sets 28.04% Certified Efficiency Record for Perovskite-Organic Tandem Solar Cells (Technology · China)

**Summary:** China Economic Net reported on July 14, 2026 that a team led by CAS Academician Li Yongfang and researcher Meng Lei proposed a "full-stage regulation" strategy using a photo-convertible additive molecule to build perovskite-organic tandem solar cells. Third-party certification put steady-state power conversion efficiency at 28.04%, a world record for this device class, with a lab peak of 28.80%. After 625 hours of continuous illumination, the device retained 90% of initial efficiency. Results were published in Nature on July 13. The tandem architecture absorbs visible and near-infrared light in stacked layers, offering thin, lightweight, flexible form factors for building-integrated PV, wearables, drones, and space power.

**Summary:** CNBC reported on July 14, 2026 that President Donald Trump proposed a 20% fee on cargo transiting the Strait of Hormuz and reinstated a blockade on Iranian ports, reviving supply-risk concerns that had eased earlier this month. Lipow Oil Associates estimates that if applied to crude cargoes, the fee would effectively add about $16 per barrel to oil shipped through the strait, though implementation details remain unclear. Sustained traffic declines could eventually force producers to cut output if storage fills. The IEA last week still expected a surplus toward end-2026, contingent on gradual strait recovery.

## IV. Climate & Disasters

### 10. Record El Niño Intensifying: WMO Warns of Rising Extreme-Weather Odds July–September (Climate · Global)

**Summary:** Al Jazeera reported on July 14, 2026 that the International Rescue Committee warned a rapidly strengthening El Niño could bring severe floods, drought, and disease to vulnerable communities in East Africa and Asia. On July 9, the US Climate Prediction Center said there is an 81% chance El Niño becomes one of the strongest events since 1950, likely peaking October–December; the WMO confirmed in early July that El Niño has formed and is expected to strengthen rapidly July–September. East Africa typically sees a drier mid-year followed by wetter October–December rains; Kenya's meteorological service puts an 80%–82% likelihood El Niño persists through the year and has activated its national disaster plan. In Bangladesh's Cox's Bazar camps, floods and landslides since early July have killed at least 15 Rohingya refugees and displaced more than 10,000. The World Bank warns rice yields could fall 20%–50% in hardest-hit parts of South Asia and East Africa if El Niño fully develops.
