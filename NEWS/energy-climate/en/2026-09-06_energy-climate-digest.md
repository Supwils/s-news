### 1. China’s solar capacity surpasses coal for the first time as the largest power source by installed capacity (Policy)
**Summary:** People’s Daily / People.cn reported on Sep 6 that National Energy Administration data show China’s solar PV installed capacity reached 1.286 billion kW by end-July 2026 (704 GW utility-scale, 582 GW distributed), edging past coal’s 1.285 billion kW to become the largest power source by capacity. China Electricity Council Secretary-General Hao Yingjie called it another milestone after coal’s share of generation fell below 50% in the first half. CEC planning official Liu Zhiqiang cautioned that capacity is not the same as generation: solar’s intermittency means coal will remain a near-term system-security backstop, with pumped hydro, new storage, and coordinated grids needed to make solar more dispatchable and predictable.

### 3. German cabinet approves offshore wind auction reform: two-stage CfDs and up to 20% grid overbuild (Policy)
**Summary:** IN Power reported on Sep 4 that Germany’s cabinet on Sep 2 approved a Wind Energy at Sea Act (WindSeeG) amendment and a companion federal transmission-cost support bill, now heading to the Bundesrat and Bundestag. Statutory targets, including about 70 GW of offshore wind by 2045, are retained. Future auctions would use a two-stage model—market allocation first, with contracts for difference where unsupported bidding is too risky—allow offshore grid connections to be overbuilt by up to about 20%, and extend standard operating periods from 25 to 35 years. Officials say the package aims to sustain roughly 3 GW of annual offshore build-out; a separate bill would provide €5.525 billion a year in federal support for transmission costs through 2029.

### 7. ORNL: bleach side reaction more than doubles peak power in salt-water grid batteries (Storage tech)
**Summary:** Oak Ridge National Laboratory reported on Sep 1–2 that researchers found a sodium hypochlorite (bleach) side reaction in salt-water batteries offers higher voltage than the conventional oxygen path. Making that reaction dominant with about 5% added bleach keeps the cycle fully liquid, removes the gas-conversion step and costly catalysts, cuts internal resistance, more than doubles peak power, and brings voltage into a lithium-comparable range. Led by Ruhul Amin and Wooseok Go, with commercialization partner Coulomb Technology, the approach targets grid peak support and outage resilience without critical minerals.

**Links:**

- [ORNL — From side reaction to center stage: Bleach, salt water boost power in grid batteries](https://www.ornl.gov/news/side-reaction-center-stage-bleach-salt-water-boost-power-grid-batteries)

**Commentary:** Elevating an “unwanted” side reaction is the kind of chemistry cheap grid storage actually needs.

### 9. WMO: El Niño set to become very strong, with near-100% chance of lasting through February 2027 (Climate)
**Summary:** The World Meteorological Organization said on Sep 3 that El Niño is established and will intensify to very strong levels in coming months, peaking toward year-end with impacts lasting into 2027. Global Producing Centres put the chance it persists through February 2027 at a near-100% “exceptionally high likelihood”—the most unequivocal WMO El Niño/La Niña Update on record. The Niño 3.4 index averaged about +1.5°C in May–July and +2.0°C in July, with late-July to mid-August weekly values about +2.2°C to +2.6°C; some subsurface anomalies exceeded +8°C. A positive Indian Ocean Dipole and warm tropical Atlantic are also expected. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said El Niño is being “supersized”; WMO’s Celeste Saulo urged exceptional early-warning mobilisation.

### 11. Lake Mead drop threatens ~70% Hoover Dam capacity cut, leaving rural Nevada utilities exposed to rate shock (Disaster)
**Summary:** Energies Media reported on Sep 6 that Lake Mead is projected to fall below 1,035 feet within 12 months. Below that level, twelve older turbines shut down and only five newer units remain, cutting Hoover Dam output from about 1,302 MW to about 382 MW—a roughly 70% collapse. Lincoln County Power District says the dam supplies about 70% of its power today, potentially falling to about 30% by 2027, forcing wholesale market purchases. Overton Power District stresses lost hydro ramping flexibility as much as higher cost. Federal authorization of about $52 million for wide-head turbines under the Help Hoover Dam Act could shrink projected losses from 70% to about 58%, but multi-year manufacturing offers little near-term rate relief. Lincoln County is planning a roughly $3.5 million, 2 MW solar project with about $1.75 million in federal support.

**Links:**
