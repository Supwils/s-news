## I. Policy & Climate Litigation

### 1. U.S. Supreme Court to Open Term with Suncor/Exxon v. Boulder Climate Tort Case (Climate Litigation)
**Summary:** Inside Climate News reported on Aug 23 that the U.S. Supreme Court will hear oral arguments on Oct 5 in *Suncor Energy v. County Commissioners of Boulder County*—the first case of the new term. Suncor and ExxonMobil seek to overturn a Colorado Supreme Court ruling that let Boulder’s climate tort suit proceed, asking whether federal law bars state-law claims for harms tied to interstate and international greenhouse-gas emissions; the justices also added a question on their own jurisdiction. Boulder sued in 2018 over alleged decades of climate deception and local adaptation costs. Dozens of similar U.S. cases remain pre-trial, with nearly 70 amicus briefs filed and the Trump Justice Department among industry backers. A broad federal-preemption ruling could shut down state climate-accountability suits and shift rising adaptation costs onto local taxpayers.

### 2. South Korea Passes Seven Energy Laws: RPS Shifts to Government Bidding PPAs; Community Projects Get Grid Priority (Policy)
**Summary:** Korea Wind Intelligence and Newsian reported that on Aug 20 the National Assembly passed seven amendments, including to the Electricity Business Act and the renewable-energy promotion law. The Renewable Portfolio Standard in place since 2012 will move to a government-led, technology-specific competitive contract market effective Jan 1, 2027; new plants will stop receiving RECs, and winners will sign long-term fixed-price PPAs with KEPCO. The package also grants priority grid access to public-interest community projects such as “Sunshine Income Villages,” allows shared renewable interconnection facilities, and temporarily opens backbone-grid construction to private participants. Officials say the old RPS expanded capacity but did little to cut costs or build a domestic industry; the new design aims to improve bankability and align build-out with power-system planning.

**Links:**

- [Korea Wind Intelligence — Korea Overhauls Renewable Energy System: RPS Replaced, Grid Priority Access Expanded](https://www.koreawindintel.com/korea-overhauls-renewable-energy-system-rps-replaced-grid-priority-access-expanded/)
- [Newsian Biz — 'Sunshine Income Villages' Granted Priority Access; RPS Reformed After 15 Years](https://www.newsianbiz.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=1109)

**Commentary:** Replacing certificate spot markets with government long-term contracts is the classic mature-market shift from “build fast” to “financeable.”

### 3. Greece Gazettes New Renewable Spatial Framework: Tighter Wind/Solar Siting, Storage Recognized for Balancing (Policy)
**Summary:** Parapolitika and others reported that Greece’s Special Spatial Framework for Renewable Energy Sources (SSF-RES) was approved by ministerial decree and published in the Government Gazette on Aug 19, replacing the 2008 framework to match NECP goals of at least 43% renewables in final energy use and about 75.7% in electricity by 2030. The rules cover wind, solar, storage and small hydro, aiming to move from rapid rollout to organized siting, with bans or strict limits in high-tourism zones, high-altitude mountains, metropolitan areas and some protected sites; the gazette stresses storage’s role in cutting curtailment and softening prices. Earlier consultation drafts barred wind above 1,200 meters and in Attica, metropolitan Thessaloniki and small islands, while exempting advanced-licensed projects, rooftop solar and large pumped storage.

## II. Geopolitical Energy & Oil/Gas Transition

### 4. Macron and Saudi Crown Prince to Discuss Oil Pipelines Bypassing the Strait of Hormuz (Geopolitical Oil)
**Summary:** The National reported on Aug 23 that expanding pipelines and alternative export routes around the Strait of Hormuz will be a major focus of Monday talks in Paris between French President Emmanuel Macron and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. Iran has effectively closed the strait since February’s U.S.-Israeli war on Iran; about 20% of global crude previously moved through the waterway. A Macron adviser said Saudi Aramco is studying expansion of the East-West pipeline from Abqaiq to Yanbu on the Red Sea and new export routes; Sumed and the UAE Habshan–Fujairah line are also in play, with Saudi-related work described as “quite advanced.” CMA CGM’s earlier ~$500 million Oman logistics-corridor deal is cited as helping bypass efforts. The June Versailles U.S.-Iran MoU has collapsed, and Gulf shipping and insurance risks remain elevated.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** When strait reopenings become a war of attrition, pipeline and Red Sea corridor capex become the medium-term oil-market anchor.

### 5. Cleveland-Cliffs and DOE Recast Middletown Works Plan: Clean-Steel Grant Path Diverted Toward Keeping Coal (Industrial Decarbonization)
**Summary:** CleanTechnica / Sierra Club reported on Aug 21 that Cleveland-Cliffs and the U.S. Department of Energy announced a revised Middletown Works plan in Ohio: abandoning the March 2024 selection for up to $500 million under the Industrial Demonstrations Program to replace the coal blast furnace with a hydrogen-ready DRI plant and electric melting furnaces, and instead continuing coal use while seeking a permit for an “advanced” natural-gas cogeneration unit that burns blast-furnace gas—technology the company already runs at other blast-furnace sites. The original plan had projected about 1 million metric tons of annual CO₂ cuts, preserving roughly 2,500 union jobs and sharp local air-quality gains. Environmental groups argue federal funds are now reinforcing coal-based operations; nearby residents say pollution burdens will persist.

**Links:**

## III. Clean Power, Storage & Nuclear

### 6. DOE Adds up to $1 Billion for X-energy: Four Xe-100 SMRs at Dow’s Seadrift, Texas (Nuclear)
**Summary:** Energies Media reported on Aug 23 that X-energy CEO Clay Sell told an earnings call the U.S. DOE Advanced Reactor Demonstration Program will provide up to $1 billion more for the Dow partnership at Seadrift, Texas, lifting total public support since 2021 to as much as $2.15 billion; the new award still requires a 50/50 cost share. The project would install four Xe-100 small modular reactors totaling about 320 MW of electricity and industrial steam to replace aging fossil energy and steam units at the petrochemical site, aiming to be North America’s first grid-scale advanced reactor serving an industrial facility, with operations targeted for the early 2030s. An NRC environmental assessment milestone is complete and a construction-permit application is under review; Sell called the DOE grant the company’s largest revenue source at its current stage.

### 10. Vietnam’s Storm Narra Leaves One Dead; Widespread Flood and Landslide Damage in Lang Son (Disaster)
**Summary:** VietnamPlus reported that as of 11:00 on Aug 23, Storm Narra—the fourth storm to enter the East Sea this year—plus heavy rain, floods and landslides had left one person dead in Lang Son province after a landslide struck a house, with 205 houses damaged or unroofed and 2,506 inundated there. About 7,877 hectares of crops were flooded or damaged (roughly 6,100 ha in Lang Son), 35 landslide or flood sites disrupted traffic, and some 6,100 livestock and poultry died in Lang Son. Authorities evacuated 2,965 households (2,736 in Lang Son) and sent safety messages via Zalo to about 11.6 million subscribers while keeping dyke and reservoir watches on 24-hour duty. The slow-moving system had earlier forecasts of more than 500 mm of rain in places.
