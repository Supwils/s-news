## I. Climate Disasters & Extreme Weather

### 1. Contiguous U.S. posts hottest month on record in July; wildfires top 6.3 million acres (Science · U.S.)
**Summary:** NOAA and multiple outlets confirmed on Aug 10–11 that the contiguous U.S. averaged about 76.89°F (24.94°C) in July 2026, roughly 0.12°F above the Dust Bowl July 1936 mark and the hottest calendar month in records back to 1895. Below-average precipitation left about 71% of the Lower 48 in abnormal dryness or drought. National Interagency Fire Center figures through Aug 10 show roughly 6.35 million acres burned—already more than all of 2025 and the highest for this date in at least a decade. Scientists explicitly link the heat–drought–fire stack to fossil-fuel-driven climate change.

### 2. Nearly three-quarters of England in drought: 45 million affected, 27 million+ under water curbs (Disaster · U.K.)
**Summary:** England’s Environment Agency and National Drought Group said on Aug 10 that 71.3% of England is now in drought, putting about 45 million people in drought areas and more than 27 million under Temporary Use Bans. East Midlands, Lincolnshire & Northamptonshire, Kent & East Sussex, and Solent & South Downs newly entered drought status. July was the driest for England and Wales since records began in 1836; only about 14% of rivers are at normal levels, 22% are exceptionally low, and reservoir storage sits near 69%, below seasonal norms.
