## I. Climate Shock & Power Supply

### 1. Hungary’s Paks nuclear plant fully offline for first time as Danube drought cuts ~40% of power (Disaster · Europe)
**Summary:** Prime Minister Péter Magyar confirmed that Hungary’s sole nuclear plant, Paks—rated about 2,000 MW and supplying roughly 40% of national electricity—completed its first full shutdown in 44 years on Aug 2 after record-low Danube cooling-water levels. River gauges may fall to −144 cm, well below the 2018 record of −98 cm; −134 cm triggers a full stop. The government warned of “the most critical five days” ahead as Budapest faces possible 40°C heat, urged peak-hour conservation between 5–10 p.m., prepared rotating industrial curtailment, and ramped up power imports. The European Commission said it was monitoring closely and ready to convene an emergency Electricity Coordination Group meeting.

### 2. Greek wildfire near Athens spreads; two firefighting helicopters collide; France’s Gironde megafire still burns (Wildfire · Europe)
**Summary:** On Aug 2, a three-day wildfire northwest of Athens kept nearly 500 firefighters and more than 100 engines engaged, threatening Megara’s industrial zone and coastal towns and destroying over 100 homes; two firefighting helicopters collided during operations. Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said gale-force winds often near 100 km/h exceeded operational plans. In southwestern France, the Gironde blaze that scorched about 420 km² in 10 days—roughly four times the area of Paris—and once forced more than 224,000 evacuations remained burning under the highest alert; Spain’s major fronts eased. A World Weather Attribution rapid analysis found human-caused climate change at least doubled the likelihood of the compounding drivers behind severe fire weather in France and Spain.

### 3. Record-low Danube squeezes SE Europe: Romania’s Cernavoda and Serbia’s hydropower under strain (Regional · Energy)
**Summary:** Romania’s Cernavoda Unit 1 is offline due to unprecedented Danube lows; Unit 2 remains online after safety checks but officials said it can hold only about four to five more days under present flows, prompting barge-guided flow works and dredging. The plant supplies roughly one-fifth of national power. Serbia’s energy minister said Danube levels were the lowest since 1985, cutting output at major hydropower assets including Iron Gates to about 20% of normal. Bulgaria’s Kozloduy also faces low-water risk. Neighboring governments signaled readiness to assist as Hungary’s nuclear fleet went dark, underscoring tightly coupled cross-border grid and hydrological risk.

## II. Policy, Power Mix & Carbon Markets

### 4. China’s H1 coal-power generation share falls below 50% for first time; renewables top 40% (Transition · China)
**Summary:** National Energy Administration data show H1 2026 coal-fired generation at 2.5 trillion kWh, or 49.7% of total generation—the first half-year share below 50%. Renewable generation neared 2 trillion kWh (41.2%), with wind and solar together about 1.25 trillion kWh, roughly one-quarter of society-wide electricity use. By end-June, combined wind and solar capacity reached 1.95 billion kW (+16.8% YoY), and total renewable capacity 2.455 billion kW, about 60.7% of national installed capacity. Chinese media including CCTV Finance continued to frame the milestone on Aug 2.

### 5. China’s operating plus approved-under-construction nuclear fleet reaches 135 GW, largest globally (Nuclear · China)
**Summary:** Citing the National Energy Administration, People’s Daily Overseas Edition reported on Aug 2 that mainland China has 120 nuclear units operating or approved for construction totaling 135 GW—the world’s largest combined scale. Of these, 62 units (66.14 GW) are operating (second globally, 1.6% of national power capacity) and 58 units (69.51 GW) are approved under construction (first globally). The 15th Five-Year carbon-peaking action plan targets about 110 GW of operating nuclear by 2030, with officials stressing nuclear’s role as firm low-carbon support for high renewable shares.

### 6. U.S. EPA stops monetizing PM2.5 and ozone health benefits in power-plant rule analysis (Policy · United States)
**Summary:** Analyses around early August note that under Administrator Lee Zeldin, EPA’s regulatory impact analyses for combustion-turbine/power-plant rules no longer assign dollar values to health benefits from cutting fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and ozone, citing excessive scientific uncertainty, while still monetizing industry compliance costs. A Aug 2 Slate essay argues the shift systematically tips cost-benefit scales toward industry costs; critics say unmonetized health gains effectively “do not exist” in decisions and ease Clean Air Act rollbacks. EPA says it still considers health impacts qualitatively and continues to quantify emission changes.

## III. Clean Power, Storage & Oil/Gas

### 8. India Cabinet clears PM Surya Sarovar Yojana: 5,000 MW floating solar plus 10,000 MWh storage (Clean power · India)
**Summary:** India’s Union Cabinet approved the Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY) with a ₹5,070 crore outlay to build 5,000 MW of floating solar on reservoirs and inland waterbodies, mandating co-located energy storage of at least two hours totaling 10,000 MWh. Projects will be sanctioned in FY2026–27 through FY2030–31, with support disbursed through FY2032–33. Central financial assistance is ₹1 crore/MW after commissioning, plus up to ₹50 lakh per project for bathymetry and other studies. NISE estimates ~102 GW floating-solar potential versus ~700 MW installed today; officials project about 10 million tonnes of CO₂ avoided yearly.
