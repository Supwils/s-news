## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. Ember: Europe Could Add ~25 GW of Wind and Solar via Hydropower Hybridisation Without New Grid Capacity (Policy · EU)
**Summary:** Euronews reported on **Jul 21, 2026** that energy think tank Ember finds seven major EU hydropower markets—Austria, Bulgaria, France, Italy, Portugal, Romania and Spain—could integrate about **25 GW** of wind and solar without building extra grid capacity by “hybridising” renewables (and storage) behind existing hydropower connection points, covering roughly **18%** of expected new renewables by **2030** in those markets. Ember has previously warned that more than about **120 GW** of planned renewables are at risk from bottlenecks and insufficient grid capacity. Only Portugal and Spain currently have specific hybrid-project rules; the report calls for national legislation and faster prioritisation of hybrids.

**Commentary:** Europe’s bottleneck has moved from “can we build it?” to “can we connect it?”—hybridisation buys time with existing plugs, but only if regulators write the rules.

### 2. Germany Plans EEG Reforms: Lower Support in Congested Areas, Phase Down Rooftop Solar Incentives from 2027 (Policy · Germany)
**Summary:** Reporting on **Jul 21, 2026** said the German government is preparing major changes to the Renewable Energy Act (EEG) before the current support framework expires at end-**2026**. Proposals would limit financial support for new wind and solar in grid-congested areas; projects that forgo compensation when curtailed could gain automatic connections, under somewhat softer conditions than earlier drafts. Small rooftop solar incentives would gradually fall from **2027**, tapering toward about **2030**, rather than ending guaranteed payments outright. Germany still aims for roughly **80%** renewable electricity by **2030**; industry groups warn compromise drafts could still slow buildout.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Berlin is redrawing the subsidy map to relieve the grid—targets stay ambitious, but money and queue priority are shifting toward where electrons can actually land.

**Summary:** Reuters reported on **Jul 21, 2026** that investors see the European Commission’s Friday ETS overhaul—linking free CO2 allowances to decarbonisation investment in Europe—as potentially improving accountability while risking weaker near-term abatement incentives. The package would slow the pace of cap cuts and extend free permits for some sectors to about **2038**; from about **2031**, roughly **80%** of free allowances would be issued after an approved investment plan, with the remaining about **20%** released after verified delivery. ### 4. US 2026 Siting Round-Up: 29 Laws Enacted, 16 Permissive, Only One Restrictive (Policy · United States)
**Summary:** SolarQuarter reported on **Jul 21, 2026** that the non-partisan Siting Solutions Project’s *The State of Siting: 2026 Legislative Round-Up* finds enacted clean-energy siting laws mostly supported deployment despite a surge of restrictive bills. Of **29** siting-related laws enacted nationwide, **16** were classified as permissive, **12** as neutral procedural changes, and only **one** as restrictive. Virginia set statewide solar siting standards and allowed co-located storage by right in some cases; Illinois strengthened its framework; Oregon created temporary clean-energy permitting pathways; Washington backed community benefit agreements and limited overly restrictive local ordinances. Rising power demand, including from data centers, framed many debates.

## II. Clean Power & Storage

### 5. China Leads Global CSP Additions: 2.1 GW Operating, 3.2 GW Under Construction (Clean Power · China)
**Summary:** Science and Technology Daily and People’s Daily reported on **Jul 21, 2026**, citing the China Electricity Council, that by end-**June** China had **24** operating concentrating solar power (CSP) projects totaling **2.1 GW**—second globally after Spain—and **26** projects under construction totaling **3.2 GW**, making China the dominant source of new CSP capacity. Officials said China has mastered tower, trough and Fresnel technologies with equipment localisation above **95%**; unit costs have fallen from about **RMB 30,000/kW** a decade ago to about **RMB 15,000/kW**, and feed-in tariffs from about **RMB 1.15/kWh** on early demos to about **RMB 0.60/kWh**. CGN’s **350 MW** Golmud CSP demo has broken ground using the domestically developed “Yuanyao-1” 8.6-metre molten-salt trough collector, with average photoelectric conversion efficiency above **17%**.

**Links:**

- [People’s Daily — China has become the absolute main force in new global CSP capacity](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0721/c1004-40764632.html)
- [China Economic Net — China has become the absolute main force in new global CSP capacity](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/kj/202607/t20260721_3098324.shtml)

**Commentary:** CSP’s strategic value is dispatchable, storage-ready clean power—not another PV substitute—and China is industrialising that flexibility at scale.

The Saudi-led coalition said it would respond with force and protect ships in the strait.

### 8. Documents Suggest Oil and Gas Industry Long Knew Methane Emissions Were Understated (Transition · Accountability)
**Summary:** Coverage on **Jul 21, 2026** of a Center for Climate Integrity report said industry documents indicate oil and gas firms knew for decades that wells could release far more planet-warming methane than publicly acknowledged. Examples include a **1979** report commissioned by the research arm of the American Gas Association on underreported greenhouse gases from major US gas fields. Scientific evidence has since grown; a landmark **2018** *Science* study found the US oil and gas supply chain leaked roughly **60%** more methane than EPA estimates. The release comes as companies invest more in leak detection, repair and reduced routine flaring—while critics argue the historical gap between private knowledge and public messaging remains damning.

**Links:**

- [DNYUZ — Oil Firms Knew for Decades of Methane’s Danger to Planet, Documents Suggest](https://dnyuz.com/2026/07/21/oil-firms-knew-for-decades-of-methanes-danger-to-planet-documents-suggest/)
