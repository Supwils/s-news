**Summary:** Nine O'Clock and related coverage on Sep 5 reported that COP31 President-Designate Murat Kurum, on day one of the Istanbul Climate Finance Summit, called for a sharp scale-up in climate finance and argued climate investment should be treated as a strategic security priority on a par with long-term defence spending. COP31 will be held in Antalya, Türkiye, from Nov 9–20, 2026.

### 2. Colorado ranchers file Supreme Court amicus brief in Boulder climate case (Litigation)
**Summary:** The Los Angeles Times reported on Sep 5 that seven Colorado ranchers filed a friend-of-the-court brief supporting Boulder city and county in their suit against Suncor Energy and Exxon Mobil, arguing fossil-fuel firms should help pay climate damages. Ranchers cite record-low Rocky Mountain snowpack, drying streams and soaring feed costs; Colorado temperatures have risen about 2.3°F since 1980, and the Southwest has been in a megadrought since 2000. Oral argument is set for Oct 5; the companies say federal law bars state-court claims for interstate greenhouse-gas harms and note nearly 60 similar suits nationwide.

### 3. BLM proposes streamlined oil and gas permitting in Alaska’s NPR-A (Oil policy)
**Summary:** The U.S. Bureau of Land Management announced on Sep 4 a proposed rule, following an Alaska Oil and Gas Association petition, to streamline permitting across the roughly 23-million-acre National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska by replacing case-by-case environmental reviews with a standardized framework and a roughly 60-day decision clock for qualifying production sites. Publication in the Federal Register is expected Sep 8, with comments due Nov 9. Oil & Gas Journal reporting says eligibility roughly requires sites within about 25 miles of existing permanent oil and gas infrastructure and outside no-surface-occupancy areas unless otherwise allowed. BLM frames the move as energy-security policy under the Trump administration; environmental groups and some Indigenous leaders warn of weaker public oversight.

**Links:**

## III. Clean Power & Storage

### 5. Pakistan’s Nepra clears 10% battery mandate for 800 MW wind-solar auction (Storage)
**Summary:** Independent Pakistan reported on Sep 5 that the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority on Friday approved wheeling-auction amendments requiring developers bidding for a planned ~800 MW of wind and solar to install on-site battery storage equal to at least 10% of the plant’s firm capacity. The rule applies first to an initial ~400 MW tranche, with the Independent System Operator cleared to issue a formal RFP as soon as this month. Officials aim to blunt a worsening duck-curve evening ramp as rooftop solar spreads; the auction is Islamabad’s first competitive renewable tender in more than three decades. Stakeholders sought a fully independent grievance body; Nepra instead set a three-member committee chaired by the Private Power and Infrastructure Board.

**Links:**

- [Independent Pakistan — Nepra clears battery storage mandate for 800-megawatt wind, solar auction](https://independent-pakistan.com/news/nepra-clears-battery-storage-mandate-for-800-megawatt-wind-solar-auction/)

**Commentary:** South Asia is writing storage floors into auction tickets—bids now compete on dispatchable megawatt-hours, not price alone.

### 6. India’s CEA draft: mandatory co-located storage and grid-forming inverters from 2027 (Policy)
**Summary:** PV Tech reported on Sep 4 that India’s Central Electricity Authority issued a Sep 3 draft technical-standards amendment proposing that renewable plants commissioned from Jul 1, 2027 have at least 15% of inverters with grid-forming control, and that ground-mounted solar and onshore wind add co-located storage of at least 10% of installed capacity with a minimum two-hour duration (e.g., 100 MW needing at least 10 MW/20 MWh). For projects commissioned from Jul 1, 2029 through Jun 30, 2031, minimum duration would rise to four hours. Comments are due by Oct 4, 2026. India had about 288 GW of renewables as of end-June 2026, including roughly 162 GW solar and 57 GW wind.

**Links:**

- [PV Tech — India’s CEA proposes mandatory storage for new solar, wind projects from 2027](https://www.pv-tech.org/indias-cea-proposes-mandatory-storage-for-new-solar-wind-projects-from-2027/)

**Commentary:** Pairing storage mandates with grid-forming rules shows India’s next bottleneck is integration capacity, not nameplate watts.
