## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. Trump signs emergency order restricting foreign-made bulk-power grid equipment (Policy)
**Summary:** On Aug 26, President Donald Trump declared a national emergency and signed an executive order barring the acquisition, importation and installation of certain foreign-produced bulk-power system equipment, including large transformers, utility-scale inverters, battery energy storage systems, high-voltage circuit breakers, industrial control systems and generation turbines. The order covers transactions involving entities linked to U.S. arms embargoes or sanctions regimes, and countries the Energy Secretary deems detrimental to U.S. national security; China is widely seen as the primary commercial supplier affected across solar, battery and electrical-component supply chains. The Department of Energy has 120 days (until Dec 24, 2026) to issue implementing rules and may recommend isolating, monitoring or removing already-installed gear; low-voltage distribution-only assets are generally out of scope.

### 2. Thailand aims to double clean-power share to 60%, cutting reliance on imported LNG (Policy)
**Summary:** Bloomberg reported on Aug 27 that Thai Energy Minister Akanat Promphan said an updated 25-year power plan due in October will target generating about 60% of electricity from clean sources such as solar, wind and hydro within 25 years—roughly double the previous goal. Natural gas currently supplies more than 60% of Thai power, mostly imported, leaving the country exposed after Middle East conflict pushed seaborne LNG prices higher; renewables are about 15% of the mix today. The new plan will include nuclear for the first time, with the government studying small modular reactors as firm low-carbon baseload; Akanat also defended a roughly 200-billion-baht ($6.1 billion) renewables spending proposal in parliament, including village rooftop solar.

### 3. European Commission clears Greece's €4.77 billion Social Climate Plan against ETS2 costs (Carbon markets)
**Summary:** On Aug 27 the European Commission endorsed Greece's Social Climate Plan—the fifth and largest national plan under the Social Climate Fund. From 2026 to 2032 the plan will mobilize about €4.77 billion, with 75% (€3.57 billion) from the Fund and 25% from Greece, financing efficiency renovations, heat pumps and solar water heating, electric buses and charging to shield vulnerable households and micro-enterprises from higher building and road-transport fuel carbon costs under ETS2. Greek and Commission figures put expected annual CO₂ cuts at about 811,000 tonnes by 2032 and support for roughly 460,000 vulnerable households; Greece may request a first payment in Q1 2027 once implementation starts and early milestones are met.

## II. Renewables & Storage

### 5. Ember: 2026 battery additions could shift 34% of new daily solar to non-solar hours (Storage)
**Summary:** UK think tank Ember finds solar supplied just over 10% of global electricity in H1 2026, up from 8.9% in H1 2025, but output remains heavily midday-concentrated; in Chile, the Netherlands and Germany, solar can meet more than half of local demand at noon yet fall near zero after dark. Ember projects about 459 GWh of new battery installations in 2026, up roughly 50% from 307 GWh in 2025—enough in theory to shift about 34% of new daily solar generation to non-solar hours (versus ~18% for 2025 additions and ~4% in 2021). Average global battery installation costs fell about 95% from 2010 to 2025, from roughly $2,634/kWh to $140/kWh; the report still stresses market rules plus wind, hydro, nuclear and long-duration storage remain essential.

**Links:**

- [ESS News — Battery boom brings round-the-clock solar closer, Ember says](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/08/27/battery-boom-brings-round-the-clock-solar-closer-ember-says/)

**Commentary:** Round-the-clock solar is no longer mainly a module problem—it is a cells-and-dispatch-rules problem as storage costs rewrite the diurnal curve.

### 7. Australia connects Quorn Park, first solar-BESS hybrid dispatched as a single generator (Renewables)
**Summary:** ESS News reported on Aug 27 that Potentia Energy—a joint venture of Enel Group and Inpex—has completed and connected the Quorn Park solar hybrid in New South Wales, a roughly A$190 million (USD 136.4 million) project with 98 MW solar and a 20 MW / 40 MWh BESS (about 160,000 panels and 16 BYD battery units). It is the first National Electricity Market project approved and dispatched under a single hybrid generator performance standard (GPS): co-located solar and storage previously needed separate registrations and connection agreements, whereas Quorn Park uses one AC-coupled plant controller at a single point of connection, operating in solar-only, battery-only or hybrid modes under a 10-year PPA with Zen Energy.

### 8. DRC Kamoa copper mine starts Africa's claimed first 24/7 solar-baseload plant (Renewables)
**Summary:** CrossBoundary Energy said its 233 MWp solar PV plus 123 MVA / 526 MWh BESS hybrid for Kamoa Copper has reached commercial operation; Energy-Storage.News covered the Aug 27 story as Africa's first plant of its kind to deliver round-the-clock renewable power. ### 9. China deploys 100 kW bamboo-framed floating solar platform off Yantai (Solar)
**Summary:** The South China Morning Post reported on Aug 27 that China has launched Jilin-2, described as the world's first 100 kW floating solar farm built with bamboo composite frames, at a demonstration base off Yantai in Shandong. The platform uses bamboo-wound tubes and "sea bamboo" pipes and is designed to cut marine environmental impact versus steel or HDPE floats, supplying power to an integrated hydrogen-ammonia-methanol demonstration project. ### 11. Experts warn developing El Niño may peak late 2026, raising 2027 Yangtze flood risk (Climate)
**Summary:** Xinhua reporting carried on Aug 27 says the current El Niño is developing quickly and could peak in November–December 2026, with intensity potentially among the strongest on record. Experts say El Niño years tend to bring wetter autumns in southern China and a higher chance of a warm winter, while the following summer often features a stronger, southward West Pacific subtropical high that raises the odds of major Yangtze-basin flooding. Preliminary views point to generally wetter conditions across central-eastern China next year, higher rainstorm and flood risk in the middle and lower Yangtze, and possibly sharper heatwaves in the Jianghuai, Jiangnan and South China regions—while stressing El Niño is not the only driver and outcomes still need monitoring.

**Links:**

- [NetEase / Xinhua — How could a strong El Niño affect China's climate?](https://www.163.com/news/article/L5CB3OVN000189FH.html)

**Commentary:** For flood control and energy systems, the hard exam often arrives the summer after the peak—year-ahead risk communication is itself an adaptation measure.

### 12. U.S. Army Janus program: up to $2.2 billion for microreactors at five bases (Nuclear)
**Summary:** The Associated Press reported that the U.S. Army on Wednesday selected five companies under its Janus program to own, build and operate nuclear microreactors at bases from New York to Texas, with awards totaling up to about $2.2 billion over five years if milestones are met and more than 20 microreactors ultimately expected. Selected firms include Antares at Fort Bragg, BWXT at Fort Campbell, General Atomics Electromagnetic Systems at Fort Hood, Radiant at Fort Benning and Westinghouse Government Services at Fort Drum; each reactor would supply roughly 1–20 MW for critical loads and resilience, licensed by the Army rather than the NRC. Critics call the funding a hidden subsidy for designs that struggle to find private buyers; the Army frames the effort as accelerating advanced reactors toward lasting baseload and says it is working with DOE on spent-fuel removal.
