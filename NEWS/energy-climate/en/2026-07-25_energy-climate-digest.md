## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. US Government Admits Canceling $7.6 Billion in Clean-Energy Grants Based on State Politics

**Summary:** The US government acknowledged in federal court filings that the Department of Energy recommended canceling clean-energy awards solely according to recipient states' "political identity," ultimately terminating 284 grants in October 2025 across 16 states that backed the Democratic presidential candidate in 2024. The roughly $7.6 billion in affected funding covered battery factories, hydrogen, grid upgrades, and carbon capture, contradicting earlier claims that project economics drove the decisions.

### 3. India's First Mandatory Carbon Market Enters Reporting Phase Ahead of October Trading

**Summary:** About 490 Indian factories across seven energy-intensive sectors, including aluminium, cement, textiles, and petrochemicals, must submit verified emissions-intensity data to the Bureau of Energy Efficiency by Jul 31. Regulated trading is expected to begin in October, allowing outperformers to sell Carbon Credit Certificates while companies missing targets must buy certificates or potentially face penalties set at twice the average market price.

**Links:**

- [Economic Times Brand Equity — India’s first carbon compliance season](https://brandequity.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/marketing/october-2026-india-inc-s-first-compliance-season-for-carbon/132617350)

**Commentary:** India is moving corporate emissions from voluntary disclosure to a tradable, enforceable metric that creates a visible cost gap between peers.

## II. Clean Power, Storage & Grids

### 4. China Report Says Wind and Solar Reached 1.84 TW in 2025 and Overtook Thermal Power

**Summary:** China's 2026 Energy Industry Ecosystem Report, released Jul 25, says wind and solar capacity rose from 530 GW in 2020 to 1.84 TW in 2025 and surpassed thermal power during the 14th Five-Year Plan period. It says investment in major national energy projects exceeded CNY 3.5 trillion for the first time in 2025, up nearly 11%, while crude production remained near 200 million tonnes for a fourth consecutive year.

### 5. Renewables Reach 26.2% of EU Final Energy Use, Still 16.3 Points Short of 2030 Target

**Summary:** Eurostat's latest provisional data show renewables supplied 26.2% of EU gross final energy consumption in 2025, up from 25.2% in 2024, while renewable electricity reached 49.9% of gross power consumption. Reaching the binding 42.5% target for 2030 would require an average increase of about 3.3 percentage points each year from 2026 through 2030.

**Links:**

- [Eurostat — Renewables make up 26% of EU energy use](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20260723-1)

**Commentary:** Power-sector decarbonization is nearing the halfway mark, but slower heating and transport progress means the annual pace must accelerate sharply.

### 6. Slovakia's Largest Battery Facility Opens at Retired Coal Plant

**Summary:** Slovenské Elektrárne commissioned Slovakia's largest battery storage facility at the former Nováky lignite power station, with 36 MW of output and 72 MWh of capacity connected through existing substation equipment using 22 battery containers. The project will provide frequency containment reserves and shift surplus power into peak periods, with nearly €3.7 million in Recovery and Resilience Plan support for its 24 MW section.

### 7. Tunisia Authorizes Residential Solar-Plus-Storage Amid Heatwave and Outages

**Summary:** Tunisia's state utility STEG authorized low-voltage customers to install photovoltaic storage systems with hybrid inverters, allowing households and businesses to use stored solar electricity during load-shedding outages. The move follows record electricity demand and recurring blackouts during a sustained heatwave, with STEG also issuing minimum technical rules for system design, installation, operation, and maintenance.

**Links:**

- [pv magazine — Tunisia authorizes residential solar-plus-storage amid heatwave](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/07/24/tunisia-authorizes-residential-solar-plus-storage-amid-heatwave/)

**Commentary:** Extreme heat is turning distributed solar and batteries from an efficiency investment into resilience infrastructure needed by households and grids alike.

### 8. Ordos Zero-Carbon Airport Reports a 95.15% Comprehensive Emissions Reduction

**Summary:** Ordos Ejin Horo International Airport unveiled results from an integrated wind-solar-storage-cooling-heating-power system that has replaced gas boilers and refrigerant-based cooling since March 2025, lifting clean energy above 90% of supply. The project absorbs more than 21 million kWh of wind and solar power annually, cuts heating and cooling energy use by 60% and operating costs by 50%, and has received a verified comprehensive emissions-reduction rate of 95.15%.
