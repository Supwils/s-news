### 1. UN Climate Chief: Faster Energy Transitions Are Now Core to National Security

**Summary:** UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell told a UN General Assembly dialogue that fossil fuel dependence is amplifying price volatility, conflict, and climate-disaster risks, making the clean energy transition an issue of national security and economic resilience. He reiterated commitments to triple renewable capacity and double the rate of energy-efficiency improvement by 2030, while urging developed countries to deliver $300 billion a year in adaptation finance by 2035.

### 2. China Establishes Minimum Renewable Consumption Shares With Binding Assessments From Aug 1

**Summary:** Measures issued by China's economic planning and energy authorities take effect on Aug 1, 2026, assigning renewable consumption and offtake responsibilities to both provincial regions and key energy-intensive industries. The system brings non-electric renewable consumption into minimum-share targets for the first time and introduces quarterly monitoring and annual evaluation, with noncompliant key enterprises facing interviews, public notices, and credit-record consequences.

### 4. EU Opens Carbon-Market Reform Talks as Divisions Emerge Over Competitiveness and Climate Ambition

**Summary:** EU member states began discussing an overhaul of the Emissions Trading System under Ireland's presidency, with disputes centered on slowing allowance reductions, extending some free allocation, permitting limited international credits, and requiring countries to direct half of ETS revenue to industrial decarbonization. The European Commission says the changes remain consistent with the bloc's target to cut net emissions 90% by 2040, but governments differ over how to balance costs, competitiveness, and binding reductions.

**Links:**

## II. Clean Power, Storage & Grids

### 5. IEA: Renewables Are Set to Overtake Coal in Global Power Generation in 2026

**Summary:** The International Energy Agency expects global electricity demand to grow 3.6% in 2026 and 3.8% in 2027, reaching 30,700 TWh by 2027. Renewable generation is forecast to rise by more than 8% and surpass coal in 2026, increasing its global share from 33% in 2025 to 37% in 2027, while solar output grows by about 600 TWh this year and overtakes wind as the second-largest renewable source after hydropower.

**Links:**

- [IEA — Electricity Mid-Year Update 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-mid-year-update-2026)
- [IEA — Global electricity demand growth set to accelerate](https://www.iea.org/news/global-electricity-demand-growth-set-to-accelerate-as-power-systems-adjust-to-recent-shocks)

**Commentary:** Renewables passing coal is a structural milestone, but grid capacity, storage, and flexibility are replacing generation cost as the transition's main constraints.
