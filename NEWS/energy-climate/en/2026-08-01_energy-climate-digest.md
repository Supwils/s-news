## I. Policy & Transition

### 1. China’s renewable consumption floor and RPS rules take effect today (Policy · China)
**Summary:** The Implementation Measures on Minimum Renewable Energy Consumption Targets and Renewable Power Consumption Responsibility Weights (Order No. 42), jointly issued by China’s NDRC, MIIT, MOHURD and MOT, took effect on August 1, 2026. The rules for the first time fold non-electric renewable use (heat/cooling, hydrogen, biofuels, etc.) into minimum consumption targets and refine provincial renewable power responsibility weights. Priority energy-intensive sectors—electrolytic aluminum, steel, cement, polysilicon and new data centers at national hub nodes—face green-power share monitoring and assessment. Non-compliant entities may buy green certificates to catch up, or face interviews, public notice and credit penalties.

**Commentary:** Shifting assessment from provincial balancing alone onto key industrial users embeds green-power procurement and certificates directly into compliance costs.

### 2. Huaneng Changjiang Unit 3 connected to the grid: Hainan’s first Hualong One online (Nuclear · China)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on August 1 that China Huaneng said Unit 3 of the Changjiang Phase II project—Hainan’s first Hualong One plant and Huaneng’s controlling large PWR project—was connected to the grid that day. The project plans two 1.2 GW units using China’s third-generation PWR design; once complete it is expected to generate about 18 TWh a year, save more than 6.32 million tonnes of standard coal and cut roughly 11.6 million tonnes of CO₂ annually, supplying baseload clean power for the Hainan Free Trade Port.

### 3. Carbon Brief: Germany’s wind and solar outpaced fossil fuels in 2025 (Transition · Europe)
**Summary:** Carbon Brief’s analysis of Energy Institute Statistical Review data finds that in 2025 German wind and solar together generated 225 TWh (44% of electricity), edging past 217 TWh (43%) from fossil fuels—the first such crossover—while the EU as a whole hit a similar milestone the same year. Germany still aims for 80% renewable electricity by 2030 and economy-wide net zero by 2045, and approved a record 20.8 GW of onshore wind in 2025; coal use remains far higher than most European peers, with an official phaseout no later than 2038.

**Links:**

- [Carbon Brief — Wind and solar overtake fossil fuels in Germany for first time](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-wind-and-solar-power-overtake-fossil-fuels-in-germany-for-first-time-ever)

## II. Climate Shock & Power Security

### 4. Record-low Danube forces historic full shutdown of Hungary’s Paks nuclear plant (Disaster · Europe)
**Summary:** Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar confirmed around August 1 that the country’s only nuclear plant at Paks—nearly half of national generation, normally about 2,000 MW—will shut entirely for the first time because Danube cooling levels have fallen below what intake pumps can reach, with weeks possibly needed before a full restart. Levels could drop to −144 cm, well below the 2018 record of −98 cm; −134 cm triggers a full shutdown. Romania’s Cernavoda has already taken one reactor offline for the same reason. Budapest asked large users to cut peak use and floated rotational restrictions as Central Europe’s drought and heatwaves continue; Austria logged a July national high of 40.3°C.

**Links:**

- [AP News — Hungarian nuclear plant to shut down due to record low Danube levels](https://apnews.com/article/hungary-nuclear-power-plant-shutdown-danube-drought-aebc3b54c9c5987e99c489b4652c91fe)
- [phys.org — Hungary to shut nuclear plant as heatwave hits central Europe](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-hungary-nuclear-heatwave-central-europe.html)

**Commentary:** Drought cutting nuclear cooling water shows how heat-plus-low-flow has become a new single point of failure for European baseload.

### 5. Guterres: Strengthening El Niño is “adding fuel to a planet already on fire,” world in “uncharted territory” (Climate · Global)
**Summary:** UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned Friday that a strengthening El Niño is “adding fuel to a planet already on fire” and pushing the world into “uncharted territory.” WMO’s latest outlook shows sea-surface temperatures in key monitoring regions nearly 3°C above normal and still rising, with almost every land area likely warmer than average in August–October and extremes possibly lasting into spring 2027. Guterres said this summer’s heat domes and wildfires are only a “warm-up,” urging an end to fossil-fuel expansion and stronger extreme-heat protection.

### 6. Back-to-back US heat waves plus AI data-center load trigger emergency alerts on PJM and beyond (Security · US)
**Summary:** Bloomberg and others reported around August 1 that successive heat waves and round-the-clock AI data-center demand are stressing US grids. PJM, serving about 67 million people, hit 168 GW on July 2—a roughly 20-year peak—issued repeated emergency alerts and asked the Energy Department to let data centers run backup generators to shave peaks. MISO and Southwest Power Pool, covering more than half of US states, also warned; SPP already holds federal authority to force large loads onto backup power if needed. ERCOT set demand records but has not declared an energy emergency in nearly two years thanks to solar, wind and storage growth.

## III. Clean Power & Storage

### 8. California solar supplied 51% of May power—seen as a first for a large grid month (Clean Power · US)
**Summary:** Ember’s analysis of US EIA data finds that in May 2026 solar (utility-scale plus rooftop) provided 51% of California’s in-state generation—the first known month above 50% for a large power system. Battery capacity has grown to about 16 GW, shifting midday solar into the evening peak. Solar outproduced gas in every month of 2026 through May and could beat gas for the full year for the first time; mild May demand and higher imports also lifted solar’s share of in-state output.

**Links:**

- [Canary Media — California was mostly solar-powered in May — a global first](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/solar/california-solar-power-record-may)

### 9. Macquarie backs Italy’s Chiron in a 1 GW solar + 500 MW storage JV (Storage · Europe)
