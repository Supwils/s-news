## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. Indonesia launches first phase of 100 GW solar plan: about 13 GW solar-plus-storage to displace diesel (Policy · Indonesia)
**Summary:** GreentechLead reported on Aug 14 that Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said President Prabowo Subianto would inaugurate the first phase of Indonesia's 100 GW solar programme in Bali, targeting about 13 GW of solar plus battery storage in diesel-dependent regions. Jakarta has also begun preparing about 17 GW of PV, suggesting the pipeline may exceed the original first-phase figure. The government aims to build 100 GW between 2026 and 2028 — about 33 GW a year if compressed into three years. IEEFA estimates replacing diesel with the planned 13 GW of solar-plus-storage could cost $15–19.5 billion, with storage needs cited as high as about 320 GWh. Installed solar is still only about 1 GW, so schedule and financing remain the binding constraints.

### 2. Rajasthan amends land rules: gigawatt-scale renewables and green hydrogen can secure land before grid hook-up (Policy · India)

### 3. Uttar Pradesh drafts a new grid code covering BESS, pumped storage and renewable dispatch (Policy · India)
**Summary:** Saur Energy reported on Aug 14 that Uttar Pradesh issued draft Uttar Pradesh Electricity Grid Code (UPEGC) Regulations, 2026, intended to replace the 2007 code and bring renewable generators, BESS, pumped-storage plants, renewable park developers and qualified coordinating agencies into a single state framework for connection, scheduling and planning. The draft would require rolling five-year demand forecasts and have the STU take renewable addition plans from UPNEDA. It explicitly treats flexible resources, energy-shifting storage and demand response as tools for managing intermittency. The text remains a consultation draft, not yet a binding obligation.

**Links:**

- [Saur Energy — UP Charts Clearer Path for Renewable, BESS and PSP Integration Into Grid](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/up-charts-clearer-path-for-renewable-bess-and-psp-integration-into-grid-12260190)

**Commentary:** After high solar and wind shares, the real policy bottleneck is dispatch rights and reserve duty — writing storage into the grid code is closer to an operable system than another capacity target.

## II. Green Power, Storage & Hydrogen

### 4. Ember: European solar output rose by as much as 17% in summer heatwaves, but evening prices still spiked (Green power · Europe)
**Summary:** Think tank Ember published analysis on Aug 14 finding solar output across several European markets rose by as much as 17% during the June–July 2026 heatwaves — about 28% in Italy, 23% in Hungary, 14% in France and 13% in Spain — while peak demand rose about 17% in Hungary, 14% in Italy and 11% in Spain. Solar supplied about 25% of EU electricity in June and July, the first months on record at that share. Nuclear was curtailed in France, Romania, Switzerland and Hungary as rivers such as the Danube ran low; five UK gas plants cut a combined 2.5 GW in June. Average daily power prices were about 119% higher in Hungary, 44% in France, 13% in Italy and 7% in Spain than on comparable pre-heatwave days, with Hungary exceeding €900/MWh on June 30. Ember said the gap opens after sunset, when cooling demand stays high, and called for batteries and demand response.

**Links:**

- [edie — EU’s solar output boosted 17% during heatwave](https://www.edie.net/heatwave-spiked-eu-solar-output-17-research-finds/)
- [PV Tech — Solar output rises during European heatwaves — Ember](https://www.pv-tech.org/solar-output-rises-during-european-heatwaves-as-extreme-heat-strains-power-grids/)

**Commentary:** Solar already covers the daytime heatwave; Europe's weak point has moved to after sundown — the shortage is flexibility, not more modules.

### 5. Ember: 2026 battery additions of about 459 GWh could shift roughly a third of new daily solar off sunshine hours (Storage · Global)
**Summary:** Renewables Now reported on Aug 14 on Ember's “Batteries have unlocked the era of anytime solar” study: expected 2026 battery additions of 459 GWh, up about 50% year on year, could theoretically shift 34% of new daily solar generation into non-sunny hours, versus about 18% in 2025. Ember stressed this is a ceiling, because not all batteries shift solar and many are underused. Bulgaria and Chile installed enough batteries in 2025 to shift more than three-quarters of their new daily solar (about 77% and 76%), with Australia at about 60%. Bulgaria's fleet grew from near zero in 2023 to 3 GWh in 2025 and about 8.6 GWh by May 2026; solar plus batteries met nearly a quarter of evening-peak demand (7–9 pm) in H1 2026. In Chile, most new batteries sit with solar plants, covering more than 10% of evening demand in H1 2026. The US added about 58 GWh in 2025, enough to shift roughly a quarter of new solar.

**Links:**

- [Renewables Now — Bulgaria, Chile lead as BESS unlocks anytime solar era](https://renewablesnow.com/news/bulgaria-chile-lead-as-bess-unlocks-anytime-solar-era-ember-1299661/)

**Commentary:** “Anytime solar” is not a capacity slogan; it is the evening-peak megawatt-hours actually served by solar plus batteries — Bulgaria and Chile show a replicable mix.

**Summary:** Gulf News reported on Aug 14 that US War Secretary Pete Hegseth said on Thursday the United States could maintain its blockade of Iranian ports “indefinitely” because warships can be rotated. Refiners, airlines and governments are being pushed to bid for alternative crude, rebuild stocks and pay more for freight and insurance.

### 14. South Africa's Constitutional Court kills Shell's Wild Coast exploration right after about R1.1 billion spent (Transition · South Africa)
**Summary:** Daily Maverick, IOL and Mail & Guardian reported on Aug 14 that the Constitutional Court on Friday overturned the Supreme Court of Appeal's 2024 remedial order and restored the high court's setting-aside of the Impact Africa/Shell offshore exploration right and its renewals. The right was granted to Impact Africa in 2014; Shell took a 50% interest in 2021; the pair had spent about R1.1 billion. The high court in 2022 found inadequate consultation and failure to weigh marine harm, cultural rights and climate. The SCA agreed the grant was unlawful but suspended the set-aside so a third renewal could proceed with extra public participation; the Constitutional Court majority held that the renewal process could not carry that consultation. Dissenting justices including Rogers would have left open a final three-year exploration term after proper consultation.
