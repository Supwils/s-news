## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. EU launches Electrification Action Plan: indicative 46% electricity share by 2040, paired with ETS overhaul (Policy · EU)
**Summary:** EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen unveiled the Electrification Action Plan (COM(2026) 595) on **July 17, 2026**, proposing to raise electricity’s share of final energy consumption from about **23%** to an indicative **46%** by **2040**—roughly a doubling—without making the target legally binding for now; a formal assessment is due with the Energy Union package in **Q4 2026**. The Commission estimates hitting the target could cut oil demand by about **40%** and gas demand by about **70%**. More than half of EU energy use still comes from imported fossil fuels, with extra fossil import costs exceeding about **€50 billion** since the Middle East conflict escalated. The plan targets the electricity–gas price gap (power can cost up to about **2.5×** gas), heat pumps, EV charging, and industrial electrification, alongside the same-day ETS revision and roughly **€100 billion** Industrial Decarbonisation Bank.

### 3. China State Council issues 15th Five-Year Carbon Peaking Action Plan: 25% non-fossil share and 17% intensity cut by 2030 (Policy · China)
**Summary:** China’s State Council has issued the *15th Five-Year Carbon Peaking Action Plan*, targeting about a **17%** cut in CO₂ intensity per unit of GDP by **2030** versus **2025**, and raising non-fossil energy’s share of consumption to about **25%**, to secure a timely carbon peak. The plan calls for wind and solar capacity above **2.8 billion kW** by **2030**, conventional hydro around **410 GW**, and nuclear operating capacity around **110 GW**, plus major projects on interprovincial power mutual aid, clean coal substitution, zero-carbon parks and factories, and greener computing facilities. Full text was carried by the Ministry of Ecology and Environment and Xinhua in mid-**July**, framing the 15th Five-Year period as the decisive push to peak.
