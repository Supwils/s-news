## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. NRDC: Trump policies could double US power-sector emissions by 2035, costing $700B in clean-energy investment (Policy)
**Summary:** The Japan Times reported on Aug 26 that a Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) analysis finds President Trump's fossil-fuel agenda could erase roughly $700 billion in clean-power investment over the next decade and double the US power sector's climate footprint by 2035. The report aggregates impacts from the July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act gutting wind and solar tax credits, tariff-driven cost inflation for generation equipment, forced coal-plant life extensions, and stalled federal wind permitting. It projects 390–540 GW less new wind, solar and storage by 2035—against India's current installed capacity of about 520 GW. US consumers could spend up to $30 billion more on electricity annually by 2035, with household bills rising as much as 25% in some regions; more coal and legacy gas running harder could mean up to 69,000 additional premature deaths and 85,000 extra ER visits and hospital admissions over the decade.

### 2. China's environment ministry approves 2025–26 power and 2026 steel, cement and aluminum ETS allocation plans (Carbon markets)
**Summary:** Industry media citing an Aug 24 ministry meeting report that China's Ministry of Ecology and Environment has approved in principle the national carbon market allocation plans for the power sector in 2025 and 2026 and for steel, cement and aluminum in 2026. The meeting stressed that allocations should reflect national decarbonization goals, incentivize faster green transition in covered industries, and be backed by rigorous quota issuance, compliance and full-chain emissions data quality control. The same session advanced vehicle emissions-inspection reform and a ban on new multi-split air conditioners using HCFC-22 refrigerant.

## II. Renewables & Storage

### 4. Japan to end conventional solar-panel subsidies from FY2027, quadruple perovskite support (Policy)
**Summary:** The Japan Times reported on Aug 26 that the Environment Ministry plans to stop subsidizing purchases of conventional silicon solar panels for new projects from fiscal 2027 (starting April 2027), while installation costs remain eligible in principle and projects already underway keep current support. With module prices down roughly half from their peak and over 90% of domestic panel supply imported—mostly from China—the ministry will shift focus to perovskite cells whose key inputs can be sourced domestically. It expects to request about ¥28 billion for related programs in the FY2027 budget, roughly four times the FY2026 level, toward a government goal of 20 GW of perovskite generation by 2040 (about 20 reactor equivalents). Industry voices warn expansion could slow while perovskite supply and cost hurdles remain.

**Links:**

- [The Japan Times — Japan to end subsidies for solar panels](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/08/26/japan/solar-panels-environment-ministry-subsidies/)

**Commentary:** Moving subsidies from "buy panels" to "bet on perovskite" puts industrial-security narrative ahead of near-term deployment speed—another front in Asia's solar supply-chain contest.

### 5. World's first 16 MW tension-leg floating wind platform "Haiyou Anlan" enters operation (Offshore wind)
**Summary:** People's Daily reported on Aug 26 that the world's first 16 MW tension-leg floating wind platform, Haiyou Anlan, successfully connected to the Lufeng oilfield grid on Aug 6 in the Pearl River Mouth Basin about 200 km southeast of Shenzhen, supplying green power directly to offshore oil operations. It is China's first and the world's deepest, farthest offshore and largest single-unit tension-leg floater, equipped with a 16 MW typhoon-resistant turbine covering about 7.5 standard football fields of swept area. It is expected to deliver roughly 54 GWh per year—enough for about 70,000 people—saving about 15,000 cubic meters of fuel oil and cutting CO₂ by roughly 35,000 tonnes. China's 15th Five-Year Plan targets over 100 GW of cumulative offshore wind, with deep-water floating routes seen as the key incremental resource.

**Commentary:** Tension-leg mooring swaps "pile in the seabed" for "tether to the bottom"—deep-water wind economics and typhoon survivability are being tested on the same yardstick for the first time.

### 6. CZTSSe thin-film module hits 13.0% efficiency, setting a new world record (Solar technology)
**Summary:** China Economic Net reported on Aug 26 that a team led by Meng Qingbo at the Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, achieved 13.0% certified efficiency on a large-area copper zinc tin sulfide selenide (CZTSSe) thin-film module—a world record for the technology—published online in Nature Materials. Using a molecular coordination strategy and solution coating suited to continuous production, the group made uniform films on 10 cm² substrates; a 1 cm² cell reached 14.2%, while a six-subcell series module totaling 10.5 cm² still certified at 13.0%. Built from abundant, low-cost elements, CZTSSe is viewed as a promising next-generation low-cost, eco-friendly thin-film path; the team has now broken the efficiency record for this chemistry ten times.

**Links:**

- [China Economic Net — New thin-film PV module efficiency sets world record](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202608/t20260826_3170964.shtml)

**Commentary:** Thirteen percent at module scale means "earth-abundant" thin-film PV has crossed the psychological bar for industrialization—the next fight is on the factory floor, not in the lab.
