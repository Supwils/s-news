## I. Policy & Transition

### 1. China NEA: Coal’s share of power generation falls below 50% for first half; wind and solar near half of capacity (Policy · China)
**Summary:** At a July 30 briefing, China’s National Energy Administration said coal-fired generation totaled 2.5 trillion kWh in H1 2026, or 49.7% of total generation—the first half-year below 50%. Combined wind and solar capacity reached 1.95 TW (+16.8% YoY), about 48.3% of national power capacity, with solar roughly matching coal capacity. Renewables added 117 GW in H1 (73.9% of all new capacity); renewables supplied about 41.2% of generation, and wind-plus-solar about one-quarter of electricity use. National installed capacity has surpassed 4 TW; summer peak load hit a record 1.553 TW after three successive highs.

### 2. China releases New-Type Energy Storage Development Report 2026: storage capacity reaches 136 GW (Storage · China)
**Summary:** The NEA also released the China New-Type Energy Storage Development Report (2026). It says national new-type storage grew from about 3 GW early in the 14th Five-Year Plan period to 136 GW, roughly doubling year after year, with lithium-ion dominant and multiple chemistries advancing. Utilization improved: equivalent full-load hours rose from 611 (2023) to 911 (2024) and 1,195 (2025). The report reviews policy and industry experience and outlines priorities for the 15th Five-Year Plan on applications, markets, and governance.

**Links:**

### 3. Rhodium: U.S. 2040 emissions may fall only 27%–41% below 2005, well short of Biden-era goals (Policy · U.S.)
**Summary:** Semafor on July 30 cited Rhodium Group’s Taking Stock 2026: under federal and state policies as of June 2026, U.S. GHG emissions in 2040 are on track to be 27%–41% below 2005 levels—far short of Biden-era aims of roughly 61%–66% cuts by 2035. Clean power still grows, but with tax-credit phase-downs and higher oil and gas output, gas and renewables compete for dominance through the 2030s; one power-sector path shows only about a further 24% drop by 2040 versus last year’s outlook. A Columbia analysis separately found the “One Big Beautiful Bill” still preserves about 74% of planned new clean capacity, while oil-and-gas-sector emissions could rise about 15%.

**Links:**

- [Semafor — US emissions reductions fall short of Biden-era goals](https://www.semafor.com/article/07/30/2026/us-emissions-reductions-set-to-falter-on-trump-energy-policies)

## III. Extreme Weather & Energy Security

### 7. Record-low Danube forces Hungary’s Paks nuclear plant toward full shutdown; Romania units constrained (Disaster · Europe)
**Summary:** BBC, Euronews, and Reuters reported on July 30 that extreme heat and drought drove Danube levels to record lows. Hungarian PM Péter Magyar said the ~2,000 MW Paks plant—nearly half of national power—could shut entirely on July 30 or 31; it cools with river water and had previously only curtailed output in summer, never fully closed. Romania’s Cernavoda complex already shut one reactor over low water, with the second under pressure; both countries are preparing contingency cutbacks for large users. Romanian hydrologists said Danube inflow was about one-third of the July average.

**Links:**

- [BBC — Danube's record low levels force shutdown of Hungary's only nuclear plant](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cn0nqv05g0do)

**Commentary:** Climate stress is punching through the cooling constraint on “zero-carbon baseload,” elevating Europe’s summer nuclear vulnerability from a technical issue to a system-security crisis.

### 8. “Exceptionally serious” flash drought hits half of England; southern rain near 1% of normal (Climate · UK)
**Summary:** CNN reported on July 30 that England’s Environment Agency declared a flash drought across large parts of central, eastern, and southern England, calling it “exceptionally serious” with lasting impacts on environment, wildlife, and the economy. Successive heat waves plus scant rain left national rainfall at about 7% of the seasonal average—and about 1% in parts of the south—with little relief forecast; about 78% of rivers are below normal. England is in its fourth heat wave of the year, with temperature records challenged and heat-related excess deaths in May–June nearly double the whole of 2025, per health authorities.

**Links:**
