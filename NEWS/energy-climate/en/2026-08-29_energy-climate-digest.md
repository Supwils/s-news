### 1. Trump announces “historic” U.S.–Venezuela oil deal for majority control of 65+ billion barrels (Oil & Gas)
**Summary:** On the evening of Aug 28, President Donald Trump said the United States, working through Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Venezuelan interim President Delcy Rodríguez and private partners, had secured majority U.S. control of more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuela’s proven oil reserves “at no cost to the American taxpayer,” claiming the deal would more than double U.S. reserves and lower gasoline prices. ### 2. EU gas stores ~63% full: winter inventories may hit a 13-year low amid “winter panic” (Energy Security)
**Summary:** The Guardian reported on Aug 29 that EU gas stocks were about 63% full in the last week of August, well below the roughly 80% late-August average of recent years. At the current slow injection pace, Europe may enter the heating season about a fifth below the five-year average and at the lowest level since 2013. Drivers include Gulf oil and gas export disruption from the U.S.–Israel war on Iran, a cold end to last winter, and higher gas-fired power use during this summer’s heatwaves. Benchmark European gas has climbed above €68/MWh, a three-year high; Goldman Sachs and others say prices may need to move above €100/MWh to attract enough LNG if Middle East supply does not return. German storage is about half full; Belgium and the Netherlands stand near 51% and 45%, while the UK’s thin domestic storage leaves it exposed to import volatility.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — ‘Winter panic’: EU gas stores at their lowest level in 13 years](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/aug/29/european-gas-stores-lowest-13-years-eu-price)

**Commentary:** Physical shortage is not the base case, but the inventory gap hands winter pricing power to Asian bidding and Hormuz transit expectations.

## II. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 4. Delaware signs “bring your own clean power” package; data-center costs stay off ratepayers (Policy)
**Summary:** Governor Matt Meyer this week signed a consumer-protection energy package requiring large loads, including data centers, to bring their own generation or procure clean power; facilities unable to hit 100% immediately must submit a ramp-up plan of up to ten years. Separate measures create a higher rate class for large users and remove data centers from tax-incentive programs. Meyer said Delaware will not ask workers, seniors and small businesses to subsidize hyperscale power use. Lawmakers called it the first state to enact a legislative “bring your own generation” rule, a potential template for other PJM states.

### 5. Thailand scraps 2,000 MW data-centre clean-power cap, opens direct PPAs to all industry (Policy)
**Summary:** Energy Minister Akanat Promphan said the National Energy Policy Council has lifted a two-year regulatory sandbox that capped direct clean-power PPAs at 2,000 MW for data centres only, opening the scheme to every industry. Firms can buy renewables via Third Party Access wheeling charges covering EGAT transmission and distribution networks. Thailand is also creating a new “category nine” tariff for data centres so costly imported LNG—around $23 per unit on the spot market—does not socialize into household bills. Akanat argued direct solar (~THB 2.16/kWh) and wind (~THB 3/kWh) can still undercut fossil grid power even after TPA fees.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Southeast Asia is pairing green-power market opening with data-centre ring-fencing—chase compute investment without dumping LNG costs on households.

### 6. CBAM verification guidance: renewable power exports need hour-by-hour “electron identity” proof (Carbon Market)
**Summary:** After the European Commission’s Aug 24 CBAM verification and accreditation guidance, Aug 29 industry analysis stressed that non-EU electricity exporters seeking actual embedded emissions face an evidence chain spanning contracts, metering, nominations and network proof—not just plant carbon factors. Typical conditions include a PPA with an authorised CBAM declarant, direct connection to the EU grid or proof of no physical congestion, fossil emissions below 550 g CO₂/kWh, firm TSO nominations for periods of no more than one hour, and at least monthly evidence to the verifier. Wind, solar and hydro near zero emissions can still fail the actual-emissions path without an hourly ledger.

## III. Clean Power & Storage

### 7. Michigan clears first large solar under state siting law: 90 MW Acceleration Solar settlement approved (Clean Power)
**Summary:** On Aug 29 the Michigan Public Service Commission unanimously approved a settlement between developer Ranger Power and Leslie, Onondaga and Vevay townships for the 90 MW Acceleration Solar project—the first major case under Public Act 233, which shifted large renewable approvals to the state. The project spans about 870 acres; the deal adds screening and stricter noise limits, assigns decommissioning to the state, and provides hundreds of thousands of dollars in township payments plus firefighter training funds. Michigan aims for 100% clean energy by 2040; 79 townships and counties have petitioned the state Supreme Court to challenge the law, making this a test of local versus state authority.

### 10. UNCCD COP17 closes in Ulaanbaatar with Ulaanbaatar Declaration (Global Governance)
**Summary:** China’s National Forestry and Grassland Administration said on Aug 29 that the 17th Conference of the Parties to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification closed in the early hours in Ulaanbaatar, adopting the Ulaanbaatar Declaration plus decisions on the 2027–2028 work programme and budget, science, drought, sand and dust storms, and grasslands, and agreeing on a future strategic framework. The declaration urges integrated land and water management, stronger drought resilience, source-area control of dust storms, and inclusive grassland governance, including an International Day of Grasslands and Pastoralists. China’s delegation ran a “China Pavilion,” more than 20 side events, and released a China–Arab five-year action plan on drought, desertification and land degradation; COP18 will be held in Egypt.
