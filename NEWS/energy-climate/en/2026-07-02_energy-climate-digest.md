### 1. America's largest grid PJM wins federal emergency authority; **July 2** demand may break **2006** summer record (Policy · US)
**Summary:** Fox Business reported on **July 2, 2026**, that PJM Interconnection — serving about **67 million** people across **13** states and Washington, D.C. — expects electricity demand of roughly **166,147 MW** on **July 2**, which would exceed the summer record of **165,563 MW** set in **2006**. Under Section **202(c)** of the Federal Power Act, the Department of Energy approved two emergency orders (effective **11:59 PM June 30** through **11:59 PM July 3**): if needed, transmission operators may direct data centers and other large customers with backup generators to switch off-grid; and power plants received temporary relief from certain environmental permit limits. PJM has recalled units from maintenance and issued Maximum Generation and Load Management Alerts; spot power prices in northern Virginia's data-center hub surged as temperatures neared **100°F (~38°C)**. NYISO and MISO are also urging conservation or monitoring record-challenging loads.

### 2. China issues mandatory renewable consumption minimum-share targets; steel, cement, data centres face "hard constraints" (Policy · China)
**Summary:** Guangming Daily cited Economic Daily on **July 2, 2026**, that NDRC and the National Energy Administration have issued implementing rules for minimum renewable energy consumption shares and renewable power consumption responsibility weights — China's first mandatory minimum-share regime. The rules set binding consumption floors for high-intensity users such as steel, cement, and data centres; localities and firms missing annual targets face interviews, public notices, and credit-record penalties. Non-electric uses — renewable heating/cooling, green hydrogen, bio-liquid fuels — are included in assessment for the first time, closing a multi-carrier regulatory loop across power, heat, hydrogen, and fuels. Coverage frames the shift from "capacity-driven" renewables growth to dual constraints on consumption and absorption.

**Links:**

- [Guangming Daily — Renewable energy consumption gets "hard constraints" (Chinese)](https://tech.gmw.cn/2026-07/02/content_38863249.htm)

**Commentary:** Green-power assessment is pivoting from "how much is built" to "how much is used" — binding end-user absorption targets are the lever that turns installed capacity into real emissions cuts.

### 4. North Carolina House committee advances bill to end **80%** property-tax break for utility-scale solar (Policy · US)
**Summary:** pv magazine USA reported on **July 2, 2026**, that the House Finance Committee voted **12–8** to advance a substitute for H.B. 1213, ending the state's long-standing **80%** property-tax exclusion for new utility-scale solar: projects placed in service on or after **July 1, 2027**, would pay full local property tax, while existing and pre-deadline assets keep the abatement. ### 5. New Jersey legislature passes Balcony Solar Act; systems up to **1200W** exempt from interconnection approval (Policy · US)
**Summary:** PV Tech reported on **July 2, 2026**, that the Garden State Balcony Solar Act passed the Senate **40–0** and Assembly **79–1** (one abstention), making New Jersey the tenth state to advance plug-in-solar-specific legislation. Residents may install systems up to **1200W** connected via standard **120V** outlets without executing an interconnection agreement, and net metering requirements do not apply; Governor Sherrill awaits signature. The bill follows Maine's precedent decoupling user-side generation from utility approval — Sherrill's office has previously criticised PJM "mismanagement."

## II. Green Power & Storage

### 6. Three mandatory national PV energy-efficiency standards released, covering the full chain from polysilicon to inverters (Green power · China)
**Summary:** China Economic Net cited CCTV on **July 2, 2026**, that MIIT, NDRC, and SAMR approved three mandatory national standards: limits on monocrystalline silicon unit energy consumption; minimum efficiency and grading for crystalline PV modules and inverters; and limits on polycrystalline silicon and germanium unit consumption. They set tiered energy and efficiency metrics across polysilicon, wafers, modules, and inverters, tighten high-energy/low-efficiency capacity, and add coupled environmental-stress decay metrics for modules. The trio is designed to raise green entry barriers and unify accounting rules; industry bodies will accelerate implementation guidance.
