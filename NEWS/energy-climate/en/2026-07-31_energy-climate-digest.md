## I. Policy & Carbon Markets

### 1. China Issues 15th Five-Year Climate Plan: 17% Carbon Intensity Cut by 2030 vs 2025 (Policy · China)
**Summary:** China’s Ministry of Ecology and Environment, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, NDRC and other agencies have jointly issued the National Climate Change Plan for the 15th Five-Year Period, People’s Daily reported on July 31. The plan calls for peaking carbon emissions on schedule and fully delivering the 2030 NDC; by 2030, CO₂ intensity per unit of GDP should fall 17% from 2025, product-level CO₂ intensity in ETS-covered sectors should drop about 3%, a nationally aligned voluntary GHG reduction market should be in place, and non-CO₂ gases should deliver roughly 30 million tonnes of CO₂e abatement capacity. It also advances dual controls on emissions volume and intensity, aims to peak coal and oil consumption, and expands the national ETS with more paid allowances and a quota reserve/adjustment mechanism.

### 2. CEC: China’s Solar Capacity Set to Overtake Coal in Q3 (Transition · China)
**Summary:** China Daily reported on July 31, citing a China Electricity Council mid-year report, that total installed generation capacity reached 4.01 billion kW by end-May—the first country past the 4-billion-kW mark—and that solar had effectively tied coal by end-June, with an official overtake expected in the third quarter. In the first half, non-fossil generation rose 8.5% year on year to 44.5% of total output and contributed 70.7% of national incremental generation, while coal’s share of generation fell to 49.7%, below 50% for the first time. Demand from advanced manufacturing, AI, and EV infrastructure continues to pull new energy to the center of capacity additions.

### 3. UN Article 6.4 Body Clears Grid-Connected Renewables Methodology (Carbon Markets · International)
**Summary:** The Paris Agreement Article 6.4 Supervisory Body on July 30 adopted a methodology for grid-connected renewable electricity, Carbon Herald and others reported on July 31, allowing eligible projects to generate UN-backed carbon credits for the first time. The rules define eligibility, how reductions are measured, and verification before issuance, aiming to unlock revenue where clean-power finance remains a barrier; renewables join earlier methodologies for landfill methane and nitric-acid N₂O. The Body also updated registry procedures and deferred a household cooking-efficiency methodology pending more work on permanence and uncertainty; its final 2026 meeting before COP31 is set for October 5–9 in Bonn.

**Links:**

- [Carbon Herald — Renewable Energy Methodology Cleared In Major Expansion Of UN Carbon Market](https://carbonherald.com/renewable-energy-methodology-cleared-in-major-expansion-of-un-carbon-market/)
- [CCarbon — UN Carbon Market Expands To Renewable Power Sector](https://www.ccarbon.info/news/un-carbon-market-expands-to-renewable-power-sector/)

**Commentary:** Bringing grid green power into Article 6.4 can plug financing gaps—if additionality and integrity standards hold against low-quality credit floods.

### 4. India Cabinet Approves ₹5,070 Crore Floating Solar Scheme With Storage (Policy · India)
**Summary:** India’s Union Cabinet on July 31 approved the Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY), a roughly ₹5,070 crore program to build 5,000 MW of floating solar on reservoirs and industrial ponds with co-located storage of at least two hours, totaling 10,000 MWh. Projects are to be sanctioned from FY2026–27 through FY2030–31, with disbursements continuing to FY2032–33; commissioned projects receive ₹1 crore per MW in central aid, plus up to ₹50 lakh for feasibility studies. NISE estimates about 102 GW of floating-solar potential versus roughly 700 MW installed today; the government projects about 10 million tonnes of annual CO₂ cuts and 16,000–17,000 full-time-equivalent jobs.

### 5. Federal Judge Presses New York’s Climate Superfund Law (Policy · United States)
**Summary:** E&E News by POLITICO reported on July 31 that Chief Judge Brenda Sannes of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of New York sharply questioned the 2024 New York Climate Change Superfund Act at a July 30 hearing, asking whether it intrudes on uniquely federal powers. Modeled on polluter-pays Superfund logic, the law would collect about $75 billion over 25 years from fossil producers based on historical emissions to fund climate adaptation. Trump’s DOJ, red states, and energy trade groups have sued New York and a similar Vermont law; the judge also appeared receptive to challengers’ standing, with the dispute likely headed toward the Supreme Court and shaping other states’ appetite to follow.

**Links:**

- [E&E News — Judge casts doubt on New York’s polluter pays climate law](https://www.eenews.net/articles/judge-casts-doubt-on-new-yorks-polluter-pays-climate-law/)

## III. Extreme Weather & Energy Security

### 7. WWA: Climate Change Made Extreme Fire Conditions ~20× More Likely in Central Spain (Disaster · Europe)
**Summary:** World Weather Attribution analysis finds human-caused climate change made fire-prone conditions at least twice as likely in southwestern France and at least about 20 times as likely in central Spain; similar-intensity events are now roughly once in 20 years in SW France and once in six years in central Spain. EL PAÍS reported on July 31 that mainland Spain’s July average was 25.7°C—2.6°C above the 1991–2020 normal—tying among the hottest Julys on record, with about 26 wildfires affecting roughly 150,000 hectares and Spain’s first national emergency for a natural catastrophe of this kind. A wet winter followed by successive heatwaves and drought loaded then dried fuel; the UN climate chief called Europe’s megafires national-scale disasters.

### 8. Danube Drought Deepens: Hungary Says Paks May Stay Offline for Weeks (Energy Security · Europe)
**Summary:** Reuters and other outlets reported on July 31 that Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar said the Paks nuclear plant—about 2 GW and nearly half of national power—could remain shut for weeks because Danube cooling levels will stay too low; output is already below half of capacity, with a full shutdown expected around Monday and little near-term recovery in sight. Evening peak demand may rise about 20% in the heatwave, and large industrials were asked to cut use—MOL said it is cutting electricity about 40%, while Samsung SDI cited roughly 10% evening power cuts and 50% water cuts. Romania’s Cernavoda plant has also taken units offline for extreme low water, removing nuclear generation that normally covers about a fifth of peak demand and increasing reliance on imports and other resources.
