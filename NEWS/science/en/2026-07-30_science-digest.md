## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Curiosity finds its largest “sea of polygons” in Valle Grande (Space)
**Summary:** As NASA’s Curiosity rover entered a Martian valley nicknamed Valle Grande while climbing Mount Sharp’s foothills, it captured a 360° panorama on sols 4,930–4,931 (June 19–20, 2026) showing honeycomb polygonal fractures about 4–8 cm across extending in all directions and wrapping a ~6 m sand-capped butte nicknamed Miraflores. The mission had seen only small patches before; project scientist Ashwin Vasavada said the “sea of polygons” took the team’s breath away. Possible origins include mud cracks, warm–cold cycling, or burial-related compaction and dewatering; the team is measuring shape and chemistry to pin down the process. NASA and major outlets highlighted the find around July 29–30.

## II. Fundamental Research & Human Evolution

### 4. TRACE maps “ghost ancestor” DNA from modern genomes alone: ~0.5%–1% in everyone (Evolution)
**Summary:** UC Berkeley’s Yulin Zhang, Johns Hopkins’ Arjun Biddanda, and Priya Moorjani’s team published TRACE (TRacking Archaic Contributions via ARG Estimation) in *Science* on July 30: the method rebuilds ancestral recombination graphs from present-day genomes only, locating archaic introgression without ancient DNA. About 2% of the modern human genome is archaic; a “ghost” lineage appears in Africans and non-Africans alike (~0.5%–1% per person), with gene flow before the final Out-of-Africa dispersal ~50,000 years ago and a split timing near Neanderthals/Denisovans (~800,000 years ago). A “super-archaic” signal in Oceanian Denisovan segments likely arrived via Denisovans from a deeper lineage (possibly *Homo erectus*-like). Ghost ancestry even appears in regions once treated as Neanderthal/Denisovan deserts (including near FOXP2).

**Links:**

- [UC Berkeley News — New technique pinpoints human DNA inherited from ghost ancestors](https://news.berkeley.edu/2026/07/30/new-technique-pinpoints-human-dna-inherited-from-ghost-ancestors/)
- [Science — Recovering signatures of archaic hominin introgression using ancestral recombination graphs](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aef8874)

**Commentary:** This moves ghost admixture from statistical hints to mappable segments and timelines—human evolution looks less like a branching tree than a repeatedly anastomosing web.

### 5. Ancient stardust grains may have seeded the solar system’s earliest solids (Planetary science)
**Summary:** In work from Caltech’s François Tissot lab, Ren T. C. Marquez and colleagues report in *Science Advances* that high-precision analyses of early-formed calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions (CAIs) in the Allende meteorite confirm presolar stardust and suggest those grains acted as nucleation seeds for the first solids while the young solar system was still hot. Years of ultra-precise lab methods made subcomponent measurements possible; the authors argue the grains, though scarce, may have been a structural substrate around which CAIs coalesced. Phys.org covered the study on July 30, 2026.

### 6. IBM and UChicago demonstrate trusted quantum advantage on 70 logical qubits in ~15 minutes (Quantum)
**Summary:** On July 30, IBM and University of Chicago researchers announced that their paper *Sampling hard circuits with verifiably high fidelity* meets both pillars of quantum advantage: computation beyond leading classical simulation methods and statistical confidence in result fidelity. They ran 70 logical qubits with 2,415 logical two-qubit operations and 468 logical T gates; spacetime-code encoding and syndrome post-selection cut effective logical error rates roughly 10× below physical rates. The quantum run took about 15 minutes while many classical simulation routes faced prohibitive runtimes. Circuits and results are released on the Quantum Advantage Tracker; Jay Gambetta said the field is “firmly in the quantum advantage era.”

## III. Biomedicine & Neuroscience

### 7. Somatic mutations show marrow-derived cells routinely join the aged human microglial pool (Biology)
**Summary:** In *Nature* on July 30, Belk, Zhang, Reilly and colleagues use accumulated somatic mutations as clonal barcodes to track marrow-derived cell influx into the brain across 20 aged individuals. Every person examined showed evidence of influx; single-cell analyses, including mitochondrial DNA lineage tracing, indicate infiltrating cells resemble microglia and can form a large share of the microglial pool. Cohort analyses also find a protective association between most forms of clonal hematopoiesis and Alzheimer’s disease. The authors conclude that widespread myeloid influx into the healthy aging brain revises the mouse-derived picture of nearly pure embryonic microglial seeding in humans.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Somatic mutations reveal the ontogeny of microglia in human aging](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10939-0)

**Commentary:** “Where brain immune cells come from” moves from developmental dogma to quantifiable adult hematopoietic contribution—with a protective clue linking clonal hematopoiesis to neurodegeneration.

### 8. Engineered sulfated polysaccharide 26SCS targets RANK Lys97 to reverse osteoporosis (Biology)
**Summary:** Wang, Ji, Yu and colleagues report in *Nature Biomedical Engineering* on July 30 that synthetic pentasaccharide 2-N,6-O-sulfated chitosan (26SCS) restores ~115% bone mass therapeutically and prevents ~66% bone loss prophylactically. Geometrically matched sulfate pairs engage RANK Lys97 to block RANKL signaling and arrest preosteoclast fusion while preserving preosteoclast viability to raise PDGF-BB and drive angiogenesis-coupled osteogenesis. Unlike natural glycosaminoglycans such as heparin, whose carboxyl groups compete at K97, the carboxyl-free, sequence-controlled sulfation improves specificity; monosulfated analogues either oversuppress osteoclastogenesis or lack pro-osteogenic effects. A companion News & Views frames the work as rebalancing bone remodeling in osteoporosis.

**Links:**

- [Nature Biomedical Engineering — Rebalancing bone remodelling in osteoporosis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01702-x)

**Commentary:** Bone drugs shift from resorption-only brakes toward dual-track glycoengineering that also promotes vascularized formation—topology-controlled sulfation becomes a design knob.

### 9. Forgotten fly memories leave silent traces—reminders can restore truth or invent falsehoods (Neuroscience)
**Summary:** Wenbin Yang and colleagues in Johannes Felsenberg’s group show in *Nature Neuroscience* that after fruit flies pair an odor with mild shocks, avoidance fades by 24 hours, yet a specific reminder odor can recover the aversive memory. Neural recordings indicate the original behavior-guiding mushroom-body output pattern fades while a silent trace persists in behaviorally neutral output neurons; reminders reinstate the active memory trace. Using a training-present but shock-unpaired “innocent” odor as the reminder can create false avoidance of that odor; true recovery and false-memory formation engage separable dopaminergic pathways. MedicalXpress reported the study on July 30.

**Links:**

- [MedicalXpress — Forgotten memories can leave silent traces in the brain](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-07-forgotten-memories-silent-brain.html)

## IV. Climate, Environment & Energy

### 12. Multidecadal tropical Pacific pattern amplifies U.S. Southwest fires, buffers eastern Australia (Climate)
**Summary:** Columbia Climate School coverage on July 30 explains an *Environmental Research Letters* study: a multidecadal tropical Pacific trend since the 1980s—western tropical Pacific warming faster than the east—has shaped atmospheric dryness (vapor pressure deficit) and burned forest area in the U.S. Southwest and eastern Australia. From 1984–2022, burned forest area rose more than ~3,000% in the interior Southwest and more than ~1,000% in a California-inclusive coastal region; human-caused warming drove most dryness-linked increase, with the Pacific trend adding ~22%. In eastern Australia, the same Pacific trend cut burned area by ~19% versus a warming-only counterfactual, dampening rather than reversing drying. Future risk on both sides still hinges on how anthropogenic warming and the tropical Pacific co-evolve.

**Links:**

- [Columbia Climate School — Pacific Ocean Pattern Helps Explain Diverging Wildfire Trends in U.S. and Australia](https://news.climate.columbia.edu/2026/07/30/pacific-ocean-pattern-helps-explain-diverging-wildfire-trends-in-u-s-and-australia/)
- [Environmental Research Letters — Opposing contributions of the tropical Pacific to recent burned forest area trends](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ae6791)

**Commentary:** Regional fire risk cannot be read from global mean warming alone—Pacific patterns are a teleconnection switch that splits one greenhouse forcing into “drier” versus “somewhat buffered.”

### 13. IIASA’s RIME-X maps emissions pathways to regional climate-risk probability distributions (Climate)
**Summary:** Niklas Schwind and colleagues at IIASA and partners publish RIME-X v1.0 (Rapid Impact Model Emulator Extended) in *Geoscientific Model Development*: the open statistical emulator combines simple-climate-model warming pathways with regional information from multi-model Earth-system and impact ensembles to produce scenario- and time-dependent probability distributions that capture uncertainty in global response, model differences, and natural variability. It targets faster exploration of scenarios and indicators for scientists, finance/insurance, and adaptation planners without full supercomputer model chains; authors stress it complements rather than replaces comprehensive climate models. GMD and Phys.org highlighted the release on July 30.

### 14. CAS team reports 29.71% perovskite/CIGS four-terminal tandem via sulfide coordination engineering (Energy)
**Summary:** According to *pv magazine* on July 30, researchers affiliated with the Chinese Academy of Sciences used bis(2-pyridylmethyl) sulfide (2PyS) to tune the local coordination of Pb ions in wide-bandgap perovskite, suppressing defects, halide migration, and photoinduced phase segregation. The semi-transparent perovskite top cell reached 22.21% efficiency; the CIGS bottom cell delivered 19.65% under full spectrum and ~7.5% under the filtered top cell, for 29.71% four-terminal tandem efficiency. Devices retained more than 91% of initial efficiency after 2,000 hours of continuous operation. The team says 2PyS modulates crystallization kinetics toward more homogeneous films and stronger phase stability under coupled light–heat stress.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Tandem records are shifting from peak efficiency alone to efficiency-plus-stability on the same scoreboard—coordination chemistry is a key knob keeping wide-bandgap perovskites in a workable window.
