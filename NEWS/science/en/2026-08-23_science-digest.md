## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Zhuque-3 Flight 2 achieves China’s first orbital-class first-stage land-leg recovery (commercial space)
**Summary:** LandSpace’s reusable Zhuque-3 Yao-2 lifted off at 07:35 on Aug 19 from the Dongfeng commercial space innovation zone and delivered the Honghu-03 satellite to its planned orbit; around 07:41 the first stage soft-landed at the Minqin landing pad in Gansu. People’s Daily and the company say this is China’s first orbital-class first-stage recovery on landing legs and the first land recovery of a private liquid oxygen–methane booster; the vehicle is about 66.1 m tall with a liftoff mass near 570 tonnes. The flight marks Zhuque-3’s move from technology demonstration into engineering reuse verification, with inspection and a possible reflights to follow.

### 3. NASA: human-associated microbes may survive in lunar south-pole shade niches (planetary protection)
**Summary:** A NASA Goddard–led team reported Aug 19 in *Science Advances* that LRO topography/temperature maps plus UV models imply survivable niches for common spacecraft- and human-associated microbes at candidate south-pole sites including Nobile Rim, Connecting Ridge, and De Gerlache Rim. Niche scales range from kilometer-wide crater floors to boot-print shadows; UV-hardy *Aspergillus niger* may briefly persist even in partly illuminated zones. Authors stress the Moon lacks liquid water for growth—survival means dormancy—yet Artemis polar activity could confound local chemistry, so contamination baselines are needed before sampling.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — Human-Related Microbes May Survive Moon’s South Pole](https://science.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/human-related-microbes-may-survive-moons-south-pole-nasa-finds/)

**Commentary:** Even a boot print can be a shelter—crewed polar exploration must map shaded micro-habitats, not only sterilization checklists.

### 4. JWST confirms three accreting supermassive black holes in a z≈5 galaxy (early universe)
**Summary:** An MPE-led BlackTHUNDER team using JWST/NIRSpec-IFS identifies three actively accreting supermassive black holes in galaxy J0148-4214 at redshift ~5.02 (~12.5 billion light-years)—the first strong evidence for three active black holes in one distant galaxy—*Astronomy & Astrophysics*. Two central holes lie ~620 light-years apart in projection (~80 million and ~0.6 million solar masses); a third sits ~5,500 light-years out (~2 million solar masses); the central pair may merge within a few hundred million years. Chinese science media amplified the result on Aug 23, stressing mergers as a fast early-growth channel.

## II. Biomedicine

### 5. FDA grants accelerated approval to first GSDIa gene therapy Genglycos (rare disease)
**Summary:** On Aug 19 the U.S. FDA granted accelerated approval to Ultragenyx’s Genglycos (pariglasgene brecaparvovec-opnr, DTX401) for patients aged 8 and older with glycogen storage disease type Ia (GSDIa), as an adjunct to nutritional management to reduce daily cornstarch intake—the first approved therapy for this indication. The Phase 3 GlucoGene study (~46 participants, 48 weeks) showed an about 31% mean reduction versus placebo in daily cornstarch use (primary endpoint). The one-time liver-directed AAV8 product requires confirmatory evidence and long-term follow-up in a disease monitoring program.

### 7. NeoZanHER: zanidatamab alone yields 30% pCR in early HER2+ breast cancer (oncology)
**Summary:** An MD Anderson–led NeoZanHER Phase 2 single-arm trial published Aug 22 in *Nature Communications* treated 20 patients with 1–3 cm, node-negative HER2-positive early breast cancer with the bispecific HER2 antibody zanidatamab (6 or 10 doses). Pathologic complete response reached 30% (6/20), meeting the prespecified target, with another 20% RCB-1; treatment was well tolerated. All pCR cases were HER2 IHC 3+ with ERBB2 amplification and PAM50 HER2-high subtype. Authors say chemo-free HER2 targeting may be feasible in selected patients, pending larger selection studies.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Zanidatamab in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer (NeoZanHER)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76662-6)

## III. Climate & Energy

### 9. Multiyear tropical warm-pool warming drives ~695 Gt temporary Antarctic mass gain (polar climate)
**Summary:** A CAS Institute of Oceanology–led international team published Aug 19 in *Nature* that the Antarctic Ice Sheet gained about 695 billion tonnes of mass from July 2021 to April 2023—the largest short-term gain in the satellite gravity record since 2003—with ~68% in East Antarctica’s Queen Mary Land–Wilkes Land from persistent snowfall. Persistent tropical western Pacific–eastern Indian Ocean warm-pool warming excited a southward Rossby-wave train, a dipole circulation over East Antarctica, and stronger atmospheric rivers from the mid-latitude Indian Ocean. Xinhua covered the finding on Aug 23; authors stress similar events recur about once a decade and do not reverse the ~140.5 Gt/yr mean loss over 2003–2024.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Multiyear tropical warm pool warming drives slowdown in Antarctic mass loss](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10912-x)
- [Xinhua — Antarctic ice sheet sees temporary mass gain](http://english.news.cn/20260823/ce09ff1a3c234df0a3cb4ef09c8c80fa/c.html)

**Commentary:** Antarctic “weight gain” is short-term tropical remote control, not a reversal of long-term loss—sea-level budgets must separate noise from trend.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 11. CERN “little Big Bang”: oxygen-16 and neon-20 collisions also form quark–gluon plasma (nuclear physics)
**Summary:** University of Copenhagen / Niels Bohr Institute researchers with the ALICE collaboration report in *Physical Review Letters* that LHC collisions of oxygen-16 and neon-20—far lighter than lead—produce quark–gluon plasma droplets of the early universe; ScienceDaily featured the result on Aug 23. Final-state anisotropic flow preserves nuclear geometry: oxygen–oxygen yields a rounder pattern, while neon imprints a bowling-pin shape. The work pushes the QGP formation threshold into lighter systems; helium-4 and other light nuclei are next for mapping the limit.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Physicists create a tiny “Big Bang” with surprisingly small atomic nuclei](http://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260822015143.htm)

### 12. Point-contact spectra of pressurized UTe₂ reveal multiple superconducting phases (condensed matter)
**Summary:** *Nature Communications* on Aug 22 reports comprehensive point-contact spectroscopy of pressurized heavy-fermion spin-triplet candidate UTe₂ on the (001) surface. Andreev bound states support a finite odd-*k_z* (*p_z*-like) component in the order parameter; a single ratio 〈Δ_z〉/〈Δ_x(y)〉 distinguishes multiple superconducting phases at ambient and elevated pressure. The spectra tighten constraints on this material’s contested pairing symmetry.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Multiple superconducting phases in pressurized UTe₂](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77045-7)

**Commentary:** When a topological-superconductor candidate’s phase diagram is crowded, a spectrum that collapses phases into one ratio beats another resistivity curve.

### 13. Atomically dispersed Ru enables dehydration–reductive amination of hydroxy acids to amino acids (green chemistry)
**Summary:** *Nature Communications* on Aug 22 describes an atomically dispersed ruthenium catalyst that selectively converts biomass-derived α-/β-hydroxy acids into amino acids via a previously underappreciated dehydration–reductive amination path, bypassing the classic α-keto acid intermediate. Characterization points to isolated, partially oxidized Ru centers that mediate base-assisted α-H abstraction, β-OH elimination, and direct reductive amination. The route could map polyols with adjacent hydroxyls onto amino-functionalized products.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Ru-enabled dehydration-reductive amination into amino acids](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77053-7)

**Commentary:** Skipping the keto-acid detour in biomass “acid → amino acid” chemistry is a mechanism win as much as a yield table win.
