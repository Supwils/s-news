### 1. SpaceX Dual-Coast Falcon 9s Fly 38 Minutes Apart (Launch)
**Summary:** On the evening of August 15, 2026, SpaceX launched two Falcon 9 rockets from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Florida, and Vandenberg Space Force Base, California, 38 minutes apart, beating its prior shortest gap between orbital flights (about 65 minutes on two Starlink missions on August 31, 2024). The first liftoff was at 9:12 p.m. EDT (01:12 UTC on August 16) with eight Globalstar LEO satellites; deployments began 56.5 minutes after launch over a six-minute window. The second, at 9:50 p.m. EDT, flew USSF-366 for the U.S. Space Force. Both first stages landed: the Florida booster on its 19th flight, the USSF-366 booster on droneship Of Course I Still Love You after its 18th mission. Space.com reported the record on August 16.

### 2. Isaacman “Extremely Confident” Artemis III Can Fly in 2027 as LEO Docking Test (Human Spaceflight)
**Summary:** NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said on August 14 he is “extremely confident” Artemis III will launch in 2027. The mission is now an Earth-orbit demonstration: Blue Origin’s New Glenn would loft a Blue Moon Mark 2 lander test article, then SLS would launch a four-astronaut Orion from Kennedy Space Center for rendezvous and docking with the Blue Origin vehicle and a planned docking with a SpaceX human landing system test article, reducing risk before Artemis IV’s targeted 2028 crewed landing. SLS and Orion are being stacked at Kennedy; NASA has not set a specific launch date. IBTimes and others followed the remarks on August 16.

## II. Fundamental Physics & Energy Technology

### 5. Diamond Melts Near 1 TPa Without a BC8 Detour, Hinting at Slower Fusion First Shocks (Physics)
**Summary:** Marius Millot and colleagues at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory report in *Nature Physics* (online August 13) nanosecond shock compression of microcrystalline diamond at the University of Rochester’s OMEGA facility. Velocimetry, pyrometry, and X-ray diffraction show the cubic diamond lattice surviving until melt near 1 terapascal (about three times Earth’s core pressure) at roughly 7,300 K, without a substantial BC8 phase. The melting curve now matches quantum simulations after about 20 years of mismatch. For inertial confinement fusion, a slower first shock that still fully melts a diamond ablator (supplementary material supports dropping first-shock speed from about 33–34 km/s to about 24.5 km/s) could raise fuel compressibility; simulations suggest up to about threefold energy-gain improvement if other degradation channels stay controlled. ScienceAlert and others covered the result on August 15–16.

**Links:**

- [Nature Physics — Diamond melting in shock compression experiments at 1 TPa pressures](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03413-1)
- [phys.org — Melting diamond could unlock triple fusion gain and the secrets of ice giant planets](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-diamond-triple-fusion-gain-secrets.html)

**Commentary:** One extra phase-diagram point lets NIF ease the “must-melt” first shock—gain lives in entropy.

## III. Biomedicine

### 6. ArkBio AK0406 Wins China IND for Influenza Prevention, First Flu AFC Into the Clinic (Drug)
**Summary:** Shanghai Ark Biosciences said on August 13 that China’s NMPA approved an IND for AK0406 injection, an antiviral drug–Fc conjugate (AFC), for influenza prevention—the first influenza AFC to enter clinical development in China. The candidate conjugates a potent small-molecule antiviral to an antibody Fc fragment for long-acting pre- or post-exposure prevention and potential treatment. The company cites preclinical breadth, potency, and duration and will continue an Australian Phase 1 program. Seasonal vaccines remain the mainstay of flu prevention, with gaps from antigenic drift and weaker protection in older or immunocompromised people.

### 7. pH-Responsive Membranolytic Peptide Programs Immunogenic Lytic Death to Boost Checkpoint Therapy (Oncology)
**Summary:** Yuan and colleagues report in *Nature* (version of record August 5, 2026) a synthetic acid-responsive membranolytic peptide, aMPC16-CA50, that responds in stages to falling pH in the tumor extracellular space and lysosomes, producing time-lagged rupture from lysosomal to plasma membranes—immunogenic membranolytic cell death (mLCD). The program drives inflammatory transcription in tumor cells, enhancing MHC-I antigen presentation on dendritic cells and T-cell activation. pH-responsive kinetics and lytic activity were critical to immunogenicity. In mice, aMPC16-CA50 improved immune-checkpoint blockade and was well tolerated systemically. The work remains preclinical.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Membranolytic peptide programs immunogenic cell death for cancer therapy](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10899-5)

**Commentary:** Encoding *how* membranes rupture in time and space is closer to combination immunotherapy than simply punching holes in tumor cells.

## IV. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 8. Antarctic Peninsula Mercury Loop Speeds Up: Ice-Melt Land Release Up ~550% Since Preindustrial (Climate)
**Summary:** Chengzhen Zhou, Maodian Liu, Xuejun Wang and colleagues at Peking University analyzed 16 Antarctic Peninsula shelf sediment cores in *PNAS* (doi: 10.1073/pnas.2613472123). Modern mercury accumulation is about twice the global continental-shelf average and has risen about 160% since industrialization. About 56% of mercury in the cores is attributed to atmospheric deposition; the rest to glacier melt, weathering, and erosion. Their budget shows ice-melt-driven terrestrial mercury release up about 550% versus preindustrial, air–sea exchange up about 350%, and export to the open ocean up about 400%. The authors argue warming is turning Antarctica’s historical mercury reservoir into an active secondary source. Eos (August 14) and India’s National Herald (August 16) covered the paper.

### 9. Ambient Blade-Coated Perovskites: 26.5% Certified Single-Junction, 33.0% Certified Tandem (Photovoltaics)
**Summary:** Wang, Hu, Niu and colleagues report in *Nature Sustainability* on August 13 a hole-transporting co-polymer, PNCC (triarylamine plus carbazole phosphonic acid), enabling ambient-air blade-coated perovskite cells and modules: 26.6% power-conversion efficiency (26.5% certified) for single-junction devices and 23.2% for modules, retaining 99.8% of initial efficiency after 1,200 hours of continuous light soaking. Using PNCC’s conductivity and morphological uniformity, they also show monolithic perovskite–silicon tandems at 33.0% certified efficiency. The work targets scalable fabrication without inert atmospheres.

**Links:**

- [Nature Sustainability — Scalable ambient fabrication of single-junction and perovskite–silicon tandem solar cells](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-026-01916-6)

**Commentary:** Certified efficiency becomes factory language only when it can be blade-coated in air; 33% tandems move the lab window onto the humidity of a production line.

### 10. Model: U.S. Solar Tariffs Above ~18%–19% Trigger a Carbon Rebound in China’s PV Sector (Policy & Climate)
**Summary:** Yuan and Zheng, writing in *Frontiers in Environmental Science* on August 14, use a GTAP-E computable general-equilibrium model of U.S. tariffs on Chinese photovoltaic products. Under an extreme TTB tariff scenario, China’s PV embodied carbon rebounds about 4.9% cumulatively in 2025–2027 while U.S. emissions rise about 30.5%. They identify a critical tariff band of about 18%–19% beyond which China’s PV emissions reverse from decline to growth; even if strong decoupling returns by 2030, extreme tariffs still cut cumulative emission reductions by about 41%. Historical 2000–2022 data show 1% output growth associated with about 0.5% embodied-carbon growth.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Environmental Science — The clean energy trade war: assessing the climate costs of U.S. solar tariffs on China’s PV sector](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2026.1835650/full)

**Commentary:** Tariffs hit supply chains; the climate ledger can still show a lose–lose kink that protects industry while raising carbon.
