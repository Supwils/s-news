## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. ISS Spacewalk on Aug. 18 to Replace Space-to-Ground Antenna; Adenot to Be First French Woman on EVA (Human Spaceflight)
**Summary:** NASA updated coverage for U.S. spacewalk 97 on August 18, 2026: NASA astronaut Anil Menon and ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot will exit the Quest airlock to remove a failed Space-to-Ground antenna, stow it on an external platform, and install a spare on the Z1 truss. The antenna is a critical high-speed data link between Houston Mission Control and the station. The EVA is expected to last about 6.5 hours, with NASA live coverage from about 7:00 a.m. EDT and a start near 8:35 a.m. It will be Menon’s second spacewalk and Adenot’s first, making her the first French woman to conduct a spacewalk. On August 6, Menon and Jessica Meir spent about 6 hours 27 minutes outside preparing the 3B power channel for a future IROSA. India Today followed the story on August 14.

**Commentary:** The EVA is maintenance, but swapping a failed high-speed antenna back into redundancy decides whether subsequent science downlink stays stable.

### 2. ESA Releases Aug. 12 Totality from Space: Proba-3 Artificial Eclipse Meets the Moon’s Shadow (Sun–Earth)
**Summary:** ESA posted on August 14 that hours before the August 12, 2026 total solar eclipse over parts of Europe, the Proba-3 pair—about 60,000 km above Earth and 150 m apart—used the Occulter as an artificial Moon for the 65th ASPIICS corona image; the real Moon then crossed the field, creating a double eclipse. BBC Sky at Night reported the same day that geostationary imager MTG-I1 (about 36,000 km up) captured the lunar shadow sweeping Greenland, Iceland, and a sliver of northeastern Spain and Portugal; totality was visible about 20:26–20:34 CEST over Greenland, Iceland, Spain, and a small part of northern Portugal. NASA Earth Observatory published a sunflower-field composite from León, Spain, and recapped Iceland WB-57 corona flights and eclipse ballooning.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Proba-3’s eclipse hours before totality in Europe](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/08/Proba-3_s_eclipse_hours_before_totality_in_Europe)
- [BBC Sky at Night — The solar eclipse from space](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/news/august-2026-solar-eclipse-from-space)

**Commentary:** Natural totality is a minutes-long calibration; Proba-3 turns the same physics into a repeatable formation-flying optics experiment.

## II. Biomedicine

### 3. Whole-Brain Millisecond Voltage Imaging: Larval Zebrafish Scanned at 200.8 Hz (Neuroscience)
**Summary:** Zeguan Wang, Jie Zhang, and Ed Boyden’s MIT group report in *Nature Methods* on August 14 a remote-refocusing light-sheet microscope that voltage-images larval zebrafish brains expressing Positron2-Kv at 200.8 Hz (about once every 5 ms), covering ~85% of brain volume. Across four fish they segmented about 17,000–26,000 neuronal ROIs and retained about 13,000–19,000 after excluding blood-flow artifacts—up to roughly one quarter of the ~78,000 neurons estimated in a 7-dpf fish. Visually evoked sequences mapped across the optic tectum; stimulus-independent bursts mapped in cerebellum and medulla. MIT News the same day stressed that calcium imaging is too slow to catch single spikes.

**Links:**

- [Nature Methods — Voltage imaging of neurons distributed across entire brains of larval zebrafish](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03179-7)
- [MIT News — High-speed microscopy reveals electrical activity across the brain](https://news.mit.edu/2026/high-speed-microscopy-reveals-electrical-activity-across-brain-0814)

**Commentary:** Calcium is a proxy; voltage is the computation—whole-brain voltage imaging makes “how the network fires together” an observation, not an inference.

### 4. EMA Validates Pfizer/Valneva Lyme Vaccine Marketing Application (Vaccines)
**Summary:** Pfizer and Valneva announced on August 14 that the European Medicines Agency has validated the Marketing Authorization Application for PF-07307405, a 6-valent outer surface protein A (OspA) Lyme disease vaccine candidate, and will begin assessment. The filing rests on Phase 3 VALOR (NCT05477524): 9,437 participants aged 5 years and older in high-incidence U.S., Canadian, and European sites were randomized 1:1 to vaccine or saline at months 0, 2, and 5–9 plus a fourth dose one year later. The companies report efficacy of more than 70% against Lyme disease cases, with good tolerability and no identified safety concerns. Validation starts review; it is not an approval.

**Links:**

- [Pfizer — Lyme Disease Vaccine Candidate Marketing Authorization Application Validated by EMA](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer-and-valnevas-lyme-disease-vaccine-candidate)

**Commentary:** Vector-borne vaccines are judged on field efficacy, not titers alone—EMA validation starts the regulatory clock after a positive Phase 3.

### 5. Bioresorbable Triboelectric Implants Readable by Handheld Light (Implantable Electronics)
**Summary:** Meng and colleagues at Yonsei University, with Donghua University, Samsung Medical Center, and Hong Kong Polytechnic University, report in *Nature Nanotechnology* on August 14 a battery-free, bioresorbable triboelectric implant using machine-learning-selected nanoscale iridium(III) complexes for both phosphorescent readout and charge generation. Device outlines are visible to the unaided eye under handheld illumination; ultrasound at 0.5 W cm⁻² yields up to about 3.6 Vpp. In mice, optical readouts tracked position, morphology, and damage, correlated defects with loss of electrical output, and followed integrity and resorption over 38 weeks.

**Links:**

- [Nature Nanotechnology — Bioresorbable and optically readable triboelectric implants](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41565-026-02256-4)

**Commentary:** Implants usually fail silently—encoding structural integrity as a transcutaneous optical report gives clinicians lead time before a second surgery.

### 6. Giant-Virus Genome Catalogue Expands to 8,508 Species-Level Representatives (Viromics)
**Summary:** Yumary M. Vasquez and colleagues published GVMAGs V2 in *Nature Microbiology* on August 13: 18,727 giant-virus metagenome-assembled genomes from public assemblies and other sources, dereplicated at 95% ANI into 8,508 species-level representatives—about a sixfold increase over prior species-level frameworks. Phylogenomics supports 712 previously undescribed genera, 13 new families, and a proposed order Mycodnavirales. Improved gene calling covered more than 1,300 genomes. Mining found putative endogenous viral elements in algae, fungi, and parasitic protists; Algavirales genomes are enriched for pollutant-degradation genes, with widespread biosynthetic clusters resembling antimicrobial and antibiotic-resistance functions.

**Links:**

- [Nature Microbiology — Genomic catalogue of giant viruses reveals expanded diversity and functional potential](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-026-02435-y)

**Commentary:** Giant viruses are no longer culture rarities but a catalogued functional reservoir in environmental metagenomes—taxonomy expansion is the prerequisite for assigning ecological roles.

## III. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 7. Cost of Capital Inflates LMIC Renewables: Financing Can Be Nearly 80% of LCOE (Energy Finance)
**Summary:** Luke Hatton, Gbemi Oluleye, Malte Jansen and colleagues at Imperial College London report in *Nature Communications* on August 13 that renewable projects in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) can have more than 20 times the mitigation potential per project versus high-income countries, yet high capital costs add on average about US$26/MWh (solar) and US$24/MWh (onshore wind) to LCOE relative to high-income financing terms. Financing can account for up to 78% of LCOE in LMICs versus about 37–39% in high-income countries. Under a pathway consistent with tripling global renewable capacity by 2030, that implies extra annual costs of about US$245 billion (solar) and US$329 billion (wind).

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — High costs of capital shape the mitigation effects of renewable energy deployment globally](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75666-6)

**Commentary:** Insolation and wind speed are not the bottleneck—the price of money is; the same panel’s mitigation depends on the discount rate.

### 8. Low-Loading Nickel Grows [Ni1O1Ni4] Motifs In Situ for Methane Partial Oxidation (Catalysis)
**Summary:** Tan and colleagues report in *Nature Catalysis* on August 14 that partial oxidation of methane (POM) need not rely on ~10 wt% metallic nickel nanoparticles. They observe in situ [Ni1O1Ni4] motifs on NiO, with one Ni atom on an oxygen bridging four Ni atoms. A Ni/Al2O3-ME catalyst made by depositing preformed Ni nanoparticles at 0.8 wt% Ni achieved 92.0% CH4 conversion and 87.0% CO/H2 selectivity at 650 °C, with a stable H2/CO ratio of 2.0. Operando spectroscopy and microscopy show metallic Ni oxidizing to NiO during POM; high-resolution imaging plus DFT indicate the motif on NiO(100) lowers the C–H activation barrier to 12.5 kcal mol⁻¹.

**Links:**

- [Nature Catalysis — In situ generation of active motifs on Ni/Al2O3 during partial oxidation of methane to syngas](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41929-026-01580-1)

**Commentary:** Replacing “stack more metal” with “the reaction grows its own active site” rewrites nickel-loading and stability assumptions for syngas routes.

## IV. Basic Research

### 9. STAR Tracks Baryon Number: Evidence Favors a Y-Shaped Gluon Junction in the Proton (Particle Physics)
**Summary:** The University of Science and Technology of China, with Kent State University, Brookhaven National Laboratory and the STAR Collaboration, measured net baryon versus net charge transport. The paper *Tracking the baryon number with nuclear collisions* appeared in *Science* in the early hours of August 14 Beijing time. Isobar ruthenium–zirconium collisions and photon–gold interactions show baryon number is stopped and transported to mid-rapidity more readily than electric charge, disagreeing with a naive picture in which valence quarks each carry baryon number 1/3 and matching a Y-shaped gluon “baryon junction” proposed in the 1970s and developed by Kharzeev in 1996. Science News, Scientific American, and USTC covered the result; RHIC shut down in early 2026 to make way for an electron–ion collider.

**Links:**

- [USTC News — Scientists observe a peculiar structure inside nucleons](http://news.ustc.edu.cn/info/1055/95965.htm)
- [Science News — The glue that binds a proton may be key to its identity](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/gluons-proton-identity-baryon)

**Commentary:** Baryon-number conservation has been textbook for decades; whether it “lives” on quarks or a gluon junction now has a falsifiable collision fingerprint.

### 10. Double Chooz Detects Antineutrino Afterglow from a Shutdown Reactor (Nuclear & Particle Physics)
**Summary:** Anthony Onillon and Thierry Lasserre of the Max-Planck-Institut für Kernphysik lead the Double Chooz result in *Physical Review Letters*: the first measurement of antineutrinos from spent nuclear fuel after shutdown. The detector sits underground about 400 m from the two cores at Chooz, France, with more than 30 cubic meters of liquid scintillator. During 17.2 days with both units off, it recorded about 100 candidate events linked to residual radioactivity in the cores and nearby spent-fuel pools, matching simulations of fuel inventory and long-lived fission-product decay. ScienceDaily carried the MPIK release on August 14; JUNO-TAO also showed reactor-off data at Neutrino 2026.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Scientists detect a nuclear reactor’s ghostly afterglow for the first time](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260814011036.htm)
- [phys.org — First measurement of antineutrinos from spent nuclear fuel](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-antineutrinos-spent-nuclear-fuel-emissions.html)

**Commentary:** “Reactor off” is not “neutrino source off”—safeguards have lacked this declaration-independent fingerprint of shutdown and spent fuel.

### 11. Bilayer-Graphene Antidot as a Fractional Coulombmeter: Direct Fractional Quantum Hall Charge (Condensed Matter)
**Summary:** Di Luca and colleagues report in *Nature Physics* on August 14 a gate-defined bilayer-graphene antidot in the Coulomb-dominated regime that extracts quasiparticle charge from tunneling and yields direct fractional-charge measurements in integer and fractional quantum Hall states. Their model attributes differences in measured charge to edge re-equilibration tied to the parity of downstream integer edge modes. The simple, tunable design is proposed as a platform for other van der Waals materials.

**Links:**

- [Nature Physics — Quantum Hall antidot as a fractional coulombmeter](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03412-2)

**Commentary:** Fractional charge used to depend on interferometer interpretation; the antidot turns a controlled impurity into a coulombmeter and removes a layer of geometric ambiguity.
