## II. Quantum & Fundamental Physics

### 2. USTC team entangles quantum memories over 420 km of fiber, beating repeaterless capacity (Quantum)
**Summary:** Researchers including Xi-Yu Luo, Chao-Yang Wang, Ming-Yang Zheng, and Jian-Wei Pan at the University of Science and Technology of China report in an accepted *Physical Review Letters* paper that atomic-ensemble quantum memories were entangled over 420 km of optical fiber—more than four times prior matter–matter fiber demonstrations—after converting memory photons to the telecom S-band under the DLCZ protocol. Beyond about 320 km, they say the memory–memory success probability exceeds the repeaterless (PLOB) bound for direct entanglement distribution; ~100 km rates also matter for quantum-repeater builds. ScienceAlert covered the result on August 4, 2026.

**Links:**

- [ScienceAlert — Physicists Shatter Quantum Entanglement Distance Record With 420 Kilometers of Optical Fiber](https://www.sciencealert.com/physicists-shatter-quantum-entanglement-distance-record-with-420-kilometers-of-optical-fiber)
- [Physical Review Letters — Entangling quantum memories through a 420 km long fiber](https://journals.aps.org/prl/accepted/10.1103/ccd6-rf1s)

**Commentary:** Beating PLOB with stored entanglement is the milestone that matters—intercity quantum networks need memory, not just longer photon links.

### 3. Microcomb-driven fully connected quantum network enables 200+ km MDI QKD with an untrusted provider (Quantum)
**Summary:** Wang and colleagues published in *Nature Communications* on August 3, 2026, a large-scale fully connected quantum network based on two-photon Hong-Ou-Mandel interference, using integrated soliton microcombs and photonic encoding chips for massively parallel frequency generation, high-visibility HOM interference, and measurement-device-independent (MDI) quantum key distribution. The architecture keeps user-to-user security even if the network provider is untrusted, and the team demonstrates a fully connected network spanning more than 200 kilometers aimed at metropolitan and intercity scales.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Microcomb-driven large-scale fully connected quantum network](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75658-6)

**Commentary:** Removing trust in the operator turns a lab topology into a deployment-grade security model for fully connected QKD.

### 4. FDA clears Satellite Bio’s SB-101 IND: off-the-shelf liver cell therapy for infant UCDs (Biotech)
**Summary:** Satellite Biosciences announced on August 4, 2026, that the U.S. FDA cleared the IND for SB-101, a first-in-class off-the-shelf, cryopreserved liver cell therapy meant to restore metabolic liver function and help protect the developing brain in urea cycle disorders (UCDs). ### 5. Rocket reports safe dosing of first three Danon patients under revised RP-A501 Phase 2 protocol (Biotech)
**Summary:** On August 3, 2026, Rocket Pharmaceuticals said the first three patients in its pivotal Phase 2 Danon disease trial of RP-A501 were treated under the modified protocol restarted after a 2025 FDA hold tied to a capillary-leak-related death. Patients received a recalibrated dose of 3.8×10¹³ GC/kg with rituximab, sirolimus, and corticosteroids, dosed sequentially at least four weeks apart. So far, no thrombotic microangiopathy, capillary leak syndrome, or other major safety concerns were observed. The single-arm study is designed for 12 patients; Rocket is aligning with FDA on further dosing and expects a regulatory/program update in the second half of 2026.

### 8. Calcium isotopes pin Cretaceous foraminifer die-off to Kerguelen volcanism and ocean acidification (Paleoclimate)
**Summary:** Northwestern University researchers including Jonathan Chen, Andrew Jacobson, and Brad Sageman report in *Science*—with August 4 follow-up coverage—that ocean acidification drove one of the largest turnovers of planktic foraminifera near the Aptian–Albian boundary (~113 Ma), when ~70% of species vanished and only two thin-walled microperforate forms survived. δ⁴⁴/⁴⁰Ca records from DSDP Site 511 link the event to CO₂ from Kerguelen Plateau volcanism acidifying seawater and selectively eliminating heavily calcified taxa. It is the team’s fifth study tying large igneous provinces, acidification, and extinction across more than 60 million years.

**Links:**

- [Technology Org — Tiny fossils crack a Cretaceous cold case](https://www.technology.org/2026/08/04/tiny-fossils-crack-a-cretaceous-cold-case/)
- [phys.org — Ocean acidification triggered one of the ocean's most severe extinctions](https://phys.org/news/2026-07-tiny-fossils-cretaceous-cold-case.html)

**Commentary:** Deep-time chemistry turns “volcano → acid → extinction” into a repeatable chain—and a sharp analogue for today’s anthropogenic CO₂ uptake.

### 9. Floating PV humidity stress is not automatically worse than ground-mounted systems (Energy)
**Summary:** An international team led by Nathan Roosloot (Institute for Energy Technology, Norway) compared humidity-induced stress in floating (FPV) versus ground-mounted (GPV) PV using multi-year meteorology from seven inland waters and nearby land, Faiman module temperatures, and 25-year 3-D moisture-ingress modeling. Covered by pv magazine on August 4 and published in *Solar Energy*, the work finds mean internal moisture almost always higher in GPV—up to ~37%—while FPV module height above water and heat-transfer efficiency can shift mean moisture by up to ~47% and ~42%, respectively. Climate zone and system design often matter more than simply being over water.

**Links:**

- [pv magazine — Floating PV systems may face lower humidity stress than ground-mounted counterparts](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/04/floating-pv-systems-may-face-lower-humidity-stress-than-ground-mounted-counterparts/)
- [Zenodo — Data for Comparison of Humidity-Induced Stress Levels between Floating and Ground-Mounted Photovoltaics](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16269732)

**Commentary:** Once “over water = wetter = worse” falls, standards and warranties can follow design parameters instead of installation medium alone.

## V. Archaeology & Structural Biology

### 11. LiDAR finds 20,000+ precolonial Amazon earthworks; Aquiry peak population may hit 1.25–3 million (Archaeology)
**Summary:** Martti Pärssinen and colleagues at the University of Helsinki report in *Nature*—with continued coverage around August 3—that airborne LiDAR over ~4,497 km² resolved 432 earthworks (396 previously undocumented). Extrapolated across the ~183,000 km² Aquiry cultural area, totals may reach ~24,000–30,000 earthworks (conservatively >20,000). Peak population around AD 100–300 is estimated at 1.25–3 million, challenging the “pristine empty forest” narrative and implying larger precolonial impacts on soils, forest structure, and biodiversity than once assumed.

### 12. Flexible DNA as programmable glue turns protein crystallization into intentional design (Structural Biology)
**Summary:** Chad Mirkin’s Northwestern team shows in *Science Advances* that short flexible DNA strands grafted onto proteins act as blueprints and molecular glue, directing proteins into diffraction-quality, atomically ordered yet unusually soft crystals. The group grew more than 1,000 crystals and solved 28 distinct protein–DNA designs; varying DNA length and placement still yielded designed architectures, with DNA double helices linking neighbors visualized inside the crystals. Technology.org and related outlets continued coverage on August 4 for structural biology and drug-target applications.

**Links:**

- [Technology Org — Flexible DNA transforms protein crystallization](https://www.technology.org/2026/08/04/flexible-dna-transforms-protein-crystallization/)

**Commentary:** Crystallization shifts from hoping crystals grow to designing how proteins connect—flexibility and atomic order need not be opposites.
