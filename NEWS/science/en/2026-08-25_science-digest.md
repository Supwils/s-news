### 2. Roman Space Telescope sealed in Falcon Heavy fairing ahead of Aug. 30 launch (astronomy)

### 3. NASA modeling: Earth-associated microbes may endure in lunar south-pole shadow niches (planetary protection)
**Summary:** Led from NASA Goddard and published Aug. 19 in *Science Advances*—with continued Aug. 25 coverage—researchers combined Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter maps with heat and UV models to test whether flight- and human-associated microbes (including *Aspergillus niger* and *Bacillus subtilis*) could survive near Nobile Rim, Connecting Ridge, and De Gerlache Rim. “Survivable niches” ranged from crater-floor scales to boot-print size; UV-tough *A. niger* could persist even in some sunlit patches for at least about an Earth day in a dormant state. Authors stress there is no evidence the Moon can support growth or replication, but crewed activity will make native lunar chemistry harder to separate from Earth contamination.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — Human-Related Microbes May Survive Moon’s South Pole](https://science.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/human-related-microbes-may-survive-moons-south-pole-nasa-finds/)
- [Science / AAAS — Mighty microbes could survive on the Moon](https://www.science.org/content/article/science-adviser-mighty-microbes-could-survive-moon)

**Commentary:** The closer Artemis gets to permanently shadowed ice, the more planetary protection becomes a data-integrity problem, not a checklist ritual.

## II. Climate & Environment

### 4. Petermann Glacier calves a Manhattan-scale ice island—Arctic’s biggest break since 2020 (climate)
**Summary:** ESA’s Copernicus Sentinel-1 documented a ~76 km² slab detaching from northwest Greenland’s Petermann Glacier floating tongue on Aug. 4, 2026—up to ~150 m thick and roughly Manhattan-sized—the glacier’s largest floating-ice loss since 2012 and the Arctic’s most significant calving since 2020. Teams from the University of Ottawa, Stirling, Lancaster, Leeds, and Environment and Climate Change Canada’s ice service have tracked growing rifts since 2019; radar on Aug. 3 showed rapid centerline deterioration. Two further islands (~97 and ~87 km²) may follow. ScienceDaily featured ESA’s account on Aug. 25; Canada is tracking shipping risk as the ice island drifts and fragments.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Sentinel-1 captures major ice loss from Greenland glacier](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1/Sentinel-1_captures_major_ice_loss_from_Greenland_glacier)
- [ScienceDaily — A Manhattan-sized ice island just broke free from Greenland](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260823094145.htm)

**Commentary:** One-day repeat interferometry turned crack growth into a near-real-time record—polar change is now limited by observing cadence as much as by ice physics.

### 8. Columbia study: adult hippocampal neurogenesis stalls in major depression (neuropsychiatry)
**Summary:** Columbia University Irving Medical Center researchers report that adult hippocampal neurogenesis stalls in major depressive disorder and map molecular programs controlling the process; the work appeared Aug. 21 in *Nature Medicine*, with ScienceDaily highlighting it on Aug. 25. Nearly half a million post-mortem hippocampal cells were profiled at gene and protein level, revealing inflammation and cellular stress across the trisynaptic circuit. Impaired pattern separation—keeping similar memories and their emotional valence distinct—may help explain negative memory bias. Authors argue depression may eventually be reclassified by molecular features, akin to oncology.

### 9. *Nature*: wake-activated neuronal populations that regulate sleep drive (neuroscience)
**Summary:** Published Aug. 19 in *Nature*, a mouse study used whole-brain activity mapping, targeted manipulations, and electrophysiology to identify deprivation-sensitive regions including the anterior medial preoptic area and median raphe. Activating deprivation-responsive cells there increased sleep duration and intensity resembling recovery sleep; inhibiting them reduced sleep and blunted deprivation-related sleep propensity. Co-activating median-raphe GABAergic and serotonergic neurons synergistically promoted sleep, while co-inhibition chronically cut sleep by nearly 70%—yet most mice survived without classic compensatory drive or severe behavioral deficits.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Wake-activated neuronal populations that regulate sleep drive](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10928-3)

**Commentary:** Once sleep drive can be switched off at cell-type resolution, insomnia medicine expands from receptor affinity to whether homeostasis itself should be touched.

## IV. Basic Research & Cross-Disciplinary

### 10. Thousands of chromosome-scale genomes map irreversible “evolutionary highways” (evolutionary genomics)
**Summary:** A University of Vienna–led team reports in *Science Advances* the largest comparison of animal chromosome-scale genomes to date—more than 5,800 assemblies spanning 4,454 species and 19 phyla—introducing “evolutionary genome topology” to place architectures on one map. Genomes appear to travel limited irreversible “evolutionary highways,” driven by fusion-with-mixing: when chromosomes fuse and genes intermix, the prior arrangement cannot be restored, leaving durable ancestry markers. Lineages such as mosquitoes, glass sponges, and earthworms occupy highly isolated regions of the map. ScienceDaily featured the work on Aug. 25.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — Scientists find hidden “highways” guiding animal evolution](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260824065514.htm)
- [phys.org — Animal genomes follow irreversible 'evolutionary highways'](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-animal-genomes-irreversible-evolutionary-highways.html)

**Commentary:** A coordinate system for chromosome architecture gives conservation biology another metric—not only how many species, but how unique their genome layout is.

### 12. Ruthenium single-atom catalyst mildly cleaves lignin into aromatic chemicals (green chemistry)
**Summary:** University of Manchester and Hebei University of Technology researchers report in *ACS Catalysis* that isolated ruthenium atoms on nitrogen-doped carbon form Ru–N₄ sites that activate oxygen and oxidatively cleave stubborn lignin C–O/C–C bonds under relatively mild conditions, achieving near-complete conversion of model compounds with high phenolic yields and working on real lignin samples. Lignin can comprise up to ~35% of agricultural and forestry waste biomass and is nature’s largest renewable aromatic source, long limited by hard-to-break structure. ScienceDaily listed the advance among Aug. 25 science headlines.

**Links:**

- [University of Manchester — Turning plant waste into valuable chemicals more efficiently](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/turning-plant-waste-into-valuable-chemicals-more-efficiently/)
- [ACS Catalysis — Unveiling the Role of Ru–N₄ on Ru–N–C Single-Atom Catalyst (DOI)](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acscatal.5c08001)

**Commentary:** Circular biomass chemistry stalls on lignin’s toughness; once atomic sites are understood, catalyst design returns from alchemy to engineering.
