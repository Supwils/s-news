## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Webb finds a third giant planet, Beta Pictoris d, in a famous star system
**Summary:** Using JWST/NIRSpec’s integral field unit while studying the atmosphere of known planet Beta Pictoris b, a team led by Aidan Gibbs and Jean-Baptiste Ruffio (UC San Diego) serendipitously detected molecular fingerprints of carbon monoxide, water vapor, and methane, confirming a third giant planet, Beta Pictoris d. The young system lies about 63 light-years away and is roughly 23 million years old; the new world is estimated at least about twice Jupiter’s mass on an orbit near 30 astronomical units, inside the inner edge of the debris disk. An independent University of Edinburgh / ESO team confirmed the object with VLT/ERIS and archival imaging. ScienceDaily and others amplified the story on Jul 23, 2026.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — Webb discovers hidden planet in Beta Pictoris](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-discovers-hidden-planet-in-famous-star-system/)
- [ScienceDaily — Giant planet hiding in famous system (Jul 23, 2026)](http://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260722032111.htm)

**Commentary:** This is the first directly imaged exoplanet found primarily via moderate-resolution spectral template matching—sniffing atmospheres inside a bright disk scales better than stacking more coronagraphs alone.

### 3. FDA early-approves GSK’s Jideytro (zidesamtinib) for pretreated ROS1-positive NSCLC
**Summary:** On Jul 22, 2026, GSK said the U.S. FDA approved Jideytro (zidesamtinib), a ROS1-selective inhibitor, for adults with locally advanced or metastatic ROS1-positive non-small cell lung cancer previously treated with a ROS1 kinase inhibitor. Approval came ahead of the original Sep 18, 2026, target action date after Breakthrough Therapy and Orphan Drug designations. Support came from the global single-arm Phase 1/2 ARROS-1 study: among 117 pretreated patients, objective response rate was 44% (95% CI 34–53%), with duration-of-response rates of 82% at six months and 69% at 12 months. It is GSK’s first approved lung-cancer medicine, from the recently completed Nuvalent acquisition.

### 6. Nature: AI-redesigned starting points boost protease directed evolution (David Liu lab)
**Summary:** On Jul 22, 2026, David R. Liu and colleagues at Harvard reported in *Nature* using ProteinMPNN to redesign three botulinum neurotoxin (BoNT) proteases into more stable variants that retain full catalytic efficiency, then running side-by-side phage-assisted continuous evolution (PACE) from redesigned versus wild-type starting points. Across four campaigns, redesigned starts consistently yielded higher-activity products and unlocked highly functional sequences inaccessible in wild-type backgrounds. When evolving BoNT/E to selectively cleave the therapeutic target ataxin-2, proteases from the redesigned start achieved more than 79-fold greater selected specificity than the best wild-type-evolved variant. The authors frame AI redesign plus lab evolution as a practical general workflow.

**Links:**

- [Nature — AI-redesigned starting points enhance protein evolution](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10820-0)

**Commentary:** The paper turns “design plus evolution” from a slogan into controlled head-to-head experiments—the next bottleneck is moving the same paradigm to enzymes that are harder to express and screen.

## III. Climate, Energy & Environment

### 7. WWA: Extreme heat’s “atmospheric thirst,” not missing rain, now drives Europe’s droughts
**Summary:** A World Weather Attribution rapid analysis by scientists from 12 European countries, widely reported on Jul 23, 2026, by Euronews and Anadolu Agency, finds that this summer’s agricultural droughts are increasingly driven by heat-boosted potential evapotranspiration—the atmosphere’s “thirst”—rather than rainfall deficits alone. Human-caused climate change made western European agricultural soil droughts about five times more likely and eastern European ones about 11 times more likely; heatwave conditions capable of intense evaporative drying are about 80 times more likely in the west and about 40 times in the east. Impacts include France facing its worst maize harvest in 50 years, crop losses in Romania, and low rivers constraining shipping and power. Researchers warn that at about 1.4°C of warming the pattern is already severe; at about 2.8°C, intense evaporative conditions could roughly double in likelihood again.

**Links:**

- [Euronews — Heat rather than lack of rain drives Europe’s droughts](https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/23/scorching-dry-summers-heat-rather-than-lack-of-rain-is-now-to-blame-for-europes-droughts)
- [Anadolu Agency — Climate crisis-driven heat intensifies Europe’s drought](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/environment/climate-crisis-driven-heat-intensifies-europe-s-drought-scientists-say/4006808)

**Commentary:** Drought policy must shift from “waiting for rain” to managing heat—plans that track only precipitation anomalies will systematically miss spring soil already dried by a thirstier atmosphere.

### 8. Climate Central: Human-caused warming, not El Niño, drives most global coral bleaching
**Summary:** A Climate Central–led study in *Oceanography*, covered widely on Jul 22–23, 2026, concludes that every global coral bleaching event since 1998 required human-caused ocean warming to cross bleaching thresholds; El Niño mainly acts as a trigger rather than the root cause. Lead author Andrew Pershing and colleagues argue that without climate change, global-scale mass bleaching would essentially not occur, and project that by 2028 human-caused warming will outweigh El Niño’s influence in every coral-reef region. Attribution for the developing 2026–2027 El Niño likewise finds most bleaching risk still dominated by anthropogenic warming.

**Links:**

- [Inter Press Service — Climate change, not El Niño, drives most coral bleaching](https://www.ipsnews.net/2026/07/human-caused-climate-change-not-el-nino-drives-most-coral-bleaching-events/)
- [Carbon Pulse — Human-caused climate change bleaching coral, not El Niño](https://carbon-pulse.com/533805/)

**Commentary:** Reassigning blame from a natural oscillation to emissions does not erase El Niño early-warning value—but policies that still explain bleaching as “waiting for a strong El Niño” delay cuts and local cooling measures.

**Summary:** Yaowen Li, Xian-Kai Chen (Soochow University), Tiankai Zhang (Southeast University) and colleagues published online in *Nature* on Jul 22, 2026, a donor–acceptor–donor “electronic-resonance” self-assembled monolayer that raises negative charge density at the anchoring group, strengthening the phosphonic acid–ITO bond and suppressing desorption under heat and light. ### 10. Nature: Great Barrier Reef planktonic microbiome database maps 500+ new bacteria and 800k viral genomes
**Summary:** Yun Kit Yeoh (AIMS), Philip Hugenholtz (University of Queensland) and colleagues published the Great Barrier Reef Microbial Genomes Database (GBR-MGD) in *Nature* on Jul 22, 2026, from Nanopore plus Illumina sequencing of seawater at 48 reef sites. The resource holds 5,283 prokaryotic genomes (about 876 species, including about 584 previously undocumented), 20 chromosome-level picoeukaryote genomes, and 808,585 viral genomes estimated to represent about 362,802 viruses, plus a newly described marine Crassvirales clade. ## IV. Fundamental Research & Physics

### 11. Nature Communications: Non-local synchronization of continuous time crystals in a semiconductor
**Summary:** On Jul 22, 2026, Alex Greilich and Manfred Bayer (TU Dortmund) with Vladimir Korenev (Ioffe Institute) and colleagues reported in *Nature Communications* that continuous-time-crystal auto-oscillators based on electron–nuclear spins in InGaAs lock to a common frequency when optically pumped at separations up to about 40 μm; larger separations break synchronization. The interaction range matches the electron spin diffusion length, identifying spin transport as the coupling mediator. A wide-area optical pump can drive all oscillators in the illuminated spot into one synchronized state despite inhomogeneity, helping explain the exceptional stability of the auto-oscillations and pointing toward spin networks.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Non-local synchronization of continuous time crystals](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75714-1)

**Commentary:** Moving time crystals from a single beautiful oscillator to measurable spatial coupling is a hard gate—if spintronics wants networks, mesoscopic sync is one of the prerequisites.
