### 1. SpaceX Lofts Northrop Grumman’s MRV-MEP Robotic Servicing Mission Toward GEO
**Summary:** On Jul 21, 2026 at 5:15 p.m. ET, a SpaceX Falcon 9 lifted Northrop Grumman’s Mission Robotic Vehicle (MRV) and three Mission Extension Pods (MEPs) to geosynchronous transfer orbit from SLC-40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. MRV carries dual robotic arms developed at the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory under DARPA’s RSGS program; after roughly a year to GEO, it is slated to install life-extension pods on client satellites (including Optus and SES) and support inspection, repair, and upgrade work. First-stage booster B1069 flew its 32nd and final mission and was not recovered, reflecting the performance needed for the GTO trajectory.

### 2. NISAR Radar Data Opens to the Public; Antarctic “Hummingbird” Image Shows Hidden Ice Structure
**Summary:** NASA reported on Jul 21, 2026 that the U.S.–India NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) mission began ongoing public release of calibrated L- and S-band products as of Jul 20. The U.S. side is continuously releasing all L-band measurements collected since Jun 17; India’s team is distributing S-band data via the Bhoonidhi portal. An early East Antarctica image of Nunatak Zaterjavshijsja shows crevasse fields in green and smoother ice in magenta, resembling a hummingbird; radar penetrates snow and ice to reveal structure optical imagery largely misses. NISAR maps nearly all land and ice roughly twice every 12 days, generating on the order of tens of terabytes per day.

### 3. Near-Earth Object 1998 SH2 Confirmed as a Comet: Nongravitational Perturbations, Then a Faint Tail
**Summary:** A Jul 21, 2026 *Nature Astronomy* research briefing highlights work led by JPL’s Davide Farnocchia and colleagues: near-Earth object (875163) 1998 SH2 long looked inactive, yet 1998–2025 astrometry shows comet-like nongravitational acceleration (transverse component about \(A_2 = (-1.4 \pm 0.1)\times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)). During its August 2025 Earth approach, CFHT, ESO’s Danish Telescope, and the VLT recorded a weak low-surface-brightness tail, confirming cometary activity and prompting provisional designation P/1998 SH2. NASA notes that weak outgassing can stay hidden for decades and that nongravitational signatures are a planetary-defense diagnostic for comet-vs-asteroid identity.

**Links:**

- [Nature Astronomy — Cometary tail on a near-Earth object](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02912-8)
- [NASA — Near-Earth asteroid is actually comet](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/nasa-study-finds-near-earth-asteroid-is-actually-comet/)

**Commentary:** Looks like rock, moves like a comet—dynamics outed the identity before morphology, with direct stakes for impact-risk models.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. Johns Hopkins mKRAS-VAX Phase I: Safe Interception Shot Sparks Durable T Cells in 90% of High-Risk Volunteers
**Summary:** AACR coverage of a *Cancer Discovery* Phase I study (NCT05013216) reports that Johns Hopkins investigators including Neeha Zaidi, Elizabeth Jaffee, and Michael Goggins tested mKRAS-VAX—an off-the-shelf synthetic long-peptide vaccine against six common KRAS mutations—in 20 people at high hereditary risk for PDAC who also had radiographic pancreatic abnormalities (typically small cysts). Mutant-KRAS-specific effector and central memory T-cell responses appeared in 90% of participants and remained detectable in blood for up to about two years; after a 16.5-month median follow-up, none developed cancer, and an exploratory cyst shrinkage/resolution rate was about 37.5% versus about 6.8% in a similar unvaccinated cohort. Authors stress the small sample and non-efficacy primary design; larger studies and tissue-infiltration readouts are underway.

**Links:**

- [CancerNetwork — First-in-human mKRAS vaccine in high-risk PDAC](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/first-in-human-mkras-vaccine-elicits-durable-t-cell-responses-in-high-risk-pdac)

### 5. Functional Genomic Vaccinology (FGV) Platform Identifies Protective Pneumococcal Antigen Combos
**Summary:** Giuseppe Ercoli and colleagues, publishing in *Nature Communications* on Jul 21, 2026, introduce Functional Genomic Vaccinology (FGV): genome-wide antigen prediction, proteome-scale screening, and experimental immunogenicity validation. They expressed 222 conserved *Streptococcus pneumoniae* proteins, arrayed them, and immunized mice via magnetic-bead delivery; about 40% elicited significant IgG. Human serological filters prioritized 22 candidates; a four-antigen multicomponent vaccine protected mice from pneumonia and sepsis, with epitope mapping of potential protective sites. The authors frame FGV as a scalable path past the antigen-discovery bottleneck in bacterial vaccines.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Functional Genomic Vaccinology for S. pneumoniae](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75848-2)

**Commentary:** Antigen hunting shifts from serial guessing to a pipeline—especially useful where pneumococcal serotype churn outruns traditional design cycles.

### 7. Ember: Hydropower Hybridisation Could Unlock ~25 GW of EU Wind and Solar Without New Grid Capacity
**Summary:** Think tank Ember reported on Jul 21, 2026 that hybridising wind, solar, and storage behind existing hydropower grid connections across seven major EU hydro markets (Austria, Bulgaria, France, Italy, Portugal, Romania, Spain) could integrate about 25 GW of new renewables—roughly 18% of those countries’ expected 2026–2030 wind-and-solar additions—without adding grid capacity. Ember cites a broader EU connection gap above 120 GW and notes that only Portugal and Spain currently have clear hybrid-project rules. The analysis urges national legislation, faster permitting, and EU-level guidance.

### 8. Climate Change Reshapes Wind–Solar Adequacy Risk: New England and Texas Need Climate-Aware Siting
**Summary:** Qiu and colleagues in *Nature Energy* show that deep decarbonization with weather-dependent wind and solar faces adequacy risks that climate change compounds by reshaping both renewable supply and demand. Using ~12 km climate projections with county-level power-system optimization for New England and Texas, they find resource-inadequacy frequency can rise up to about fivefold by mid-century. Texas can preserve adequacy at near-zero extra cost by shifting wind westward; New England needs roughly a 2.34% investment increase via solar and transmission near load centers. The authors argue climate-resilient grids require high-resolution, climate-informed spatial planning—not only aggregate capacity targets.

**Links:**

- [Nature Energy — Climate change reshapes renewable siting and adequacy](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-026-02109-3)

**Commentary:** “How much” is not enough—“where it sits and which line it rides” is how climate risk enters prices and blackout odds.

## IV. Fundamental & Cross-Disciplinary Research

### 9. Ni Single Atoms / Cu Single Clusters Drive CO₂-to-Acetone Electrocatalysis at 48.8% Faradaic Efficiency
**Summary:** Yang et al., publishing in *Nature Communications* on Jul 21, 2026, report a Ni single-atom / Cu single-cluster catalyst (Ni SAC/Cu SCC) that reaches 84.1% total Faradaic efficiency for C₂₊ products in a flow cell, including 48.8% for acetone (C₃). CO spills from Ni to neighboring Cu and is confined in hierarchical pores of reduced graphene oxide, while in situ Cu⁰/Cu⁺ sites strengthen *CO adsorption, lowering C–C coupling barriers. The work offers a tandem-catalysis plus confinement template for multicarbon electrosynthesis.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — CO₂-to-acetone via Ni SAC/Cu SCC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75763-6)

**Commentary:** Nearly 50% acetone selectivity shows spillover, pore confinement, and Cu valence can pull together—industrial current density remains the next mountain.

### 10. Enzyme Cascade Turns Methanol into Acrylic Acid: Evolved Synthase Hits ~4.3 g L⁻¹ Titer
**Summary:** Zhao et al. in *Nature Communications* (Jul 21, 2026) repurpose a thiamine diphosphate (ThDP)–dependent enzyme into an acrylic acid synthase (AAS) that eliminates phosphate from DHAP; directed evolution yielded a ~13.6-fold improved variant. An enzymatic methanol-to-acrylic-acid (MAAP) pathway reached about 4.3 g L⁻¹ titer, 268.9 mg L⁻¹ h⁻¹ productivity, and 99.5% conversion. Quantum calculations map isomerization, an enol–ThDP intermediate, phosphate elimination, and hydrolysis. Authors present the route as a biomanufacturing paradigm for bulk acrylic acid and an expanded ThDP catalytic repertoire.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Enzyme cascade synthesis of acrylic acid](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75484-w)

**Commentary:** Hooking fossil-route acrylic acid onto a C1 methanol enzyme network is elegant—scale-up still hinges on titer and cofactor cycling.

### 11. Ångström Manganese-Oxide Ionic Channels Boost Rare-Earth Separation Enrichment Factors
**Summary:** Zou et al., in *Nature Chemical Engineering* on Jul 21, 2026, separate lanthanides in water using manganese-oxide solid ionic channels with tuned layer spacing. Distinct lanthanides drive different solid-state phase changes, sorting into two groups ~1.4 Å apart in spacing; narrower confinement raises dehydration barriers for lighter ions and improves cross-group separation. Pinning channel dimensions lifted La–Nd and La–Pr enrichment factors from about 1.6±0.1 and 1.5±0.1 to about 5.4±0.1 and 4.2±0.1. The approach aims to cut energy use and toxic ligands tied to solvent extraction.

**Links:**

- [Nature Chemical Engineering — Ångström-size channels for rare-earth separation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44286-026-00418-8)

**Commentary:** Rare-earth separation is ångström-scale spatial chemistry—closer to “structure is selectivity” than swapping another extractant.

### 12. Why Disease Alleles Persist: Genomic Evidence for Fertility–Longevity Antagonistic Pleiotropy
**Summary:** Brigos and colleagues in *Nature Ecology & Evolution* analyze genome-wide data across 62 complex diseases plus longevity and fertility. Disease-risk alleles associate on average with shorter longevity and higher fertility; alleles that raise both fertility and disease risk appear favored by selection over the past ~50,000 years. Mendelian randomization supports a causal effect of disease liability on longevity but not a robust causal effect on fertility. Among people with high polygenic disease risk, affected individuals had more children for most diseases, while early-onset diseases reverse the pattern. The results bolster antagonistic pleiotropy as an explanation for persistent disease alleles.

**Links:**

- [Nature Ecology & Evolution — Genetic trade-offs in fertility and longevity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-026-03140-z)

**Commentary:** Evolution’s cold answer to “why we get sick”: early reproductive advantage can buy later health costs—public-health targeting should watch those trade-off pathways.
