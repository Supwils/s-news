## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. NASA’s Swift-boost craft LINK slows spin to 1.47°/s; rendezvous timeline still under pressure (Space)
**Summary:** CNN reported on August 7 that Katalyst Space Technologies’ LINK spacecraft—built to raise the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory—had spun at about 9°/s after two reaction wheels failed, but electric-propulsion burns have cut the rate to about 1.47°/s. NASA and Katalyst are preparing a flight-software update to restore attitude control in the new configuration and begin phasing toward Swift’s orbit. Rendezvous is roughly a month later than first planned, while Swift’s decaying altitude keeps the clock running.

### 2. After Falcon 9 upper stage hits the Moon, Korea’s Danuri delivers before-and-after images (Space)
**Summary:** A roughly 4-ton spent SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage struck near Einstein Crater on August 5 (about 15:34 KST). Korea’s aerospace administration and KARI released data on August 6: lunar orbiter Danuri imaged from ~30 minutes before impact through eight passes total at 340–350 km range, with LUTI and PolCam recording terrain change and ejecta. NASA’s LRO is slated for follow-up; ground facilities including ESO also reported chemical signatures such as sodium and lithium in the plume.

### 3. Zhurong confirms first primary evaporites on Mars; gypsum may trap ~757-Myr-old brine (Planetary science)
**Summary:** Coverage on August 7 highlighted a University of Hong Kong–led team (with Brown, McGill, and Chinese partners) reporting in *Nature Astronomy* (July 29) that bright flat rocks in Utopia Planitia are primary macrocrystalline selenite gypsum crystallized directly from concentrated brine, not later alteration. The ~757-million-year-old crystals may seal microscopic pockets of ancient brine—among the most chemically specific liquid-water records yet on Mars—and strengthen the case for prioritizing primary evaporites in sample-return targeting.

**Links:**

- [TechTimes — Zhurong Finds First Primary Evaporites on Mars](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323576/20260807/zhurong-finds-first-primary-evaporites-mars-crystals-may-hold-757-million-year-old-brine.htm)

**Commentary:** Seeing hydrated minerals is one bar; possibly sealed ancient brine is another—and that is what moves sampling priority.

## II. Biomedicine & Synthetic Biology

### 4. Stanford and Arc: genome language models design self-replicating novel bacteriophages for the first time (Synthetic biology)
**Summary:** Stanford University and the Arc Institute reported in *Science* that genome language models Evo 1 and Evo 2, prompted from bacteriophage ΦX174, generated on the order of 700,000 candidate whole genomes; nearly 300 were synthesized, and 16 proved viable in *E. coli*, with some outperforming the wild-type phage and attacking strains resistant to the reference. Training deliberately excluded human-infecting viral genomes; Johns Hopkins biosecurity scholars urged stronger NIH/WHO-scale oversight. International media amplified the story on August 7.

### 5. FDA accelerates Tudriqev plus nivolumab for anti–PD-1–progressed advanced melanoma (Medicine)
**Summary:** On August 6 the U.S. FDA granted accelerated approval to Replimune’s oncolytic immunotherapy TUDRIQEV (vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg, formerly RP1) with nivolumab for adults with unresectable advanced cutaneous melanoma after anti–PD-1 progression. In IGNYTE Phase 1/2, among 91 efficacy-evaluable patients with at least one non-injected lesion (of 140 enrolled), ORR was 24.2% and median DOR 14.1 months. The product had been rejected twice within 13 months; an advisory committee voted 10–3 in favor. Confirmatory Phase 3 IGNYTE-3 is ongoing. Healio and others followed on August 7.

### 6. FDA approves world’s first mRNA seasonal flu vaccine, mFLUSIVA (Medicine)
**Summary:** Moderna announced on August 5 that the U.S. FDA approved mFLUSIVA (mRNA-1010), an mRNA seasonal influenza vaccine for adults 50 and older—the first mRNA flu shot cleared for market. Ages 50–64 received standard approval; 65+ received accelerated approval based on immune responses pending confirmatory work. The company expects supply for the 2026–2027 season. Phase 3 data showed efficacy advantage versus a standard flu shot; recommendation and coverage paths remain tied to immunization-practice committees.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. *Science*: Siberian methane nearly doubled in a decade; mid-century rise could offset ~20% of anthropogenic cuts (Climate)
**Summary:** A team led by Liu Yi at the Institute of Atmospheric Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, with UK, Japan, Germany, and Norway partners, published in *Science* (August 6–7 coverage): Siberian annual CH₄ rose by about 12.0±1.9 Tg from 2010–2023 (~1.1 Tg/yr), comparable to ~92% of the global wetland emission increase. Western Siberia is tied to winter Scandinavian-pattern moisture and wetland methanogenesis; eastern Siberia to Arctic Oscillation–linked highs, wildfire, and abrupt thaw. Under a high-emissions pathway, the 2050 increase could offset roughly 20% of targeted anthropogenic methane reductions.

**Links:**

- [IAP/CAS — Accelerating Siberian methane emissions](https://iap.cas.cn/gb/lunbo/202608/t20260807_8259449.html)
- [Phys.org — Siberia's growing methane threat could offset 20% of global methane reduction targets](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-siberia-methane-threat-offset-global.html)

**Commentary:** Beyond human cut ledgers, Arctic natural feedbacks are rewriting “saved” methane back into the budget—monitoring must treat permafrost–fire systems as first-class terms.

### 8. Xinjiang’s Yiwu Jurassic forest: ~160-Myr NPP about triple modern temperate conifer means (Paleoecology)
**Summary:** Yunnan University’s Feng Zhuo team and collaborators used in-situ gymnosperm stem fossils from Hami’s “Yiwu Jurassic Forest” to reconstruct structure in *Science Advances* (August 6): mean canopy ~28.6 m, biomass ~276–387 t/ha, and net primary productivity ~21–32 t/ha/yr—well above modern same-latitude and global temperate forest averages (~3× modern temperate conifer means). They attribute higher productivity to mid-Jurassic high CO₂ and greenhouse climate. Xinhua and others reported on August 7.

**Links:**

## IV. Fundamental Research & Astrophysics

### 9. Inouye Solar Telescope confirms ubiquitous Kelvin–Helmholtz vortices in the photosphere (Solar physics)
**Summary:** National Solar Observatory researchers including Kuridze and Wöger reported in *Nature* (August 5) that Hawaii’s 4-m Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope resolved magnetized Kelvin–Helmholtz vortices ~25–170 km across (spacing ~60–100 km) at magnetic flux boundaries in an active region, at ~19 km resolution—confirming a long-standing theoretical prediction. Simulations imply efficient transport of mass, energy, and magnetic flux, with implications for coronal heating and flux braiding. Ars Technica, CNN, and NSO covered the result through the week.

### 10. MIT: two charge-density waves in a rare-earth quantum material recover via distinct phase-transition routes (Condensed matter)
**Summary:** MIT’s Gedik lab with Stanford collaborators reported on August 7 in *Nature Physics* that in erbium tritelluride, laser pulses disrupt coexisting checkerboard charge-density waves near −230°C, then track recovery in time. The dominant wave returns via a smooth, uniform second-order transition; the subdominant wave reappears via nucleation-and-growth first-order dynamics akin to water freezing into ice. The work offers a time-domain framework for how competing electronic phases—including those linked to superconductivity and magnetism—emerge together.

### 11. Ghent–Cambridge–Oxford: particles cross a duality defect as “wisps of fog,” dissolving a monopole paradox (Theoretical physics)
**Summary:** Physicists from Ghent University with Cambridge and Oxford collaborators reported on August 7 (via *Nature Physics* / Phys.org) that in a quantum spin chain realizing Kramers–Wannier duality, a wave packet hitting a “duality defect”—an interface stitching two mirror descriptions of the same physics—transmits with 100% probability and reappears as a Nebelstreif: a fog-like excitation trailing an invisible quantum string. The result locates the long-missing outgoing state in magnetic-monopole scattering puzzles and is simulable today, with prospects for cold-atom quantum simulators.
