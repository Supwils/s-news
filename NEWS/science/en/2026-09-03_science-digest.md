## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. ESA/JAXA BepiColombo separates Mercury Transfer Module, starting arrival sequence (Space)
**Summary:** On the morning of Sept. 3, 2026 (ET), the joint ESA/JAXA BepiColombo mission completed a key milestone: the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) separated from the stacked pair of science orbiters. ESA mission control acquired a confirming signal around 9:53 a.m. ET and reported the spacecraft healthy and operating as expected. Launched in October 2018 after an ~8-year cruise of more than 10 billion kilometers, the orbiters are scheduled to enter Mercury orbit on Nov. 21, separate from each other in December, and begin science operations in April 2027. BepiColombo will be only the third mission to visit Mercury and the second to enter orbit—and Europe’s and Japan’s first Mercury mission.

## II. Fundamental Physics & Neuroscience

### 2. Quantum phase of free fall measured directly, consistent with Einstein’s equivalence principle (Quantum gravity)
**Summary:** An international team from Ben-Gurion University, the University of Ulm, and the University of Oxford (including Nobel laureate Sir Roger Penrose) reports in *Science Advances* (Sept. 2; DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aec8045) the first direct measurement of the predicted quantum phase of a freely falling object. Using a chip-scale “Quantum Galileo Interferometer,” they split ultracold rubidium atom waves into a free-falling path and a held path, then recombined them; the measured phase matched the value predicted by applying Einstein’s equivalence principle to a quantum object (coverage cites ~2.5% experimental uncertainty). The authors stress the result does not unify quantum mechanics with gravity or show that gravity itself is quantum—it shows the equivalence principle remains consistent in the regime tested.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Scientists observe Einstein's gravity in the quantum world](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-scientists-einstein-gravity-quantum-world.html)

**Commentary:** Unification is still distant, but the boundary condition is sharper—quantum matter also “falls Einstein’s way.”

### 3. HHMI Janelia and Google release complete male fruit-fly CNS connectome (Neuroscience)
**Summary:** On Sept. 3, HHMI’s Janelia Research Campus, Google Research, the University of Cambridge, and collaborators presented the first complete connectome of an adult male fruit fly’s brain and ventral nerve cord: about 166,691 neurons and 11,691 cell types, fully proofread and annotated including fruitless/doublesex expression. Synaptic-resolution comparison with the female connectome finds roughly 7,205 isomorphic, 114 dimorphic, 262 male-specific, and 69 female-specific types, with sex-specific circuitry concentrated in higher brain centers. Companion studies on vision, taste, and social behavior accompany the release; data are publicly accessible via male-cns.janelia.org and NeuPrint, building on the earlier complete female fly brain map.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** With both sexes mapped end-to-end, differences in courtship and decision circuits can move from “which cells” to full sensory-to-motor pathways.

### 4. University of Tokyo: electron spin steers hydrogen adsorption and abstraction on magnetic nickel (Surface chemistry)
**Summary:** Researchers at the Institute of Industrial Science, The University of Tokyo, and collaborators report in *Nature Communications* (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77076-0), covered Sept. 3, that controlling the spin orientation of hydrogen atoms with an external magnetic field dramatically changes the probability of both adsorption and hydrogen-abstraction reactions on a magnetic nickel surface. The work establishes electron spin as an important degree of freedom in surface chemistry and suggests magnetic fields as a new lever for reaction rates. Extending the approach to other materials will be needed before practical catalytic control can be claimed.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Electron spin unlocks magnetic control of hydrogen surface reactions](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-electron-magnetic-hydrogen-surface-reactions.html)

**Commentary:** Catalysis has long lacked a “spin knob”—if fields can stably rewrite surface reaction odds, hydrogen chemistry gains another design axis.

## III. Biomedicine

### 6. In-body CAR-T generation eases multiple sclerosis in small trial (Immunotherapy)
**Summary:** *Nature* news reports a proof-of-concept trial led by researchers including a team linked to Huazhong University of Science and Technology / Tongji Medical College, published this week in *The New England Journal of Medicine* (Cheng et al., *N. Engl. J. Med.* 395, 926–929, 2026): a modified virus delivered genetic instructions for chimeric antigen receptors so patients’ T cells made CAR-T cells inside the body targeting autoantibody-bearing B cells. Sixteen people with multiple sclerosis or other autoimmune conditions received a single intravenous dose and were followed for about six months; CAR-T cells expanded, B cells and autoantibodies fell, and replacement B cells did not produce the same autoantibodies. MS participants showed better motor and cognitive function and less fatigue; those with muscle-affecting disease improved strength scores and inflammation. Commentators call in vivo CAR-T cheaper and faster than ex vivo manufacturing but say larger trials are still required.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Immune therapy engineered inside the body eases multiple sclerosis](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02765-1)

**Commentary:** Moving the “cell factory” into the patient’s bloodstream is what could make autoimmune CAR-T scalable—if long-term viral-delivery safety holds.

Key secondary endpoints include overall survival, depth of response, and MRD negativity.

## IV. Climate, Policy & Energy Materials

### 9. UNEP: overshoot of 1.5°C now hard to avoid; charts “overshoot–peak–decline” path (Climate)
**Summary:** On Sept. 2 the UN Environment Programme released *Limiting Overshoot*, widely covered on Sept. 3: under current policies and near-term trajectories, exceedance of the Paris Agreement’s 1.5°C limit is now widely assessed as unavoidable and may occur within the next few years; even the most optimistic peak is about 1.8°C. The report frames an “overshoot, peak, and decline” pathway—immediate deep cuts (including methane), progress to net zero, then net-negative emissions to bring temperatures down—stressing that carbon dioxide removal complements, rather than replaces, mitigation. Lower peaks and shorter overshoot reduce risks of ice-sheet instability, Amazon dieback, and Atlantic overturning disruption.

**Links:**

### 11. Hebei team’s steric-gated dual-site chelation yields 27.39% inverted perovskite cell (Photovoltaics)
**Summary:** According to *pv magazine* on Sept. 3, researchers from Hebei University of Technology, Jiaxing Nanhu University, and Tianjin University report in *Joule* a steric-gated dual-site chelation strategy using adamantane-based additive ADA-DA to passivate perovskite defects without overcrowding interfaces that block charge transport. An inverted p-i-n cell reached 27.39% power-conversion efficiency under standard illumination (26.24% for the reference), said to be verified by an independent third party. Bifacial mini-modules of ~152–164 cm² exceeded 22% efficiency, and a module retained about 99.6% power after more than 5,000 hours of continuous LED illumination—pointing toward building-integrated PV potential.
