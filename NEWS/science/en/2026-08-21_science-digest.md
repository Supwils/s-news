## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Seventeen spacecraft map a “hidden” CME component, exposing a space-weather blind spot (space weather)
**Summary:** A Johns Hopkins–led team reports in *Science Advances* that observations from 17 spacecraft across the inner heliosphere—including NASA’s Europa Clipper—reconstructed a complex, highly asymmetric coronal mass ejection (CME) from December 2024. During a routine Plasma Instrument for Magnetic Sounding checkout, Clipper recorded unexpectedly hot, tenuous solar wind. From Earth’s line of sight the bulk of the eruption appeared to miss the planet, yet STEREO-A’s side view revealed a faster Earth-directed component along the Earth–Mars corridor. The authors argue that planetary missions in transit can fill forecasting gaps that threaten crewed deep-space radiation safety. Phys.org and others covered the result on Aug 21.

### 2. ESO/GRAVITY: Milky Way’s fastest star S301 reaches ~25,000 km/s and can sense black-hole spin (astrophysics)
**Summary:** The GRAVITY+ Collaboration reports in *Nature* the discovery of main-sequence star S301 (mK ≈ 19.3) on an ~8.7-year orbit around Sagittarius A* (~4.3 million solar masses). At pericenter—roughly Saturn’s orbital scale—its peak speed reaches about 25,000 km/s (over 8% of light speed), making it the fastest known star in the Milky Way and among the closest continuously trackable Galactic-center stars. Its orbit enters the regime where Kerr-metric β³ corrections matter, so a decade of follow-up with instruments such as ELT/MICADO could yield the first direct dynamical constraint on the supermassive black hole’s spin. High eccentricity fits a Hills-mechanism capture scenario. Chinese outlets including Science and Technology Daily amplified the story on Aug 21.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Discovery of a star sensitive to the spin of Sagittarius A*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10894-w)
- [ESO — Milky Way's fastest star orbits our supermassive black hole so closely it feels its spin](https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2612/)

**Commentary:** Galactic-center stars are graduating from mass meters to spin probes—strong-field GR finally has a trackable test particle.

## II. Biomedicine

### 3. Basel team: wake-activated brainstem neurons regulate sleep drive; inhibiting them cuts sleep by ~70% (neuroscience)
**Summary:** Alex Schier’s group at the University of Basel’s Biozentrum and collaborators identify in *Nature* key circuits that encode sleep debt in mice. Comparing whole-brain maps across deprivation, recovery sleep, and circadian behavior highlighted the anterior medial preoptic area and median raphe. There, GABAergic and serotonergic neurons grow more excitable with prolonged wakefulness and jointly promote recovery sleep. Chronic co-inhibition of both populations reduced sleep by nearly 70%, yet most animals lacked the severe behavioral deficits typical of extreme deprivation. The work argues that sleepiness is actively generated by specific wake-activated neurons, not a passive by-product of being awake.

### 4. Merck and Moderna: personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine Phase 3 meets RFS and DMFS endpoints (cancer immunotherapy)

Overall survival is not yet mature; detailed data and regulatory discussions are forthcoming.

### 5. Tohoku University: blood-camouflaged liquid-metal nanoplatform clears drug-resistant TNBC in mice (nano-immunotherapy)
**Summary:** Eijiro Miyako’s team reports in *Advanced Science* the multifunctional platform B-LM-DMX-αCD25: gallium-based liquid-metal nanoparticles (photothermal conversion efficiency >54%) coated with whole-blood components accumulate in tumors about fivefold more efficiently than conventional particles. Near-infrared irradiation heats tumors to ~58°C within about five minutes while anti-CD25 depletes intratumoral Tregs and a laser-triggered STING agonist (DMX) amplifies innate immunity. In orthotopic drug-resistant triple-negative breast cancer mouse models the regimen achieved 100% complete regression, suppressed lung metastases by more than 90%, and extended median survival beyond 70 days. The work remains preclinical; the team aims to extend it to pancreatic and ovarian cancers.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 7. Copernicus Sentinel-1: Petermann Glacier sheds an ~76 km² ice island in Greenland (polar observation)
**Summary:** ESA reports that on Aug 4, 2026, an ~76 km² tabular iceberg—about the area of Manhattan and up to ~150 m thick—broke from Petermann Glacier’s floating ice tongue in northwest Greenland, the glacier’s largest floating-ice loss since 2012 and among the Arctic’s most significant calving events since 2020. Sentinel-1C/1D one-day tandem interferometry captured accelerating fracture growth; teams from the University of Ottawa, Stirling, Lancaster, Leeds, and the Canadian Ice Service have monitored the tongue since 2019. Existing rifts suggest further ice islands of roughly 97 km² and 87 km² could still detach. Environment and Climate Change Canada is assessing shipping and infrastructure risk.

### 9. Nanjing University team: meter-scale perovskite modules hit 24.0% certified efficiency and pass full IEC reliability suite (photovoltaics)
**Summary:** Ke Xiao, Hairen Tan and collaborators at Nanjing University with Renshine Solar report in *Nature* replacing humidity-sensitive ammonium-halide passivators with chemically stable lead carboxylates (e.g., lead dioleate) plus a high-saturation-vapor-pressure solvent system compatible with ambient processing. They achieved certified efficiencies of 24.0% for an 810 cm² aperture-area module and 22.0% for a 0.72 m² total-area module (TÜV SÜD China and others), passed the full IEC 61215 reliability sequence, lost only ~2% after 1,300 hours of damp heat, and retained ~96% of initial efficiency after 2,200 hours of MPPT. pv magazine India’s Aug 21 coverage kept the record in circulation.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 10. Orbformer: a transferable deep QMC wavefunction foundation model reaches chemical accuracy on bond breaking (computational chemistry)
**Summary:** *Nature Communications* published on Aug 21 a transferable neural-network wavefunction model, Orbformer, pretrained on about 22,000 equilibrium and dissociating structures and fine-tunable on unseen molecules. On challenging multireference benchmarks, bond dissociations, and Diels–Alder reactions it consistently converges to chemical accuracy (~1 kcal/mol), with an accuracy–cost ratio competitive with classical multireference methods. The Microsoft-led effort turns “amortizing the Schrödinger equation across molecules” from slogan into a practical foundation-model workflow.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — An ab initio foundation model of wavefunctions that accurately describes chemical bond breaking](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76604-2)

**Commentary:** When wavefunctions can be pretrained and fine-tuned, the bottleneck shifts from per-system FLOPs to data and transfer strategy.

### 11. Caltech: neutral-atom quantum simulator makes first direct measurement of conformal-field-theory excitation spectra (quantum matter)
**Summary:** Manuel Endres, Jason Alicea and collaborators report in *Nature* measuring finite-size energy spectra of emergent (1+1)D Ising and tricritical Ising conformal field theories on a neutral-atom quantum simulator, recovering the universal level ratios long predicted by theory and using modulation spectroscopy to probe the dynamical structure factor. The technique offers a diagnostic for unknown quantum critical points and moves Cardy’s decades-old “energy ladder” from textbooks into a synthetic quantum-matter lab. Coverage clustered on Aug 20–21.
