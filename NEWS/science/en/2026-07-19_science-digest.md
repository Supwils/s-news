## I. Space & Deep Sky

### 1. JWST maps a self-feeding loop for a supermassive black hole in NGC 4696
**Summary:** An international team led by Julie Hlavacek-Larrondo (Université de Montréal) reports in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* nearly eight hours of JWST/NIRSpec observations of NGC 4696, the central galaxy of the Centaurus Cluster about 145 million light-years away. The hook-shaped ionized swirl seen by Hubble is resolved as a rotating multiphase circumnuclear disk roughly 800 light-years across, with gas speeds up to about 600 km/s, physically and kinematically linked to kiloparsec-scale filaments. Magnetohydrodynamic simulations reproduce filament condensation, angular-momentum loss, and inflow into the disk, supporting a closed AGN feedback cycle in which jets heat gas that later cools and returns as fuel—consistent with other radio-mode prototypes such as NGC 1275.

**Commentary:** The long-sought last mile between cluster-scale cooling filaments and sub-100-pc accretion is now an image, not only a model.

### 2. Rare CO chondrite fingerprinted as the dinosaur-killing impactor
**Summary:** Georgy Makhatadze, Frédéric Moynier (IPGP) and collaborators at UBC, Vrije Universiteit Brussel, and the University of Vienna report in *Science Advances* that high-precision nickel isotopes in K–Pg boundary clays from Denmark, Spain, and Italy best match an exceptionally rare CO (Ornans-type) carbonaceous chondrite—not the more volatile-rich CM types often assumed. Because CO chondrites are relatively poor in sulfur and water, the team argues the impactor may have delivered fewer volatiles than older models implied, with climate forcing more likely driven by pulverized target rock and fine debris blocking sunlight. The Chicxulub object was about 10–15 km across and struck at roughly 64,000 km/h; ScienceDaily highlighted the UBC-linked release on Jul 19, 2026.

- [IPGP — A new isotopic clue reveals the origin of the asteroid…](https://www.ipgp.fr/en/news-and-agenda/news/a-new-isotopic-clue-reveals-the-origin-of-the-asteroid-responsible-for-the-extinction-of-the-dinosaurs/)
- [Science Advances — The origin of Cretaceous-Palaeogene impactor revealed by nickel isotopes](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aef4858)

**Commentary:** Nickel isotopes shift the extinction story from “sulfur cargo on the asteroid” toward dust and vaporized crust as the main climate killers.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 3. Lilly’s oral GLP-1 orforglipron beats oral semaglutide in Phase 3 head-to-head
**Summary:** Coverage dated Jul 19, 2026 of the Phase 3 ACHIEVE-3 trial reports that Eli Lilly’s oral small-molecule GLP-1 agonist orforglipron, in 1,698 adults with type 2 diabetes across six countries, lowered HbA1c by about 1.71–1.91 percentage points over 52 weeks versus about 1.47 points for oral semaglutide (baseline HbA1c ~8.3%). Mean weight loss was about 6.1–8.2 kg versus about 5.3 kg. Gastrointestinal side effects were more common (~59% vs ~37–45%), and discontinuation for adverse events was about 10% versus about 4–5%. Convenience—no injections, refrigeration, or strict meal timing—is a clear advantage; tolerability and long-term adherence remain open questions before any real-world verdict.

**Links:**

- [Diabetes.co.uk — Orforglipron pill beats oral semaglutide in major diabetes trial](https://www.diabetes.co.uk/news/2026/jul/orforglipron-pill-beats-oral-semaglutide-in-major-diabetes-trial.html)

**Commentary:** An efficacy edge on paper still has to survive the gut—convenience only wins if patients stay on the pill.

### 4. FDA clears Celcuity’s Revtorpyk (gedatolisib) for HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer
**Summary:** On Jul 14, 2026 the U.S. FDA approved gedatolisib (Revtorpyk) with fulvestrant, with or without palbociclib, for adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer without a detected PIK3CA mutation after progression on at least one line of endocrine therapy in the metastatic setting. It is the first approved inhibitor spanning class I PI3K isoforms and both mTORC1 and mTORC2. In VIKTORIA-1 Study 1 (n=392), median PFS was about 9.3 vs 2.0 months for the triplet versus fulvestrant alone (HR 0.24) and about 7.4 vs 2.0 months for the doublet (HR 0.33). Launch is expected late in Q3 2026; a supplemental filing for the PIK3CA-mutant cohort is planned. Fred Hutch’s Sara Hurvitz led the pivotal program.

### 5. China’s Oulin Biotech reports 73.25% efficacy for recombinant staph vaccine Phase 3
**Summary:** Chengdu Oulin Biotech and the Army Medical University completed unblinding of a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial of a recombinant *Staphylococcus aureus* vaccine (E. coli–expressed). Protective efficacy reached 73.25%, above the protocol target of 60%, with mainly common local and systemic reactogenicity and overall acceptable safety. The trial was anchored at Beijing Jishuitan Hospital across more than 60 sites. The company said it will prepare the clinical study report and registration dossier; mid-July it also rejected circulating “audit report” claims as inconsistent with trial data, stating the 73.25% figure came from an independent statistician using the full analysis set and a Cox model.

## III. Climate, Energy & Materials

### 7. TU Graz FLAPTrack: sun-tracking gains near 40%, folds away from hail
**Summary:** Graz University of Technology researchers describe in *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* the FLAPTrack (Face-to-Face Lay-Down Anti-Degradation Protection) system: dual-axis tracking that raises average yield nearly 40% versus fixed racks (up to about 56% on some days), then folds panels face-to-face near the ground when hail, high wind, or snow is forecast, cutting wind load and shielding the front surface (optional hail net). A ~1 kW rooftop demonstrator is collecting weather, power, and mechanical-wear data. The design explicitly targets climate-driven extreme-weather damage to solar assets.

**Links:**

### 8. *Nature*: quasi-pure MOF membranes roll-to-roll for cheaper gas separations
**Summary:** Song et al. in *Nature* (published Jul 15, 2026) introduce quasi-pure MOF membranes (>90 vol% MOF) made via a merged-phase approach that confers polymer-like processing while retaining near-intrinsic MOF separation performance. Continuous industrial roll-to-roll fabrication of (110)-oriented ZIF-67 membranes achieved propylene permeability of about 160 barrer and mixed-gas propylene/propane selectivity near 100—enough for polymer-grade propylene in the authors’ techno-economic case, with ~80% lower purification cost versus distillation. The same architecture was shown for ethylene/ethane, carbon capture, and hydrogen purification, attacking the chronic performance-versus-scalability gap.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Scalable quasi-pure MOF membranes for energy-efficient gas separations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10655-9)

**Commentary:** Selectivity only matters if it survives the factory floor—this work puts MOF pores on a production web.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 9. LSU room-temperature quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystal sorts light by coherence
**Summary:** Omar Magaña-Loaiza’s group at Louisiana State University reports in *Nature* quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystals: gold nanoantenna “meta-atoms” on a glass chip whose near-field multiparticle dynamics create allowed and forbidden quantum-statistical bands that selectively transmit or suppress multiphoton fields by coherence properties—at room temperature. The authors frame it as a blueprint for cryogen-free, coherence-sensitive photonic materials for quantum information transport, sensing, and energy harvesting; ScienceAlert and related outlets amplified the result around Jul 19, 2026.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystals](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10782-3)

**Commentary:** Engineering statistical bands for light is a more scalable path than stacking ever-larger dilution refrigerators.

### 10. Quantinuum hardware: braiding plus fusion yields a universal topological gate set
**Summary:** Lo et al. in *Nature* (published Jul 15, 2026; issue dated Jul 16) prepare a 54-qubit ground state of the quantum double of S₃ on Quantinuum’s H2 processor, encode logical information in the global fusion space of non-Abelian anyons, and realize a universal topological gate set and readout by combining braiding with fusion—including topological magic-state preparation. The result shows that fusion as a computational primitive can make minimally non-Abelian topological order universal where braiding alone cannot, and that S₃ order is both preparable at scale and rich enough for universal computation.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Universal gates from braiding and fusing anyons on quantum hardware](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10709-y)

**Commentary:** Topological QC graduates from “braid-only folklore” to a demonstrated universal toolkit on real hardware.

### 11. All-electrical control of single-molecule quantum spins via exchange mediation
**Summary:** Teams at IBS Center for Quantum Nanoscience (Andreas Heinrich) and Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (Philip Willke) report in *Nature Physics* all-electrical, nanoscale control of individual iron phthalocyanine molecular spins using an exchange-mediated interaction with a magnetic STM tip. Resonance-frequency shifts become strongly nonlinear as bias approaches a molecular electronic level, reaching ~30%—an order of magnitude beyond most prior electrical tuning—and experimentally validate a QNS theoretical framework in which voltage-controlled exchange generates a highly localized effective magnetic field for spin control.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Scientists achieve all-electrical control of single-molecule quantum states](https://phys.org/news/2026-07-scientists-electrical-molecule-quantum-states.html)

**Commentary:** A pure electrical knob for molecular spins is what scalable molecular qubits have been waiting for.
