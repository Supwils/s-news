## I. Space & Astronomy

### 1. Chandra’s “red potato” galaxy: a neighbor’s black-hole jet may stir a cold-gas “pot” and suppress star formation
**Summary:** Multiwavelength observations with NASA’s Chandra X-ray Observatory, JWST, and the VLT show that early-universe galaxy MQN01 J004131.9-493704—nicknamed the “red potato” from its JWST appearance and lying about 11.7 billion light-years away—sits inside a vast cold-gas reservoir yet forms few young stars. A team led by Weichen Wang (University of Milan-Bicocca), publishing in *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, reports that a particle jet from a supermassive black hole in a neighboring galaxy (~200,000 light-years away) appears aimed at that gas cloud and may drive turbulence that keeps gas from collapsing into stars. Space.com and others continued coverage on July 27, 2026; the work is among the few to probe gas around passive galaxies at such distances.

**Commentary:** Extending feedback from a host black hole to a cosmic-web neighbor reframes how early galaxies can be quenched without firing their own jets.

## II. Fundamental Physics & Materials

### 2. First direct evidence of a light-induced Floquet topological state in SnTe
**Summary:** An international team led by Claude Monney (University of Fribourg) reports in *Nature Physics* the first direct experimental evidence of a Floquet topological state. Using time-resolved ARPES on tin telluride (SnTe), they observed a Dirac cone—the hallmark of a topological surface state—inside the bandgap when pump and probe pulses arrived together and the pump was tuned near the gap; the cone vanished within ~100 femtoseconds, without ionic structural change. Floquet-extended DFT calculations reproduced the light-driven band inversion. Phys.org featured the result on July 27, 2026; authors note the precise topological character of the transient state is not yet fully settled and sustaining it beyond femtosecond pulses remains hard.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Physicists capture first direct evidence of a Floquet topological state](https://phys.org/news/2026-07-physicists-capture-evidence-floquet-topological.html)

**Commentary:** Imaging a light-written topological phase moves Floquet engineering from theory toward ultrafast optoelectronics—longevity and material generality are the next gates.

### 3. Macroscopic spacetime crystal melts in three stages: space and time order decouple
**Summary:** Jie Zhang, Matteo Baggioli and colleagues at Shanghai Jiao Tong University report in PNAS a tabletop active-disk experiment that builds a macroscopic continuous spacetime crystal and, for the first time, watches it melt. At high packing fraction, disks form a triangular lattice that rotates as a rigid body (~one revolution every ~5 hours, lasting nearly a day); lowering density triggers a three-stage melt in which temporal order collapses via weakened interactions and loss of directional persistence, while spatial order later fails through defect proliferation, with a hexatic intermediate. ScienceAlert covered the work on July 27, 2026.

### 4. Liquid-metal “capacitor” enables a monolayer microfluidic pulse oscillator (*Nature Communications*)
**Summary:** Published July 27, 2026 in *Nature Communications*, researchers demonstrate a monolayer microfluidic pulse oscillator driven by a shapeshifting liquid-metal (LM) capacitor. Under AC fields, LM capacitive energy storage couples to Marangoni flow to convert interfacial energy into pulsed fluid motion; vortices dominate near the LM while pulsed oscillatory flow fills the far field, enabling frequency-tunable particle focusing and separation. Versus fixed multilayer fluidic oscillators, the design stresses flexibility, reconfigurability, and fast response for lab-on-chip flow control.

**Links:**

## III. Biomedicine & Health

### 5. UC San Diego smart ring tracks glucose and ketones simultaneously from sweat
**Summary:** Joseph Wang’s team at UC San Diego’s Jacobs School of Engineering reports in *Nature Communications* (paper dated ~July 23; Scienmag and others on July 27) a fully integrated biochemical smart ring. An osmotic hydrogel passively draws sweat to an electrochemical array that can monitor glucose and ketones together and has demonstrated panels including vitamin C, uric acid, lactate, and alcohol. In healthy volunteers and people with type 1 diabetes, glucose trends tracked commercial CGMs and ketone readings agreed well with blood ketone meters; a flexible Zn–AgO battery supports ~12 hours between charges with wireless phone readout. The device remains a prototype requiring subject-specific calibration.

**Links:**

- [Scienmag — Wearable ring monitors glucose and ketones in sweat simultaneously](https://scienmag.com/wearable-ring-monitors-glucose-and-ketones-in-sweat-simultaneously/)

**Commentary:** Moving wearables from motion proxies to metabolic molecules is the clinically meaningful step—calibration burden and sweat variability will decide whether it leaves the lab.

### 6. Yonsei builds world’s largest mouse gut-virome catalog; viral profiles alone predict aging
**Summary:** Yonsei University announced on July 27, 2026 that Lee In-suk’s team analyzed 2,638 gut metagenomic datasets to build the Mouse Reference Gut Virome (MRGV): 109,778 viral genomes spanning 28,824 species, expanding known diversity by ~68% versus prior databases. The *Nature Communications* paper (online July 21) shows gut viral profiles alone can accurately predict mouse age, with many aging-linked viruses newly cataloged. The resource also maps virus–bacteria links and human–mouse virome comparisons, aiming to underpin microbiome diagnostics for IBD, obesity, diabetes, and cancer.

### 7. FDA clears Simtriyo (centanafadine): first NDSRI for ADHD
**Summary:** Otsuka said the U.S. FDA approved Simtriyo (centanafadine) on July 24, 2026 as a once-daily extended-release capsule for ADHD in adults and pediatric patients aged 6+ weighing at least 20 kg—the first approved norepinephrine–dopamine–serotonin reuptake inhibitor (NDSRI). Four Phase 3 trials showed significant symptom improvement as early as week 1; labeling carries boxed warnings for suicidal ideation/behaviors and abuse/misuse/addiction, with DEA scheduling pending and launch expected later in 2026. ### 9. Synthetic K⁺ channel inhibitors close stomata and boost Arabidopsis drought tolerance
**Summary:** A July 27, 2026 *Nature Communications* study identifies NS5806 and UA49 as inhibitors of plasma-membrane voltage-gated potassium channels that induce stomatal closure, lower guard-cell K⁺, and increase drought resistance in Arabidopsis. The chemically induced closure pathway is distinct from ABA signaling (while sharing cytosolic K⁺ decrease and SLAC1 dependence); K⁺ channel inhibition elevates cytosolic Ca²⁺, an effect absent in inward K⁺ channel mutants. Authors position the compounds as tools to dissect shared stomatal-closure mechanisms and as potential horticultural/agricultural biostimulants.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Synthetic ion channel inhibitors enhance plant drought tolerance](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75894-w)

**Commentary:** Closing stomata off the classic ABA axis offers a chemical drought handle—field residues, off-targets, and crop extrapolation still need proof.

### 11. Conditional diffusion model disaggregates behind-the-meter PV from half-hourly smart meters—with uncertainty
**Summary:** Researchers at Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC) introduce in *Energy and AI* a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model (cDDPM) that reconstructs behind-the-meter PV generation—and uncertainty intervals—from 30-minute smart-meter net load, irradiance, and location features. Trained on one year of half-hourly data from 257 Australian PV homes and tested for cross-region generalization on Dutch data, it outperformed physics-proxy, quantile-regression, and CVAE baselines on both deterministic and probabilistic metrics. pv magazine reported on July 27, 2026; the authors say the work began from a DSO request for visibility of hidden rooftop PV.

### 12. MIT / *Nature Energy*: climate-informed siting can hedge mid-century adequacy shortfalls at little or no extra cost
**Summary:** An MIT team publishing in *Nature Energy* (July 16, 2026; continuing citation in science media) couples ~12 km climate projections with county-level power-system optimization for deep-decarbonized New England and Texas grids. Systems sited on historic climate can see resource-inadequacy frequency rise up to fivefold by mid-century as cooling demand meets renewable droughts and transmission bottlenecks. Climate-informed designs keep Texas adequacy at near-zero added cost by shifting wind westward, while New England needs about a 2.34% investment increase via solar and transmission near load centers—showing resilience depends on high-resolution spatial planning, not aggregate capacity targets alone.

**Links:**
