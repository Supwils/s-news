## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Skyroot Vikram-1 reaches orbit on first flight: India’s first private orbital rocket
**Summary:** On July 18, 2026, Indian startup Skyroot Aerospace lofted Vikram-1 from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre after a ~35-minute technical hold late in the countdown, reaching a planned ~450 km low Earth orbit about 15 minutes after liftoff—the first commercial Indian rocket to reach orbit and making India the third country with private orbital launch capability after the U.S. and China. Mission Aagaman used three solid stages plus a liquid kick stage (orbit adjustment module) that burned ~six minutes, carrying cubesats from Skyroot and Grahaa Space plus hosted payloads from Dcubed and Cosmoserve. Vikram-1 is designed for up to ~350 kg to LEO; an upgraded Vikram-1U aims for ~550 kg. The company plans further test flights before commercial service.

### 2. First atmosphere detected on habitable-zone rocky exoplanet LHS 1140 b (*Science*)
**Summary:** A Harvard-led team reports in *Science* helium escaping from LHS 1140 b, a rocky world ~48 light-years away (~5.6 Earth masses, ~1.73 Earth radii, ~24.7-day orbit, Teq ~226 K in the liquid-water habitable zone). WINERED near-IR transit spectra on Magellan/Clay showed helium escape in 2024; no helium was seen for more-irradiated companion LHS 1140 c, and 2025 revisits found no helium absorption—pointing to time-variable escape. The team interprets a helium-rich, hydrogen-poor upper atmosphere with other volatiles possibly trapped lower down; the atmosphere may have lasted >~3 billion years—among the strongest evidence yet for an atmosphere on a habitable-zone rocky exoplanet.

**Links:**

### 3. Eulalia parent-body breakup may explain ~800-Myr inner-solar-system impact shower
**Summary:** A Southwest Research Institute–led study in *The Planetary Science Journal* links the catastrophic breakup of the Eulalia asteroid family’s parent body near Jupiter’s 3:1 resonance ~800 million years ago to a prolonged bombardment of the Moon and terrestrial planets. Models suggest ~three-quarters of fragments entered the resonance over ~100–150 Myr via prompt injection and Yarkovsky drift, plausibly matching the lunar large-crater / Apollo impact-glass age spike. Roughly twenty Earth impacts of similar or larger size are expected per large lunar impact; timing overlaps proposed biosphere shifts on Earth and possible Martian volcanic pulses. ScienceDaily recirculated the finding on July 18, 2026.

**Links:**

- [SwRI — Research connects asteroid collision to impact showers 800 million years ago](https://www.swri.org/newsroom/press-releases/swri-led-research-connects-asteroid-collision-impact-showers-800-million-years-ago)

**Commentary:** A specific main-belt “blast” may set a shared clock for lunar craters, Earth climate/biology clues, and Martian volcanism.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. “Double neural bypass” restores hand movement and touch after complete tetraplegia (*Nature Medicine*)
**Summary:** Feinstein Institutes researchers report in *Nature Medicine* a double neural bypass (DNB) linking an intracortical BCI to patterned spinal and cortical neuromodulation, with recurrent networks and reinforcement learning for fine grasp plus “cortical mirroring” microstimulation to drive plasticity. In a participant with chronic C4 sensory / C5 motor complete tetraplegia, the system enabled self-feeding and handling delicate objects, with lasting gains in elbow flexion and wrist tactile sensation even after the device was off. Movement decoding remained usable for >five months without repeated retraining; wrist touch persisted >two months after stimulation stopped. Authors stress the need for larger, more diverse trials and note the system still requires specialized operators.

### 5. FDA clears Merck’s Lipfendra: first once-daily oral PCSK9 inhibitor
**Summary:** Merck announced U.S. FDA approval of Lipfendra (enlicitide) 20 mg tablets as an adjunct to diet and exercise to lower LDL-C in adults with hypercholesterolemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). The macrocyclic peptide is the first approved oral PCSK9 inhibitor. Pivotal Phase 3 CORALreef Lipids and HeFH trials showed ~56% and ~59% placebo-adjusted LDL-C reductions at 24 weeks, plus drops in non-HDL-C, Lp(a), and ApoB. Whether hard cardiovascular outcomes improve awaits the ongoing CORALreef Outcomes trial (>14,500 participants). Review used the Commissioner’s National Priority Voucher pathway.

### 6. Magdeburg chemists use relay synthesis for Neosorangicin A key fragments

### 8. MIT: climate-informed siting can cut wind–solar adequacy shortfalls at little or no extra cost
**Summary:** MIT researchers report in *Nature Energy* (published July 16, 2026) that pairing ~12 km climate projections with county-level power-system optimization for decarbonized New England and Texas grids shows up to a fivefold rise in resource-inadequacy frequency by mid-century if plants are sited on historic weather alone. Climate-informed designs keep Texas adequacy near today’s cost by shifting wind westward, while New England needs only ~2.34% more investment via solar and transmission nearer loads. The work argues aggregate capacity targets miss fine-scale weather–demand coupling that drives blackout risk.

## IV. Basic Research

### 10. NV magnetometry images gate-controlled superconductivity quenching via the Meissner effect
**Summary:** A July 18, 2026 *Nature Communications* paper shows gate-controlled supercurrent suppression (GCS) also extinguishes Meissner screening—not just transport. Using a sub-Kelvin scanning nitrogen-vacancy magnetometer, researchers mapped the nanoscale GCS-suppressed region in micron-scale niobium islands. Results favor a quasiparticle hot-spot generation and diffusion model over Joule heating or pure electric-field alternatives, strengthening the case for superconducting transistor concepts and local probes of quasiparticle dynamics.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Imaging of gate-controlled suppression of superconductivity via the Meissner effect](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75567-8)

**Commentary:** Spatial Meissner imaging turns “can a gate kill supercurrent?” from an I–V claim into a field-topology test.

### 11. Hidden bubble and four-light-year hydrogen filament mapped in the Orion Nebula
**Summary:** An international team led by Juan Diego Soler (University of Vienna) reports in *Astronomy & Astrophysics* the first detailed large-scale 21-cm hydrogen map of the extended Orion Nebula by combining VLA and FAST data. The maps reveal a previously uncatalogued lower-mass second bubble inside the main shell and a ~four-light-year hydrogen filament (~80 solar masses) extending from the western edge. No known star or young stellar object sits at the second bubble’s center; the filament’s origin remains unclear—leakage through a weak shell spot or a remnant of the natal cloud. Neutral-hydrogen and ionized-carbon views now match in scale.

**Links:**

- [The Debrief — Astronomers Uncovered a Hidden Bubble Inside the Orion Nebula](https://thedebrief.org/astronomers-uncovered-a-hidden-bubble-inside-the-orion-nebula/)

**Commentary:** Even the nearest great nebula still hides uncatalogued cavities—multi-wavelength mapping keeps rewriting star-formation feedback in 3D.
