## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. Webb and ESO independently confirm Beta Pictoris d: spectral fingerprint and decade-old imaging published the same day
**Summary:** Per NASA Science and ESO/phys.org on July 15, 2026, two teams reported in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters* a third giant planet, β Pic d, in the Beta Pictoris system (~63 light-years away, ~23 million years old). Aidan Gibbs and colleagues at UC San Diego found it via JWST/NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy through CH₄, CO, and H₂O atmospheric features; Ben Sutlieff (University of Edinburgh) and Markus Bonse (ESO) spotted a point source ~100× fainter than β Pic b in VLT/ERIS data and traced it through more than a decade of SPHERE and other archives. Models put the mass at roughly 2–4 Jupiter masses and the semimajor axis near 30 AU (Neptune-like), the widest of the three known planets and a plausible sculptor of the debris disk’s inner edge—making β Pic only the second system with at least three directly imaged planets.

**Commentary:** Planet hunting is shifting from “see a bright speck” to “read a molecular fingerprint”—a path that can work even inside bright debris disks.

### 2. Chang’e-6 farside soils show Earth’s magnetosphere “speed-governs” the solar wind
**Summary:** A team led by Zhang Xuhang and He Huaiyu at the Institute of Geology and Geophysics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, with the University of Science and Technology of China and partners, published noble-gas analyses of Chang’e-6 farside samples online in *Nature Geoscience* on July 15, 2026. Compared with Chang’e-5 nearside soils, farside release patterns for Kr and Xe indicate deeper solar-wind implantation. When the Moon passes through Earth’s magnetosheath, solar wind can slow from ~400 km/s to ~200 km/s; decelerated wind mainly bathes the nearside—about 25% of exposure time at the Chang’e-5 site—while the farside is unaffected. The magnetosphere thus acts less as a simple shield than as a speed governor that reshapes volatile implantation depth; heavy noble gases may serve as a fossil record of past magnetosphere–solar-wind boundaries.

### 3. Perseverance reads Jezero’s “Broom Point”: a 75-meter stack records repeated early Mars impacts
**Summary:** NASA/JPL reported on July 15, 2026, that Perseverance identified a ~75-meter-thick (245-foot) layered bedrock sequence in the Broom Point member on Jezero’s western rim—likely more than 3.9 billion years old and among the oldest terrains a Mars rover has examined; results appear in *Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets*. Instruments show repeated alternation of breccias and fine pulverized dust, with vesicular clasts indicating past melting and recurrent high-energy impacts. A “one-two punch” scenario posits Isidis Basin formation (~1,900 km wide) tilting the strata, then the Jezero-forming impact (~45 km) fracturing and uplifting them. Core samples “Bell Island” and “Main River” await Earth-lab dating that could also constrain early Earth’s erased impact record.

**Links:**

- [NASA JPL — NASA’s Perseverance Rover Reads Record of Ancient Mars Impacts](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-perseverance-rover-reads-record-of-ancient-mars-impacts/)
- [phys.org — NASA's Perseverance rover reads record of ancient Mars impacts](https://phys.org/news/2026-07-nasa-perseverance-rover-ancient-mars.html)

**Commentary:** A crater-rim section becomes a solar-system “impact weather archive”—return-sample ages would recalibrate the inner Solar System’s early catastrophe timeline.

## II. Biomedicine & Health

### 4. FDA approves gedatolisib (Revtorpyk) combinations for HR+/HER2−, PIK3CA wild-type advanced breast cancer
**Summary:** Per The ASCO Post, Celcuity, and CancerNetwork, the U.S. FDA on July 14, 2026, approved gedatolisib (Revtorpyk) with fulvestrant, with or without palbociclib, for adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer without a detected PIK3CA mutation after progression on or after at least one line of endocrine therapy in the metastatic setting. Celcuity calls it the first approved inhibitor of all class I PI3K isoforms plus mTORC1/2. In phase 3 VIKTORIA-1 (NCT05501886; n=392), median PFS was 9.3 months for the triplet and 7.4 months for the doublet versus 2.0 months for fulvestrant alone (HRs 0.24 and 0.33); objective response rates were about 32%/28% versus 1%. A supplemental filing for the PIK3CA-mutant population is planned for Q3 2026.

**Links:**

- [The ASCO Post — Gedatolisib Combinations Approved by FDA for HR-Positive/HER2-Negative Breast Cancer](https://ascopost.com/news/july-2026/gedatolisib-combinations-approved-by-fda-for-hr-positiveher2-negative-breast-cancer/)

### 5. *Nature*: “metabolite glues” — purine nucleotides tether PPAT–NUDT5; thiopurines hitch the same ride
**Summary:** Samuel R. Witus, Michael Rapé and colleagues published in *Nature* on July 15, 2026, that purine nucleotides act as endogenous metabolite glues, tethering the rate-limiting purine-biosynthesis enzyme PPAT to its inhibitor NUDT5 so cells can sense purine levels and enforce feedback control. Thiopurine chemotherapeutics used since the 1950s glue the same complex in distinct orientations with greater potency; unlike many known glues, this pocket can remodel to accommodate compound changes, enabling stronger on-target glue activity. The work links nutrient sensing, proximity pharmacology, and a decades-old drug class into one druggable mechanism.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Metabolite glues as a means of purine sensing and chemotherapeutic response](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10790-3)
- [Nature News & Views — Nucleotides act as ‘molecular glue’ and can sense their own abundance](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01945-3)

**Commentary:** Metabolites as molecular glues open a structural path to optimize old thiopurines—and extend proximity drugs beyond degraders into metabolic rewiring.

### 6. Population dynamics reveal “hidden individuality” in phage lysis timing
**Summary:** An international team led by the University of Maryland reported in *Science Advances* on July 15, 2026, that population-scale cell measurements alone can accurately infer cell-to-cell heterogeneity in bacteriophage infection outcomes. Modeling across infection rounds showed that rupture timing varies substantially among cells for a given phage–bacteria pair, and that both the mean and variability of lysis timing may themselves be under evolutionary pressure—providing a quantitative tool where direct single-cell assays are impractical.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Eighty years of averaged infection curves now yield explicit lysis-timing parameters for epidemiology and phage-therapy models.

### 7. LONGi’s silicon–perovskite tandem hits 35.5% certified efficiency, a new world record
**Summary:** *pv magazine* reported on July 15, 2026, that LONGi announced a 35.5% power-conversion efficiency for a two-terminal crystalline silicon–perovskite tandem cell at its 2026 Solar and Storage Innovation Technology Conference at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. ## IV. Fundamental Research & Physics

### 9. LSU team reports room-temperature “quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystals” in *Nature*
**Summary:** Omar S. Magaña-Loaiza and colleagues at Louisiana State University published in *Nature* on July 15, 2026, quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystals: gold-film nanoantenna “meta-atoms” whose near-field multiparticle dynamics create allowed and forbidden quantum-statistical bands that selectively transmit or suppress multiphoton fields of different quantum statistics—all at room temperature without cryogenics. Band structure is set by meta-atom geometry and arrangement, offering a design blueprint for coherence-sensitive photonic materials aimed at quantum computing, secure communications, sensing, and energy harvesting. A companion *Nature* News & Views frames the device as an optical filter that sorts light by quantum statistics.

**Links:**

### 10. *Nature Physics*: all-mechanical coherence protection for a diamond spin qubit, Rabi frequency to 800 MHz
**Summary:** A Cornell-led team published in *Nature Physics* on July 15, 2026, all-mechanical coherence protection and fast control of a silicon-vacancy spin in diamond. Optical initialization, gates, and readout run in a dressed basis compatible with a phononic cavity, suppressing low-frequency noise while preserving spin–phonon coupling; Rabi frequencies reach 800 MHz for ultrafast control. The result advances high-fidelity phonon-mediated gates and on-chip phononic quantum networks.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Noise protection and strong cavity coupling can coexist for spins—opening a compact phonon path beside photonic interconnects.

### 11. LMU: cyanobacteria perform oxygenic photosynthesis without photosystem I, upending a 50-year textbook rule
**Summary:** phys.org reported on July 15, 2026, on a *Nature Communications* paper (version of record July 10, 2026) from Dario Leister’s LMU team: through genetic engineering and adaptive laboratory evolution of *Synechocystis*, they obtained strains lacking photosystem I that still grow photoautotrophically, fix CO₂, and evolve oxygen. After electron-transport remodeling, a steep proton gradient enables reverse NDH-1 activity to generate NADPH—a role long ascribed solely to PSI—challenging the dogma that oxygenic photosynthesis requires two photosystems and revealing unexpected thylakoid plasticity.
