## I. Space & Deep-Sky Astronomy

### 1. Webb releases new Lion Nebula images: infrared view of dust and ionized gas in a planetary nebula (Space)
**Summary:** On August 10, NASA released new James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) NIRCam and MIRI images of planetary nebula NGC 2392, the Lion Nebula. Compared with Hubble’s 2000 visible-light view, infrared resolution more clearly reveals compact dust clumps and ionized gas; radiation from the central white dwarf drives the “lion-face” bubble and destroys dust in its path, while comet-like “mane” features are surviving dust clumps. Astronomers estimate the nebula will disperse in roughly 10,000 years. The release is a joint NASA–ESA–CSA–STScI product.

**Links:**

- [NASA Science — Lion Nebula Roars to Life With NASA’s Webb](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/lion-nebula-roars-to-life-with-nasas-webb/)

**Commentary:** An everyday stellar death is frozen into a high-resolution teaching sample—how planetary nebulae make cosmic dust, now comparable in visible and infrared light.

### 2. Ahead of Europe’s total eclipse: Proba-3 and Solar Orbiter team up to test corona and solar-wind models (Space)
**Summary:** The August 12 total solar eclipse will cross Greenland, Iceland, Spain and nearby regions. Around August 10, ESA highlighted that formation-flying Proba-3 (two spacecraft ~150 m apart creating artificial eclipses) has run continuous coronagraphy from July 28 through August 19; earlier published results found slow solar wind in the inner corona moving roughly three to four times faster than older models, forcing forecast revisions. Solar Orbiter opened a dedicated remote-sensing window from August 9 (EUI, PHI, Metis, SPICE) and is supplying far-side magnetograms for cross-checks with ground and airborne observers. TechTimes and others framed the natural eclipse as a live validation test of the revised models.

**Links:**

- [ESA — How ESA mimics and models the 2026 total solar eclipse](https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Engineering_Technology/How_ESA_mimics_and_models_the_2026_total_solar_eclipse)
- [ESA COSMOS — Proba-3 plans for 12 August 2026 eclipse](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/proba-3/total-solar-eclipse-2026)

**Commentary:** Artificial eclipses rewrote inner-corona physics; the natural eclipse supplies the multi-geometry stress test a single spacecraft cannot self-calibrate.

### 3. JWST archive yields a z≈4.4 gravitational-arc candidate: another early-universe object to confirm (Astronomy)
**Summary:** On August 10, phys.org reported that astrophysicist Homer Dávila Gutiérrez identified an uncatalogued gravitational-arc candidate, A1, in public JWST GO-5293 NIRCam imaging of galaxy cluster MACS J0308.9+2645. Multi-band photometry gives a redshift of about z≈4.4 (within the universe’s first billion years), with a lensing magnification factor of roughly seven. The GO-5293 team confirmed the source was previously uncatalogued and is refining its nature with an updated lens model; the preprint is arXiv:2607.12129.

**Links:**

- [phys.org — Astronomers find a new object from the early universe using Webb data](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-astronomers-early-universe-webb.html)
- [arXiv — Discovery of a gravitational arc candidate at photometric redshift 4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12129)

**Commentary:** Public archives can still surface missed high-redshift candidates—JWST deep fields are far from mined out.

## II. Biomedicine

### 4. Silence: siRNA drug divesiran meets Phase 2 primary endpoint in polycythemia vera (Biomedicine)
**Summary:** On August 10, Silence Therapeutics reported SANRECO Phase 2 topline results: in 48 phlebotomy-dependent polycythemia vera (PV) patients, divesiran (6 mg/kg subcutaneously every 6 or 12 weeks) met the primary endpoint in the 36-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled portion—clinical response in 88% versus 19% on placebo (placebo-adjusted ~+69%); Q6W and Q12W arms reached 93.8% and 81.3%. Responders needed no standard-of-care phlebotomy and kept hematocrit below 45% during weeks 18–36. A Phase 3 trial with Q12W dosing is planned for the first half of 2027. The drug silences hepatic TMPRSS6 to raise hepcidin and restrict iron available for excess red-cell production.

### 7. Nature Microbiology: retron recombineering (recombitrons) ported across 15 bacterial species (Synthetic Biology)
**Summary:** A nine-lab collaboration including Gladstone Institutes published on August 10 in *Nature Microbiology* showing that retron-mediated recombineering modules (recombitrons) can be moved from *E. coli* into 15 species spanning Proteobacteria, Bacillota and Actinomycetota. The systems functioned in all tested hosts, with editing efficiencies above 20% in six species, above 40% in three, and above 90% in two—including clinically and industrially relevant strains such as *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and fast-growing biotech hosts. Retron-architecture tweaks further boosted weaker hosts. A companion same-day paper introduced the metastrand framework, linking microbial antisense transcription programs to inflammatory activity biomarkers in IBD.

**Links:**

- [Nature Microbiology — Recombitrons enable bacterial genome editing across phyla](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-026-02459-4)
- [Nature Microbiology — Antisense transcription in the human gut microbiome](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-026-02442-z)

**Commentary:** Bacterial genome editing is shifting from model-organism privilege to portable modules—opening doors for microbiome and industrial strain engineering alike.

## III. Climate & Earth Science

### 8. Nature Geoscience: AMOC as a “heat valve” setting the planetary energy budget in abrupt glacial events (Climate)
**Summary:** On August 10, Christo Buizert (Oregon State University), Ayako Abe-Ouchi (University of Tokyo) and colleagues published in *Nature Geoscience* a synthesis using spontaneous abrupt-change simulations in three climate models. Strong and weak AMOC modes map onto oceanic/planetary heat loss and heat gain, set jointly by North Atlantic deep convection and radiative feedbacks at the top of the atmosphere. Antarctic and Greenland ice-core temperatures track ocean heat content and the rate of North Atlantic heat loss, respectively. The authors argue AMOC strength acts as a “heat valve” that alters planetary temperature via the radiative balance, implying amplified planetary heat uptake if AMOC weakens in the future.

**Links:**

- [Nature Geoscience — Planetary energy budget set by Atlantic Ocean heat valve](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-026-02070-6)

**Commentary:** Recasting the “bipolar seesaw” as a planetary energy-budget problem directly scales expectations for future AMOC weakening.

### 9. Greenland’s Petermann Glacier calves a 76.4 km² ice island: largest floating-ice loss there since 2012 (Polar)
**Summary:** An international team including University of Ottawa doctoral researcher Adam Garbo reported on August 10 that Petermann Glacier in northwest Greenland calved a ~76.4 km² tabular ice island up to ~150 m thick at 20:00 UTC on August 4—the glacier’s largest floating-ice loss since 2012 and among the largest Arctic calving events since 2020. Sentinel-1 imagery showed clear centerline deterioration on August 3. Researchers expect two further ice islands of roughly 94 km² and 84 km², which together with the new island could shrink the ice tongue by about 254 km² (~22%). The Canadian Ice Service is tracking navigation and offshore-infrastructure hazards.

### 10. Caltech: Zaonega carbon-isotope anomaly may be local magmatic heating, challenging a global-event reading (Geochemistry)
**Summary:** Nivedita Thiagarajan and John Eiler at Caltech, with Aivo Lepland of the Geological Survey of Norway and colleagues, report in *Geology* that molecular and isotopic analysis of gases in fluid inclusions from pyrobitumen-rich Zaonega Formation rocks (Karelia, Russia; ~2 Ga) can explain the anomalous carbon-isotope signal locally: a magma sheet heated organic-rich sediments, generating methane and propane that migrated upward and fed seafloor methane-consuming microbes, producing light-carbon biomass. Temperatures ranged from ~350°C next to the intrusion to ~72°C at an ancient seafloor asphalt spill ~300 m above. The result weakens treating the Shunga–Francevillian anomaly as a global carbon-cycle event at this reference site; comparison with Gabon’s GOE-DEEP cores is planned. phys.org carried the institutional release on August 10.

**Links:**

- [phys.org — Trapped gases challenge global explanation for 2-billion-year-old carbon anomaly](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-gases-ancient-global-explanation-billion.html)
- [Geology — Paleoproterozoic thermogenic hydrocarbon generation, Zaonega Formation](https://doi.org/10.1130/g54338.1)

**Commentary:** Once a type section can be told as a local story, the Paleoproterozoic “global carbon anomaly” narrative must re-prove itself elsewhere.

## IV. Materials & Fundamental Research

### 11. Nature Synthesis: flash Joule heating synthesizes diverse MXenes in ~30 seconds (Materials)
**Summary:** James M. Tour, Shichen Xu and colleagues at Rice University report on August 10 in *Nature Synthesis* an FJH-ClF strategy: sequential flash Joule heating chlorination and fluorination selectively remove interstitial atoms from nine different MAX phases within about 30 seconds each, yielding high-quality layered MXenes with strong electrochemical performance. Versus conventional HF, Lewis-acid and molten-salt etching, the route cuts reaction time, energy use and chemical hazards; DFT and HRTEM clarify selective-etching mechanisms and atomic-scale structural evolution, while LCA/TEA indicate favorable cost and environmental metrics.

**Links:**

- [Nature Synthesis — Flash Joule heating for rapid MXenes synthesis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44160-026-01132-2)

**Commentary:** Compressing hazardous slow etching into a seconds-scale electrothermal flow brings MXenes another step toward manufacturable scale.

### 12. Southeast University: programmable metasurface performs Fourier transforms and convolution in the EM-wave domain (Physics)
**Summary:** Tie Jun Cui, Qiang Cheng and colleagues at Southeast University published in *Nature Communications* (4 August 2026) a programmable RF calculation system based on a space-time-coding metasurface (STCM), implementing Fourier transforms and convolution directly in the electromagnetic-wave domain and reducing hardware complexity and power tied to digital conversion. Radar tests demonstrated accurate target range and velocity detection, including under outdoor multipath and clutter. ScienceAlert and others amplified the work in English-language coverage on August 10.
