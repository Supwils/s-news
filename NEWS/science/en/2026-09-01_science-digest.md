## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. NASA–ESA astronauts conduct history’s sixth all-female spacewalk (spaceflight)
**Summary:** At about 8:40 a.m. EDT on Sept. 1, NASA astronaut Jessica Meir and ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot began an International Space Station extravehicular activity to replace a retroreflector on the forward port of the Harmony module for rendezvous and docking navigation, install jumper cables, prepare the Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer for future maintenance, and replace a high-definition camera. It is the sixth all-female spacewalk on record; Adenot recently became the first French woman to perform an EVA, and the outing is Meir’s seventh. NASA billed the activity as U.S. Spacewalk 99, expected to last roughly 6.5 hours.

### 2. BepiColombo nears Mercury arrival: transfer module separation set for Sept. 3 (deep space)
**Summary:** After nearly eight years in cruise, the ESA/JAXA BepiColombo mission is entering its Mercury arrival phase. Mission timelines call for ESA’s Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) to separate from the stacked science orbiters on Sept. 3, 2026, after which ESA’s Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) chemical propulsion takes over; MPO and JAXA’s Mio remain stacked for a planned Mercury polar-orbit capture around Nov. 21, separate in December, and settle into science orbits ahead of full science operations around April 2027. ESA describes the sequence as among its most demanding planetary arrivals; Sept. 1 coverage noted ESOC teams in Darmstadt completing contingency simulations.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Eight years of cruise end not with arrival alone but with irreversible separations and burns—near-Earth ops playbooks will be repriced in Mercury’s thermal environment.

## II. Particle Physics & Cosmology

### 3. LUX-ZEPLIN reports its most compelling single-event dark-matter hint yet (particle physics)
**Summary:** On Sept. 1, the LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) collaboration reported at the 2026 TeV Particle Astrophysics meeting in Japan that a 220-day dataset from its ~10-tonne ultrapure liquid-xenon detector ~1.6 km underground at the Sanford Underground Research Facility contains one particle interaction hard to explain with known backgrounds and consistent with a WIMP-like nuclear recoil. Analysis led by the University of Bristol group reaches about 2.6σ (~0.5% chance from known backgrounds alone), well below the 5σ discovery threshold; if dark matter, the WIMP mass would likely be at least ~200 GeV/c² with interactions beyond the simplest models. The paper is slated for arXiv and Physical Review Letters; LZ has more data in hand and plans to run through at least 2028, with XENON and PandaX able to cross-check.

**Links:**

- [UKRI — UK-led analysis behind intriguing dark matter results](https://www.ukri.org/news/uk-led-analysis-behind-intriguing-dark-matter-results/)
- [Science News — Have scientists glimpsed the first dark matter particle?](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/dark-matter-particle-wimp-lz-experiment)

**Commentary:** One event is not a discovery—whether it grows or fades with more exposure will decide if this is the opening of a new chapter or another false dawn.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 4. Chinese team maps “en-route mass gain” that amplified the Gyirong debris-flow disaster (hazards)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported on Sept. 1 that a consortium led by the Institute of Tibetan Plateau Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, reconstructed how a high-elevation ice-rock avalanche on Nepal’s Langtang Lirung north slope on Aug. 26, 2026, evolved into a debris flow that struck Tibet’s Gyirong port; results appear in Chinese Science Bulletin. The study finds no clear short-burst heavy rain trigger near the event; long-running glacier motion, warm-season melt, and high-elevation permafrost degradation more likely weakened ice-rock contacts. Downstream damage hinged on ~22 km of channel “en-route mass gain,” as debris scoured the bed, entrained loose material, and coupled with river water. Seismic anomalies hours before the main event offer precursor clues; authors urge risk assessment spanning source stability, channel entrainment potential, and downstream exposure—not source volume alone.

**Links:**

- [Xinhua — Scientists reveal amplification mechanism of Tibet Gyirong debris flow](http://xz.xinhuanet.com/20260901/60d7bad568e94d6b9d352fc16ed3325c/c.html)
- [China Economic Net — Scientists reveal amplification mechanism of Tibet Gyirong debris flow](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202609/t20260901_3186207.shtml)

**Commentary:** How much failed on the mountain is incomplete—how much a gorge can entrain, and what sits exposed downstream, set the true kill radius of transboundary cryosphere disasters.

### 5. Copernicus confirms a 21.1°C daily global SST record as a strong El Niño builds (climate)
**Summary:** The Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S), using ERA5, confirmed that on Aug. 22, 2026, the daily average sea-surface temperature over the extra-polar ocean (60°S–60°N) reached 21.1°C, above the prior record of 21.09°C from March 2024—and unusual because the domain’s annual peak normally falls in March–April, not August (prior August high: 20.98°C in 2023). C3S/ECMWF place the spike in long-term anthropogenic ocean warming, with a developing El Niño adding heat; marine heatwave days have risen sharply since the early 1990s. Parallel food-security analyses warn that the 2026–2027 El Niño could push tens of millions more people in already food-insecure countries into acute hunger, with peak impacts spanning late 2026 into 2027.

**Links:**

- [Copernicus — Daily global sea surface temperature breaks 2024 record](https://climate.copernicus.eu/copernicus-daily-global-sea-surface-temperature-breaks-2024-record)

## IV. Biomedicine

### 6. FDA clears Besremi for essential thrombocythemia, first new ET approval in nearly three decades (hematologic oncology)
**Summary:** On Aug. 31, PharmaEssentia announced FDA approval of long-acting interferon BESREMi (ropeginterferon alfa-2b-njft) for adults with essential thrombocythemia (ET)—the first new FDA-approved ET therapy in nearly three decades—across genotypes and disease status, including newly diagnosed, cytoreductive-naive patients. The Phase 3 SURPASS ET trial (NCT04285086) showed superior durable modified ELN responses and hematologic control versus anagrelide, with fewer thromboembolic events over 12 months. Besremi was already approved for polycythemia vera (PV); the decision expands its U.S. label, following ET clearances in Japan and Taiwan.

**Links:**

- [CancerNetwork — FDA OKs Ropeginterferon Alfa for Essential Thrombocythemia](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/fda-oks-ropeginterferon-alfa-for-essential-thrombocytopenia)

**Commentary:** Myeloproliferative neoplasms have long been underserved—extending a proven interferon platform from PV to ET changes options faster than starting from zero.

### 7. FDA approves first hepcidin-mimetic Mimrylo for erythrocytosis in polycythemia vera (hematologic oncology)
**Summary:** On Aug. 28, FDA approved Takeda/Protagonist’s MIMRYLO (rusfertide) for erythrocytosis in adults with polycythemia vera (PV)—the first hepcidin-mimetic peptide cleared for the indication, under priority review. In the global Phase 3 VERIFY study (NCT05210790) of 293 adults with uncontrolled hematocrit despite standard care including phlebotomy, 76.9% on Mimrylo achieved the Weeks 20–32 phlebotomy-eligibility-free response, with secondary wins on mean phlebotomy count, hematocrit maintenance below 45%, and fatigue scores versus placebo plus standard care. Injection-site reactions and anemia were among common adverse events; iron-restriction differentiates it from JAK-pathway agents.

**Links:**

- [FDA — Approves First Drug of Its Kind for Polycythemia Vera](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-its-kind-polycythemia-vera-rare-blood-disorder)
- [Takeda — FDA Approval of MIMRYLO (rusfertide)](https://www.takeda.com/en-us/newsroom/news-releases/2026/fda-approval-mimrylo/)

**Commentary:** From repeated phlebotomy to weekly subcutaneous iron control—PV care is shifting from subtractive maintenance toward a measurable pathophysiologic lever.

## V. Materials & Fundamental Research

### 8. Ultrafast snapshots reconstruct exciton wave-function evolution in organic semiconductors (condensed matter / optoelectronics)
**Summary:** Phys.org reported on Sept. 1 that researchers at the University of Graz, with partners at Marburg University and Forschungszentrum Jülich, published in Physical Review X (DOI: 10.1103/3zmg-276c) the first experimental reconstruction of an exciton’s spatial distribution and temporal evolution in the earliest moments after light absorption in an organic semiconductor, with matching theory for the first steps from light to electricity. The team excites excitons with an ultrashort pulse, then photoemits electrons with a second high-energy laser pulse; the work is a milestone of the EU “Orbital Cinema” project. Next, they aim to watch electron–hole separation itself—the bottleneck that limits organic photovoltaic and optoelectronic efficiency.

**Links:**

- [Phys.org — Ultrafast snapshots reveal the first moments on the way from light to electricity](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-ultrafast-snapshots-reveal-moments-electricity.html)
- [Physical Review X — Observing the Spatial and Temporal Evolution of Exciton Wave Functions in Organic Semiconductors](https://doi.org/10.1103/3zmg-276c)

**Commentary:** Filming the first frame of light-to-charge conversion is a sharper efficiency lever than stacking another active layer—exciton dynamics finally has a timeline.

### 9. Chinese team synthesizes ultralong carbon-sheathed single-copper-atom chains at the micron scale (chemistry / materials)
**Summary:** A team led by Kuo Li at the Centre for High Pressure Science and Technology Advanced Research, with Nankai University, Peking University and others, used high-pressure solid-state topological polymerization to make carbon-sheathed single-metal-atom chains, published in Science (DOI: 10.1126/science.aeg0028). Exfoliated individual chains exceed 1 µm—more than ~4,000 copper atoms—roughly two orders of magnitude longer than prior single-metal-atom chains, remaining intact under ambient pressure, acid, and ultrasound; the route extends to cobalt, nickel, and zinc. Transport is strongly anisotropic, with conduction attributed mainly to an intermediate carbon layer rather than the copper core, which shows antiferromagnetic character. Chemistry World and peers call the chains among the longest ever, while flagging scalability limits of extreme-pressure synthesis.

**Links:**

- [Chemistry World — Copper chains more than 4000 atoms long are the longest ever produced](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/copper-chains-more-than-4000-atoms-long-are-the-longest-ever-produced/4024095.article)
- [Science — DOI: 10.1126/science.aeg0028](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeg0028)

**Commentary:** High pressure cracked the length–stability–batch triangle—next is not another record, but whether the wires can leave the press and enter measurable devices.
