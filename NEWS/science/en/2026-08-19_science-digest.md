## I. Space & Deep Space

### 1. NASA’s LRO Captures a New Falcon 9 Impact Crater on the Moon: Size and Coordinates (Planetary Science)
**Summary:** NASA says its Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) captured images of a new impact crater on Aug. 11–12, after a Falcon 9 upper-stage booster struck the Moon on Aug. 5. Using shadow measurements, scientists estimate the crater is about 60 feet wide (about 18 meters) and less than 10 feet deep (about 3 meters), and note that the LRO Narrow-Angle Camera can resolve features down to roughly 3 feet. The team also refined the crater-center coordinates to 19.4759°N, 266.7138°E, with an elevation of about 511 meters.
**Links:**

**Commentary:** Turning a “space hardware mishap” into measured geometry gives stronger calibration for lunar surface processes and impact modeling.

### 2. ESA: Sophie Adenot Completes Her First Spacewalk by Removing the Failed SGANT-2 Antenna (Space Station Operations)
**Summary:** ESA reports that astronaut Sophie Adenot and NASA astronaut Anil Menon successfully completed US EVA-97 at 20:52 CEST on Tuesday, Aug. 18. The 6-hour 23-minute spacewalk was Adenot’s first EVA and the first conducted by a French woman. The mission focused on removing Space-to-Ground antenna 2 (SGANT-2), which had been unable to track the TDRS relay-satellite constellation since November 2025; stuck bolts and connectors forced a plan change, culminating in a long-duration tie-down on the station truss and deferring the spare antenna installation to a future spacewalk.
**Links:**

### 3. Chandra at High Redshift: Evidence for Thermal Hot Gas in a Protocluster Node Around a Hyperluminous Quasar (Space Astronomy)
**Summary:** Astronomers analyzed 634 ks of Chandra observations in the MQN01 cosmic structure at z around 3 and reported extended X-ray emission around a hyperluminous quasar. In the 0.5–2 keV band they detect about 66 net counts (around 8 sigma) extending to at least about 30 kpc from the quasar. The emission morphology and spectrum are consistent with thermal hot plasma, with an average gas temperature of kT ≈ (1.8 ± 0.4) keV; under their assumptions this implies a halo mass of roughly Mvir ~ 3×10^13 M⊙ and a 2–10 keV luminosity within the central 30 kpc of L2−10 ≈ 2.25×10^45 erg s−1.
**Links:**
- [Astronomy & Astrophysics — X-ray view of a massive node of the cosmic web at z ∼ 3 - II. Discovery of extended X-ray emission around a hyperluminous quasar](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2026/07/aa57020-25/aa57020-25.html)
**Commentary:** Observing proto-ICM-like thermal emission helps pin down how early-universe gas transitions toward cluster-scale hot phases.

### 4. CHIME/FRB: A Scale-Dependent Constraint on the Plasma Distribution with a Dispersion–Galaxy Cross-Power Spectrum (Cosmology)
**Summary:** A new analysis measured the dispersion–galaxy cross-power spectrum between 2,873 FRBs from the Second CHIME/FRB catalog and nearly 6 million galaxies from the DESI surveys, using five photometric redshift bins spanning 0.05 < z < 0.5. The work reports a first definitive detection at 5.1 sigma and finds a small-scale cutoff relative to the matter power spectrum, with k−1 cut approximately 0.9 (+0.4/−0.4) Mpc. The authors interpret the result as indicating that, below about 1 Mpc, the plasma does not strongly cluster with galaxies, consistent with feedback redistributing baryons to larger group scales.
**Links:**
- [arXiv — Measurement of the Dispersion–Galaxy Cross-Power Spectrum with the Second CHIME/FRB Catalog](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2506.08932)
**Commentary:** FRBs turn “missing baryons” from an indirect story into a scale-aware statistical probe tied to feedback physics.

## II. Biomedicine

### 5. FDA Approves Merck’s Oral PCSK9 Inhibitor LIPFENDRA (enlicitide) (Cardiometabolic Health)
**Summary:** Merck announced FDA approval of LIPFENDRA (enlicitide) 20 mg tablets as an adjunct to diet and exercise to reduce LDL-C in adults with hypercholesterolemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). In CORALreef Phase 3 results, placebo-adjusted LDL-C reductions at week 24 reached 56% in CORALreef Lipids and 59% in CORALreef HeFH. In the HeFH trial, the most common adverse reactions occurring at higher frequencies than placebo included diarrhea (7% vs. 2%) and dizziness (9% vs. 4%).

### 6. Merck/Moderna: Phase 3 INTerpath-001 Shows Positive RFS and DMFS for Intismeran Autogene + KEYTRUDA in Adjuvant Melanoma (Cancer Immunotherapy)
**Summary:** INTerpath-001 evaluated adjuvant intismeran autogene combined with pembrolizumab (KEYTRUDA) versus KEYTRUDA alone in patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV cutaneous melanoma. ### 8. Amylyx: Phase 3 LUCIDITY Meets FDA-Agreed Primary Endpoint for Avexitide in Post-Bariatric Hypoglycemia (Endocrine/Metabolic)
**Summary:** Amylyx reported Phase 3 LUCIDITY results for avexitide in post-bariatric hypoglycemia (PBH). ### 10. Nature Geoscience Quantifies Oxygen Transport: Labrador Sea Deep Convection “Injects” Oxygen into the AMOC Lower Limb (Ocean Biogeochemistry)
**Summary:** A Nature Geoscience study used newly developed calibration protocols for multi-year oxygen measurements across a mooring array spanning the Labrador Sea. The results show that local air–sea exchange adds about 4.1 Tmol yr−1 of oxygen to dense Labrador Sea waters, while deep convection transfers about 23.5 Tmol yr−1 of oxygen (accumulated in upstream lighter waters) into those dense waters. The authors estimate that this export can be sufficient to balance biological respiration across much of the deep North Atlantic, implying that future deep-ocean deoxygenation risk depends strongly on changes in Labrador Sea deep convection.
**Links:**
- [Nature Geoscience — Central role of Labrador Sea convection for transport of oxygen into the deep North Atlantic Ocean](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-026-02057-3)
**Commentary:** Deoxygenation risk depends on the “ventilation pathway,” not just on whether the AMOC weakens.

### 11. Proton-Trap Engineered Electric Swing Adsorption: Direct Air Capture Costs Projected at $48–62 per Ton (Carbon Removal Technology)
**Summary:** Nature Communications reports a DAC approach that improves amine-based electric swing adsorption (ESA) by engineering a point defect-mediated proton trapping network. This enables a 1:1 amine–CO2 stoichiometry, addressing the traditional 2:1 penalty. The engineered sorbent achieves a CO2 uptake of 6.57 mmol g−1 from 400 ppm CO2 (a 28.8% improvement), with regeneration requiring 3.4 GJ t−1 and producing a CO2 release rate 48% faster than conventional thermal methods. A techno-economic analysis projects DAC operating costs of $48–62 t−1 using renewable electricity, up to 78% lower than temperature swing adsorption DAC and below the $100 t−1 target.
**Links:**
- [Nature Communications — Proton trap engineered electric swing adsorption for scalable and cost-effective direct air capture](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75916-7)
**Commentary:** Cost reductions become plausible when energy demand and chemical uptake efficiency are improved together—not traded off in isolation.

### 12. ATLAS: Longitudinally Polarized Z and ZZ Studies Tighten Tests of the Higgs Mechanism (Particle Physics)
**Summary:** ATLAS presented rare polarisation results linked to the Higgs mechanism. Using 164 fb⁻¹ of pp collision data at 13.6 TeV collected between 2022 and 2024, the collaboration achieved the first observation of ZZ events containing two longitudinally polarized Z bosons, with an observed (expected) significance of 6.5 (5.6) standard deviations. ATLAS also reported evidence in electroweak WZjj production: longitudinal Z bosons show an observed (expected) significance of 4.0 (2.9) standard deviations, corresponding to an observed (expected) longitudinal Z polarisation fraction of 32% ± 9% (27.45% ± 0.08%).
**Links:**
- [ATLAS — Polarised bosons: a window into the Higgs mechanism](https://www.atlas.cern/index%2ephp/Updates/Briefing/Polarised-Weak-Bosons)
**Commentary:** Longitudinal polarization measurements are a direct “handle” on electroweak symmetry breaking, and ATLAS is now pushing that handle into cleaner territory.

### 13. Synthetic Biology in Action: Intermolecular Nitrene Transfer Inside Living Cells Enables Unnatural Nitrogen Products (Biocatalysis)
**Summary:** Researchers report that living cells can carry out chemical reactions involving intermolecular nitrene transfers on metabolic intermediates produced intracellularly. In the presented pathway, biosynthesis of N-acetoxyanilines together with cytochrome P450-catalyzed nitrene transfer generates amino alcohols, diamines, diarylamines, and aminoalkyl arenes from simple carbon feedstocks—products described as challenging for standard organic synthesis. The paper also includes quantified performance for a representative enzyme step (BezERI18 catalysis of styrene nitrene transfer in vitro, reported at 21% yield and 85% enantiomeric excess), and highlights that engineered evolution can increase titres while preserving high enantioselectivity.
**Links:**
- [Nature Chemistry — Nitrene generation and transfer for unnatural biosynthesis in living cells](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-026-02224-4)
**Commentary:** Moving nitrene transfer from “purified enzymes plus reagents” into full intracellular metabolism expands the practical space of C–N bond-forming chemistry.

### 15. Quantum Hall Metrology: A Gate-Defined Bilayer-Graphene Antidot Extracts Fractional Quasiparticle Charge (Quantum Measurement)
**Summary:** Nature Physics demonstrates a gate-defined bilayer-graphene antidot operating in the Coulomb-dominated regime to extract the tunneling quasiparticle charge in both integer and fractional quantum Hall states. For filling factors such as 4/3 and 7/3, the observed tunneling quasiparticles carry charge e/3, while hole-conjugate states (including 2/3, 5/3, and 8/3) show different measured charges. The authors link the differences to variations in edge re-equilibration driven by the downstream integer edge-mode structure, and emphasize the platform’s tunability for extending fractional-charge measurements to other van der Waals materials.
**Links:**
- [Nature Physics — Quantum Hall antidot as a fractional coulombmeter](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03412-2)
**Commentary:** Directly measuring fractional charge is essential for validating anyon properties—and this device design offers a scalable route to more materials.
