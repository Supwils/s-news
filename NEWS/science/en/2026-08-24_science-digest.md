### 1. Starship Ship 40 loads onto Forte at Christmas Island for 17,000-km return to Starbase (commercial space)
**Summary:** ABC reported on Aug 24 that the Maltese-flag semi-submersible heavy-lift vessel Forte reached Christmas Island and began loading the roughly 52-meter SpaceX Starship upper stage Ship 40 directly from the water for a ~17,000-km voyage back to Starbase, Texas. ### 2. Chang'e-7 scrubbed: agency says launch conditions not met; planned window this year will not be used (lunar exploration)
**Summary:** China's human spaceflight engineering office announced on Aug 23 (Beijing time) that after a comprehensive assessment, the Chang'e-7 mission does not meet launch conditions and cannot proceed within the planned window this year, citing prudence, reliability, and absolute mission success without specifying causes. The stack and Long March 5 had rolled out to Wenchang on Aug 19. The mission targets lunar south-pole water ice and resources as part of the crewed landing path before 2030. Space.com and others link the delay to Tropical Depression Narra near Hainan and caution after an early-August Wenchang failure, but the official release does not name weather; no new window has been set.

### 4. Moderna/Merck individualized mRNA vaccine intismeran plus Keytruda hits RFS and DMFS in Phase 3 melanoma trial (oncology)
**Summary:** Merck and Moderna announced on Aug 19 that the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 prespecified interim analysis was positive: among 1,137 patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV cutaneous melanoma, individualized neoantigen mRNA therapy intismeran autogene (V940) plus Keytruda beat Keytruda alone on recurrence-free survival (primary) and distant metastasis-free survival (key secondary)—the first positive Phase 3 for an individualized neoantigen therapy and for an mRNA cancer therapy, and the first Phase 3 to beat Keytruda alone in adjuvant melanoma. Media coverage continued on Aug 24, but hazard ratios and subgroup data remain undisclosed; full results are planned for a scientific meeting and regulatory talks.

### 5. Takeda's ORZEYFUL (oveporexton) approved in Japan as first NT1 therapy targeting orexin deficiency (sleep neurology)
**Summary:** Takeda announced on Aug 24 that Japan's MHLW approved oral OX2R agonist ORZEYFUL (oveporexton) for adults with narcolepsy type 1 (NT1)—the first in-class therapy aimed at underlying orexin deficiency rather than symptom-only management. ### 8. Global daily SST hits 21.1°C on Aug 22, breaking March 2024 record (climate monitoring)
**Summary:** Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) confirmed a daily global mean sea surface temperature of 21.1°C on Aug 22, exceeding the prior record of 21.09°C from March 2024 in the ERA5 reanalysis back to 1979. SSTs usually peak in March–April after austral summer, yet August set a record above August 2023's 20.98°C; extra-polar oceans saw repeated monthly records or near-records through 2026. C3S notes an extreme El Niño developing in the tropical Pacific atop decades of anthropogenic warming; whether the exceedance is transient or persistent remains under watch.

### 9. Intense storms raise global mean ice area ~13% annually, but August impacts turn destructive in recent years (polar climate)
**Summary:** *Nature Communications* on Aug 20 reports that Mundi and L'Ecuyer analyzed ~20 years of intense cyclones interacting with global marginal ice zones: storms amplify equatorward winds and ice drift in cold seasons and poleward motion in warm seasons, collectively raising global annual mean ice area about 13% (~26 million km² scale in the 2010s); the Southern Hemisphere contributes over two-thirds. Yet in the recent decade, August storm effects flipped from ice growth to destruction, with low Antarctic summer ice limiting further losses—yielding net gains April–October but net losses in November–December.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Intense storms increase global ice area](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76931-4)

**Commentary:** Cyclones are not one-way "ice factories"—the same physics can flip sign by month and decade; polar trends need seasonal decomposition, not a single annual headline.

### 10. Italy's CNR four-terminal spectral-splitting PV tile: GaAs plus bifacial Si, ~7% outdoor photocurrent gain (renewables)
**Summary:** CNR researchers featured on Aug 24 in pv magazine describe a four-terminal photovoltaic tile prototype: a wedged right-angle prism optical core directs visible light to a GaAs cell and infrared to a bifacial silicon cell, reducing self-shading and boosting albedo collection. Outdoor tests in Catania showed ~7% photocurrent gains for 23%-efficiency bifacial Si around summer-solstice mornings and evenings, with best clearance near 19 cm. Authors say stacking bandgaps per unit area suits low-impact building integration, but optical efficiency and commercialization still need work.

## IV. Fundamental Research

### 11. ETH/EPFL PLATON: plenoptic camera plus SPAD array enables ~200 μm 3D tracking in unsegmented scintillator (particle physics)
**Summary:** An ETH Zurich–EPFL team reports in *Nature Communications* on PLATON (PLenoptic imAge of Tracked photONs): a plenoptic light-field camera with the EPFL SwissSPAD2 single-photon avalanche diode array reconstructs 3D particle interaction positions inside an unsegmented plastic scintillator at ~200 μm resolution, detecting signals down to about five photons, with a neutrino case study showing full event reconstruction. The approach replaces millions of fiber segments with computational imaging, targeting cost cuts for next-generation neutrino, neutrinoless double-beta, and medical PET-scale detectors.

**Links:**

### 13. MIT: ~14% of merging black holes are second-generation hierarchical products, GWTC-4 analysis finds (gravitational-wave cosmology)
**Summary:** MIT physicists Cailin Plunkett and Salvatore Vitale analyzed LIGO, Virgo, and KAGRA's latest GWTC-4 catalog (~155 binary black-hole mergers) and report in *Physical Review Letters* that about 14% of merging black holes may be second-generation products of hierarchical merging—formed when smaller black holes merged before joining a later collision, not directly from single-star supernovae. Second-generation masses cluster around ~20 or 40+ solar masses, the upper band lying in the pair-instability "gap" where stellar models predict no remnant—offering a path to explain heavy black holes.

**Links:**

- [MIT News — Many black holes had past lives, new research shows](https://news.mit.edu/2026/many-black-holes-had-past-lives-new-research-shows-0707)

**Commentary:** Mergers begetting mergers expands black-hole genealogy beyond "stellar endpoint"—cosmic heavyweights may not be born only once.
