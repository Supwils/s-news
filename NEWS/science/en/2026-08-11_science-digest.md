### 1. Japan’s H3 Rocket Successfully Launches Michibiki-7 Navigation Satellite (Space)
**Summary:** JAXA launched H3 Rocket No. 9 from Tanegashima Space Center at about 4:23 a.m. Japan time on August 11, 2026, carrying Quasi-Zenith Satellite System (QZSS) navigation satellite Michibiki No. 7, which reached its planned orbit about 29–30 minutes later. It was the first H3 flight carrying an operational satellite since a December failure; seven of nine H3 flights have now succeeded. Michibiki augments GPS for smartphones, car navigation, and emergency alerts; after Michibiki-5 was lost, Japan plans to operate with six satellites rather than the original seven-satellite goal.

### 2. Webb Detects Dust and Water Near the Milky Way’s Central Black Hole (Astronomy)
**Summary:** ESA reported on August 11 that an international team used JWST’s MIRI to obtain the most detailed continuous mid-infrared spectrum yet of evolved star IRS 3, only about 0.55 light-years from Sagittarius A*. The spectrum shows oxygen-rich silicate dust and, for the first time for this object, clear water signatures in its envelope; modeling indicates a layered shell extending roughly 10,000 AU with temperatures from about 1200 K inward to about 100 K outward. Stellar modeling suggests roughly six solar masses and an age of about 72 million years, implying dust and molecules can still form and survive in the galactic center’s harsh radiation field. Observations came from the MICONIC GTO program; the paper appears in *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, with a preprint on arXiv.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Dust and water spotted close to giant black hole](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Webb/Dust_and_water_spotted_close_to_giant_black_hole)
- [arXiv — Dust production in the harsh environment of Sgr A*](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09511)

**Commentary:** The galactic center is no longer a presumed “dust desert”—AGB stars can still return star-forming feedstock to the harshest neighborhood.

### 3. DESI Legacy Surveys Release the Largest 2D Color Map of the Universe (Astronomy)
**Summary:** Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, NSF NOIRLab, and collaborators released around August 10–11 the latest DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys 2D color sky map: about 5.6 trillion pixels covering nearly 4 billion objects (mostly stars and galaxies) across roughly 75% of the sky in visible and near-infrared light. Built over about 13 years from more than 263,000 telescope frames, the map is public via the Legacy Survey Sky Viewer and has guided DESI target selection since June 2026. DESI finished its original five-year survey early in April 2026; improved dark-energy results using the full dataset are expected in 2027.

**Links:**

- [phys.org — DESI releases biggest 2D map of the universe](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-desi-biggest-2d-universe.html)
- [PetaPixel — Biggest 2D Map of the Universe Took 13 Years and 263,407 Photos](https://petapixel.com/2026/08/11/biggest-2d-map-of-the-universe-took-13-years-and-263407-photos-to-make/)

**Commentary:** An open, searchable sky “base plate” is infrastructure—deeper 3D dark-energy maps still need a trustworthy 2D targeting catalog.

## II. Biomedicine

### 4. Biosyngen’s EBV-Specific CAR-T BRG01 Cleared by FDA for Pivotal Phase II (Biomedicine)
**Summary:** On August 11, Biosyngen said the U.S. FDA approved pivotal Phase II trials of BRG01, an EBV-specific CAR-T therapy for relapsed/metastatic EBV-positive nasopharyngeal carcinoma. China’s NMPA/CDE had already cleared a pivotal Phase II, making BRG01 the first cell therapy to enter Phase II for this indication in both the U.S. and China. Phase I enrolled patients from late January; nine advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients with prior checkpoint inhibitor exposure completed infusion and DLT observation. Early data suggest good tolerability with no dose-limiting toxicity, and about 75% of high-dose patients showed lesion necrosis and metabolic reduction on PET-CT.

**Links:**

- [BioPharma APAC — Biosyngen’s BRG01 Secures FDA Approval for Pivotal Phase II Trial](https://www.biopharmaapac.com/news/31/5129/biosyngens-ebv-specific-car-t-therapy-brg01-secures-fda-approval-for-pivotal-phase-ii-trial-in-u-s-.html)

**Commentary:** For solid-tumor CAR-T, confirmatory efficacy and manufacturability—not Phase I anecdotes—are the real gate.

### 5. Luzhu’s LZ901 Shingles Vaccine Phase III Results Published in *Nature Communications* (Vaccines)
**Summary:** Beijing Luzhu Biotechnology reported that Phase III results for recombinant herpes zoster vaccine LZ901 appeared in *Nature Communications* on August 7, with industry and Chinese-language coverage continuing through August 9–11. The multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled about 26,000 adults aged ≥40; overall vaccine efficacy against herpes zoster within about 12 months was 91.6% (95% CI 86.3–95.3), with about 95.5%–95.9% efficacy against post-herpetic neuralgia. Efficacy was higher at ages 40–69 (~93.9%) than at ≥70 (~66.9%). The BLA has been under NMPA review since 2025 and could yield China’s first domestically developed recombinant zoster vaccine.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — LZ901 phase 3 trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76313-w)
- [Insight / association reprint — Phase III data for domestic Class 1 vaccine with >26,000 enrolled](http://rrrry.com/art_88135.htm)

**Commentary:** A 91.6% efficacy readout puts a domestic recombinant shingles candidate on evidence footing comparable to the import benchmark; approval timing will decide access.

## III. Climate & Environment

### 6. Weather Predictability Hits a Theoretical Ceiling Near 129 Days (Atmospheric Science)
**Summary:** The Chinese Academy of Sciences / phys.org reported on August 11 that Wei Zhang (University of Miami/CIMAS) and colleagues including retired NOAA scientist Zoltan Toth, writing in *Advances in Atmospheric Sciences*, estimate an internal weather predictability limit of about 129±7 days. Under idealized initial conditions and known dynamics, uncertainty injected via the phase of incoming solar photons eventually erases memory of the initial state through the atmospheric energy cycle. Operational skill today is roughly 14 days; under ideal conditions, meaningful skill might extend toward ~62 days before low-confidence guidance. DOI: 10.1007/s00376-026-5621-8; the team is pursuing independent cross-checks.

**Links:**

- [phys.org — Weather prediction hits a theoretical 129-day ceiling](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-weather-theoretical-day-ceiling.html)

**Commentary:** Recasting “how far can forecasts go” as an energy-turnover bound gives numerical weather prediction a testable theoretical target.

### 7. Late Paleozoic Icehouse Extreme Warming Event Offers a Geological Analogue (Paleoclimate)
**Summary:** Science and Technology Daily and CCTV reported on August 11 that an international team led by the Nanjing Institute of Geology and Palaeontology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, published in *PNAS* a reconstruction of a severe warming episode (reported as the KGTM extreme heat event) during the Late Paleozoic icehouse about 300 million years ago. Using conodont oxygen isotopes from South China and Ural sections, warming unfolded in two stages: low-latitude sea-surface temperatures rose ~2°C, then surged another ~3.5°C; global mean surface temperature rose about 7.5°C as atmospheric CO₂ climbed from ~300 to ~700 ppm. The chain points from volcanic carbon release to orbital pacing, ice/permafrost carbon feedbacks, and a crossed tipping threshold—framed as a rare icehouse sample with CO₂ levels comparable to today.

**Links:**

- [Science and Technology Daily — Icehouse warming 300 Ma ago as a key analogue for modern warming](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-08/11/content_562208.html)
- [CCTV — Chinese scientists map a full icehouse extreme-heat event](https://news.cctv.cn/2026/08/11/ARTIVCABQw0eCJJbeGAgAaLh260811.shtml)

**Commentary:** A “small warming → carbon feedback → abrupt shift” chain under icehouse conditions tracks today’s risk narrative more closely than many greenhouse-era analogues.

### 8. U.S. NOAA Ends Support for the Annual Arctic Report Card (Climate Monitoring)
**Summary:** POLITICO’s E&E News and other outlets reported August 10–11 that the Trump administration will end NOAA facilitation of the Arctic Report Card, published annually since 2006; authors were informed Monday. A NOAA spokesperson confirmed the agency will not coordinate the 2026 edition but said historically contributed data will still be collected and made public. The peer-reviewed synthesis typically draws on about 100 scientists worldwide and has been a flagship December product; whether nonfederal leads can finish the 21st edition and where it will appear remain unclear amid broader federal climate-science cutbacks.

## IV. Fundamental Research & Energy Materials

### 9. New Octocoral Family Named from Costa Rica’s Deep-Sea Seamounts (Biodiversity)
**Summary:** EurekAlert reported on August 11 that Odalisca Breedy (Universidad de Costa Rica) and colleagues discovered a bright yellow, tree-like octocoral, *Laurinque elenya*, on Pacific seamounts off Costa Rica at 360–529 m depth—so distinct that they erected a new family, Laurinqueidae, and genus *Laurinque*. Specimens were collected by Schmidt Ocean Institute’s ROV SuBastian in 2019 and 2023; three standard barcode genes disagreed, and phylogenomics of hundreds of genes placed it nearest Eunicellidae yet warranting a separate family. The open-access paper is in *ZooKeys* (DOI: 10.3897/zookeys.1283.191899).

### 10. KAIST: Framework-Templated “Gas Lattices” and AI Inverse Design (Materials)
**Summary:** Around August 11, KAIST’s Jihan Kim team announced a computational pipeline combining large-scale MOF screening with machine-learning-guided inverse design to stabilize ordered “gas lattices” of confined adsorbates. Using xenon as a model, GCMC simulations identify cobalt-based Co-CAU-36 hosting a body-centered cubic xenon lattice; in Xe/Kr mixtures, xenon preferentially forms an ordered shell while krypton shifts to the pore core—a separation motif distinct from conventional adsorption selectivity. The work is published in *Nature Communications* (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74776-5), with EurekAlert coverage dated August 10–11.

**Links:**

- [EurekAlert — KAIST templates a ‘gas lattice’ in porous materials](https://sciencesources.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1139443)
- [Nature Communications — Framework-templated gas lattices in MOFs](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74776-5)

**Commentary:** Designing how gases arrange—not only how strongly materials bind them—could reframe energy-hungry industrial separations.

### 11. Perovskite/Silicon Tandem Reaches 30.77% Certified Stabilized Efficiency via Healing Strategy (Energy)
**Summary:** On August 11, Hairen Tan and colleagues at Nanjing University reported in *Nature Communications* a “healing intervention” using MASCN solution on as-prepared wide-bandgap perovskite films grown by hybrid two-step methods on industrial textured silicon, inducing secondary crystallization into through-thickness columnar grains. Champion 1.68 eV perovskite cells reached 21.1% efficiency; a tandem with 1.164 cm² active area achieved a certified stabilized efficiency of 30.77% and Voc of 1.915 V. An encapsulated device retained initial performance after about 3400 hours of continuous one-sun MPP tracking in ambient conditions. Silicon wafers were provided by Trina Solar.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Healing intervention for tandem devices on industrial textured silicon](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76713-y)

**Commentary:** Beyond the efficiency headline, textured-silicon process compatibility and thousand-hour stability are the real factory-gate metrics for tandems.
