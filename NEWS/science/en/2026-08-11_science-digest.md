### 2. Roman Space Telescope enters launch integration: payload adapter mate, NET Aug. 30 liftoff (Space)
**Summary:** NASA updated on August 10 that the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has begun integration with SpaceX hardware at Kennedy Space Center’s Payload Hazardous Servicing Facility. After mating to its payload adapter, teams lifted Roman onto a strut payload attach fitting sized for heavy loads, enabling attachment to Falcon Heavy’s second stage. Next steps include fairing encapsulation and transfer to pad 39A. NASA and SpaceX are targeting launch no earlier than 7:26 a.m. EDT on Sunday, August 30—about nine months ahead of the prior schedule—with science goals spanning dark energy, dark-matter mapping, and exoplanet surveys.

### 3. Webb finds dust and water near the galactic center: IRS 3’s envelope survives next to Sagittarius A* (Astronomy)
**Summary:** ESA released James Webb Space Telescope results showing that the asymptotic-giant-branch star IRS 3—only about 0.55 light-years from Sagittarius A*—continues to shed material despite intense radiation. MIRI mid-infrared spectra reveal clear silicate (oxygen-rich) dust features and, for the first time, water in the star’s envelope. Modeling indicates a layered shell extending roughly 10,000 astronomical units, with temperatures falling from about 1200 K near the star to about 100 K outward. The star is estimated at roughly six solar masses and about 72 million years old. Data come from the 2025 MICONIC GTO program; Florian Peißker (University of Cologne) led the team.

**Links:**

- [ESA — Dust and water spotted close to giant black hole](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Webb/Dust_and_water_spotted_close_to_giant_black_hole)

**Commentary:** The galactic center looks less sterile than assumed—evolved stars can still seed dust and molecules for future stars and planets even beside a supermassive black hole.

### 4. Purple Mountain Observatory: MWISP survey maps widespread corrugations in the Milky Way’s outer CO disk (Astronomy)
**Summary:** Using China’s 13.7-meter millimeter telescope Milky Way Imaging Scroll Painting (MWISP) survey and more than 30,000 molecular clouds, Purple Mountain Observatory researchers subtracted a global CO warp model and systematically identified widespread vertical corrugations across the outer Galactic molecular disk. Characteristic scales include radial wavelengths of about 3.9–7.9 kpc and vertical amplitudes of about 100–200 pc, plus a coherent azimuthal mode near Galactocentric radius 12.7 kpc extending roughly 40 kpc. The paper *Widespread corrugations superimposed on the Galactic warp in the outer CO disk* appeared online August 7 in *Nature Astronomy*, with CAS coverage around August 10. The authors link the ripples to bending waves possibly excited by external gravitational perturbations.

**Links:**

- [Nature Astronomy — Widespread corrugations in the outer CO disk](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02938-y)
- [Chinese Academy of Sciences — Rippled folds in the outer Galactic gas disk](https://www.cas.cn/syky/202608/t20260810_5118166.shtml)

**Commentary:** The outer disk is no longer a smooth warped plate—cold-gas ripples turn galactic dynamical history into a measurable 3D terrain map.

## II. Biomedicine

### 5. Oral nano-delivery of gut metabolite DHB boosts T-cell stemness and sensitizes anti–PD-1 therapy (Biomedicine)
**Summary:** A Sungkyunkwan University–University of Michigan team led by Young Seok Cho and James J. Moon reported in *Nature Nanotechnology* on August 10 that the gut microbial metabolite 3,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid (DHB) enhances CD8+ T-cell stemness by suppressing glycolysis and modulating the Akt–mTORC1–Myc pathway. Because native DHB clears within minutes after oral dosing, they engineered an oleic-acid nano-emulsion prodrug (Prodrug 201) that raised oral bioavailability about 14.3-fold. In murine colorectal, melanoma, and breast cancer models, oral dosing plus anti–PD-1 shrank tumors, with complete clearance and durable immune memory in some settings. Institutional coverage via Newswise circulated around August 11.

**Links:**

- [Nature Nanotechnology — Oral nano-delivery of a gut microbial metabolite](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41565-026-02235-9)
- [Newswise — SKKU oral microbiome-based nanomedicine](https://www.newswise.com/articles/skku-researchers-develop-world-s-first-oral-microbiome-based-nanomedicine-to-supercharge-anti-cancer-immunity)

**Commentary:** Turning a fleeting microbial metabolite into a scalable oral nanomedicine sketches a practical drug path for microbiome-informed immunotherapy.

### 6. Heart rate control by “listening to light”: optical traps drive otolith rhythms to rescue drug-induced arrhythmia (Biomedicine)
**Summary:** Published August 11 in *Nature Communications*, a Guangzhou University / Jinan University team (Xiaoshuai Liu, Baojun Li, and colleagues) used dynamically reconfigurable optical traps (1064 nm, 100 kHz refresh) to translate musical rhythms into programmable otolith oscillations, bypassing acoustic propagation limits. Hindbrain circuits—chiefly the vagal motor area—mediate the opto-mechano-cardiac effect via adrenergic activation and a sympathetic–parasympathetic balance shift. Real-time rhythm transcoding quantified how spectral content modulates cardiac rhythm and rescued drug-induced pathological rhythms in model systems, aiming to move music-based interventions from empiricism toward quantitative sensory–autonomic biomedicine.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Heart rate control by listening to light](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76695-x)

**Commentary:** When a beat becomes a writeable mechanical program in an optical trap, cardiac modulation exits the black box and enters reproducible input–output science.

**Summary:** POLITICO/E&E News reported August 11 that the Trump administration will end NOAA support for the annual Arctic Report Card; authors were informed at a Monday (August 10) meeting. ### 8. AMS State of the Climate 2025: record greenhouse gases, sea level, and ocean heat (Climate)
**Summary:** On August 10 the American Meteorological Society released the 36th annual State of the Climate, a peer-reviewed synthesis by 625 scientists in 60 countries. For 2025 it confirms record greenhouse-gas concentrations, global mean sea level, and ocean heat content. Global mean CO₂ reached about 425.6±0.1 ppm (~53% above the preindustrial ~278 ppm); fossil carbon emissions were estimated at about 10.3±0.5 Pg C per year. Despite cooler equatorial Pacific conditions, mean annual global sea-surface temperature ranked third-highest in a 172-year record, and about 87% of the ocean surface saw at least one marine heat wave. Global mean sea level set a record for the 14th consecutive year, about 111.2 mm above the 1993 satellite altimetry baseline.

### 9. Certified 30.77% perovskite/silicon tandem on industrial textured silicon via a “healing” crystallization step (Energy)
**Summary:** On August 11 in *Nature Communications*, Nanjing University’s Hairen Tan, Wenchi Kong, and colleagues addressed wide-bandgap perovskite growth on industrial textured silicon with a hybrid two-step process by applying an MASCN “healing intervention” that triggers secondary crystal growth into through-thickness columnar grains. Champion 1.68 eV top cells reached 21.1% efficiency; the tandem delivered a certified stabilized 30.77% efficiency and 1.915 V Voc over a 1.164 cm² active area. An encapsulated device retained initial performance after 3,400 hours of continuous one-sun maximum-power-point tracking in ambient air. Silicon wafers were supplied by Trina Solar; collaborators include Uppsala University.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Healing intervention for tandem devices on textured silicon](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76713-y)

**Commentary:** Nailing lab tandem efficiency onto factory-textured silicon for thousands of hours is the page that actually matters for perovskite scale-up.

## IV. Materials & Engineering

### 10. CAS Institute of Physics / Beihang: 3D carbon-nanotube network lifts diamond toughness ~5–6× (Materials)
**Summary:** Researchers at the Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, with Beihang University, reported in *Nature Synthesis* (online July 9) an extrinsic toughening strategy: under high pressure and high temperature, highly dispersed multi-walled carbon nanotubes fill gaps between diamond grains to form a continuous 3D network with mixed sp²–sp³ interfacial bonding. The composite reaches hardness of about 91.6 GPa and average SENB fracture toughness of about 31.9 MPa·m¹/² (maximum about 36.4)—roughly five times that of single-crystal/synthetic diamond and exceeding some tungsten alloys. South China Morning Post highlighted the work on August 11.

**Links:**

- [Nature Synthesis — MWCNT network-toughened diamond composite](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44160-026-01113-5)
- [SCMP — China’s unbreakable crystal breakthrough](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3362770/tougher-armour-hard-diamond-chinas-unbreakable-crystal-breakthrough)

**Commentary:** A 3D nanotube scaffold pries open the hardness–toughness trade-off—promising longer life for cutting tools and extreme-environment structures.

### 11. Yale’s Tribar: tensegrity robot walks on after a 5.7 m fall (Robotics)
**Summary:** Published August 10 in *Nature Machine Intelligence*, Yale’s Tribar is a three-bar tensegrity rolling robot that absorbs impact by deforming a network of elastic cables suspending rigid struts. Stretchable sensor “tendons” and onboard motion sensing estimate shape and orientation in real time. It locomotes on grass, ice, gravel, and sand, climbs slopes up to about 28°, and follows straight, curved, and triangular paths. In a key demo it continued moving after falling 5.7 m from a bridge onto asphalt—cited as a record drop height for autonomous tensegrity robots. Chinese science media amplified the result on August 11 for planetary exploration and disaster-response use cases.

**Links:**

- [Nature Machine Intelligence — Impact-resistant autonomous tensegrity robots](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01280-2)
- [People’s Daily Online — Tensegrity robots withstand higher impacts](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0811/c1004-40777214.html)

**Commentary:** A deformable skeleton that replaces heavy landing gear could let planetary rovers and search robots drop straight into the mission zone.
