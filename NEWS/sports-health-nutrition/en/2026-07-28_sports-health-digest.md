## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. NUTRITION 2026 / WHI: Higher Planetary Health Diet adherence tied to ~28% lower CVD risk in postmenopausal women (cohort)

**Summary:** Using the Women's Health Initiative observational cohort, UC Irvine-led investigators analyzed 66,892 postmenopausal women free of CVD at baseline (mean age ~63), scored Planetary Health Diet Index (PHDI) from food-frequency questionnaires, and followed them for ~20 years. Women in the highest-adherence group had about 28% lower overall CVD risk versus the lowest-adherence group, with lower risks also for coronary heart disease, stroke, and heart failure; even moderate adherence was beneficial, and each ~10-point PHDI increase tracked further risk reduction. Findings were presented at NUTRITION 2026 (Jul 25-28) and published in *American Journal of Preventive Cardiology*. Observational design cannot prove causation.

**Links:**

- [EurekAlert! / ASN — Plant-forward planetary health diet linked to lower heart disease risk](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1136409)
- [UCI CGCHN — Planetary Health Diet and CVD risk study](https://sites.uci.edu/cardiometabolic/2026/07/22/cgchn-faculty-publish-study-linking-the-planetary-health-diet-to-lower-cardiovascular-disease-risk/)

**Commentary:** You do not need a perfect plant-only checklist—half a plate of vegetables, olive oil or avocado instead of butter, and one lower-red-meat meal per week are realistic steps that match the signal for postmenopausal heart protection.

### 2. Rutgers pilot RCT: A ~9-hour eating window plus 4 hours pre-bed fasting showed extra cognitive gains during weight loss

**Summary:** Sue Shapses' Rutgers team reported a pilot trial at NUTRITION 2026: 47 women aged 50-79 with overweight/obesity all cut ~500 kcal/day for six months; 26 confined eating to a ~9-hour window (often 10 a.m.-6 p.m.; mean ~8.2 hours) while others ate across ~12 hours. Both groups lost similar weight (~7 kg / 15 lb), but the shorter-window group improved more on spatial planning and problem-solving tests, with a trend toward fewer memory/learning errors; multitasking and reaction time did not differ. Authors called effects "modest"; the work is a conference abstract with a small sample and needs larger replication.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — Restricted eating hours may reduce cognitive decline in older age](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/jul/26/restricted-eating-hours-may-reduce-cognitive-decline-in-older-age-researchers-find)
- [ScienceDaily / ASN — Eating within 8-9 hours and aging cognition](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260727012125.htm)

**Commentary:** If you are already in a calorie deficit, consolidating daytime meals and stopping snacks ~4 hours before bed is a low-cost add-on—protect sleep and the deficit first; do not treat time-restricted eating as an anti-dementia drug.

### 3. Texas shelf audit: ~81% of toddler packaged foods were ultra-processed; nearly half missed WHO nutrient standards (NUTRITION 2026)

**Summary:** Erin Hudson and Marissa Burgermaster (UT Austin) audited 2,783 products marketed for ages 6-36 months across 21 Texas grocery stores: ~81% were ultra-processed by Nova; ~49% failed at least one WHO Nutrition and Promotion Profile Model (NPPM) criterion—about 17% for sugar, 25% sodium, 11% fat, and 12% energy density. Products failing sugar standards were ~4× as likely to be ultra-processed. This is a cross-sectional shelf audit presented at the meeting; national extrapolation needs care. CNN and others amplified the findings on Jul 28.

**Links:**

- [EurekAlert! — 80% of toddler foods on grocery shelves are ultra-processed](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1136466)
- [CNN — 4 out of 5 toddler foods are ultraprocessed](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/28/health/toddler-foods-ultraprocessed-wellness)

**Commentary:** The baby/toddler aisle is not a nutrition-certified zone—once kids can chew, lean on family meals, and check sugar, sodium, and ingredient lists rather than cartoon packaging.

### 4. Large Japanese cross-sectional MRI study: Lower plasma vitamin C linked to less gray matter and weaker default-mode connectivity

**Summary:** Nagaya et al. (Hirosaki University) published in *PLOS One* (Jun 10, 2026) a cross-sectional analysis of 2,044 Japanese adults ≥64 years (median age ~69) with 3T MRI and plasma vitamin C. After adjustment for age, education, physical activity, chronic disease, and lifestyle, lower plasma vitamin C was independently associated with lower gray-matter/intracranial-volume ratio and weaker default mode network (DMN) connectivity (both p<0.001). This does not prove supplements protect the brain; Chinese media coverage on Jul 28 reiterated that megadose vitamin C pills are not justified.

**Links:**

- [PLOS One — Plasma vitamin C and brain structural networks (large cohort)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0348504)
- [SETN — Vitamin C, gray matter, and memory-related networks (Jul 28 report)](https://www.setn.com/news/1879658)

**Commentary:** Cover vitamin C with fruit and vegetables first—association is not causation, and high-dose pills add kidney-stone and other risks without proven brain benefit here.

### 5. PNAS: Plant-derived digestion-resistant protein and fiber reshape gut phenol metabolite profiles (mouse mechanism)

**Summary:** Ludwig Princeton investigators Jenna AbuSalim, Joshua Rabinowitz and colleagues report in *PNAS* that stable-isotope tracing in mice shows "bad" phenols largely arise when microbes catabolize host mucus proteins, while "good" phenols come almost exclusively from dietary digestion-resistant protein; fiber dampens mucus foraging, and resistant protein increases substrate reaching distal microbes. A companion *Nature Metabolism* paper argues many indoles/phenols long attributed to microbes can also be made by host metabolism. Animal tracer work cannot be translated into marketing for a specific plant-protein powder.

**Links:**

- [News-Medical — Plant-based diets reshape gut microbes to boost healthy metabolites](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260727/Plant-based-diets-reshape-gut-microbes-to-boost-healthy-metabolites.aspx)
- [PNAS — Digestion-resistant proteins and plant-based metabolite profiles](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2605226123)

**Commentary:** Mechanistically this supports whole grains, legumes, and vegetable fiber—not a green-light for supplement ads; human intervention data still need to catch up.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 6. Sedentary young-men RCT: After 8 weeks of resistance training, multiple sets beat single sets for hypertrophy and strength

**Summary:** A randomized trial published Jul 18 in *BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation* assigned 34 sedentary males to single-set (SS, n=12), multiple-set (MS, n=11), or control (n=11) for eight weeks, twice weekly, seven exercises. MS did three sets per exercise; SS did one. Leg muscle cross-sectional area rose ~11.3% with MS versus ~6-8% with SS; bench-press and squat 1RM rose ~19.8% / ~14.8% with MS, with clearer gains in vertical jump and Yo-Yo IR1. Both training arms beat control, supporting an early volume dose-response. Small sample, untrained men only.

**Links:**

- [BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil — Single vs multiple-set RT on neuromuscular performance and morphology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01874-8)

**Commentary:** When starting strength work, 2-3 sets per lift usually beats one-set-only routines—if time is tight, lock in two full-body sessions weekly first, then add sets.

### 7. Eight-week FIFA 11+ RCT: Better post-fatigue landing mechanics in adolescent female soccer players with dynamic knee valgus

**Summary:** A parallel-group RCT published Jul 21 in *BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation* enrolled 21 adolescent female soccer players (ages 13-17) with dynamic knee valgus (DKV): 11 completed eight weeks of FIFA 11+, 10 kept usual warm-ups. Landing Error Scoring System (LESS) was assessed before and after a functional fatigue protocol. The 11+ group significantly improved landing mechanics and attenuated fatigue-related deterioration. Sample is tiny; endpoints are biomechanical risk markers, not ACL injury incidence.

**Links:**

- [BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil — FIFA 11+ and LESS after functional fatigue](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01906-3)

**Commentary:** Do not skip FIFA 11+ in adolescent women's soccer warm-ups—especially late in sessions when fatigue rises, cleaner landing mechanics target ACL risk factors better than a few extra stretches.

### 8. Meta-analysis: Exercise-based knee-injury prevention programs cut overall knee and ACL injury rates

**Summary:** A systematic review and meta-analysis in *Frontiers in Public Health* pooled 10 studies (~15,296 athletes). Groups receiving preventive training that included strength, balance, and plyometrics had lower overall knee injury incidence (RR≈0.68, 95% CI 0.51-0.92) and lower ACL injury rates (RR≈0.43, 95% CI 0.25-0.74). Heterogeneity was high; subgroup claims need caution, and optimal dose/population targeting still requires standardized trials.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Public Health — Preventive training and knee injury incidence meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1746109/full)

**Commentary:** Writing strength + balance + landing drills into the weekly warm-up beats buying another brace—finishing the program consistently matters more than chasing the newest drill.

## IV. Sleep & Recovery

### 10. Tohoku mouse imaging: REM sleep shows an energy paradox—supply up, neuronal ATP down

**Summary:** Takahashi et al. (Tohoku University) published Jul 27 in *Communications Biology* wide-field fluorescence imaging through intact mouse skull during natural sleep, tracking brain blood volume, astrocytic pyruvate, and neuronal ATP. In NREM, theta activity predicted subsequent vascular responses; ~50 seconds before REM, posterior-cortex blood volume rose and propagated anteriorly, astrocytic pyruvate increased, yet neuronal ATP fell sharply. Authors propose REM may preferentially allocate energy to costly processes such as memory reorganization. Mouse mechanism work—not a human supplement or sleep-drug brief.

**Links:**

- [Communications Biology — Energy paradox in REM sleep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-026-10646-6)
- [News-Medical — Hidden brain energy dynamics during REM sleep](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260727/Scientists-uncover-hidden-brain-energy-dynamics-during-REM-sleep.aspx)

**Commentary:** Dreaming sleep is not a metabolic free lunch—protect full-night sleep for memory and mood rather than trying to shrink REM with stimulants.

### 11. Chinese mid/older-adult cohort: Frailty mediates links between sleep duration and physical-psychological-cognitive multimorbidity

**Summary:** Ding et al. reported Jul 19 in *Scientific Reports* analyses of sleep duration, frailty, and co-occurring physical, psychological, and cognitive multimorbidity in Chinese middle-aged and older adults. Sleep duration related to multimorbidity burden, with frailty acting as a mediator—suggesting sleep problems may partly operate by accelerating frailty accumulation. Observational epidemiology; causal direction and residual confounding require caution.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Frailty mediates sleep duration and multimorbidity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-63040-x)

**Commentary:** If midlife/older sleep is chronically short or fragmented, watch frailty signals (strength, gait, unintended weight loss) too—pair sleep repair with resistance training, not pills alone.
