## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Plant-Based Diet Meta-Analysis: Overall and “Healthful” Patterns Linked to Lower CVD/CHD; “Unhealthful” Patterns Raise Risk (Nutrition)
**Summary:** A systematic review and meta-analysis in *Nutrients* (August 25, 2026) pooled prospective cohorts searched through February 2026: 22 articles (19 cohorts), 846,589 participants, and 139,652 events. Highest vs. lowest adherence to an overall plant-based diet (PBD) was associated with lower CVD risk (RR ≈ 0.90, 95% CI 0.85–0.95); a healthful PBD (hPBD) was more protective (RR ≈ 0.83), while an unhealthful PBD (uPBD) raised risk (RR ≈ 1.13). CHD followed the same pattern (overall RR ≈ 0.91; hPBD ≈ 0.77; uPBD ≈ 1.24); stroke associations were not significant. Observational evidence; not proof of causation and not equivalent to every “vegetarian” label.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Plant-based diet and CVD/CHD/stroke meta-analysis (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172779)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172779)

**Commentary:** Treat plant-based eating as whole grains, vegetables, legumes, fruits, and nuts—not refined carbs and sugary drinks standing in for meat; quality matters more than the “plant-only” label.

### 2. Chinese Community-Dwelling Older Adults: Later Breakfast and Dinner Linked to Higher Prevalent CVD; “Late-Phase” Eating Pattern ~56% Higher Odds (Nutrition)
**Summary:** A cross-sectional study in *Nutrients* (August 25, 2026) interviewed 1,386 adults aged ≥60 from September 2021 to October 2025; 1,305 were analyzed (mean age ~69.6 years; 54.6% women), with 196 prevalent CVD cases (15.0%). Breakfast after 7:00 vs. ≤6:00 had OR ≈ 1.75; dinner after 18:00 vs. ≤17:00 had OR ≈ 2.61. Latent profile analysis identified typical, early-phase, and late-phase temporal eating patterns; vs. typical, late-phase eating had OR ≈ 1.56, while early-phase odds were lower but not statistically significant. Cross-sectional; cannot establish causality or replace clinical care.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Meal timing and CVD in Chinese older adults (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172778)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172778)

**Commentary:** If your schedule allows, pull breakfast earlier and avoid pushing dinner late into the night—for older adults, meal timing may deserve as much attention as food choice.

### 3. Older Adults with MCI: 12-Month Vitamin D3 + DHA Combo Outperforms Either Alone on Cognition and Pyroptosis Markers (Nutrition/Supplements)
**Summary:** A double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT in *Nutrients* (August 25, 2026) randomized 360 older adults with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) to VD3 (800 IU/day), DHA (1 g/day), VD3+DHA, or placebo for 12 months; 355 completed. All active arms improved WAIS-RC cognition and lowered blood pyroptosis-related markers (IL-1β, IL-18, NLRP3, GSDMD, cleaved caspase-1) vs. placebo; the combination outperformed either single agent on full-scale, performance, and verbal IQ plus several subtests, with path analysis suggesting longitudinal GSDMD reduction may mediate FIQ gains. Single trial; do not extrapolate to people without MCI or to unsupervised high doses.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Vitamin D3 plus DHA in MCI: 12-month RCT (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172777)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172777)

**Commentary:** If MCI is confirmed and VD/ω-3 status is at risk, discuss moderate combined VD3+DHA as an adjunct—not a substitute for cognitive training and chronic-disease care.

### 4. Brazilian National School Surveys: High Ultra-Processed Adherence and Low Healthy/Traditional Adherence Linked to Worse Mental-Health Indicator (Nutrition/Mental Health)
**Summary:** *Nutrients* (August 25, 2026) analyzed school-attending adolescents aged 13–17 in Brazil's 2015 and 2019 PeNSE surveys. Principal component analysis derived ultra-processed/unhealthy and healthy/traditional patterns each year; the outcome was membership in the highest category of a survey-specific adverse mental-health-related indicator (not a clinical diagnosis). Vs. highest ultra-processed quartile (Q4), lowest quartile (Q1) had adjusted prevalence ratios ≈ 0.85 (2015) and ≈ 0.81 (2019); for the healthy/traditional pattern, Q1 had higher prevalence (≈ 1.75 and 1.34). Cross-sectional with edition-specific measures; cannot establish causality or invariant temporal change.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Dietary patterns and adolescent mental health indicator (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172781)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172781)

**Commentary:** Adolescent mental-health efforts should not stop at screens and school stress—nudging family tables away from ultra-processed snacks toward traditional/plant-forward patterns may be an underused lever.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Endurance Exercise in Hypoxia: Acute Beetroot Juice Attenuates Extra Muscle Deoxygenation; Most Outcomes Lack Robust Interaction (Training/Sports Nutrition)
**Summary:** A randomized crossover trial in *Nutrients* (August 25, 2026) tested 12 healthy young men across normoxia/hypoxia (FiO₂ 20.9%/14.5%) × placebo/beetroot juice (70 mL ≈ 400 mg NO₃⁻, 2.5 h pre-exercise) during 30 min of submaximal exercise at 70% HRmax. Hypoxia lowered mechanical load and raised physiological stress; Deoxy_Hb showed an environment × treatment interaction, with HBRJ statistically indistinguishable from normoxic conditions, suggesting attenuation of hypoxia-driven oxygen extraction; most other variables lacked robust interactions, and circulating nitrate/nitrite were not measured. Small acute trial; plateau training or race transfer needs more evidence.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Beetroot juice under hypoxia during endurance exercise (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172780)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172780)

**Commentary:** For occasional hypoxic or altitude endurance sessions, beetroot juice is a plausible peripheral oxygenation adjunct—not a fix for every performance gap.

### 6. Sitting-Interruption Meta-Analysis: Walking Breaks Best Improve Postprandial Glucose/Insulin; Strongest Effects Every ~15–20 Minutes (Training)
**Summary:** A systematic review and meta-analysis in *Obesity Reviews* (2026; DOI: 10.1111/obr.70152) screened acute laboratory randomized crossover trials through October 9, 2024: 53 eligible studies, 39 in the meta-analysis. Vs. prolonged sitting, activity breaks lowered postprandial glucose and insulin iAUC (both SMD ≈ −0.30); walking breaks had the largest effects (glucose SMD ≈ −0.33; insulin ≈ −0.44); interrupting every 15–20 minutes produced the biggest reductions (glucose SMD ≈ −0.51). Most primary studies used ~2–3-minute breaks; translating lab protocols into offices remains challenging.

**Links:**

- [Obesity Reviews — Activity breaks and postprandial glucose/insulin (DOI: 10.1111/obr.70152)](https://doi.org/10.1111/obr.70152)
- [Medical Xpress — Movement breaks reduce health risks](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-07-movement-health.html)

**Commentary:** Desk workers can upgrade “once an hour” to roughly every 20 minutes of 2–3 minutes of walking—frequency beats one long catch-up workout for post-meal glycemic control in this evidence base.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 9. Peruvian Secondary Students: ~7 Hours of Sleep and Higher Physical Activity Associated with Healthier Eating Habits (Sleep/Lifestyle)
**Summary:** A cross-sectional study in *Nutrients* (August 25, 2026) surveyed 275 Peruvian secondary students aged 12–17 on sleep duration, physical activity, and eating habits, analyzed with multivariable logistic regression. Vs. ≤6 hours of sleep, sleeping about 7 hours was associated with higher odds of healthy eating habits (aOR ≈ 2.53, 95% CI 1.18–5.54); low vs. high physical activity was linked to lower odds (aOR ≈ 0.097). Observational and modestly sized; cannot prove sleep or exercise directly cause better diets.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Sleep, physical activity and healthy eating in Peruvian students (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172775)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172775)

**Commentary:** When teens try to cut snacks or lose weight, protect ~7 hours of sleep and raise daily movement first—the three behaviors often improve together.

### 10. SLEEP 2026: Expecting Poor Postpartum Sleep Predicts Worse Actual Sleep, Outweighing Prior Sleep and Psychiatric History (Sleep)
**Summary:** A prospective study to be presented at SLEEP 2026 (University of Pennsylvania and collaborators) found that 70% of 432 pregnant women (301/432) expected poor postpartum sleep; the level of anticipated disruption significantly predicted postpartum sleep concerns. Among first-time pregnant women without prior health concerns, greater predicted disturbance was linked to worse objective (actigraphy) and subjective postpartum sleep; among those expecting the worst sleep, higher postpartum anxiety further worsened sleep independent of prenatal anxiety. Conference-abstract evidence; intervention trials are still needed.

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 12. Morning 300 mg Caffeine: No Significant Cognitive or Reactive-Agility Gains in Naive–Mild Male Consumers (Evidence Clarification)
**Summary:** A double-blind crossover trial in *Nutrients* (August 25, 2026) enrolled 51 recreationally active men (naive to mild habitual caffeine use) who ingested 300 mg caffeine (~2.5–5.3 mg/kg), placebo, or no-pill control around 06:30, then completed trail-making, Rey auditory verbal learning, Stroop, and reactive agility tests (RAT). Whether or not adjusted for body mass, condition main effects on cognitive and RAT outcomes were not significant; rectal and skin temperatures, mood, and withdrawal scores likewise showed no condition differences. Findings are limited to this population and morning setting and do not overturn caffeine's known ergogenic roles in other endurance/strength contexts. A companion narrative review the same day notes sparse athlete-specific evidence on dependence and withdrawal.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients — Acute morning caffeine and cognitive/agility measures (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172771)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172771)
- [Nutrients — Caffeine dependence/withdrawal evidence in athletes (narrative review) (DOI: 10.3390/nu18172770)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172770)

**Commentary:** A large pre-morning-training coffee “for sharper reactions” may not pay off if you already drink lightly—match dose and context to evidence rather than treating caffeine as universally performance-boosting.
