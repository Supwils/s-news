## I. Nutrition and Diet

### 1. NHANES analysis: U.S. men with higher ultra-processed calorie shares had about 24%–31% higher prostate-cancer risk (Nutrition)

**Summary:** Florida Atlantic University investigators including Hunter Scott and Charles H. Hennekens reported in *The American Journal of Medicine* (DOI: 10.1016/j.amjmed.2026.07.043); Newsweek covered the paper on August 14. They analyzed 17,024 U.S. men aged 18 and older in NHANES 2003–2023, estimating ultra-processed food (UPF) calories from two 24-hour recalls and NOVA, in four groups from under about 20% to over 44% of energy. The three higher groups had about 29% greater prostate-cancer risk versus the lowest; the two highest together about 30%; after age, race/ethnicity, smoking, and poverty adjustment the excess remained about 24%–31%. The top group alone was about 34% higher but not statistically significant. Cancer was self-reported and diet was short-term recall—observational association, not a trial.

**Links:**

- [Newsweek — UPFs and prostate-cancer risk (2026-08-14)](https://www.newsweek.com/the-foods-making-up-most-americans-diets-linked-to-higher-prostate-cancer-risk-12322017)
- [Florida Atlantic University — AMJ study release](https://www.fau.edu/newsdesk/articles/ultra-processed-foods-prostate-cancer-risk.php)

**Commentary:** For men, demoting sodas, processed meats, packaged snacks, and ready meals from staple calories is closer to this evidence than debating a single additive; the study does not prove that cutting UPFs prevents cancer, but it lines up with metabolic and cardiovascular findings.

### 2. *Nature*: East China Normal University team tests an oral “sense-and-respond” glucose-lowering probiotic in mice and monkeys (Nutrition / Mechanism)

**Summary:** Hai-Feng Ye’s group published August 12 in *Nature* (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10909-6); Chinese science media followed on August 14. They engineered *E. coli* Nissle 1917 into GIFT, an oral glucose-sensing living drug that temporarily resides in the gut, expresses GLP-1 when glucose exceeds a threshold, and down-regulates afterward. Diabetic mice showed improvements in glycemia, lipids, and some complication markers with repeated dosing; spontaneously diabetic cynomolgus monkeys had GLP-1/insulin rises and glucose lowering lasting up to about 3 days after one dose, and better glycemic stability over about 5 weeks of dosing every 3 days without clear CBC or liver/kidney lab flags. Animal and non-human primate work—not a human trial.

**Links:**

- [Nature — Glucose-responsive engineered probiotics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10909-6)
- [Sina / China Science Daily — Smart glucose-lowering probiotic (2026-08-14)](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-08-14/doc-ininfvzs4269083.shtml)

**Commentary:** This is a lab living-drug platform, not supermarket yogurt; people with diagnosed diabetes should stay on prescribed GLP-1 or insulin rather than imitating the protocol with commercial ferments.

### 3. Cambridge perspective paper: a health–environment–access score of 20 protein foods ranks tofu first and cheddar last (Nutrition)

**Summary:** Chris Macdonald of the University of Cambridge introduced the BPI Score in *Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems* (DOI: 10.3389/fsufs.2026.1677796); BBC Science Focus ranked the foods on August 14. Twenty common high-protein items were scored on greenhouse gases, water pollution, and resource use plus sodium, saturated fat, price, and availability. Top five: tofu, chia seeds, beans, quinoa, sunflower seeds; bottom three: lamb chops, mozzarella, cheddar. The top quintile was entirely plant-based; the bottom quintile entirely animal-based. A scoring perspective, not a new human RCT, and not a claim that animal protein has no nutritional value.

**Links:**

- [BBC Science Focus — Protein-source ranking (2026-08-14)](https://www.sciencefocus.com/news/best-source-protein-tofu)
- [Frontiers — BPI Score paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1677796/full)

**Commentary:** Use tofu, beans, and lentils as default protein more often; chicken breast and tuna can stay in the middle of the list—you need not drop eggs for a composite score, but treating cheddar as a primary protein raises both saturated fat and environmental cost.

### 4. *Pharmaceuticals* review: precision nutrition may improve GLP-1 effectiveness and tolerability, but joint prospective trials remain scarce (Nutrition)

**Summary:** Antonio Giordano of the Sbarro Health Research Organization and colleagues published a narrative review in *Pharmaceuticals* (MDPI volume/issue at the link below); Nutrition Insight summarized it. About one in seven patients respond poorly to GLP-1 drugs. The authors discuss individualized diet around body composition, micronutrients, microbiome, inflammation, metabolism, and genetics, with emphasis on protein for lean-mass preservation, micronutrient adequacy, microbiota modulation, anti-inflammatory patterns, and GI-symptom management. They state that prospective trials of integrated precision nutrition plus GLP-1 on long-term outcomes are limited; most advice is extrapolated from general weight-loss or metabolic populations.

**Links:**

- [Nutrition Insight — Precision nutrition and GLP-1](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/precision-nutrition-glp1-therapy.html)
- [Pharmaceuticals — High-quality weight loss and precision nutrition](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8247/19/6/897)

**Commentary:** If you are on semaglutide or a related agent, prioritize protein at meals, add resistance training, and manage nausea or constipation before waiting for a gene-customized meal plan—that matches the current evidence grade.

### 5. PNAS: Yaghnob kefir yeasts show efficient galactose use, helping explain low lactose in fermented milk (Mechanism)

**Summary:** Duccio Cavalieri’s University of Florence team published in *PNAS* (DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2537942123); Florence media reported on August 14. They sequenced *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* from traditional goat-milk ferments and compared them with more than 1,000 strains from wine, grapes, and the human gut. Yaghnob yeasts sit near the root of dairy-adapted lineages and metabolize galactose efficiently, competing with lactobacilli for lactose-hydrolysis products—a plausible explanation for lower lactose in live ferments and for microbiota interactions. Genomics and microbe–microbe ecology, not a clinical trial of kefir as therapy.

**Links:**

- [Florence Daily News — Kefir yeasts and the gut (2026-08-14)](https://www.florencedailynews.com/2026/08/14/university-of-florence-study-explores-how-kefir-yeasts-may-support-gut-health/)
- [PNAS — Genomic signatures of dairy adaptation (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2537942123)

**Commentary:** People with lactose intolerance can trial a properly live fermented dairy as a tolerance test; this paper does not replace lactase or medical evaluation, and it is not a prescription to treat inflammation with trendy kefir.

## II. Exercise and Training Science

### 6. *Cell Reports Medicine*: six 30-second all-out sprints immediately shifted about a quarter of measured plasma proteins, far more than 90 minutes of moderate cycling (Training)

**Summary:** Paul Cohen, Luke Olsen, and colleagues at Rockefeller University published August 13 in *Cell Reports Medicine* (DOI: 10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102988); Korean coverage recirculated on August 14. They compared sprint intervals (six 30-second all-out bouts with about 4 minutes rest; about 3 minutes of work) with 90 minutes of moderate continuous cycling and with running. Sprints immediately changed about a quarter of measured proteins (reports cite about 714 proteins, more than 98% rising); moderate cycling changed under 0.25%. Sprints also shifted more than 200 metabolites, with ectodomain shedding implicated. Against UK Biobank data on more than 53,000 people, 32 of 33 proteins tied to lower obesity/type 2 diabetes risk moved after sprints versus three after moderate exercise. Participants were mainly young healthy men, with a small sprint subsample—an acute molecular study, not a clinical-endpoint RCT.

**Links:**

- [Rockefeller University — Sprints and plasma proteins (2026-08-13)](https://www.rockefeller.edu/news/40328-sprinting-intense-exercise/)
- [EurekAlert — Cell Reports Medicine release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1139915)

**Commentary:** Trained adults can add a few near-maximal short intervals to the week for high-intensity stimulus; people who are sedentary or have cardiovascular or joint risk should not start with Wingate-style sprints—this proteomics snapshot does not cancel the long-term benefits of moderate aerobic work.

### 7. Meta-analysis: high-intensity, change-of-direction training raised osteocalcin; walking and conventional lifting were weaker on bone-turnover markers (Training)

**Summary:** Viktória Barna, Nóra Sydó, and colleagues at Semmelweis University pooled 24 RCTs and 1,238 healthy adults in *Sports Medicine - Open* (DOI: 10.1186/s40798-026-00986-2). They used blood markers such as osteocalcin rather than waiting for BMD. High-intensity training significantly increased osteocalcin (MD = 6.88; 95% CI 0.37–13.38); endurance training did not change osteocalcin but lowered BMI; power and mixed training did not significantly change osteocalcin. Recreational football and handball, with explosive efforts, cuts, and turns, were linked to about 45%–46% higher bone-formation markers. GRADE certainty for osteocalcin after high-intensity training was moderate to low; marker shifts precede density and are not the same as fracture prevention.

**Links:**

- [EurekAlert — Walking and weights may not suffice for bone](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1138559)
- [Sports Medicine - Open — High-intensity training and osteocalcin](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-026-00986-2)

**Commentary:** Bone-protective training should not be walking or machine circuits alone; adding jumps, cutting, or small-sided games when safe tracks this marker evidence better than adding another low-impact session.

## III. Public Health and Disease Prevention

### 8. WHO restates combination shots and existing schedules: splitting MMR or delaying doses is not safer; a Wisconsin measles cluster is mostly unvaccinated (Public health)

**Summary:** After a U.S. executive order on August 10 seeking to cut routine childhood immunizations from about 18 diseases to 11 and to split MMR, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said on August 12 that vaccines including MMR are safe and do not cause autism, and that unnecessary delay or dose-splitting does not increase safety and can leave children unprotected. Wisconsin health officials reported on August 14 that a southwest outbreak in Iowa and Lafayette counties grew by 13 confirmed and 6 probable cases; all but one 2026 patient were unvaccinated against measles, and one was hospitalized. Two-dose MMR remains the core control tool. How far the order changes practice still depends on states, insurers, and litigation.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Complete two MMR doses on the pediatric/national schedule rather than stretching unprotected gaps to “split shots”; if measles is circulating, check records first instead of waiting for symptoms.

## IV. Sleep and Mental Health

### 9. Meta-analysis of 11 RCTs: non-drug sleep–circadian programs reduced depression and insomnia in adults with mental disorders (Sleep)

**Summary:** A *Frontiers in Psychiatry* systematic review searched through April 12, 2026, and included 11 RCTs in depression, bipolar disorder, severe mental illness, ADHD, and mixed psychiatric samples. Combined sleep–circadian interventions versus controls: depressive symptoms SMD = −0.51 (95% CI −0.72 to −0.30); treatment response RR = 1.68 (1.25–2.27); insomnia severity MD = −5.23 (−6.41 to −4.04); sleep quality SMD = −0.92, with gains in sleep-related and global function. Remission rested on only two trials. Certainty ranged from very low to moderate, and some outcomes had few studies—not a replacement for psychiatric medication.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Psychiatry — Sleep–circadian intervention meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1901978/full)

**Commentary:** When depression and insomnia travel together, treat a fixed wake time, morning light, time-in-bed restriction, and CBT-I-style routines as first-line behavior, alongside medication, rather than only adding sleep drugs or waiting for mood to self-correct.

## V. Evidence and Clarification

### 10. FDA proposes mandatory GRAS notification to close a voluntary additive loophole; the rule is still out for comment (Food safety)

**Summary:** CNN reported that on August 10 the U.S. FDA proposed requiring manufacturers to notify the agency when they use “generally recognized as safe” (GRAS) ingredients, converting a long-standing voluntary program into mandatory notice. A March study said at least 111 substances of unknown safety had entered foods, drinks, and supplements without FDA notice. Acting Commissioner Kyle Diamantas framed the plan as a transparency blueprint; NYU’s Marion Nestle noted notification is not independent pre-market review or a cleanup of chemicals already on sale. A federal ultra-processed-food definition remains under interagency review. The proposal needs public comment and is not an immediate ban.

**Links:**

- [CNN — FDA move to close the GRAS loophole (2026-08-10)](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/health/fda-food-ingredients-gras-wellness)

**Commentary:** Keep leaning away from products whose ingredient lists are stacks of unfamiliar industrial additives; “mandatory notice is coming” does not mean those chemicals are proven safe or already gone from shelves.

### 11. Meta-analysis of 13 RCTs: neuromuscular training cut athlete ankle-injury incidence by about 55% (Sports medicine)

**Summary:** *Frontiers in Physiology* pooled 13 RCTs and 1,476 athletes comparing neuromuscular training (NMT) with no training or usual training. Ankle-injury RR = 0.45 (95% CI 0.25–0.81, I² = 62%); Star Excursion Balance Test reach increased posteromedially, posterolaterally, and anteriorly; FAAM-Sports rose, while FAAM-ADL did not. Heterogeneity was moderate and GRADE cautions apply; the sample is athletes, not casual walkers.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Physiology — NMT and ankle injuries](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.1854319/full)

**Commentary:** Court and running athletes should put 10–15 minutes of single-leg balance, landing mechanics, and hip–knee control into the warm-up rather than relying on an ankle brace; acute sprain or chronic instability still needs clinical clearance before load.
