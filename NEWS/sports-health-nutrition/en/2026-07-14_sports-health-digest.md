## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Overweight/obesity: high-protein diet outperforms calorie restriction on weight and glycemic markers (retrospective)
**Summary:** Per **Nutrition & Metabolism** on **July 9, 2026**, a Dongguan team retrospectively compared **24** weeks of intensive lifestyle intervention in **63** overweight/obese adults: high-protein diet (**HPD, n=32**) vs calorie-restricted diet (**CRD, n=31**). The **HPD** group showed greater reductions in body weight, **BMI**, waist circumference, and body fat; clinically significant weight loss (**≥10%**) was **58.1%** vs **19.4% (P=0.006)**. **HPD** also improved **HbA1c**, **2-hour** postprandial glucose, free fatty acids, and adiponectin more. No significant between-group differences were seen for **HOMA-IR** or lipids. This is a small retrospective study, not an **RCT**.

**Links:**

- [Nutrition & Metabolism — HPD vs CRD in overweight/obese adults (July 9, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12986-026-01163-8)

**Commentary:** For fat loss, don't focus on calories alone—under clinician/dietitian guidance, modestly raising high-quality protein may better preserve lean mass and glycemic control, though this small study cannot replace individualized plans.

### 2. Iranian adults: higher egg intake linked to lower odds of depression and psychological distress (cross-sectional)
**Summary:** Per **Nutrition Journal** on **July 9, 2026**, **533** Iranian adults (**46.2%** female, mean age **42.6**) were assessed with a **168-item FFQ**, **HADS/GHQ** scales, and fasting oxidative stress/inflammation biomarkers. In fully adjusted models, the highest egg-intake tertile had lower odds of depression (**OR=0.45; 95% CI 0.24–0.82**) and high psychological distress (**OR=0.44; 0.27–0.72**), with significant trends. Associations were stronger in normal-weight participants; in women, egg intake correlated with lower malondialdehyde and higher superoxide dismutase. Cross-sectional design—no causal inference.

**Links:**

- [Nutrition Journal — Egg consumption, psychological disorders and oxidative stress (July 9, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12937-026-01362-2)

**Commentary:** Eggs are an affordable complete protein—if lipids are normal, one whole egg daily within a balanced diet is reasonable to track alongside mood and checkups, but don't treat this as an antidepressant substitute.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 3. ACSM releases 2026 resistance training position stand: consistency beats the "perfect plan"
**Summary:** The American College of Sports Medicine (**ACSM**) issued its first major update in **17** years, synthesizing **137** systematic reviews with **>30,000** participants. Key message for healthy adults: the biggest gains come from moving from no resistance training to any resistance training; training all major muscle groups at least **twice weekly** matters more than complex periodization, training to failure, or equipment choice. Strength goals favor heavier loads (~**80% 1RM**, **2–3** sets); hypertrophy favors higher weekly volume (~**10** sets/muscle group); bands, bodyweight, and home routines work. This is a guideline/position stand, not a new **RCT**.

**Links:**

- [ACSM — 2026 Resistance Training Guidelines update (March 17, 2026)](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

**Commentary:** The main enemy of fitness is an overly complex plan you'll quit—lock in **two** sustainable full-body sessions weekly before chasing advanced splits.

### 4. Football players: network meta-analysis ranks strength training highest for jump, sprint, and squat 1RM gains
**Summary:** Per **Frontiers in Physiology** on **July 6, 2026**, a systematic review and network meta-analysis of **53 RCTs** (**1,580** football players) compared strength, endurance, speed, and technical training. Strength training ranked highest on multiple outcomes (**SUCRA** up to **99.9%** for **1RM** squat, **94%** for **CMJ**, strong ranks for **5–30 m** sprints and **T-test** agility), while endurance training ranked highest for **VO2max** (**SUCRA 84.7%**). Heterogeneity exists and optimal dose varies by outcome.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Physiology — Optimal dose of S&C training in football (July 6, 2026)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.1791257/full)

**Commentary:** Recreational players short on time should prioritize **1–2** weekly lower-body strength sessions (squat, jump, single-leg work) and add dedicated aerobic work for **VO2max** as needed.

### 5. Sprinters crossover trial: low-load BFR warm-up acutely outperforms high load on 20 m sprint
**Summary:** Per **PLOS One** on **July 7, 2026**, **17** collegiate male sprinters completed a randomized crossover of high-load (**90% 1RM** hip thrust/half-squat) vs low-load (**30% 1RM** + blood flow restriction **BFR**). Five minutes post-intervention, **30%** hip thrust **+BFR** improved **0–20 m** time; **30%** half-squat **+BFR** improved **10–20 m** split, with pennation-angle decreases and muscle-tendon stiffness increases. Acute effects in a small crossover—caution extrapolating to season-long programming.

**Links:**

- [PLOS One — High vs low load BFR on sprint performance (July 7, 2026)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0353131)

**Commentary:** Pre-race warm-ups may trial light resistance **+BFR** for explosive priming—but only with qualified cuff-pressure guidance; don't replace main training with acute tricks.

### 6. ACL injury prevention: neuromuscular training protective effect stronger in athletes under 18
**Summary:** Per **Frontiers in Public Health** on **May 26, 2026**, a systematic review and meta-analysis of **19** studies found **NMT** reduced overall **ACL** injury risk (**OR=0.456; 95% CI 0.331–0.627; p<0.001**). Age-stratified effects were significant under **18** (**OR=0.365; 0.252–0.529**) but not **≥18** (**OR=0.567; 0.291–1.104**); meta-regression suggested attenuation with older mean age (**β=0.099; p=0.027**). Evidence is largely from female cohorts—limited male extrapolation.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Public Health — NMT and ACL injury prevention by age (May 26, 2026)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1801019/full)

**Commentary:** Youth academies should keep **FIFA 11+**-style **NMT** in-season; adult pros still benefit, but expected gains may be smaller outside the adolescent window.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. LatAm-FINGERS: two-year multidomain lifestyle trial across 12 Latin American countries improves cognition (The Lancet)
**Summary:** Reported at **AAIC 2026** on **July 13, 2026** and published simultaneously in **The Lancet**, **LatAm-FINGERS** randomized **1,065** adults aged **60–77** at dementia risk to Systematic Lifestyle Intervention (**SLI, n=539**) vs Flexible Lifestyle Intervention (**FLI, n=526**). **SLI** included supervised exercise (culturally adapted activities such as salsa), adapted **MIND**-style nutrition counseling, cognitive training, cardiovascular risk monitoring, and **38** group social sessions; **FLI** received **four** health-education meetings. After **two** years, **SLI** showed **55%** greater improvement in global cognition vs **FLI**, plus better episodic memory, executive function, and processing speed. Large **RCT**—structured coaching plus peer support was the key difference.

**Links:**

- [AAIC — LatAm-FINGERS improves brain health in Latin America (July 13, 2026)](https://aaic.alz.org/releases-2026/latam-fingers-improves-brain-health-latin-america.asp)

**Commentary:** Dementia risk reduction needs a bundled, coached program—exercise, brain-healthy eating, cardiometabolic control, cognitive training, and social connection together beat self-guided handouts alone.

### 8. NEXIS trial: in-bed cycling plus IV amino acids did not beat usual ICU care in acute respiratory failure
**Summary:** Per the **American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine** on **July 13, 2026**, the **NIH**-funded **NEXIS** trial at **nine** U.S. academic centers randomized **115** ICU patients with acute respiratory failure to early in-bed cycling plus IV amino acids plus usual care vs usual care alone. From hospitalization through **six** months post-discharge, no significant differences emerged in mobility, strength, physical function, quality of life, or cognition; the intervention was safe. Authors note possible early "anabolic resistance" in critical illness and call for individualized timing. A negative but landmark trial.

**Links:**

- [AJRCCM — NEXIS trial of in-bed cycling and IV amino acids (July 13, 2026)](https://academic.oup.com/ajrccm/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/ajrccm/aamag287/8732445)
- [News-Medical — NEXIS trial reshapes ICU recovery research (July 13, 2026)](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260713/New-trial-helps-reshape-the-future-of-recovery-research-for-critically-ill-patients.aspx)

**Commentary:** Don't port "ICU protein + early exercise" straight into post-op home rehab—critical illness muscle loss differs; follow staged medical guidance rather than blind protein loading.

### 9. Chinese hospitalized adults: nonlinear link between TCBI and hospital-acquired malnutrition
**Summary:** Per the **Journal of Health, Population and Nutrition** on **July 10, 2026**, a prospective multicenter Chinese cohort of **3,382** non-critically ill adults without admission malnutrition across **34** tertiary hospitals found **21.6%** developed **GLIM**-defined hospital-acquired malnutrition (**HAM**) within **7–30** days. Fully adjusted models showed each **1-unit** increase in log-transformed triglyceride-cholesterol-body weight index (**TCBI**) associated with **14.7%** lower **HAM** risk (**OR=0.853**); restricted cubic splines identified inflection points (**TCBI-LN 7.27, 8.50**). Associations were stronger in surgical patients and those without hypertension. Observational—high-**TCBI** high-risk stratum had small samples.

**Links:**

- [J Health Popul Nutr — TCBI and hospital-acquired malnutrition in Chinese adults (July 10, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41043-026-01394-8)

**Commentary:** Inpatient care should trigger early nutrition screening beyond the primary diagnosis—this exploratory **TCBI** metric isn't clinic-ready, but flags that metabolic profiles may track malnutrition risk.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 10. GLP-1R activation and mental health: drug-target Mendelian randomization suggests lower depression and bipolar risk
**Summary:** Per **Translational Psychiatry** on **July 13, 2026**, Hong Kong and Cambridge researchers performed drug-target Mendelian randomization and colocalization using the largest European **GWAS** datasets, replicated in **FinnGen**. After multiple-testing correction, genetically predicted lower **BMI** via **GLP-1R** activation per **1 kg/m²** was associated with better well-being spectrum (**0.06 SD; 0.03–0.08**), lower major depressive disorder risk (**OR=0.82; 0.75–0.88**), and lower bipolar disorder risk (**OR=0.61; 0.47–0.79**); associations exceeded genome-wide **BMI/HbA1c** instruments. Suggestive evidence also for lower substance-use disorder risk. Genetic epidemiology—not a substitute for clinical psychiatric safety monitoring.

**Links:**

- [Translational Psychiatry — GLP-1R activation and mental health MR study (July 13, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-026-04244-7)

**Commentary:** **GLP-1** users worried about mood effects can treat this as genetically supportive calibration—still monitor mood and sleep per prescription; never self-adjust dosing based on MR alone.

### 11. Young adults' oral microbiome: childhood adversity and parasympathetic stress response tied to distinct taxa
**Summary:** Per **Scientific Reports** on **July 13, 2026**, UCLA researchers studied **76** undergraduates with acute-stressor parasympathetic function (**HRV**), retrospective childhood adversity, **16S** oral microbiome profiling, and anxiety/depression symptoms. Childhood adversity correlated with lower microbiome diversity; independently, parasympathetic withdrawal during stress correlated with higher **Alysiella** (linked to latent viral infections); depressive symptoms negatively correlated with neuroprotective-metabolite producer **Butyrivibrio**. No significant adversity-by-stress interaction. Small cross-sectional exploratory study.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Oral microbiome, stress, adversity and mental health (July 13, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60803-4)

**Commentary:** Oral hygiene and stress management may belong in the same resilience toolkit—regular brushing, flossing, and stress reduction are low-risk, but we're far from "brush your way out of depression."

### 12. CHARLS cohort: jointly worsening sleep-duration deviation and frailty tracks fastest cognitive decline
**Summary:** Per **BMC Public Health** on **July 8, 2026**, **7,749** middle-aged and older participants in China's **CHARLS** (**2011–2020**) were grouped by joint trajectories of sleep deviation outside **6–8 h** and frailty. Versus stable sleep plus non-frailty, the group with persistently worsening sleep deviation and worsening frailty showed the fastest cognitive decline (**β=−0.044 SD/year**) and highest incident mild cognitive impairment risk (**HR=1.48; 95% CI 1.20–1.82**). Joint trajectories outperformed sleep-only or frailty-only models. Prospective cohort with self-reported sleep duration.

**Links:**

- [BMC Public Health — Joint sleep deviation and frailty trajectories in CHARLS (July 8, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-026-28448-0)

**Commentary:** Older adults shouldn't optimize sleep hours in isolation—if weight loss, weak grip, and slow gait coexist, treat sleep and frailty together rather than chasing naps alone.

## V. Evidence & Clarifications

### 13. Resistance-trained men: whey plus collagen 8-week RCT beats either alone on muscle, lumbar BMD, and strength
**Summary:** Per **BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation** on **July 9, 2026**, a double-blind **RCT** randomized **40** trained men to **WP+C (30 g/day whey + 10 g/day collagen)**, whey alone, collagen alone, or placebo during **8** weeks of supervised resistance training (**3×/week**). **WP+C** produced the largest muscle-mass gain (**Δ2.66±0.25 kg**), lumbar **BMD** increase (**Δ0.108±0.027 g/cm²**), squat/bench **1RM** gains, and favorable bone-turnover markers (**P1NP** up, **CTX-I** down). Young men only, short follow-up—limited extrapolation to women and older adults.

**Links:**

- [BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil — Whey plus collagen in resistance-trained men (July 9, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01846-y)

**Commentary:** Lifters concerned about bone density can trial whey plus a modest collagen peptide dose as a hypothesis-generating stack—total protein and training volume still come first.
