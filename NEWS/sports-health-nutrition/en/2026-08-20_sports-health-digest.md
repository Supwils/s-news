## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Longer Habitual Meal Gaps Linked to Faster Chronic-Disease Accumulation in Older Adults (Nutrition)

**Summary:** A Karolinska Institutet analysis of the Swedish SNAC-K cohort (2,981 adults aged ≥60 at baseline; follow-up up to about 15 years) was published online today in the *Journal of Internal Medicine*. Using self-reported usual meal times to estimate the longest daily meal interval, researchers found faster multimorbidity accumulation among those with a longest interval of 14–24 hours versus 6–11.5 hours. The association appeared for total chronic disease count and for cardiovascular and neuropsychiatric conditions, but not musculoskeletal disease, and was strongest among adults aged ≥78. This is an observational cohort; the authors emphasize that causality cannot be inferred and that fasting itself was not proven to drive disease acceleration.  

**Links:**

- [Karolinska Institutet — Longer meal intervals linked to faster disease accumulation in older adults](https://news.ki.se/longer-meal-intervals-linked-to-faster-disease-accumulation-in-older-adults)
- [Journal of Internal Medicine — Habitual fasting duration and accelerated multimorbidity in older adults (DOI: 10.1111/joim.70150)](https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70150)

**Commentary:** Popular long fasting windows from younger adults should not be defaulted onto frail older adults; prioritize regular, adequate intake before experimenting with extended gaps.

### 2. About a Handful of Nuts a Day Linked to Lower Hypertension Risk; Benefit Curve Flattens Near 30–35 g (Nutrition)

**Summary:** A systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis in the *British Journal of Nutrition* pooled eight prospective cohorts (~143,000 adults; 20,665 hypertension events). Highest versus lowest nut intake had a summary RR of 0.84 (95% CI 0.77–0.91); each additional ~28 g/day was associated with RR 0.80 (95% CI 0.71–0.89). Nonlinear modeling suggested about a 26% lower risk at ~30 g/day versus 0 g, with little further gain at higher intakes. Evidence is observational with substantial heterogeneity and does not prove nuts directly treat hypertension.  

**Links:**

- [British Journal of Nutrition — Nut consumption and the risk of hypertension: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/nut-consumption-and-the-risk-of-hypertension-a-systematic-review-and-dose-response-metaanalysis-of-prospective-studies/F598AFD9FD35B00A41A428C9D65F2C14)
- [News Medical — How much do nuts matter for blood pressure?](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260812/How-much-do-nuts-matter-for-blood-pressure-This-study-points-to-about-a-handful-a-day.aspx)

**Commentary:** The practical move is to swap ~30 g of unsalted or lightly salted nuts for refined carbohydrate calories, not to keep adding handfuls on top of an unchanged diet.

### 3. Large Cohorts: ≥1 Daily Sugar-Sweetened Drink Linked to Higher Gastric Cancer Risk; Artificially Sweetened Drinks Showed No Similar Signal (Nutrition)

**Summary:** Mass General Brigham investigators analyzed ~112,000 participants from the Nurses’ Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study in *Gastro Hep Advances*, identifying 278 gastric cancers over decades of follow-up. After multivariable adjustment, people drinking at least one sugar-sweetened beverage (SSB) per day had about 2.45 times the gastric cancer risk of rare consumers (95% CI 1.49–4.04). Artificially sweetened beverages were not associated with higher incidence; higher fructose intake tracked in the same direction as SSB risk. This is prospective observational evidence with a limited case count; mechanisms and causality still need confirmation.  

**Links:**

- [Gastro Hep Advances — Association between sugar-sweetened and artificially sweetened beverage intake and gastric cancer incidence](https://www.ghadvances.org/article/S2772-5723(26)00218-9/fulltext)
- [News Medical — A decades-long study raises questions about sugary drinks and stomach cancer](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260819/A-decades-long-study-raises-questions-about-sugary-drinks-and-stomach-cancer.aspx)

**Commentary:** The highest-yield habit change is cutting habitual daily SSBs; diet drinks are not a free pass, but this cohort did not show the same gastric cancer signal.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. ACSM Updates Resistance-Training Position Stand: Hit ≥2 Sessions Weekly First, Then Fine-Tune Load and Volume by Goal (Training / Guideline)

**Summary:** The American College of Sports Medicine published a new position stand in *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*—an overview of 137 systematic reviews (>30,000 participants) updating the 2009 guidance. Versus no training, resistance training improved strength, hypertrophy, power, and multiple physical-function outcomes. Strength was favored by heavier loads (≥80% 1RM), full range of motion, 2–3 sets, early-session placement, and ≥2 sessions/week; hypertrophy by higher weekly volume (≥10 sets/week) and eccentric overload; power by moderate loads (~30%–70% 1RM) and fast concentric intent. Training to failure, equipment type, and complex periodization did not consistently change outcomes for average healthy adults.  

**Links:**

- [ACSM / MSSE — Resistance Training Prescription for Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews (DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897)](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0000000000003897)
- [PMC — Full text of the ACSM Position Stand overview](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)

**Commentary:** For most adults, lock in progressive resistance training at least twice weekly before chasing advanced techniques that add little for general fitness.

### 5. Circuit Strength Training: 1:1 Work–Rest Duration Changes Rep Output, but Acute Neuromuscular Fatigue Magnitude Looks Similar (Training)

**Summary:** A crossover study in *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* tested 34 physically active adults across 14-minute circuit protocols with 10:10, 20:20, and 30:30 (seconds) 1:1 work–rest intervals. Mean propulsive velocity in squat and bench press fell significantly after all three protocols, indicating acute neuromuscular fatigue, without statistically significant between-protocol differences in fatigue magnitude; the 30:30 protocol allowed higher repetition output. This was an acute experiment and does not map directly to long-term adaptation.  

**Links:**

- [Frontiers — Acute neuromuscular fatigue in circuit strength training: effects of varying work-to-rest durations](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1761646/full)

**Commentary:** Choose longer intervals when you need more completed reps; shorter intervals may raise cardio demand, but do not expect them to eliminate fatigue.

### 6. Neuromuscular Training Cuts ACL Injury Odds Overall; Protective Signal Stronger in Younger Athletes (Training / Injury Prevention)

**Summary:** A systematic review and meta-analysis in *Frontiers in Public Health* included 19 studies and found neuromuscular training (NMT) associated with lower ACL injury odds (OR 0.456, 95% CI 0.331–0.627). In age-stratified analyses, the effect was significant in athletes <18 years (OR 0.365) but the ≥18-year estimate crossed 1; meta-regression suggested attenuation with higher mean age. Evidence remains dominated by female cohorts, so male extrapolation should stay cautious.  

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Public Health — Age-related differences in neuromuscular training for preventing ACL injuries](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1801019/full)

**Commentary:** Youth programs should treat NMT warm-ups as default practice; adult teams should still use them, without assuming teen-sized risk reductions automatically transfer.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. U.S. Measles Risk: County-Level Vaccination Averages Can Hide School-Level Epidemic Thresholds (Public Health)

**Summary:** A *Nature Medicine* study assembled MMR coverage across ~50,000 schools, 13,000 districts, and 3,000 counties (2013–2025) and modeled transmission risk by spatial scale. School-level effective reproduction numbers crossed the epidemic threshold in 2022–2023, while county aggregates remained below that threshold; average susceptibility roughly doubled from ~5% to nearly 10% after the pandemic. A companion research briefing dated Aug 20, 2026 underscores that county surveillance missed school-level supercritical risk. This is modeling and surveillance analysis about monitoring granularity, not a new vaccine-efficacy trial.  

**Links:**

- [Nature Medicine — County, district and community-level measles transmission in the United States in 2013−2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04561-w)
- [Nature Medicine — County surveillance failed to detect epidemic threshold in schools](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04612-2)

**Commentary:** Families should verify two-dose MMR completion; communities should not treat a reassuring county average as proof that local school clusters are safe.

### 8. Nearly 29-Year Cohort: Cumulative Smoking Tracks Sudden Cardiac Death Risk; Cessation Lowers Risk Only Gradually (Public Health)

**Summary:** The Copenhagen City Heart Study followed ~10,000 participants for a median 28.6 years and adjudicated 890 sudden cardiac deaths (SCD). Versus never smokers, adjusted HRs were about 1.88 for current smokers and 1.34 for former smokers; the highest cumulative-exposure category reached HR ~2.50. Among former smokers, SCD risk declined with years since quitting but often remained elevated for a long period before approaching never-smoker levels. Findings are observational and may not fully generalize to other populations or contemporary smoking patterns.  

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Long-term risk of sudden cardiac death according to cumulative smoking exposure and smoking cessation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-67157-x)
- [News Medical — This 29-year heart study tracked what happens after smokers quit](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260820/This-29-year-heart-study-tracked-what-happens-after-smokers-quit.aspx)

**Commentary:** Quitting earlier is the highest-value cardiovascular move; after cessation, keep managing blood pressure, lipids, and activity rather than assuming risk instantly resets.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 9. Across ~4,800 Person-Nights, Higher Fiber Density Linked to More Deep/REM Sleep and Lower Nocturnal Heart Rate That Night (Sleep / Nutrition)

**Summary:** *Nature Health* analyzed 4,793 free-living person-nights with real-time diet logs and multi-stage wearable sleep recordings, using a lagged design and inverse-probability weighting. Higher fiber density was associated with +0.59 percentage points deep sleep, +0.76 REM, −1.35 light sleep, and −1.14 bpm mean nocturnal heart rate. Greater plant diversity and whole-plant intake similarly related to lower nocturnal heart rate. Heavier energy intake in the 6 hours before bed linked to ~7.7 more minutes of total sleep but also ~0.73 bpm higher nocturnal heart rate. Short-term macronutrient energy-share shifts showed no robust associations. This is observational causal-inference work, not an RCT.  

**Links:**

- [Nature Health — Impact of daily diet on sleep quality](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44360-026-00182-2)

**Commentary:** For same-night sleep architecture, raising fiber and plant diversity is a more promising lever than micro-tweaking protein/fat percentages; a heavier late meal may lengthen sleep without making it more restorative.

### 10. Longitudinal Data: Multiple Sleep Symptoms Can Precede Psychiatric Diagnosis and Intensify Before Onset (Sleep / Mental Health)

**Summary:** In a study published in *European Archives of Psychiatry and Clinical Neuroscience*, 716 participants (2.9%) received a psychiatric diagnosis during follow-up. Sleep duration showed a U-shaped association with incident illness; all assessed sleep-related symptoms raised risk, with mental fatigue strongest (HR 2.09, 95% CI 1.77–2.48). Insomnia and fatigue often worsened before diagnosis and partly improved afterward while remaining elevated versus unaffected peers. Sleep problems may mark prodromal vulnerability, but a single bad night does not equal inevitable illness.  

**Links:**

- [European Archives of Psychiatry and Clinical Neuroscience — Sleep disturbances as early indicators of incident psychiatric disease](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00406-026-02332-9)

**Commentary:** Progressive insomnia plus daytime mental fatigue that impairs function warrants combined sleep and mental-health evaluation rather than indefinite self-medication.

## V. Evidence & Clarification

### 11. Meta-Analysis: Canola and Sunflower Oils Show Comparable Effects on Adult Lipid Profiles (Evidence Clarification)

**Summary:** A systematic review and meta-analysis in the *Journal of Health, Population and Nutrition* pooled 13 RCTs (771 adults) comparing canola oil with sunflower oil. Between-oil differences were not statistically significant for total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides, or VLDL-C. The authors conclude either oil can fit lipid-lowering or heart-healthy diets, while calling for more high-quality trials across diverse populations.  

**Links:**

- [Journal of Health, Population and Nutrition — Comparison of sunflower oil and canola oil on serum lipid profile](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41043-026-01430-7)

**Commentary:** Stop treating “canola versus sunflower” as a make-or-break choice; total oil volume, frying habits, and overall saturated-fat load matter more.

### 12. EU 15-Country Survey: Most RTE Plant-Based Alternatives Meet Quality Bars, but Sporadic Pathogens and Growth-Supporting Conditions Need Attention (Food Safety)

**Summary:** Food agencies from 15 EU member states surveyed ready-to-eat plant-based dairy, meat, and fish substitutes; the report was published via EFSA channels. Most samples had satisfactory microbiological quality, yet isolated pathogen detections and elevated counts occurred, and some physico-chemical profiles (pH/water activity) could support microbial growth. Prior literature also links plant-based dairy substitutes to listeriosis and salmonellosis outbreaks. Authors call for continued monitoring and more harmonized microbiological criteria.  

**Links:**

- [Food Safety News — EU countries test safety of plant-based products](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/08/eu-countries-test-safety-of-plant-based-products/)
- [Food Safety Magazine — EU survey of pathogens in RTE plant-based foods](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11724-eu-survey-of-pathogens-in-rte-plant-based-foods-suggests-microbial-growth-potential-need-for-regulatory-attention)

**Commentary:** Plant-based does not mean sterile—keep cold-chain storage, eat promptly, and watch recalls, especially for RTE cheese and meat analogues.
