## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Matched-weight-loss RCT: Ketogenic diet outperforms Mediterranean and low-fat plant-forward plans for liver fat and glycemia (nutrition)
**Summary:** A Washington University–led randomized controlled feeding trial in *Cell Metabolism* (Aug 27, 2026; DOI: 10.1016/j.cmet.2026.07.020) assigned about 55 adults with obesity, prediabetes, and fatty liver to a ketogenic, Mediterranean, or low-fat plant-forward diet, with all food provided, targeting ~10% weight loss over roughly 4–5 months (about 42 participants in reported analyses). Muscle insulin sensitivity improved by ~50% in all groups. Liver fat fell ~67% on keto versus ~45% on the other diets; 24-hour glucose fell ~20% versus ~8%; circulating insulin fell ~74%, ~44%, and ~27%, respectively; and about 50%, 29%, and 7% no longer met prediabetes criteria. Highly supervised feeding and a modest sample limit free-living generalization.

**Links:**

- [WashU Medicine — Low-carb diet liver benefits](https://medicine.washu.edu/news/low-carb-diet-delivers-added-liver-benefits-beyond-weight-loss/)
- [NCSTI / China Science Daily — Keto cut liver fat by 67% (2026-09-02)](https://www.ncsti.gov.cn/kjdt/kjrd/yyjk_kjrd/202609/t20260902_255030.html)

**Commentary:** For obesity plus fatty liver and prediabetes, weight loss remains primary, but macronutrient mix may add liver and glucose benefits—try very-low-carb only with clinical oversight of lipids, adherence, and sustainability, not as a population-wide keto mandate from a small feeding trial.

### 2. RCT: Self-selected vegan diets cut protein intake and thigh muscle volume in older adults; resistance training partly buffers (nutrition / sarcopenia)
**Summary:** In *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* (Sept 2026 issue; DOI: 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101416), 72 healthy adults aged ≥65 years were randomized for 12 weeks to a self-selected vegan diet (VEG), habitual omnivorous diet (OMNI), or vegan diet plus resistance exercise (VEG-RE). VEG lowered protein intake by about 19–28 g/day versus baseline. Thigh muscle volume and appendicular lean mass fell in VEG (~−0.61 L; −1.15 kg), differing from near-stable OMNI and smaller losses in VEG-RE (~−0.35 L; −0.62 kg); early muscle protein synthesis rates were also lower in VEG. Self-composed vegan eating without deliberate protein planning raises muscle risk; resistance training mitigates but may not fully erase losses.

**Links:**

- [Wageningen portal — AJCN vegan muscle RCT](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/a-self-selected-vegan-diet-reduces-skeletal-muscle-mass-in-health/)
- [DOI: 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101416](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101416)

**Commentary:** Older adults shifting plant-forward should treat daily protein targets plus weekly resistance training as non-negotiable metrics—not merely deleting animal foods.

### 3. Prospective cohort: Flavoring, coloring, and sweetener markers of UPFs show dose-linked incident depression risk (nutrition / mental health)
**Summary:** *European Journal of Nutrition* (2026; DOI: 10.1007/s00394-026-04084-7) analyzed 171,818 UK Biobank participants with ≥1 dietary recall, mean follow-up ~10.7 years, and 5,424 incident depression cases. Exposure was estimated as the share of intake from foods likely containing predefined ultra-processed markers. Among 10 marker categories, flavorings, colorings, and sweeteners showed positive dose–response links: modeled risks were about 46% higher at ~40% versus ~5% flavoring-related intake, ~49% higher at ~20% versus ~3% coloring-related intake, and ~45% higher at ~20% versus 0% sweetener-related intake. Self-report diet, probabilistic marker assignment, and possible reverse causation limit causal claims.

**Links:**

- [Eur J Nutr — 39 UPF markers and incident depression](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00394-026-04084-7)
- [News-Medical — Flavorings, colors and sweeteners (2026-09-01)](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260901/Flavorings-colors-and-sweeteners-emerge-as-UPF-markers-linked-to-depression-risk.aspx)

**Commentary:** Prefer scanning labels for recurring flavorings, colors, and non-sugar sweeteners over vague "cut UPFs" slogans; this is an observational shopping cue, not a standalone explanation or treatment for depression.

### 4. Mouse mechanism: Small-intestinal fructose catabolism may boost fat absorption via microbiome and lacteal remodeling (evidence check)
**Summary:** ScienceAlert (Sept 2, 2026) reported a University of California, Irvine mouse study in *Science Advances*: deleting intestinal KHK-C, the main fructose-metabolizing enzyme, then feeding high-dose HFCS for 12 weeks yielded less weight and fat gain with microbiome shifts. Mechanistically, fewer ileal macrophages and shortened lacteals reduced dietary fat absorption; fecal transplants partly transferred the phenotype. Human confirmation is required. Animal and mechanistic evidence only—not a license to fear whole fruit or a clinical weight-loss protocol.

**Links:**

- [ScienceAlert — Fructose may promote obesity via fat absorption (2026-09-02)](https://www.sciencealert.com/scientists-uncover-a-surprising-way-fructose-may-promote-obesity)

**Commentary:** The actionable takeaway is cutting added fructose/HFCS in drinks and ultraprocessed foods—not panicking over fruit; the story is intriguing but still mouse-level.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Systematic review/meta-analysis (acute): Exercise snacks lower postprandial glucose and insulin AUC in people with metabolic disorders (training)
**Summary:** *Diabetes, Metabolic Syndrome and Obesity* (published Sept 2, 2026) pooled 20 randomized crossover trials (21 study units; 392 participants) comparing brief "exercise snacks" that interrupt sitting versus prolonged sitting, and versus a single continuous exercise bout, in adults with metabolic disorders or high risk (including T2DM and overweight/obesity). Versus sitting, snacks reduced postprandial glucose AUC (Hedges' g ≈ −0.37) and insulin AUC (≈ −0.49) without significant triglyceride benefit; versus continuous exercise, short-term glycemic differences were not statistically significant (only four studies; equivalence unproven). Authors suggest a low-barrier complement such as ~2–5 minutes of light-to-moderate aerobic activity every ≤30 minutes.

**Links:**

- [Dove Press — Acute effects of exercise snacks meta-analysis (2026-09-02)](https://www.dovepress.com/acute-effects-of-exercise-snacks-on-glucose-and-lipid-metabolic-outcom-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-DMSO)

**Commentary:** On desk-bound days, schedule short walks or stair bouts as timed sit-breaks for post-meal glucose—complementary to, not a replacement for, weekly structured training.

### 6. Systematic review/meta-analysis: Neuromuscular training cuts ankle injury incidence and improves dynamic balance in athletes (sports medicine)
**Summary:** *Frontiers in Physiology* included 13 RCTs (1,476 participants) comparing neuromuscular training (NMT) with no training or conventional training for ankle injury and function outcomes in athletes. NMT was associated with about a 55% lower ankle injury incidence (RR 0.45, 95% CI 0.25–0.81, I²=62%), longer Star Excursion Balance Test reaches in posteromedial, posterolateral, and anterior directions, and higher FAAM-Sports scores, without significant FAAM-ADL gains. Moderate-to-high heterogeneity and athlete-specific samples apply.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Physiology — NMT for ankle injury prevention](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.1854319/full)

**Commentary:** For court and field sports, lock balance, landing control, and proprioception into the warm-up; bracing helps, but training a stable ankle is usually cheaper and more durable.

## III. Sleep & Circadian Rhythm

### 7. Cross-sectional: Breakfast seafood/EPA/DHA linked to earlier chronotype and less social jetlag in ~6,000 Japanese adults (sleep / meal timing)
**Summary:** *Journal of Nutritional Science and Vitaminology* (DOI: 10.3177/jnsv.72.272) analyzed 5,975 Japanese adults aged 18–75 (~64.9% women) using app-based diet logs and the Munich ChronoType Questionnaire. Higher breakfast seafood/EPA/DHA intake associated with an adjusted sleep midpoint ~19–21 minutes earlier and ~5–6 minutes less social jetlag; dinner links were weak, while snack-sourced EPA/DHA tracked later chronotype and greater jetlag (authors note possible confounding by evening snacking habits). Cross-sectional, self-reported diet, with industry funding disclosed—correlation, not proof that shifting breakfast changes sleep.

**Links:**

- [News-Medical — Seafood at breakfast (2026-09-01)](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260901/Seafood-at-breakfast-stood-out-in-a-study-of-nearly-6000-adults.aspx)
- [JSTAGE — Timing of seafood, EPA, DHA and sleep pattern](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jnsv/72/4/72_272/_article)

**Commentary:** If you already eat seafood, try moving long-chain omega-3 portions to breakfast or lunch as a circadian hypothesis—not a substitute for fixed sleep timing and light exposure.
