## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Small acute trial: low-sugar vegetable cold-pressed juice with white bread linked to ~10% lower peak glucose in non-obese adults (Nutrition)

**Summary:** Korean outlets reported on Aug 10, 2026 that KOFRUM cited an Inje University team led by Kwak Jeong-hyeon: 10 healthy adults drank two low-sugar cold-pressed juices (celery–cabbage–apple; celery–kale–cabbage–lemon; 240 mL each). Juice alone barely moved peak glucose vs fasting. With 100 g white bread, the higher-vegetable lemon blend vs water lowered 30-minute glucose by about 4%; among five non-obese participants, peak glucose was about 145.6 vs 162.2 mg/dL (~10% lower). Authors point to polyphenols and possible lemon inhibition of carbohydrate-digesting enzymes. Extremely small acute meal study; not tested in people with diabetes.

**Links:**

- [Herald Business — Vegetable cold-pressed juice and post-meal glucose](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10835566)
- [Sports Khan — Korean coverage of the same study](https://sports.khan.co.kr/article/202608100958003)

**Commentary:** To blunt a carb spike, swap sugary drinks for near-zero-sugar vegetable juice and add protein/nuts—do not treat juice as glucose therapy; diabetes care still follows clinical carb counting and prescribed treatment.

### 2. RCT: nine months of iron-/zinc-biofortified pearl millet showed no detectable adverse toddler gut-microbiome shifts (Nutrition)

**Summary:** *Nature Communications* published a Cornell-led randomized trial (NCT02233764): children aged 12–18 months in Mumbai urban slums (hemoglobin ≥9.0 g/dL) ate iron-/zinc-biofortified pearl millet (~8.70 mg iron/100 g) or control millet (~3 mg/100 g) daily for nine months (n=223 enrolled; paired metagenomics n≈81; endpoint n≈108). Unlike concerns raised for high-dose iron drops, FeZnPM was not linked to detectable adverse microbiome shifts; exploratory analyses suggested possible upregulation of antibiotic-like, antioxidant, and pollutant-degradation pathways. Primary outcomes remain iron status and growth; microbiome findings are secondary.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Biofortified pearl millet and toddler gut microbiota RCT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75674-6)
- [News-Medical — Biofortified millet and toddler gut health overview](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260805/Biofortified-millet-improves-toddler-gut-health-without-adverse-effects.aspx)

**Commentary:** Food-based iron strategies can be more microbiome-friendly than high-dose drops for toddlers—but individual iron therapy still needs a pediatrician guided by hemoglobin and ferritin.

### 3. Food-composition update: farmed Atlantic salmon fatty-acid profiles diverge sharply from legacy SR values (Nutrition / Evidence check)

**Summary:** *Frontiers in Nutrition* (Aug 3, 2026) compared USDA Foundation Foods individual-sample data with legacy Standard Reference composites for farmed Atlantic salmon. Total PUFA looked similar, but linoleic acid rose ~2.4-fold (0.900→2.152 g/100 g) and α-linolenic acid ~3.4-fold, while EPA/DPA/DHA fell roughly 63.8%, 58.5%, and 47.5%—consistent with plant-oil aquaculture feeds. Authors propose a concordance framework to flag food–nutrient pairs needing database review. This directly affects ω-3 intake estimates that still rely on older tables.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Nutrition — Farmed Atlantic salmon Foundation Foods vs SR fatty acids](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1870100/full)

**Commentary:** “Eat fish weekly” no longer guarantees textbook EPA/DHA—check labels or choose products that state EPA/DHA amounts rather than trusting decade-old salmon fatty-acid charts.

### 4. Nationwide cross-section: Chinese MIND (cMIND) scores associate with lower odds of functional disability in older adults (Nutrition)

**Summary:** *Aging Clinical and Experimental Research* (Aug 9, 2026) analyzed 9,511 adults ≥65 years from the 2018 CLHLS. BADL and IADL disability prevalence were about 23.74% and 57.95%. Versus the lowest cMIND quartile (Q1), Q2 had lower odds of BADL disability (OR≈0.821, 95% CI 0.676–0.999), and Q2–Q4 had lower odds of IADL disability. Cross-sectional design with subgroup heterogeneity; changing diet alone is not proven to restore independence.

**Links:**

- [Aging Clinical and Experimental Research — cMIND and BADL/IADL disability](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40520-026-03444-x)

**Commentary:** For older adults, turn MIND-style eating into a concrete list—leafy greens, berries, whole grains, limited red meat—aimed at slowing functional dependence, not as an anti-dementia miracle diet.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 5. Three cohorts over ~30 years: 90–119 weekly minutes of resistance training linked to ~13% lower all-cause mortality (Training)

**Summary:** *British Journal of Sports Medicine* reports a Harvard Chan analysis pooling the Health Professionals Follow-Up Study and Nurses’ Health Studies I/II (147,374 adults; up to ~30 years; 35,798 deaths). Versus no resistance training, 90–119 min/week was associated with all-cause mortality HR≈0.87 (95% CI 0.81–0.95), CVD mortality HR≈0.81, and neurological mortality HR≈0.73 after adjusting for aerobic activity; no extra all-cause benefit above 120 min/week. Combining adequate aerobics with resistance further lowered risk (some joint strata HR≈0.55). Observational questionnaire data with residual confounding. Widely covered around Aug 8–9, 2026.

**Links:**

- [British Journal of Sports Medicine — Long-term resistance training and cause-specific mortality](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/60/12/874)
- [Harvard T.H. Chan — Moderate weekly strength training and longevity](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/moderate-amount-of-strength-training-each-week-could-boost-longevity/)

**Commentary:** You do not need daily lifting—target about 1.5–2 hours of strength work weekly plus steady aerobic activity; that mix maps better to this long follow-up than cardio-only or endless gym volume.

### 6. Meta-analysis: SportsMetrics improves ACL-related biomechanics and function, but does not yet prove fewer ACL tears (Training / Sports medicine)

**Summary:** *BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation* (Aug 8, 2026) pooled nine studies (310 athletes, mostly female; search through May 10, 2026). SportsMetrics training (SMT) was associated with improvements in dynamic knee valgus and Landing Error Scoring System scores (SMD ≈ −1.09 and −1.08), plus agility, dynamic balance, power, and speed; core strength change was non-significant. Authors stress low-to-very-low certainty evidence, many quasi-experimental designs, and that surrogate gains are not established as reducing actual ACL injury incidence.

**Links:**

- [BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation — SportsMetrics and ACL-related outcomes meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01948-7)

**Commentary:** Keep neuromuscular warm-ups like SMT—but do not equate “better knee-valgus angles” with “half as many ACL tears” until prospective trials with injury registration confirm it.

### 7. 12-week controlled lab study: VR-assisted neuromuscular training beat usual training on agility and injury-risk markers in basketball athletes (Training)

**Summary:** *Scientific Reports* (Aug 5, 2026) randomized 70 university basketball athletes (~20.95±1.5 years) 1:1 to VR-assisted neuromuscular training or conventional training for 12 weeks. The VR group showed larger agility gains (d ≈ 0.89–1.34); neuromuscular markers improved ~15–30% more than controls, dynamic knee valgus fell ~18.7% vs 8.2%, and reactive strength index rose ~27.2% vs 12.3%. Outcomes were biomechanical and performance markers, not in-season injury counts. Equipment access and long-term adherence remain barriers.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — VR-assisted neuromuscular training in basketball athletes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-48999-x)

**Commentary:** Without VR, still prioritize landing, cutting, and single-leg stability; with VR, treat it as a high-resolution feedback tool—not a substitute for strength and warm-up basics.

### 10. Phase 3 RCT: recombinant zoster vaccine LZ901 ~91.6% efficacy over ~12 months; ~66.9% in adults ≥70 (Public health)

**Summary:** *Nature Communications* (Aug 7, 2026) reported a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial (ChiCTR2300076253) in ~26,018 adults ≥40 years receiving two doses of LZ901 or placebo 30 days apart. Confirmed herpes zoster: 15 vs 178 cases; overall vaccine efficacy ~91.6% (95% CI 86.3–95.3); ~93.9% at ages 40–69 and ~66.9% (95% CI 14.6–89.2) at ≥70. Efficacy against post-herpetic neuralgia was ~95%-range; grade 3 reactions were similarly rare in both arms. Aluminum-adjuvanted platform; head-to-head clinical efficacy vs GSK’s Shingrix needs separate interpretation; BLA under NMPA review.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — LZ901 herpes zoster vaccine phase 3 efficacy and safety](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76313-w)

**Commentary:** Adults 50+—especially older and immunocompromised people—should still complete an approved shingles vaccine; if LZ901 launches, read age-stratified label numbers, not only the headline “90%+ overall.”

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 12. Small human study: wearable ultrasound patch shortened time to REM by ~43 minutes and extended REM by ~16 minutes (Sleep)

**Summary:** *Nature Communications* describes UT Austin–led NEUSLeeP, a soft bioadhesive patch combining gentle focused ultrasound deep-brain stimulation with real-time electrophysiology. In 28 participants (healthy and insomnia subgroups; ~26 with usable PSG), STN-FUS vs sham increased REM percentage by ~4.6 points on average, cut REM latency by ~43 minutes, and lengthened REM by ~16 minutes, without significant changes in arousals or sleep efficiency. ScienceDaily covered results around Aug 7, 2026. Small sample, not commercial; PTSD/depression uses need larger trials.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Skin-attached ultrasound patch for REM enhancement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73787-6)
- [ScienceDaily — Wearable ultrasound patch and REM sleep](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260806050709.htm)

**Commentary:** For better REM and insomnia today, prioritize sleep hygiene, CBT-I, and apnea screening; the patch is a promising device path—not tonight’s over-the-counter sleep fix.

### 13. Foundation model: routine sleep studies reveal mortality and chronic-disease risk groups that AHI severity misses (Sleep)

**Summary:** *Nature Communications* (Aug 3, 2026) trained a foundation model on >10,000 clinical sleep recordings linked to electronic medical records and identified five patient risk groups with divergent mortality, cardiovascular, and neurological trajectories. The highest-risk group showed more than double the mortality risk of the lowest, while conventional apnea–hypopnea index severity categories had limited predictive value. The framework generalized to the independent Sleep Heart Health Study. Methodologic/retrospective validation; it does not yet rewrite home sleep-test workflows.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Sleep foundation model for risk stratification and outcomes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75326-9)

**Commentary:** After a sleep study, ask more than “is my AHI moderate/severe?”—bring daytime sleepiness, cardiometabolic comorbidities, and the full report to a sleep clinician.
