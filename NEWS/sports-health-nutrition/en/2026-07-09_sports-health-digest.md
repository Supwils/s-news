### 4. Balance training beats strength training for ankle sprain prevention in **1,517** recruits
**Summary:** A **Scientific Reports** study published **July 9** randomized **1,517** male military recruits to **12** weeks of balance training (**n=750**) or conventional strength training (**n=767**) alongside routine drills. Both groups improved, but at week **12** the balance group showed greater gains in dynamic balance (YBT), eyes-closed single-leg stance, self-reported ankle stability (CAIT), and zigzag runs. Ankle sprain incidence was **5.33%** (**40/750**) vs **8.87%** (**68/767**) in the strength group (**p<0.05**).

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Balance vs strength training for ankle stability (July 9, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60966-0)

**Commentary:** Runners and court-sport athletes should program proprioception and single-leg balance regularly — not only load-based lifting.

## III. Nutrition & Diet

### 9. **CALERIE 2**: **24-month** caloric restriction improves glycemic control via adiponectin–ceramide axis
**Summary:** Per **Nature Communications** on **July 9**, the CALERIE 2 RCT in non-obese adults (**BMI 22–27.9**, ages **21–50**) found **24** months of caloric restriction raised high-molecular-weight adiponectin and lowered insulin-resistance-linked ceramides (**C16:0, C18:0, C24:0**). Mediation analysis suggested ceramide reductions partly explain improved insulin secretion, sensitivity, and **IGF-1** signaling — strongest at **12** months, attenuated by **24**, implying partial metabolic adaptation.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Caloric restriction and adiponectin–ceramide axis (July 9, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74468-0)

**Commentary:** Moderate caloric restriction may work through identifiable lipid mediators, but long-term strict protocols need supervision — most people should prioritize diet quality over extreme restriction.
