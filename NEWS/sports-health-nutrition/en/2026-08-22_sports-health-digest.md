## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. Daily Tomatoes in POMOSANO Trial: ~Twice Greater CAP Liver-Fat Drop in MASLD, Without Weight Loss (Nutrition)

**Summary:** Italy's National Institute of Gastroenterology team published the exploratory POMOSANO randomized controlled trial (NCT06389851) in *Nutrients*: 79 adults aged 18–65 with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and BMI ≤30 were randomized to 200 g/day raw tomatoes plus 50 g/day tomato sauce (n=42) or a tomato-free diet (n=37) for 6 weeks while maintaining usual lifestyle. Controlled attenuation parameter (CAP), a FibroScan ultrasound surrogate for hepatic fat, fell in both arms, but the tomato group showed a median decline of about 35 dB/m versus about 18 dB/m in controls—roughly double—with no significant between-group differences in weight, BMI, body fat, waist circumference, glucose, lipids, liver enzymes, or inflammation markers. The trial was single-center, small, short, open regarding allocation, did not measure circulating lycopene separately, and CAP is not a biopsy gold standard. Seoul Economic Daily reported the study on Aug 22, 2026.  

**Links:**

- [Seoul Economic Daily — Daily Tomatoes Cut Liver Fat Without Weight Loss](https://en.sedaily.com/culture/2026/08/22/daily-tomatoes-cut-liver-fat-without-weight-loss-study-finds)
- [Nutrients — POMOSANO Randomized Controlled Trial (DOI: 10.3390/nu18152476)](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18152476)

**Commentary:** If you have fatty liver but weight is not the main lever, a daily large tomato plus modest sauce is a low-bar food trial—not a substitute for weight management or medication, and 6-week CAP shifts may not translate to long-term clinical outcomes.

### 2. Gut Microbes Convert Nitrate and Non-Heme Iron into DNICs: Blood Pressure, Glycemic, and Liver-Fat Benefits in Mice (Nutrition/Mechanism)

**Summary:** Karolinska Institutet researchers reported in *Cell* (online Aug 19, 2026) that gut bacteria convert dietary nitrate (beets, leafy greens) and non-heme iron (legumes, whole grains) into dinitrosyl iron complexes (DNICs) absorbed and distributed to liver, kidney, and other organs. DNICs were absent in germ-free mice, implicating the microbiota. In cardiovascular and metabolic disease mouse models, nitrate-plus-iron supplements or synthetic DNIC raised DNIC levels and improved blood pressure, vascular function, glycemic control, and hepatic fat accumulation; mechanisms involve Fe(NO)₂ activation of soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC), leucine uptake inhibition, and mTORC1 regulation rather than free NO alone. Human fecal and cellular data support the pathway, but human DNIC levels and interventional effects remain unvalidated.  

**Links:**

- [Karolinska Institutet — Gut bacteria convert dietary nitrate and iron into protective molecules](https://news.ki.se/gut-bacteria-convert-dietary-nitrate-and-iron-into-protective-molecules)
- [Cell — Gut microbiota generate dinitrosyl iron complexes with cardiometabolic benefits (DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.055)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.07.055)

**Commentary:** Pair nitrate-rich vegetables with plant iron sources on the same plate—nitrate alone may fail on a low-iron background; skip DNIC supplements until human evidence exists.

### 3. Isoleucine and Valine Fuel Prostate Cancer via P-CoA: Mouse Diet Cut to 25% Slows Growth and Metastasis (Nutrition/Tumor Mechanism)

**Summary:** Weill Cornell Medicine researchers reported in *Nature Metabolism* (Aug 20, 2026) that isoleucine (Ile) and valine (Val) catabolism is the primary source of intracellular propionyl-CoA (P-CoA) in prostate cancer cells (~70%+ of the P-CoA/PLC pool). P-CoA signals through SREBP2 lysine propionylation to sustain cholesterol biosynthesis, steroidogenesis, and androgen receptor (AR) signaling under metabolic and androgen-deprivation stress. Reducing dietary Ile/Val to 25% of standard intake in mice lowered serum and tumor P-CoA, inhibited growth, and reduced lung metastasis; patient tumor P-CoA-related metabolites correlated with aggressiveness. Preclinical mechanism work—human dietary intervention safety and efficacy are unproven.  

**Links:**

- [Weill Cornell — Prostate Cancer Hijacks Amino Acid Metabolism to Signal Hormone Therapy Resistance](https://obgyn.weillcornell.org/news/prostate-cancer-hijacks-amino-acid-metabolism-signal-hormone-therapy-resistance)
- [Nature Metabolism — Isoleucine and valine promote prostate cancer progression (DOI: 10.1038/s42255-026-01583-z)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-026-01583-z)

**Commentary:** This is tumor metabolism science, not a diet prescription for men or prostate cancer patients—do not sharply restrict protein on your own; any Ile/Val restriction belongs in oncology and nutrition supervision.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. L-BAIBA: Exercise-Released Metabolite Drives Muscle Adaptation via PGC1α–PPARδ; Human Data Remain Associational (Training)

**Summary:** University of Leeds–led work in *Nature Communications* identifies L-beta-aminoisobutyric acid (L-BAIBA) as the primary active enantiomer of muscle-derived BAIBA regulating metabolism, morphology, and exercise performance through PPARδ; knockdown of its biosynthetic enzyme blunts training adaptations. BAIBA mitigated muscle dysfunction in a diabetic mouse model. In humans, plasma L-BAIBA correlates with aerobic fitness and rises after single endurance bouts and training, but these are observational—not interventional—data. No approved therapy or supplement replicates exercise-induced effects.  

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — The metabokine β-aminoisobutyric acid mediates exercise performance (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-76307-8)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76307-8)
- [Medical Daily — An Exercise-Released Molecule Drives Muscle Adaptation in Mice](https://www.medicaldaily.com/l-baiba-exercise-molecule-muscle-adaptation-mice-study-477555)

**Commentary:** Another exercise-molecule story whose actionable takeaway remains consistent aerobic training—not L-BAIBA pills as a fitness shortcut.

### 5. Midlife Resistance Training: ≥2 Hours/Week Linked to ~27% Lower T2D Risk; Plus Aerobic and Less Sedentary Time ~62% (Training/Metabolic)

**Summary:** Harvard T.H. Chan–led analysis in *JAMA Network Open* (June 22, 2026) pooled 143,715 adults across Nurses' Health Study, Nurses' Health Study II, and Health Professionals Follow-up Study with at least three midlife (ages 40–60) resistance-training assessments. ≥2 hours/week of resistance training was associated with ~27% lower type 2 diabetes risk versus none; meeting aerobic recommendations and limiting sedentary TV time alongside resistance training yielded ~62% lower risk versus doing none of these. Harvard highlighted the findings on Aug 19, 2026, urging clinicians to recommend simple strength work beyond "walk more." Cohort, self-reported habits—not individualized prescription.  

**Links:**

- [Harvard T.H. Chan — Resistance training may substantially reduce type 2 diabetes risk](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/resistance-training-may-substantially-reduce-type-2-diabetes-risk/)
- [JAMA Network Open — Resistance Training and Type 2 Diabetes Risk in Midlife (journal search)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen)

**Commentary:** After midlife checkups, bundle weekly dumbbell/machine/bodyweight sessions with aerobic work and less sedentary time—the metabolic payoff tracks the study's best-adherence cluster.

### 6. College Exercise and Common Mental Disorders: Lowest Risk Around ~5–6 Hours/Week, With Dose Response (Training/Mental Health)

**Summary:** *PLOS ONE* (Aug 19, 2026) reported Norway's national SHOT2023 prospective cohort: 18,404 students reported exercise frequency, intensity, duration, and guideline adherence at baseline; common mental disorders (CMD) were assessed one year later. Frequent exercise, meeting weekly activity recommendations, and higher total weekly volume each linked to lower CMD risk with dose response—steep declines at lower activity levels, plateauing at higher volumes; lowest risk around ~5–6 hours/week in women and progressively higher volumes in men. Effects attenuated after adjusting demographics and baseline psychological distress. Self-reported activity and residual confounding limit extrapolation.  

**Links:**

- [PLOS ONE — Physical exercise and risk of common mental disorders among higher education students (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0353787)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0353787)

**Commentary:** For students, treat ~5–6 hours/week of steady moderate activity as a mental-health maintenance dose—not cram-season exercise bursts.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 7. UK 2.2 Million Pregnancies: Pre-Pregnancy Multimorbidity Raises Miscarriage Risk ~20%; Antenatal Anxiety/Depression ~Fourfold (Public Health)

**Summary:** *The Lancet Public Health* (online Aug 19, 2026) MuM-PreDiCT consortium retrospective cohort across five UK datasets (2000–2022) analyzed 2,225,701 pregnancy/birth events comparing women with multiple long-term physical or mental conditions (MLTC) versus none. MLTC was associated with miscarriage adjusted risk ratio ~1.20 (95% CI 1.18–1.22), hyperemesis/vomiting ~1.69, pre-eclampsia ~1.42, venous thromboembolism ~2.19, and antenatal anxiety/depression ~4.25 and ~4.09; risks rose with each additional condition. About one in five UK pregnant women have MLTC; condition-centric maternity pathways may be insufficient. Observational routine records; some outcomes may be under-recorded. Media followed on Aug 22, 2026.  

**Links:**

- [University of Birmingham — Risk of pregnancy complications increases for women with multiple long-term conditions](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/news/2026/risk-of-pregnancy-complications-increases-for-women-with-multiple-long-term-health-conditions)

### 9. HPV Vaccination: Ages 9–26 Linked to ~68% Lower Head/Neck Cancer Risk; Oropharyngeal Signal Stronger (~91%) (Public Health)

**Summary:** CIDRAP covered a new *International Journal of Infectious Diseases* study analyzing ~1.4 million matched US medical records from 66 healthcare organizations (vaccinations Jan 2011–Jun 2024). HPV vaccination from ages 9–26 versus controls vaccinated only for other diseases was associated with ~68% lower head and neck cancer diagnosis and ~91% lower oropharyngeal cancer; ages 27–45 showed no significant reduction, likely reflecting pre-vaccination HPV exposure. Total cases were few (~40 in HPV-vaccinated vs ~130 controls), limiting precision. Retrospective observational design—not an RCT.  

**Links:**

- [CIDRAP — People vaccinated against HPV have lower risk of head and neck cancer](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/human-papillomavirus-hpv/people-vaccinated-against-hpv-have-lower-risk-head-and-neck-cancer-study)

**Commentary:** Real-world data are reinforcing HPV vaccination for boys and young men—not only cervical cancer—catch up if you're eligible and unvaccinated.

### 10. Measles Surveillance Scale Bias: County Aggregates Masked School-Level Threshold Crossing in 2022–2023 (Public Health)

**Summary:** *Nature Medicine* (Aug 18–20, 2026) nationwide multiscale analysis integrated MMR vaccination records from >50,000 US schools, 13,000 districts, and 3,000 counties (2013–2025) across 45 states, modeling effective reproduction numbers (Re). School-level Re crossed the epidemic threshold in 2022–2023 while county aggregates stayed below it; average susceptibility rose from ~5% to ~10% post-pandemic. Misaligned district/county boundaries can push well-vaccinated counties above threshold via spillover. Authors urge school/district surveillance and intervention before imported cases ignite sustained transmission.  

**Links:**

- [Nature Medicine — County, district and community-level measles transmission in the US (DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04561-w)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04561-w)

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 11. Sleep-Drive Circuits: Median Raphe GABA/5-HT Neurons Activate During Wake; Inhibition Can Chronically Cut Sleep ~70% (Sleep)

**Summary:** *Nature* mouse whole-brain mapping and circuit manipulation compared sleep deprivation, recovery sleep, and circadian cycles, identifying the anterior medial preoptic area (aMPO) and median raphe (MR) as encoding sleep deficit. Activating deprivation-responsive cells induced longer, more intense NREM resembling recovery sleep; inhibition reduced sleep and abolished the usual post-deprivation sleep propensity increase. Co-activation of MR serotonergic and GABAergic neurons synergistically promoted sleep; co-inhibition chronically reduced NREM by nearly 70%, with most surviving mice lacking typical severe-deprivation behavioral deficits. Human translation is distant—mouse experiments.  

**Links:**

- [Nature — Wake-activated neuronal populations that regulate sleep drive (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10928-3)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10928-3)
- [University of Basel — Why do we get sleepy? How neurons control sleep drive](https://www.unibas.ch/en/News-Events/News/Uni-Research/Why-do-we-get-sleepy-sleep-drive-sleep-regulation-neuroscience.html)

**Commentary:** The "why staying awake makes you sleepy" mechanism is clearer, but your near-term lever remains avoiding chronic sleep debt—these neurons translate wake duration into sleep pressure.

### 12. Shift Work and Cognition: Basal Forebrain–Hippocampal Cholinergic Circuit Disruption; Optogenetic Activation Partially Rescues Mice (Sleep/Mental Health)

**Summary:** *Communications Biology* (Aug 20, 2026) combined human shift workers and male mouse models: shift workers showed lower Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) scores and altered cholinergic factor profiles; circadian disruption in mice impaired cognition, reduced basal forebrain cholinergic neuron excitability, and disrupted basal forebrain–hippocampal synaptic transmission. Optogenetic activation of this cholinergic projection in ChAT-IRES-Cre mice improved cognitive performance. Human sample limitations and cross-sectional design constrain causality; animal work in male mice.  

**Links:**

- [Communications Biology — Circadian disruption and basal forebrain–hippocampal cholinergic system (DOI: 10.1038/s42003-026-10787-8)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10787-8)

**Commentary:** If night/rotating shifts are eroding memory, prioritize fixed sleep windows and cognitive screening—not just "catch-up sleep"—cholinergic circuit injury may be an early, partially modifiable signal.
