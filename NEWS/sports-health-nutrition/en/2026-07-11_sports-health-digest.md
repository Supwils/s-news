## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. **Glucoraphenin** from radish greens/sprouts eases Western-diet obesity and **MASLD** in mice
**Summary:** Per **npj Science of Food** online **July 11, 2026**, a Korea Food Research Institute team fed Western diet (WD) mice **1 mg/kg** body weight **glucoraphenin (GRE)** for **8** weeks. GRE cut weight gain, adipocyte hypertrophy, serum **leptin**, liver **ALT/AST**, and lipogenic signaling (**FAS, SREBP1, mTOR**), and partly restored WD-induced gut dysbiosis and shifts in branched-chain amino acids and purine metabolites (**inosine, hypoxanthine**). This is a mouse study suggesting coordinated gut–liver axis regulation.

**Links:**

- [npj Science of Food — Glucoraphenin, Western diet and MASLD in mice (July 11, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41538-026-01000-5)

**Commentary:** Radish greens and sprouts are fine everyday vegetables, but mouse doses are not supplement prescriptions—fat loss and liver health still hinge on fewer ultra-processed foods, energy balance, and regular activity.

### 2. Cardiometabolic **trade-offs** of carb- vs fat-focused diets: network meta-analysis of **47** RCTs
**Summary:** Per **European Journal of Nutrition** **July 8, 2026**, a network meta-analysis pooled **47** randomized trials (**n=3,450**) in adults aged **35–75** with at least one cardiometabolic risk factor (**2013–2025**). Versus low-fat moderate-carb reference diets, high-fat low-carb patterns modestly lowered **BMI** (**−0.48 kg/m²**), but ketogenic diets raised **LDL** most (**+0.43 mmol/L**) while also raising **HDL**; high-carb patterns more often raised **triglycerides**. Waist, blood pressure, and glycemic markers showed limited macronutrient effects. No single macro split was universally optimal.

**Links:**

- [European Journal of Nutrition — Cardiometabolic trade-offs of carb- and fat-focused diets (July 8, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00394-026-04028-1)

**Commentary:** Before going low-carb or keto, check **LDL** and family history—triglyceride gains may come with LDL costs; most people should prioritize food quality (whole grains, legumes, unsaturated fats) over macro ratios alone.

### 3. Intermittent fasting vs daily calorie restriction: similar weight loss, less "constant monitoring" on fasting days
**Summary:** Per **Diabetes.co.uk** **July 11, 2026** and a **Clinical Nutrition** **Adelaide** University **18**-month RCT (**NCT03689608**, **209** adults with obesity, **57%** female), groups were: **3** non-consecutive days of **30%** energy between **8 a.m.–noon** then **20 h** fast (**iTRE**), daily **70%** energy restriction (**CR**), or standard care (**SC**). At **6** months, **iTRE** and **CR** lost ~**7 kg** vs ~**2 kg** for **SC**; **CR** showed greater increases in dietary restraint and disinhibition changes, with disinhibition mediating ~**15%** of weight loss—**iTRE** did not report the same ongoing calorie-counting burden.

**Links:**

- [Clinical Nutrition — Intermittent fasting vs calorie restriction RCT (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106686)
- [Diabetes.co.uk — Intermittent fasting may feel easier than calorie counting (July 11, 2026)](https://www.diabetes.co.uk/news/2026/jul/intermittent-fasting-may-feel-easier-than-constant-calorie-counting.html)

**Commentary:** If you keep failing because every meal requires math, structured fasting days may help under medical guidance—but diabetes, eating disorders, pregnancy, and other conditions need clearance first, and long-term habits still matter.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 4. UK adults **65+**: aerobic and mind-body/balance exercise best preserve daily function; moderate-frequency strength beats high frequency
**Summary:** Per **BMC Medicine** **July 10, 2026**, **28,419** person-waves from the **UK Household Longitudinal Study** (**2015–2023**) modeled activity limitations in adults **65+**. Moderate exercise frequency vs none linked to **59.1%** lower **ADL** limits (**IRR=0.409**) and **59.4%** lower **IADL** limits; aerobic and mind-body/balance activities showed the lowest predicted limitation trajectories. Strength training was frequency-dependent: moderate frequency cut **ADL** limits **62.0%**, while high frequency showed no extra benefit (**IRR=1.086**), resembling non-exercisers. Observational design—causality not established.

**Links:**

- [BMC Medicine — Exercise frequency, type and activity limitations in UK older adults (July 10, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-026-05058-6)

**Commentary:** Older adults should anchor weekly aerobic plus balance/tai-chi work; strength training every other day with good form may beat daily high-volume lifting.

### 5. Adolescent female football **HIIT**: time at **≥90% HRpeak** tracks sprint endurance and aerobic gains
**Summary:** Per **Scientific Reports** **July 11, 2026**, **15** adolescent female players (**14.9±1.6** years) completed **16** off-season **HIIT** sessions (**4** weeks, small-sided games plus repeated sprints). **30 s** all-out and **Yo-Yo IR1** improved (**d=1.88, 1.21**). Weekly **TRIMP**, time **≥90% HRpeak**, and **sRPE-TL** correlated with performance changes (**T≥90%HRpeak** vs **30 s** test **r=0.87**; **sRPE-TL** vs **Yo-Yo IR1** **ρ=0.94**). No control group—interpret cautiously.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Internal training load and HIIT in adolescent female football (July 11, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-61983-9)

**Commentary:** In short off-season windows, coaches should track high-heart-rate time plus **RPE**, not just total distance—the stimulus may live in the top intensity zone.

### 6. **8**-week **HIIT** improves myocardial work efficiency more than **MICT** in women with obesity
**Summary:** Per **International Journal of Obesity** **July 10, 2026**, **25** women with obesity (**BMI 35.0±3.2 kg/m²**) were randomized to **8** weeks of **HIIT** (**4×4 min at 85–95% HRmax**) or **MICT** (**41 min at 65–75% HRmax**). Both improved LV/LA strain; only **HIIT** significantly lowered global wasted work (**GWW, d=0.64**) and raised global work efficiency (**GWE, d=0.83**), with greater **VO₂peak**, weight, and fat loss in **HIIT**. Small sample; long-term outcomes unknown.

**Links:**

- [International Journal of Obesity — HIIT vs MICT on myocardial work in women with obesity (July 10, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41366-026-02158-4)

**Commentary:** If moderate cardio stalls weight and subclinical cardiac strain persists, structured **HIIT** after clearance may offer efficiency gains—not everyone is a candidate.

### 7. Exercise-trained gut metabolites **pipecolic acid** and **succinate** attenuate immobilization muscle atrophy in mice
**Summary:** Per **Nature Communications** **July 10, 2026**, University of Kentucky researchers transplanted cecal contents from exercise-trained female mice into immobilized recipients: exercise donors reduced atrophy. Metabolomics highlighted **pipecolic acid** and **succinate**; oral dosing attenuated atrophy and preserved function, possibly via energy and translational capacity. Female mouse preclinical work only.

**Links:**

- [Nature Communications — Exercise-associated microbial metabolites and muscle atrophy (July 10, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74852-w)

**Commentary:** Regular exercise plus dietary diversity that supports the gut still beats hoping to swallow these metabolites as "exercise in a pill."

### 8. Sniffing **90%** dark chocolate before fasted leg work: ~**18** extra reps without higher **RPE**
**Summary:** Per **Frontiers in Physiology** **July 9, 2026**, **23** moderately trained men fasted **≥10 h**, then sniffed **90%** dark chocolate, **60%** milk chocolate, or water for **30 s** between **80% 10RM** leg-extension sets to failure. Dark chocolate added ~**18** total reps vs control, milk chocolate ~**9**, with no between-group **Borg CR10** differences; dark chocolate mainly suppressed pre-exercise appetite ratings, milk chocolate raised odor pleasantness. Hormones/neural pathways not measured; young men only.

**Links:**

- [Frontiers in Physiology — Chocolate odor and fasted resistance exercise (July 9, 2026)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2026.1834757/full)
- [Frontiers — Sniffing chocolate could make leg day easier (July 9, 2026)](https://www.frontiersin.org/news/2026/07/09/sniffing-chocolate-workout-easier)

**Commentary:** Fasted lifters can try high-cocoa scent as a psychobiological cue, but it is not breakfast—and mechanisms remain unproven in women and older adults.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 9. High polygenic **type 2 diabetes** risk: **DASH/Mediterranean** patterns plus activity still partly offset genetic load
**Summary:** Per **Human Genomics** **July 8, 2026**, cohort analyses in European and African Americans linked ancestry-specific **PRS**, diet patterns (**DASH, Mediterranean, Southern**), physical activity, and incident **type 2 diabetes**. Top **PRS** tertile ORs were **1.61** (African American) and **1.24** (European American); **DASH/Mediterranean** patterns associated with lower incidence (**p<0.025**). In high **PRS** plus high metabolic burden, mid/high **DASH/Mediterranean** tiers with activity attenuated risk; low-quality Southern patterns amplified **PRS** risk. Observational— not a substitute for individualized care.

**Links:**

- [Human Genomics — PRS, diet patterns, physical activity and type 2 diabetes (July 8, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40246-025-00900-z)

**Commentary:** Family history is not destiny—quality diet plus regular activity can still lower absolute risk; fried, low-produce Southern-style patterns are especially risky with high **PRS**.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 10. Young women after sexual assault: shorter time since event linked to greater night-to-night sleep variability
**Summary:** Per **Journal of Behavioral Medicine** **July 8, 2026**, **82** young women with **PTSD** symptoms and hazardous alcohol use completed baseline surveys plus **3** weeks of actigraphy. Less time since assault predicted greater intraindividual variability in sleep duration, efficiency, and **WASO**; sexual minority women showed lower duration variability than heterosexual women. Other predictors (**PTSD**, alcohol) were not significant. Suggests an early window to stabilize sleep rhythms after trauma.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Behavioral Medicine — Sleep variability after sexual assault (July 8, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10865-026-00695-x)

**Commentary:** If nightly sleep feels like a lottery after trauma, fixed bed/wake times and pre-sleep wind-down may help more than reflexively adding sedatives.

### 11. **SSRIs/SNRIs** associated with higher **REM sleep without atonia (RSWA)**; **TCA** monotherapy not
**Summary:** Per **Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine** **July 9, 2026**, video polysomnography in **1,474** patients with suspected parasomnia included **624** on antidepressants. Versus no meds, **SSRI** monotherapy raised **RSWA%** by **3.39%**, **SNRI** by **6.66%**, and **SSRI+SNRI** by **10.44%** (**p<0.001**); **TCA** monotherapy or **SSRI/SNRI+TCA** combos showed no consistent rise. Cross-sectional, mostly White sample—causality not proven.

**Links:**

- [Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine — Antidepressants and RSWA (July 9, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44470-026-00127-2)

**Commentary:** If you take **SSRIs/SNRIs** and have dream enactment or parasomnia symptoms, tell sleep specialists your medication list—switching classes requires individualized psychiatric trade-offs.
