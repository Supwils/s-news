## I. Nutrition & Diet

### 1. NUTRITION 2026: Unsweetened sparkling water raises oral acidity less than sugary soda (small crossover pilot)

**Summary:** Purdue graduate student Wajiha Zulfiqar and colleagues presented at NUTRITION 2026 on Jul 26 (ScienceDaily coverage dated Jul 27) a preliminary crossover study in 20 adults who drank sugar-sweetened soda, plain unsweetened sparkling water, calcium-fortified sparkling water, and still water while salivary pH was tracked. Enamel demineralization risk rises when oral pH falls below about 5.5. Soda produced significantly lower salivary pH than water at both 2 and 20 minutes; plain sparkling water dipped at ~2 minutes and calcium-fortified sparkling water at ~5 minutes, with both near baseline by 20 minutes and short-term pH remaining above the enamel-damage threshold. Conference abstract; only acute salivary pH was measured—real-world erosion also depends on drinking frequency, contact time, and sugar content.

**Links:**

- [ScienceDaily — What sparkling water really does to your teeth](http://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260727012126.htm)
- [EurekAlert! / ASN — Could sparkling water be a better choice for your teeth?](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1136424)

**Commentary:** If you want soda fizz without the sugar hit, unsweetened sparkling water is a safer swap—still sip in moderation, don't swish for long, and keep oral hygiene consistent.

### 2. INSPIRE trial: Six weeks of inulin eased knee osteoarthritis pain; grip strength and pain sensitivity also improved

**Summary:** A University of Nottingham 2×2 factorial RCT published in *Nutrients* (INSPIRE) randomized 117 community adults with knee osteoarthritis to 20 g/day inulin, digitally supported physiotherapy exercise (PSE), both, or maltodextrin placebo for six weeks. Versus placebo, inulin and PSE each reduced pain (baseline-adjusted between-group differences about −1.11 and −1.55); PSE improved timed up-and-go and sit-to-stand, while inulin improved grip strength, pressure pain thresholds, and temporal summation and raised butyrate and GLP-1; no synergistic interaction was detected. Dropout was about 3.6% with inulin versus about 21% with PSE. Short-term, single-center; does not show cartilage reversal.

**Links:**

- [Nutrients / PMC — Prebiotic supplementation with/without physiotherapy for knee OA pain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12986947/)
- [University of Nottingham — Gut health supplement relieves arthritis pain](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/news/gut-health-supplement-relieves-arthritis-pain)

**Commentary:** Treat fermentable fiber as a stickier adjunct for knee pain—not a replacement for progressive movement and physio when function is the goal.

## II. Exercise & Training Science

### 3. RCT in CrossFit athletes ≥40: IPC favored perfusion; cold compression favored perceived and neuromuscular recovery

**Summary:** A randomized trial published Jul 15 in *Scientific Reports* assigned 60 recreational CrossFit athletes aged ≥40 years 1:1:1 to ischemic preconditioning (IPC), cold compression (CC), or passive control after plyometric fatigue, with assessments through 14 days. At day 14, resting superficial cutaneous/subcutaneous perfusion was higher versus control by about 1.94 PU with IPC and 0.88 PU with CC; creatine kinase at 72 h was lower with both IPC and CC; CC better improved reactive strength index at 72 h and Total Quality Recovery at 14 days. Authors stress secondary endpoints as supportive/hypothesis-generating and caution against reading results as proof of endothelial function or deep muscle perfusion gains.

**Links:**

- [Scientific Reports — Differential effects of IPC and cold compression on recovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60885-0)

**Commentary:** For midlife high-intensity training, pick the recovery tool for the outcome you care about—cold compression for feel and bounce-back power signals, IPC for the clearer perfusion readout—while sleep and load management still do the heavy lifting.

### 4. Eight weeks of resistance training in sedentary adults: diversity stable overall; high strength responders showed larger microbiome shifts

**Summary:** *Sports Medicine - Open* reports a University of Tübingen–led longitudinal intervention: 150 sedentary healthy adults (ages 24–61) completed eight weeks of supervised resistance training (~2–3 sessions/week) with serial stool 16S and metabolomics. Cohort-level diversity and community composition did not change consistently, yet within-person community shifts correlated with strength gains (r≈0.167); participants with ≥33% strength gains showed stronger beta-diversity shifts than those with ≤12.2% gains, and high responders had 27 differentially abundant taxa by week 8, including enrichment of health-associated *Faecalibacterium* and *Roseburia hominis*. Observational association—not proof that "training good bugs" causes strength.

**Links:**

- [Sports Medicine - Open — Resistance training reshapes the gut microbiome in sedentary adults](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-026-00990-6)
- [CMFI / Uni Tübingen — Paper in a Minute](https://www.cmfi.uni-tuebingen.de/news/paper-in-a-minute-resistance-training-reshapes-the-gut-microbiome-in-a-longitudinal-8-week-intervention-in-sedentary-adults)

**Commentary:** Don't lift to chase a microbiome score—chase measurable strength; any gut shifts look more like a byproduct of real adaptation than a reason to stack probiotics.

## III. Public Health & Disease Prevention

### 5. Swapping gas for induction stoves: poorly controlled asthma improved on a medication-trial scale (before–after)

**Summary:** Case Western Reserve / MetroHealth investigators reported in *Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology: In Practice* replacing gas stoves with induction units at no cost for 85 adults and children with poorly controlled asthma (ACQ >1.5). ACQ fell from about 2.6 to 1.5 (difference −1.1); past-4-week ED/hospitalization rates fell from 11.8% to 3.5%; indoor NO₂ fell from about 21.0 to 6.3 ppb (~70%); missed work/school also dropped. The Conversation and Euronews (Jul 27 coverage) note no concurrent gas-stove control arm, modest sample/follow-up, and that stove change is not a cure or a license to stop prescribed meds.

**Links:**

- [The Conversation — Replacing gas stoves with electric stoves greatly reduces asthma symptoms](https://theconversation.com/replacing-gas-stoves-with-electric-stoves-greatly-reduces-asthma-symptoms-287753)
- [DOI — Asthma Control Before and After Changing Gas Stoves to Electric Stoves](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaip.2026.06.039)

**Commentary:** If asthma stays poorly controlled and you cook on gas, ask your clinician about the stove as an indoor NO₂ source—exposure reduction complements, never replaces, controller therapy.

### 7. Nationwide French cohort: nirsevimab linked to ~36% lower odds of invasive pneumococcal disease hospitalization

**Summary:** An observational cohort in *The Lancet Infectious Diseases* (around Jul 21; covered by CIDRAP) included about 528,000 infants born in France Feb 6, 2023–Jan 31, 2024; ~119,000 (~23%) received RSV monoclonal antibody nirsevimab. Within six months, invasive pneumococcal disease occurred in 17 immunized infants (~14.2/100,000) versus 95 non-immunized (~23.3/100,000); after inverse-probability weighting, odds of IPD hospitalization were about 36% lower at six months (OR ~0.64) and ~34% lower at nine months. Observational design with relatively few events; needs replication for other RSV products and settings; does not replace pneumococcal conjugate vaccination.

**Links:**

- [CIDRAP — One immunization may protect newborns against 2 serious diseases](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/respiratory-syncytial-virus-rsv/one-immunization-may-protect-newborns-against-2-serious-diseases)
- [DOI — Effectiveness of nirsevimab immunisation on invasive pneumococcal disease](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00253-7)

**Commentary:** When eligible, complete local RSV prevention and pneumococcal vaccination as complementary layers—not an either/or choice based on this secondary signal alone.

## IV. Sleep & Mental Health

### 8. SJTU–Stanford fly study: sleep deprivation drives gut–brain ROS via dopamine-linked mitochondrial reverse electron transport

**Summary:** Per a Jul 22 Shanghai Jiao Tong University release, Yong Ping (Bio-X Institute) and Bingwei Lu (Stanford) report in *PNAS* that mechanical or thermogenetic sleep deprivation first triggers ROS in anterior midgut-resident immune cells via upregulated dopamine synthesis (TH); dopamine modifies complex I subunit NDUFV1 to promote reverse electron transport (RET) and mitochondrial ROS. Acute RET ROS can activate antimicrobial immunity, but sustained deprivation recruits peripheral immune cells to the CNS, downregulates NLaz (an APOE/D homolog), and worsens neurodegenerative phenotypes; blocking RET or immune-cell dopamine synthesis limits cross-tissue ROS spread and extends survival. Animal mechanism work—not a human supplement or drug prescription.

**Links:**

- [Shanghai Jiao Tong University News — Sleep deprivation, RET, and gut–brain oxidative stress](https://news.sjtu.edu.cn/jdzh/20260722/224912.html)

**Commentary:** The mechanism story reinforces why chronic sleep loss is biologically costly—until human interventions exist, the actionable step remains protecting sleep duration and regularity, not DIY RET blockers.

### 9. Single-arm outpatient study: five-session modular CBT-I–based care improved sleep and psychiatric symptom severity

**Summary:** Seoul National University Hospital investigators published online Jun 30, 2026 in *Chronobiology in Medicine* a single-arm study of 56 psychiatric outpatients (mean age ~39) with insomnia or related sleep disturbance who completed five sessions (sleep hygiene in sessions 1–2; modular CBT-I–based care tailored to the primary sleep problem in sessions 3–5). Post-intervention gains spanned insomnia severity, sleep quality, fatigue, anxiety, depression, and HRQoL (all p<0.001), largely sustained at 3 months; sleep diaries showed higher sleep efficiency and lower WASO, nap time, and snoozing. No randomized control and incomplete follow-up limit causal claims; signals feasibility and preliminary benefit.

**Links:**

- [Chronobiology in Medicine — Transdiagnostic modular sleep intervention in psychiatric outpatients](https://www.chronobiologyinmedicine.org/journal/view.php?number=239)

**Commentary:** When psychiatric illness and insomnia travel together, structured behavioral sleep care is a guideline-aligned adjunct—seek clinician-guided CBT-I rather than stacking hypnotics alone.
