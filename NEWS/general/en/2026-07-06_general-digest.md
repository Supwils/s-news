### 6. Supreme Court Lifts Party–Candidate Coordinated Spending Caps, Reshaping Midterm Finance
**Summary:** Per Reuters (July 6), POLITICO, and CRBC News, the U.S. Supreme Court last week ruled 6–3 to strike down federal limits on coordinated spending between national party committees and individual campaigns, finding such caps violate the First Amendment. The decision immediately affects the 2026 midterms: national party committees may accept $44,300 per year in donations versus $3,500 per cycle for candidates; with coordination limits removed, parties can deploy far larger sums directly on advertising and other expenses aligned with candidates. Reuters analysis on July 6 suggests this could erode Democratic cash advantages in key Senate races—e.g., Georgia Democrat Jon Ossoff has raised $81 million with $33 million on hand, roughly $30 million more than his Republican opponent, but Republican national committees hold larger reserves. Whether parties can access candidates' discounted TV ad rates remains uncertain under FCC rules. Justice Brett Kavanaugh's majority opinion called it a level playing field; Justice Elena Kagan's dissent said the majority bypassed Congress to rewrite campaign finance rules.
