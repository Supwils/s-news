### 2. Southern Spain Wildfire Kills at Least 12, Leaves 23 Missing
**Summary:** According to the BBC on July 11, a wildfire in Almería province in southeastern Spain, fueled by sustained heat around 40°C (104°F), has killed at least 12 people and left 23 missing as hundreds of firefighters battle flames around the village of Bedar. Local officials said four of the dead were British nationals and that most or all victims may be foreign; some who died did not follow recommended evacuation routes. Southern Europe has faced severe heatwaves this summer, with major wildfires also in France and Portugal; the Copernicus climate service notes Europe is warming about twice as fast as the global average, raising extreme-heat and wildfire risk.

### 4. Ebola Outbreak in DRC Spreads as U.S. Citizen Working There Tests Positive
**Summary:** According to CNN and CBS on July 10–11, the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention confirmed that a U.S. citizen working for a humanitarian organization in the Democratic Republic of the Congo tested positive for the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola. CDC entry restrictions for travelers from Congo, Uganda, and South Sudan have been in place since May.

## II. U.S. Politics and Economy

### 5. Bipartisan Housing Bill Becomes Law Automatically Without Trump's Signature
**Summary:** According to CBS, CNN, and PBS on July 11, the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act—passed by wide bipartisan margins in both chambers—became law automatically at midnight on July 11 without the president's signature. Trump posted on Truth Social on July 10 that he would not sign it in protest of the Senate's failure to pass his SAVE America Act election reform bill (requiring proof of citizenship for voter registration and photo ID at the polls), calling the housing measure a "minor" issue; he did not veto it. Speaker Mike Johnson transmitted the bill to the White House on June 29, starting the Constitution's 10-day (Sundays excluded) review window. The law aims to expand housing supply, limit large institutional investors from acquiring single-family homes, and includes an FHA small-dollar mortgage pilot and higher FHA multifamily loan limits; the Senate passed it 85–5 and the House 358–32.

The report newly analyzes M2 money supply, noting about 4.7% year-over-year growth in the first five months of 2026, returning to typical 2010s pace. June FOMC minutes show growing concern about persistent inflation, with markets pricing a higher probability of at least one rate hike by year-end.

## III. China's Policy and Economy

### 7. State Council Deploys Plan to Cultivate Six Emerging Pillar Industries, Advances Digital China
**Summary:** According to Xinhua, China Economic Net, and Sina Finance on July 11, a State Council executive meeting on July 10 studied work on cultivating emerging pillar industries. It identified integrated circuits, aerospace, biomedicine, low-altitude economy, new energy storage, and intelligent robots as six emerging pillars—related output neared 6 trillion yuan in 2025 and could exceed 10 trillion yuan by 2030. Key directives include full-chain scaled development, stronger basic research and key hardware/software breakthroughs, multi-technology-route layout, and application-driven iteration; enhanced factor support, optimized regulation, and locally differentiated, non-duplicative development. The same meeting reviewed Digital China progress, calling for moderately ahead-of-schedule digital infrastructure, faster next-generation communications and computing networks, and stronger assessment and control of new security risks.

**Summary:** According to National Bureau of Statistics data and official interpretation released July 9, June 2026 CPI rose 1.0% year-on-year (down 0.2 percentage points from May) and fell 0.3% month-on-month; PPI rose 4.1% year-on-year (up 0.2 points from May) and fell 0.3% month-on-month. The CPI-PPI growth gap has widened for four consecutive months since February, showing production-side costs are not effectively passing through to consumers amid a persistent "strong supply, weak demand" pattern. Institutions expect that if Middle East tensions push oil prices up again, July PPI may rebound while CPI could stay relatively low.
