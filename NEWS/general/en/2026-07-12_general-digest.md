**Summary:** According to the AP, Al Jazeera, and CNN on July 12, U.S. Central Command said Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps "blatantly attacked" a Cyprus-flagged container ship transiting the Strait of Hormuz, causing severe engine-room damage; Oman rescued 23 crew members while one Indian national remains missing. Iran again declared the Strait of Hormuz closed; the U.S. insisted "Iran does not control the strait—traffic is flowing" and pledged to ensure freedom of navigation. Talks between Oman and Iran on strait transit had taken place just a day earlier.

### 3. Republican Senator Lindsey Graham Dies at 71 After Sudden Illness
**Summary:** According to the AP, CNN, and CBS on July 12, South Carolina's senior Republican senator Lindsey Graham died on the evening of July 11 from a "brief and sudden illness" at age 71; his office confirmed Sunday morning without disclosing a cause. Graham had just returned from a Ukraine visit on July 10; emergency responders were dispatched to his Washington residence Saturday evening after reports of chest pains. First elected in 2002 and serving four terms, Graham was one of President Trump's closest congressional allies and a prominent foreign-policy hawk on Russia, Ukraine, and Iran; on Friday he had reached agreement with the Trump administration on a Russia sanctions package. Trump called Graham "like a member of the family" and said funeral arrangements would follow. Under South Carolina law, Governor Henry McMaster will appoint an interim successor through January 3, 2027; a Republican special primary is set for August 11, with the November winner completing the six-year term.

### 4. IMF Cuts 2026 Global Growth to 3%; Fed Divisions Deepen
**Summary:** According to the Economic Times and the IMF's World Economic Outlook update released this week, the IMF lowered its 2026 global growth forecast to 3%—down from 3.5% in 2025 and from the 3.1% projected in April—citing the energy shock from the Iran war, with a rebound to 3.4% expected in 2027. The U.S. forecast held at 2.3%; the euro area was cut to 0.9% on higher energy prices. Global headline inflation was revised up to 4.7% while core inflation was broadly unchanged; the global disinflation trend since early 2024 has "stalled." June Fed minutes under Chair Kevin Warsh's first release showed deepening splits on the inflation path: about half of officials expected rate hikes by year-end while the other half favored holding or cutting; Warsh did not submit a dot-plot forecast. The federal funds target range has stayed at 3.5%–3.75% since the start of the year.

### 5. Typhoon Bavi Weakens to Tropical Storm After Zhejiang Landfall; Nearly 2.2 Million Evacuated
**Summary:** According to the AP, Al Jazeera, and France 24 on July 12, Typhoon Bavi—the strongest storm to hit China so far this year—made landfall in Yuhuan, Zhejiang, at 11:20 p.m. on July 11 with maximum winds of 40 m/s, followed by a second landfall in Yueqing, Wenzhou; by July 12 it had weakened to a tropical storm and moved northwest into Anhui. Zhejiang evacuated roughly 1.72 million to 2.2 million people; Fujian evacuated more than 180,000 and Shanghai more than 290,000; northern Beijing evacuated over 100,000 under heavy-rain alerts. The storm toppled more than 1,300 trees in Yueqing and flooded farmland; Hangzhou and Shanghai canceled hundreds to more than a thousand flights and rail services. The national weather center warned of continued heavy rain across eastern and northern China in coming days, raising flood risk in Anhui, Jiangsu, and Shandong. Bavi had earlier struck Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands as a super typhoon and brushed northern Taiwan, injuring 134 people.

**Summary:** According to IANS and multiple outlets on July 12, China's embassy in Washington said at a Friday press briefing that Beijing is "strongly dissatisfied" with U.S. trade investigations targeting dozens of economies and has lodged formal representations, reserving the right to take all necessary measures to safeguard its interests. Reports also noted Beijing and Washington continue talks on tariffs and a proposed Board of Trade mechanism.

### 9. Southern Spain Wildfire Stabilizes; 12 Dead, ~1,500 Evacuees May Return
**Summary:** According to Euronews, The Straits Times, and Phys.org on July 12, authorities in Andalusia's Almería province declared the Los Gallardos-area wildfire "stabilized and contained" after burning about 6,600–7,000 hectares. Since Saturday afternoon, weaker winds and higher humidity helped firefighters secure much of the perimeter; regional leader Juan Manuel Moreno said about 1,500 evacuees could gradually return home; the Civil Guard inspected more than 250 homes, most without major damage. The official death toll remained at 12 with eight hospitalized (four in burn units); the missing count awaits autopsies and identification, with most victims believed to be foreign nationals trapped in vehicles or while fleeing on foot. Authorities suspect a power-line break in heat above 40°C (104°F) sparked the fire Thursday. Moreno warned mid-July is only halfway through a summer of extreme fire risk.
