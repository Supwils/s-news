### 3. Last-minute U.S.–Canada talks race a Wednesday 12:01 a.m. deadline for 50% tariffs (trade)

**Summary:** President Trump’s threatened 50% tariffs on about $20 billion of Canadian goods—roughly 5% of Canada’s exports to the United States, from hockey sticks to tongue depressors—are due at 12:01 a.m. Wednesday if there is no deal. Prime Minister Mark Carney told reporters Monday the talks were “very intense and delicate” and not for public negotiation; his office said he spoke with Trump that afternoon. Canadian trade minister Dominic LeBlanc met U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer on Monday and said only that “the work is continuing.” Washington is using Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act—a Smoot-Hawley tool never used before—which allows tariffs of up to 50% with no investigation and no time limit. The levy also adds leverage for a USMCA rewrite and for U.S. asks on weapons purchases, the “Golden Dome” missile shield and critical minerals. About 72% of Canada’s goods exports went to the United States last year; AP noted the White House may hesitate to raise consumer prices ahead of November’s midterms.
