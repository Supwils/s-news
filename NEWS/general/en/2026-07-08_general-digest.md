**Summary:** According to the BBC, AP, and Al Jazeera on July 8, President Donald Trump, attending the NATO summit in Ankara, said the memorandum of understanding (MoU) signed with Iran on June 17 is "over, as far as I'm concerned," and called negotiations with Tehran "a waste of time." Trump warned of further strikes and raised the possibility of reinstating a naval blockade, targeting electricity and water plants, or "taking over" Kharg Island. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte called the U.S. action "absolutely necessary."

### 2. NATO Ankara Summit Closes: €70 Billion Pledged for Ukraine, Article 5 Reaffirmed
**Summary:** According to AP, Al Jazeera, and NATO's official site on July 8, the 36th NATO summit concluded in Ankara with leaders adopting the Ankara Summit Declaration, reaffirming their "ironclad commitment" to Article 5 of the Washington Treaty—"an attack on one is an attack on all." The declaration pledges €70 billion ($80 billion) in military equipment, assistance, and training for Ukraine in 2026, with at least equivalent support in 2027; it welcomes the EU's multi-year Ukraine Support Loan. Russia is characterized as a long-term threat to Euro-Atlantic security, and leaders announced more than $50 billion in new procurements and expanded collective manufacturing capacity. Trump told reporters the room was filled with "a lot of love" and unity, a contrast to his recent criticism of allies; Secretary General Mark Rutte said allies "warmly welcomed President Trump's leadership." During the summit, Trump also met with President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, announcing plans to lift CAATSA sanctions on Turkey and consider F-35 sales.

Warsh has signaled he wants to end forward guidance and did not submit a personal economic forecast; some officials saw his first meeting as an opportunity to revise post-meeting statement language. New York Fed data Tuesday showed one-year consumer inflation expectations rose to 3.7%, a nearly three-year high.

## III. China Policy and Economy

### 7. PBOC Q2 Meeting: Continue Moderately Accommodative Monetary Policy
**Summary:** According to Xinhua and CNA on July 8, the People's Bank of China Monetary Policy Committee held its Q2 2026 meeting on July 4, chaired by Governor Pan Gongsheng. The central bank said via WeChat that China's economy still faces challenges of "strong supply, weak demand, structural divergence, and external shocks," with an external environment that has become "more complex and volatile." It will continue moderately accommodative monetary policy, strengthen counter-cyclical and cross-cyclical adjustments, and promote stable growth and reasonable price recovery. Compared with Q1, the meeting added "structural divergence" to the challenge list and emphasized integrated effects of incremental and existing policies, with greater "proactiveness, flexibility, and targeting" on timing and intensity. The meeting also called for strengthening policy-rate guidance, lowering intermediary financing costs, keeping the yuan basically stable, and using structural tools to support domestic demand, tech innovation, and SMEs. Yicai cited analysts saying the macro outlook may remain "consolidating recovery with weak momentum" in H2.

### 10. China Buys 472,000 Tons of U.S. Soybeans in Largest Daily Sale Since November
**Summary:** According to TT News on July 8, the USDA reported China purchased 472,000 metric tons of U.S. soybeans—the largest daily export sale to China reported since November 2025—including multiple cargoes by Cofco. The purchase follows the May Trump–Xi summit in Beijing and is seen as a positive signal of both sides fulfilling trade-truce commitments. The White House says China agreed to purchase at least an additional $17 billion in agricultural products annually for 2026–2028 and at least 25 million tons of soybeans per year, though Beijing has not publicly confirmed those figures. Chinese officials say both sides are working to ease tariffs on certain farm goods and preserve last year's truce. Analysts say additional buying would support U.S. farmers ahead of the September marketing season; Chicago soybean futures have strengthened on Chinese demand expectations, though tariffs and cheaper Brazilian supplies still weigh on overall demand. USTR is collecting public comments through July 10 on a $30 billion reciprocal tariff-relief program under the proposed U.S.–China Board of Trade.
