### 2. Nepal–Tibet flood death toll passes 1,000 with thousands still missing (Disaster)
**Summary:** Cross-border flash floods after the Aug. 26 Himalayan glacier collapse have pushed the combined death toll past 1,000 as of Sept. 1: Nepal’s disaster agency reported about 987 bodies recovered and roughly 3,916 missing (including 583 foreign nationals) in a morning bulletin, while China had earlier reported 16 dead and 546 missing on its side. Kathmandu police began mass burials and asked relatives for DNA to identify victims; about 600 people remained trapped at damaged hydropower sites, and Prime Minister Balendra Shah said more than 11,000 had been rescued nationwide. Landslides, debris, and cut transport links continue to slow recovery.

## III. China Policy and Economy

### 7. Housing sales and financing rules tighten pre-sales, extend mortgages to 40 years (Property)
**Summary:** Rules issued Friday by housing, natural-resources, and financial regulators generally require developers to wait until a building’s main structure is topped out before pre-selling units, with banks holding buyer mortgages until completion conditions are met. The PBOC and financial regulator also reformed property credit rules, extending the maximum individual home-loan term from 30 to 40 years and stressing equal treatment for developers of all ownership types. China Daily and others on Sept. 1 framed the package as accelerating a new real-estate development model and stronger homebuyer protection; A-share property stocks saw a broad morning rally.

### 8. 20% tax on foreign individuals’ FIE dividends takes effect (Tax)
**Summary:** China’s finance and tax authorities said that from Sept. 1, 2026, dividends and bonuses that foreign individuals receive from foreign-invested enterprises are subject to a 20% individual income tax, ending a 1994-era exemption meant to attract investment. Officials tied the change to cleaning up preferential tax policies and building a unified national market; enterprises generally must withhold the tax, and analysts note home-country foreign tax credits may offset part of the net burden for some recipients.
