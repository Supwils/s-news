## I. Global Headlines

### 1. Magnitude 7.7 quake off Indonesia’s Flores Island; official death toll rises to at least 47 (disaster)

**Summary:** Indonesia’s National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB) said a shallow, powerful earthquake struck off Flores early Saturday, with the death toll rising from about 38–40 earlier in the day to at least 47, and about 50 people injured. The U.S. Geological Survey put the quake at magnitude 7.7, about 10 km deep, northwest of Ende in East Nusa Tenggara. A tsunami warning was lifted after roughly three hours when no significant sea-level rise was detected. BNPB said more than 150 houses were severely damaged and landslides blocked roads; the government sent about 50,000 aid packages totaling 275 tonnes. A separate quake of about magnitude 6.9 later struck Sumatra, with no immediate casualty reports.

### 5. U.S. July retail sales fall for the first time in nine months; September hike odds fade (economy)

**Summary:** The Census Bureau said Friday that advance July retail and food-services sales were $763.6 billion, down 0.6% (±0.4 percentage point) from June and up 5.0% from a year earlier—the first drop in nine months and the largest in about 14 months. ## III. China Policy and Economy

### 6. PBOC Q2 monetary-policy report: adjust tools in a timely way and step up countercyclical easing (policy)

**Summary:** The People’s Bank of China released its 2026 Q2 monetary-policy implementation report, pledging to keep a moderately accommodative stance, use existing measures fully, plan practical incremental tools, and strengthen countercyclical support for domestic demand. Language on instruments shifted from Q1’s “flexibly use various tools” to “comprehensively use and timely adjust” them, while keeping liquidity ample and financing conditions relatively easy. The report calls for refining short-end rate control so money-market rates orbit the policy rate, and for more diversified loan-pricing benchmarks. Overnight reverse-repo operations have recently covered end-June, end-July, and mid-August. Official coverage said the yuan would be kept basically stable at an adaptive, equilibrium level.

## IV. U.S.–China Relations

### 7. White House report accuses 40-plus countries of helping China dodge tariffs; Beijing says a trade war has no winners (trade)

**Summary:** The White House Office of Trade and Manufacturing Policy on Aug. 13 released “The Great Transshipment Scam,” arguing that Chinese and other exporters route goods through about 40 countries—Mexico and Malaysia among them—for limited assembly, relabeling, and paperwork changes to mask origin and avoid higher China tariffs. Adviser Peter Navarro said China is “laundering” exports. The report cites government and private estimates of $34.2 billion to $303 billion a year in transshipped goods, with a central case near $75 billion and $19–26 billion in lost U.S. tariff revenue. CBP has begun an AI pilot to flag transshipment and may apply duties retroactively for about a year if origin is falsified. China’s embassy in Washington said it opposes U.S. tariffs and pressure on firms, and that unilateral transshipment actions “must not target or harm third parties”; Global Times carried expert criticism on Aug. 15.
