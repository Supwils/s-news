## IV. U.S.–China Relations

### 9. U.S. imposes Section 232 tariffs of up to 100% on imported drones, speeding a split in the supply chain (trade)

**Summary:** The White House said Thursday that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick found the United States “too reliant” on foreign unmanned aircraft. Drones heavier than 25 kg or with certain capabilities face duties of up to 100%; smaller drones and some parts face 25%. Partners with trade deals—including the EU, Japan, Switzerland and Taiwan—may pay 15% if hardware and technology originate only there and in the United States; the United Kingdom faces 10%. Most levies take effect 21 days after the proclamation; less-sensitive components after 180 days. Bloomberg, via SWI, cited Chinese customs: drone exports to the United States were about $50 million in the first half, roughly half a year earlier, with nearly $300 million in drone and aircraft parts. After the Supreme Court limited emergency tariff powers in February, the administration used Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962.
