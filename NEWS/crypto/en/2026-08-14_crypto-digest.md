## I. Regulation & Policy

### 1. SEC cancels Friday's Regulation Crypto open meeting, with no new date (Regulation)
**Summary:** The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission canceled the open meeting set for **Friday, Aug. 14, at 10:00 a.m. ET**. The Sunshine Act notice states only that the meeting "has been cancelled" and lists no replacement. A spokesperson told reporters the session would move to a later date "due to an unforeseen scheduling issue"; as of **Aug. 14** no new date had been posted. The sole agenda item had been whether to issue a release proposing a tailored offering regime for certain investment contracts involving crypto assets (widely called Regulation Crypto) — the agency's first step toward crypto-specific rulemaking. A yes vote would only have opened public comment, not created binding rules. The cancellation follows the Senate's recess without advancing the CLARITY Act; the next procedural test is widely expected around **Sept. 15**.

### 2. Binance to restrict transactions involving HTX and 10 other platforms from Aug. 23 (Compliance)
**Summary:** Binance said on **Aug. 14** it will stop processing transactions involving **11** crypto platforms and service providers from **Aug. 23**, citing recent regulatory developments. The list includes HTX, Rapira, Aifory Pro, ABCeX, WhiteBird, NoOnecrypto INC., Tradex, Monease Ltd, BitPapa, Exnode, and EXMO. Transfers on or after the effective date may be held for compliance review, and affected wallets may be restricted. HTX (formerly Huobi) was added to the EU's Russia-related sanctions package in **July**; the U.K. previously designated Huobi Global S.A., and OFSI has treated the HTX exchange itself as in scope because of ownership. HTX has argued the U.K. designation applies only to a separate legal entity and does not affect its online exchange or user funds.

### 3. CFTC reportedly reviews "mention markets"; Kalshi pulls sports contracts (Prediction markets)
**Summary:** NPR reported late **Aug. 13**, citing two people with knowledge of the matter, and other outlets followed on **Aug. 14**, that the Commodity Futures Trading Commission is reviewing prediction-market "mention markets" — contracts on whether a person utters a given word — because outcomes may be unusually easy to manipulate. Kalshi removed sports mention markets "until further notice" while keeping political and earnings-related markets live. Neither the CFTC nor Kalshi commented to NPR, and no public enforcement action had been announced. Designated contract markets may list only contracts not readily susceptible to manipulation and must maintain surveillance. The CFTC's Innovation Advisory Committee is set to discuss crypto, AI, and prediction markets on **Aug. 20**, with written comments due by **Aug. 27**.

## IV. Stablecoins & DeFi

### 6. Tether completes first full financial-statement audit; KPMG U.S. issues an unqualified opinion (Stablecoin)
**Summary:** Tether announced on **Aug. 13**, with follow-up confirmation on **Aug. 14**, that KPMG U.S. completed a full independent audit of Tether International, S.A. de C.V.'s financial statements for the year ended **Dec. 31, 2025**, and issued an unqualified opinion. The company said reserves exceeded liabilities by **$6.814 billion**. The audit covered the balance sheet, income statement, and cash flows, including a physical count of gold bars, going beyond prior quarterly reserve attestations. A KPMG spokesperson confirmed the unqualified opinion under applicable AICPA standards. Tether said it generated more than **$10 billion** in net profit in **2025** and about **$1.5 billion** in net operating profit in the second quarter of this year. The review is the first Big Four annual-statement audit after a pledge dating to around **2017**.

## V. Protocols & Infrastructure

### 8. Ethereum Foundation plans to drop Poseidon from the L1 post-quantum path in favor of SHA/BLAKE (Protocol)
**Summary:** Ethereum Foundation researcher Justin Drake said on **Aug. 13** that the Foundation is abandoning the Poseidon hash for Layer-1 post-quantum architecture and pivoting to established hashes such as SHA or BLAKE; market and technical coverage continued on **Aug. 14**. Drake argued that binary-field SNARKs (including Binius and Flock) now let conventional hashes perform well enough inside proof circuits to replace "SNARK-friendly hashes." Related systems such as leanVM are not on mainnet; a strawmap points to a production-grade leanVM in **2027** and consensus, data, and execution-layer deployments in **2028**, still preliminary. There is not yet a formal Foundation blog post or EIP, and existing Poseidon users are not required to migrate immediately; a Poseidon cryptanalysis bounty program is slated to continue through **December 2026**.

**Links:**

- [Cointelegraph — Ethereum Foundation pivots away from Poseidon in post-quantum plan](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ethereum-foundation-drops-poseidon-post-quantum-plan)

**Commentary:** This is a research-roadmap choice, not a hard-fork notice; delivery still depends on leanVM and proof systems holding performance at mainnet scale.
