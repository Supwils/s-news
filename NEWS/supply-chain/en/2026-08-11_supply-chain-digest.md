## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. Micron says AI-driven memory crunch may last beyond 2027; U.S. investment raised to $250 billion (memory / capacity)
**Summary:** At KeyBanc’s Technology Leadership Forum, Micron Chief Business Officer Sumit Sadana said customer demand signals have strengthened further since the latest earnings report, leading Micron to expect calendar 2027 to be “even tighter than 2026,” with no visibility yet on when industry supply catches demand; constraints are expected to persist beyond 2027. He called DRAM customers’ “number one constraint,” ahead of power, real estate, data-center space, or logic wafers, and reiterated that HBM3E can displace DDR at roughly a 3:1 bit trade-off, potentially approaching 4:1 with HBM4E. Micron raised planned U.S. investment to $250 billion from $200 billion, cited a $500 million GlobalWafers raw-wafer commitment inside a broader ~$3 billion supply-chain push, and said Idaho 1 is targeted for mid-next-year and Idaho 2 for end-2028.

### 2. SpaceX confirms ~$16.8 billion first phase of Terafab in Grimes County, Texas (advanced manufacturing / AI)
**Summary:** Around Aug 10, Texas officials and multiple outlets reported SpaceX will advance its Terafab semiconductor project in Grimes County with about $16.8 billion for phase one, roughly 3,000 jobs, and about $30 million in Texas Enterprise Fund support. The campus is pitched at about 100 million square feet under one roof, combining logic, memory, and advanced packaging for SpaceX and Tesla compute needs. Up to four phases are contemplated, with a total investment range of about $55–119 billion and phase-one construction targeted for completion around 2028. Elon Musk has framed the long-term goal as on the order of 1 trillion watts of annual computing capacity—far above current global supply levels.

## II. Batteries & Critical Minerals

### 3. Samsung SDI to buy out GM’s SynerCells stake and take full ownership of Indiana plant (batteries / JV reset)
**Summary:** On Aug 11, Samsung SDI said it will acquire GM’s entire 49.99% stake in their SynerCells (also reported as Synergy Cells) joint venture, taking full ownership of the New Carlisle, Indiana plant. The JV, formed in 2024 with about $3.5 billion of combined investment and roughly 27 GWh of planned annual capacity, is still under construction. The same day the companies signed a joint pre-development agreement on next-generation prismatic EV batteries for future GM models. Samsung SDI said the site will become its first wholly owned North American battery plant and plans ESS lines there as well, after already converting three of four lines at its Kokomo StarPlus Energy JV with Stellantis to energy storage amid softer EV demand and stronger ESS orders.

### 4. BIS temporary final rule: 100% domestic sales for battery black mass and tungsten scrap from Aug 27 (recycling / export controls)
**Summary:** On Aug 6, the U.S. Commerce Department’s BIS issued a temporary final rule under a July 30 Defense Production Act presidential determination, requiring covered persons to allocate 100% of monthly sales of lithium-ion battery black mass and tungsten waste/scrap to U.S. persons unless BIS grants an adjustment or exception. The restriction takes effect Aug 27, 2026, and is expected to last at least about one year. Counsel notes U.S. refining capacity for black mass is very limited while most global refining sits in Asia, so a tight exemption posture could disrupt recycling loops; requests may be filed on a rolling basis from Aug 6, 2026 through Aug 27, 2027. The broader determination also names end-of-life rare-earth magnets and finished lithium-ion batteries, signaling possible further controls.

**Links:**

- [Beveridge & Diamond — New U.S. Restrictions on Export of Black Mass](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-u-s-restrictions-on-export-of-black-mass-from-recycling-lithium-ion-batteries-pose-major-challenges-for-battery-recycling/)

**Commentary:** “Keep the feedstock home” hits intermediate recycling first—without matching refining plants, the ban may protect ore narratives while choking the circular chain itself.

## III. Policy & Geopolitics

### 5. Treasury and SBA launch Strategic Vendor Program to fill domestic supplier gaps as FDI rises (reshoring / suppliers)
**Summary:** Reuters reported from Philadelphia on Aug 10 that Assistant Treasury Secretary Chris Pilkerton and SBA Administrator Kelly Loeffler are working with foreign investors to map U.S. manufacturing supply-chain gaps and help SMEs scale as tariffs and deregulation draw inbound factories. Pilkerton is piloting a Strategic Vendor Program to strengthen domestic vendors supporting foreign-invested plants, though vendor-selection guidance has not yet been published; Treasury is also pushing faster, clearer CFIUS reviews and a Known Investor Program for repeat investors. Loeffler said the SBA provided about $3 billion to manufacturers in 2025, including about $32 million for shipbuilders. Officials cited cases where foreign-backed plants could not find domestic suppliers for basic parts such as dry compressors.

**Links:**

### 6. China countersanctions RBA and five other U.S. entities, pressuring audits and CCC factory follow-ups (compliance / geopolitics)
**Summary:** Xinhua and other outlets reported that from Aug 5 China’s Ministry of Commerce placed the Responsible Business Alliance (RBA), Verité, and four other U.S. entities on a countermeasure list, barring organizations and individuals in China from transactions or cooperation with them for allegedly assisting Xinjiang-related U.S. sanctions. Legal and ESG commentary notes that RBA’s VAP audits are widely used in electronics supply chains, creating a direct clash between Western due-diligence mandates and Chinese legal prohibitions.

## V. Logistics & Trade

### 9. August ocean update: Trans-Pacific spots ease, but Asia hub congestion and Hormuz diversions still limit flexibility (logistics / capacity)
**Summary:** C.H. Robinson’s August 2026 ocean update says eastbound Trans-Pacific spot rates and bookings into the U.S. West Coast are easing, yet carrier network changes, container shortages, limited allocations, weather, and geopolitics continue to restrict shipper flexibility. Congestion at Asian transshipment hubs including Shanghai, Singapore, and Port Kelang is raising schedule disruption; carriers are responding with port omissions, blank sailings, and redeployments that lift rollover risk and vessel bunching. Recovery from recent weather shocks at Shanghai and Ningbo may still take several weeks. Many carriers still avoid the Strait of Hormuz, Red Sea, and Suez, continuing Cape of Good Hope diversions and related surcharges, with no clear timetable for a full return to traditional Middle East routings.

**Links:**
