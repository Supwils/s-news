## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. TSMC opens Kaohsiung Baipu supplier campus to compress packaging validation cycles (Semiconductors)
**Summary:** On Sep 2, at the 3D IC Global Summit on the eve of SEMICON Taiwan 2026, TSMC said it will build a dedicated advanced-packaging materials and equipment supplier campus in Kaohsiung's Baipu Industrial Park—aimed not at adding CoWoS wafer starts, but at shortening qualification cycles for new materials, bonding processes, and inspection tools. The roughly **3**-hectare site will house two reconfigurable cleanroom buildings, developed with Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs and the Kaohsiung city government; VP of Advanced Packaging Technology and Service Jun He said validation efficiency could improve **25%–50%**. CoWoS lead times remain about **52–78** weeks, and TrendForce still sees a roughly **20%** supply-demand gap in **2026**; meaningful relief from Chiayi, Tongluo and related capacity is more a **2027–2028** story—Baipu tackles iteration distance, not today's starts.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** The AI-chip bottleneck has shifted from "is there cleanroom space" to "can materials qualify faster"—co-located validation is a race for the next CoWoS ramp window.

### 2. Lutnick: more chip tariffs likely, with relief for companies that build in America (Tariffs)
**Summary:** Bloomberg Law reported on Sep 2 that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told CNBC on Wednesday the Trump administration is weighing a fresh round of tariffs on imported semiconductors to pull more manufacturing into the United States, likely paired—as with pharmaceuticals—with tariff relief for firms that invest in domestic plants. "If you build in America, we will give you tariff relief, and if you don't build in America, you will pay tariffs," he said. TechTimes and others note Phase 2 discussions could broaden duties from advanced chips into finished goods such as laptops, gaming consoles, and AI servers, colliding with the Advanced Manufacturing Investment Credit construction-start deadline of **Dec 31, 2026**—leaving planners stuck between costlier imports and uncertain domestic incentives.

### 4. ACMA: China's rare-earth magnet curbs exposed India's EV supply-chain vulnerability (Rare earths)
**Summary:** Fortune India on Sep 2 cited a new Automotive Component Manufacturers Association of India (ACMA) report saying China's licensing rules on rare-earth magnets and other critical minerals disrupted Indian EV motor lines in **2025**, with supplies easing only around September **2025**. China accounts for about **90%** of global rare-earth processing, while India sources nearly **85%** of its rare-earth magnets from China; Chinese magnet exports fell about **74%** year on year in May **2025**, leaving some makers with only weeks of stock and one leading OEM cutting planned output for a new EV by roughly two-thirds. Battery risk is layered on top: graphite, gallium, germanium, and antimony face licensing regimes, gallium prices hit about **2.5x** pre-control levels in **2025**, and India imports nearly all lithium-ion cells.

**Links:**

- [Fortune India — China’s curb on rare-earth minerals puts India’s EV ambitions to the test: ACMA](https://www.fortuneindia.com/auto/chinas-curb-on-rare-earth-minerals-puts-indias-electric-vehicle-ambitions-to-the-test-in-2025-acma/157099)

**Commentary:** India's "third-largest auto market" story does not clear upstream licensing—magnets and graphite remain the hard gates to EV localization.

### 6. Honda internal documents: more than $9 billion in cuts, suppliers pressed on price and China-sourced parts (Autos)
**Summary:** Reuters reported exclusively on Sep 2 that Honda aims to save about **¥1.5 trillion** (roughly **US$9.4 billion**) by **2030** and has instructed suppliers to cut prices sharply. Managers briefed major suppliers in Utsunomiya this spring, then issued company-specific targets seeking about **30%** cost reductions in pressed and forged parts, electrical components, and software-defined vehicle (SDV) parts; tier-ones were urged to use more standardized lower-tier parts and expand Chinese-made content, while Honda itself said it would source more from Chinese suppliers. The push comes as BYD and other Chinese EV makers take share in Southeast Asia and beyond, forcing Japanese OEMs to compete on cost and hybrid strategy.

### 7. Europe's rare-earth countdown: China's second-wave export-control suspension runs to Nov 10 (Geopolitics)
**Summary:** European Parliament research and EU media note that China's October **2025** second wave of rare-earth export controls—covering additional elements, processing equipment and technology, plus extraterritorial licensing for foreign-made magnets with as little as **0.1%** Chinese-origin REE content—remains suspended only until **Nov 10, 2026** after the Busan truce; suspension is not repeal. EU RESourceEU funding and strategic projects are underway, but near-term substitute capacity is limited, while April **2025** case-by-case licensing for medium and heavy rare earths never paused, leaving European buyers with approval delays and price premia. With roughly **10** weeks to the restart window, auto, wind, and defence buyers are treating the date as a hard procurement risk.
