## I. Chips & Critical Materials

### 1. AI Server Demand Drives MLCC Distributor Inventories to Record Lows; Unit Price Index Up ~13% YoY (Passives)
**Summary:** South China Morning Post reported on Aug 26 that UBS Evidence Lab data show multilayer ceramic capacitor (MLCC) inventories — often called "the rice of the electronics industry" — continuing to fall at distributors worldwide. By Aug 9, inventory volume dropped another ~8% over four weeks while average unit prices kept climbing and total inventory value rose ~10%. Year-on-year data through end-July showed a ~13% rise in the unit price index, a ~22% plunge in distributor volumes, and a ~6% increase in inventory value. Murata Manufacturing and Samsung Electro-Mechanics saw volume declines of ~8% and ~20%, respectively, over the same period. UBS analysts believe tightness may start in AI-related and distributor channels before spreading across the broader market, with some viewing it as potentially the longest demand cycle in the segment's history.

**Commentary:** After memory and optical modules, MLCCs are entering "volume down, price up" mode — AI infrastructure is pulling the most basic passives into the shortage list.

### 2. South Korea DRAM Export Unit Prices Surge ~401% YoY in August; SK Hynix Warns 2027 Could Be the Tightest Supply Year on Record (Memory)
**Summary:** According to trade data cited by South Korea's Chosun Ilbo, South Korea's DRAM export unit price from Aug 1–20 reached ~$92,183 per kilogram, up ~401% year-on-year and roughly 12.5 times the low in January 2023, as AI-driven HBM expansion squeezes standard DRAM supply. Goldman Sachs expects the DRAM supply gap to widen from ~5.0% this year to ~5.9% next year. TrendForce projects that by the end of next year, HBM wafer input at Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron will account for ~30% of total DRAM wafer input but only ~13% of actual DRAM bit supply. SK Hynix has said that from the supply side, 2027 could become the most severe shortage year in memory industry history; meaningful new capacity is unlikely until after 2028.

**Summary:** Seoul Economic Daily reported on Aug 26 that Samsung Electronics is advancing both foundry and memory within Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin AI platform. The foundry business, long in the red, may be nearing a turning point.

## II. Capacity & Key Industries

### 5. Pentagon Locks In $1.55B Structure with $750M Bet on Brazil's Serra Verde Rare Earth Offtake (Rare Earths)
**Summary:** Metal Tech News reported on Aug 26 that the U.S. Department of War is investing $750 million in US SIIE LLC through its Industrial Base Analysis and Sustainment program as the largest single component of a $1.55 billion purchasing and capitalization structure around Serra Verde Group's Pela Ema rare earth mine and processing project in Brazil. US SIIE signed a 15-year offtake in April covering 100% of neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium, and terbium in phase-one output, with floor prices (~$2,050/kg terbium, ~$575/kg dysprosium, ~$110/kg Nd+Pr) rising ~2% annually. The Defense Logistics Agency also committed to purchase at least $300 million from US SIIE over five years; a major bank proposed up to $500 million in credit. Pela Ema targets ~4,000 metric tons of rare earth oxide equivalent annually by end-2026, with first deliveries expected in early Q4 2026.

### 6. Electra Finalizes C$17.5M Ontario Funding for Cobalt Refinery, Targets Q4 2027 Commercial Production (Battery Materials)
**Summary:** Electra Battery Materials announced on Aug 26 that it finalized definitive agreements with Invest Ontario for C$17.5 million in previously announced funding to build its battery-grade cobalt sulfate refinery in Temiskaming Shores — described as North America's first such facility. Structural steel, concrete, and equipment installation are underway; partial commissioning is targeted in Q4 2026, mechanical completion in Q2 2027, full commissioning and ramp-up in Q3 2027, and commercial production in Q4 2027. Design capacity is ~5,120 tonnes of contained cobalt annually (crystallizer nameplate up to ~6,500 tonnes). An extended Glencore supply agreement through 2031 locks in ~10,000 tonnes of cobalt feedstock, covering 100% of commissioning and ramp-up needs.

### 9. Canada Officially Announces ~$20B Retaliatory Tariffs on ~700 U.S. Products Effective Sept 8 (North America Trade)
**Summary:** Nikkei Asia on Aug 26 cited Reuters reporting that Canada unveiled counter-tariffs effective Sept 8 at 15%, 25%, and 50% across ~700 U.S. product lines, covering ~$20 billion in annual imports (~4.5% of Canada's U.S. imports in 2024 trade data), matching dollar-for-dollar and rate-for-rate the U.S. Section 338 50% duties that took effect over the weekend. Ottawa rolled out a ~C$7.5 billion business and worker support package, including interest-free loans with up to 36 months before repayment.

## IV. Logistics & Geopolitical Disruption

### 12. Up to ~70 Ships Queue at Sulina Canal; Ukraine Grain Traffic Limited to 2–3 Vessels per Day (Shipping / Grain)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on Aug 26 that traders and analysts estimate up to ~70 vessels waiting near the Danube's Sulina Canal to load grain at Ukrainian ports. Pilot shortages, higher-priority cargoes such as fuel, and frequent air-raid alerts have cut throughput to only ~2–3 vessels per day bound for Ukrainian ports. After Russian attacks blocked Black Sea ports that once handled ~90% of grain exports, some flows shifted to Danube river ports with far lower capacity. ASAP Agri said >50 vessels were waiting on Aug 25 while only ~5–7 per day moved toward Ukrainian Danube ports. Avalon Shipping said each day of delay can add ~$8,000 in carrier costs. Ukraine exported ~539,000 tonnes of grain Aug 1–21 versus ~1.73 million tonnes in the same period last year.
