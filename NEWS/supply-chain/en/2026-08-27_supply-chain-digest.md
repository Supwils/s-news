### 1. Nvidia says memory bottleneck lasts into early 2028 as supplier commitments jump to about $279 billion (Memory)
**Summary:** Seoul Economic Daily reported on Aug 27 that Nvidia told its earnings call the memory shortage will persist at least through early fiscal 2028 (roughly February 2027–January 2028) and will constrain growth. Nvidia’s aggressive HBM lock-ups are spilling into shortages of standard server DRAM and enterprise SSDs.

## II. Capacity Layout & Key Industries

### 4. SK hynix breaks ground on ~$4 billion Indiana HBM advanced-packaging plant; cleanroom targeted for October 2028 (Reshoring)
**Summary:** CNBC and Asia Business Daily reported that on **Aug 27** local time, SK hynix held a groundbreaking for its first U.S. plant in West Lafayette, Indiana—about **$4 billion** focused on HBM advanced packaging and R&D, not front-end wafer fabs. DRAM will still be made in Korea and elsewhere, with some chips finished in the U.S.; the first cleanroom is slated for **October 2028**, with next-gen HBM mass production targeted for the **second half of 2029**. The project can receive up to about **$458 million** in CHIPS Act direct funding and about **1,000** direct jobs; CEO Kwak Noh-Jung said Indiana will be a key U.S. HBM production base by **2030**.

### 5. India nears up to about ₹13,000 crore incentives for battery-cell components, covering five upstream categories (Batteries)
**Summary:** Business Today and others reported on Aug 27, citing Bloomberg, that India is nearing a fresh incentive program of up to about **₹13,000 crore** (about **$1.37 billion**) for advanced battery-cell component makers, with the proposal headed to the finance ministry’s Expenditure Finance Committee. Coverage spans five critical parts: anode and cathode active materials, electrolytes, separator film, and copper foil. Under the existing ~**50 GWh** cell scheme, about **40 GWh** has been awarded, but milestones have slipped amid technology, labor, equipment, and “non-availability of upstream components.” The policy aim is to cut dependence on Chinese suppliers that dominate the global battery chain.
