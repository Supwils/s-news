## I. Policy & Regulation

### 1. **OpenAI** Proposes Giving U.S. Government a **5%** Stake, Modeled on Alaska's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Policy / U.S.)
**Summary:** Per **Financial Times**, **Axios**, and **CNN** on **July 2, 2026**, **OpenAI** CEO **Sam Altman** has floated giving the U.S. government a **5%** equity stake in preliminary talks with Trump administration officials, and has suggested similar arrangements for other leading U.S. AI labs including **Google** and **Meta**. The proposal would channel shares into a sovereign-wealth vehicle akin to the **Alaska Permanent Fund**, allowing the public to share in AI-driven economic upside; at **OpenAI**'s roughly **$852 billion** valuation from its **March** funding round, **5%** would be worth about **$42.6 billion**. **Altman** has spoken with Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessent**, Commerce Secretary **Howard Lutnick**, and Senator **Bernie Sanders**; **Sanders** has previously pushed for an independent commission to hold roughly half of major AI firms, and the two sides remain far apart. Implementation would likely require congressional action; discussions remain at a conceptual stage.
**Links:**

- [Axios — OpenAI courts Trump administration as its latest investor](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/02/openai-stake-trump-altman)
- [CNN — OpenAI in talks to give Trump administration a 5% stake in the company](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/02/business/openai-trump-stake-intl)

**Commentary:** Government equity and model-release review are converging on the same timeline—**OpenAI** is trading regulatory breathing room for a stake too small to represent the public interest, yet large enough to give the White House financial leverage over frontier model approvals.

### 2. **Google** Loses Final EU **Android** Antitrust Appeal; **€4.1 Billion** (~**$4.7B**) Fine Stands (Regulation / Europe)
**Summary:** Per **The Verge** and **FinTelegram** on **July 2, 2026**, the Court of Justice of the European Union (**CJEU**) dismissed the final appeal by **Google** and parent **Alphabet**, upholding the European Commission's **€4.125 billion** (~**$4.68 billion**) antitrust fine imposed in **2018** for abusing **Android**'s market dominance. The EU found **Google** used pre-installation of **Google Search** and **Chrome**, anti-fragmentation clauses, and revenue-sharing conditions to entrench search dominance; the nearly decade-long case becomes a landmark on platform bundling and default-setting compliance risk. **Google** still faces other major EU antitrust cases involving shopping search and ad tech.
**Links:**

- [The Verge — Google's $4.7 billion Android fine has been upheld](https://www.theverge.com/tech/960561/googles-4-7-billion-android-fine-has-been-upheld)
- [FinTelegram — Google Loses Final EU Appeal: €4.1 Billion Android Fine](https://fintelegram.com/google-loses-final-eu-appeal-e4-1-billion-android-fine-becomes-big-tech-compliance-landmark/)

**Commentary:** A decade of litigation ends, but the real compliance cost isn't the penalty—it's that the **EU** has written "ecosystem lock-in" into a reusable enforcement template, raising pressure on AI app stores and default model distribution.

### 3. **FTC** Proposes Policy on Deceptive **AI** "Steering"; Public Comment Open Through **July 31** (Regulation / U.S.)
**Summary:** Per **Reed Smith** and **GovInfoSecurity** on **July 1–2, 2026**, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (**FTC**) issued a proposed policy statement that would apply Section 5 of the FTC Act to AI companies that manipulate model outputs away from users' reasonable expectations of "truthful and accurate" results without adequate disclosure. Chairman **Andrew Ferguson** said the move responds to concerns about models being used for "ideological" purposes; the statement also signals implied preemption conflicts with state AI laws such as Colorado's. Public comments may be submitted via **Regulations.gov** through **July 31, 2026**.
**Links:**

- [Reed Smith — FTC proposes policy prohibiting deceptive steering of AI products](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102n7u7/ftc-proposes-policy-prohibiting-deceptive-steering-of-ai-products-and-services/)
- [GovInfoSecurity — US FTC Eyes AI Model Behavior in New Policy Push](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/us-ftc-eyes-ai-model-behavior-in-new-policy-push-a-32135)

**Commentary:** Washington's first federal framing of "whether model outputs are secretly steered" under consumer protection—compliance focus shifts from data privacy to reasonable user expectations and disclosure obligations, with state-federal enforcement conflicts about to surface.
