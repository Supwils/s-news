## II. Models, Security & Leadership

### 2. “Stolen Thoughts” research decrypts encrypted reasoning traces; public logs leak keys and passwords (Security)
**Summary:** A team of eight researchers from MATS, ELLIS Tübingen, MPI-IS, Snyk, and others published “Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs” (arXiv 2608.09867, submitted August 10) and launched stolen-thoughts.com. They show Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and others return chain-of-thought as encrypted blocks to clients—and those blocks are portable across sessions, users, and even weaker vs. stronger models within the same provider. Attackers inject a strong model’s encrypted trace into a weak model and jailbreak it to transcribe the frontier model’s full reasoning in plaintext, for roughly two API calls. From 6,708 public agent trajectories on GitHub and Hugging Face they decoded 315,320 blocks and recovered 704 privacy artifacts, including 62 API keys and 33 passwords; they also found instances where Kimi K3 reasoning closely resembles Claude Opus 4.8. GIGAZINE and The Decoder followed on August 12.

**Links:**

- [arXiv — Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09867v1)
- [Stolen Thoughts — Project site](https://stolen-thoughts.com/)
- [The Decoder — Leaked passwords in ChatGPT's hidden reasoning](https://the-decoder.com/but-marinade-and-leaked-passwords-are-what-researchers-found-in-chatgpts-hidden-reasoning/)

**Commentary:** Hidden reasoning was meant to block distillation—portable client-side ciphertext became a reasoning side channel, and public agent logs are now part of the attack surface.
