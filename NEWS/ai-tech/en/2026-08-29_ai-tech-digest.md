### 1. OpenAI moves to cut model supply to SpaceX-owned Cursor; Musk replies he “couldn’t care less” (Product)
**Summary:** On Aug 28, OpenAI said it notified SpaceX that it intends to wind down supplying OpenAI models to the coding tool Cursor, with a proposed shutoff date of Nov 12, 2026—the maximum notice its contract allows. The company said it cannot be confident SpaceX will use its technology within its terms of service, citing past contract breaches by Elon Musk–linked firms including X/Twitter and xAI. The move follows SpaceX’s roughly $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor parent Anysphere earlier this month, which triggered a change-of-control clause; forthcoming model Astra will not be provided to Cursor. On Aug 29 Musk replied on X that he “couldn’t care less,” while Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown said the firm would keep increasing compute for Claude models inside Cursor.

## III. Policy, Chips & Geopolitics

### 4. China’s CXMT sues the Pentagon to overturn its “Chinese military company” listing (Policy)
**Summary:** Reuters and others reported on Aug 28–29 that ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China’s top DRAM maker, sued the U.S. Department of Defense and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth in the District of Columbia, seeking to reverse its designation as a Chinese military company. CXMT says it designs, makes, and sells DRAM only for civilian and commercial use, calling the listing arbitrary, unsupported by evidence, and a due-process violation. The complaint says CXMT was first listed in January 2025, briefly saw a February 2026 removal notice that was withdrawn the same day, then was kept on the June update without adequate explanation. The Pentagon declined to comment on pending litigation; the list can restrict government contracting and damage reputation.
