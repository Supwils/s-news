## I. Policy & Regulation

### 1. EU designates ChatGPT a “Very Large Online Search Engine”; Reddit and Roblox elevated in parallel (Policy)
**Summary:** On Aug 31 the European Commission designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine (VLOSE) and Reddit and Roblox Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) under the Digital Services Act (DSA). All three declared at least about 45 million average monthly users in the EU, crossing the threshold for direct Commission oversight. After notification they have about four months (multiple authoritative outlets point to end-December 2026) to assess and mitigate systemic risks covering illegal content, minors, users’ physical and mental well-being, fundamental rights, elections and public security. The Commission treats ChatGPT as a “hybrid service” that answers prompts and can search the web—hence search-engine rules, not a generic chatbot bucket—the first generative AI chatbot in this top DSA tier. Breaches can draw fines of up to about 6% of global annual turnover; the strictest DSA list now covers about 28 services.

- [The Verge — ChatGPT to face tougher regulation in the EU](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/986682/openai-chatgpt-eu-dsa)

**Commentary:** Brussels just fitted conversational AI into the DSA’s search-engine bucket—answer-synthesis services will face infrastructure-grade accountability once they clear the user threshold.

### 2. China’s MIIT launches “AI application service provider” cultivation drive with 2,000/3,000 targets (China policy)
**Summary:** On Aug 31 the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) issued a notice launching a national special action to cultivate artificial-intelligence application service providers—the first national policy package centered on that delivery layer. Goals include building a provider resource pool and raising innovation, integration/delivery and safety-compliance capacity: more than 2,000 providers in the pool by end-2026 and at least 3,000 by end-2027. Priorities include packaging “small, fast, light, precise” solution kits, first-buy / risk-compensation mechanisms, compute vouchers, frontline deployment engineer (FDE) teams on site, and embedding cyber, data and ethics compliance end-to-end. The move implements the State Council’s “AI+” call to nurture application service providers and service chains, shifting the bottleneck from “having a model” to “who can deliver and who dares to buy.”

## II. Government Adoption & Big-Tech Transitions

### 3. Pentagon adds ChatGPT Mil to GenAI.mil for unclassified work across 3M+ personnel (U.S. · Product)
**Summary:** On Aug 31 the U.S. Defense Department launched OpenAI’s ChatGPT Mil on its internal generative-AI portal GenAI.mil for military and civilian work on controlled unclassified information (CUI). Coverage says the deployment is accredited to Impact Level 5 cloud security standards and aimed at document-heavy unclassified tasks—planning, policy, logistics and administration—with design scale for more than 3 million Department personnel. GenAI.mil began with Google Gemini and is expanding into a multi-model shelf; officials and OpenAI stress this is an enterprise experience in a secure environment, not classified combat AI. OpenAI’s government sales lead said the integration pairs powerful tools with safeguards, human judgment and accountability for high-stakes decisions.

## III. Chips, Compute & Infrastructure

### 5. Nvidia puts ~$3.5B into MediaTek convertibles to pipe custom XPUs into NVLink racks (Funding · Chips)
**Summary:** On Aug 31 Nvidia and MediaTek deepened their partnership: MediaTek will adopt the NVIDIA NVLink Fusion platform so hyperscalers and frontier labs can design custom XPUs that plug into Nvidia’s rack-scale AI factories; Nvidia is also investing about $3.5 billion in MediaTek convertible bonds. The collaboration spans cloud AI infrastructure, local AI computing (continued RTX Spark / DGX Spark work) and software-defined vehicles. The backdrop is Amazon, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic and others building custom silicon to reduce GPU dependence; the deal is widely read as Nvidia ceding some custom-silicon share while locking the interconnect and rack standard—and it revived investor debate over “circular financing.”

### 6. EuroHPC signs ~€387.8M LUMI-AI deal with Bull for Kajaani, Finland (Europe · Infrastructure)
**Summary:** On Aug 31 the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking signed a procurement contract with French vendor Bull to deploy the AI-oriented LUMI-AI supercomputer at CSC’s data center in Kajaani, Finland, powering the LUMI AI Factory. Total budget is about €387.8 million, split roughly 50/50 between EuroHPC and a hosting consortium of Finland, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Norway and Poland, with user availability targeted for the second half of 2027. Officials say AI capacity should rise about tenfold versus today’s LUMI and conventional HPC capability nearly double. The liquid-cooled BullSequana XH3500 system will use AMD Instinct MI430X GPUs and 6th Gen AMD EPYC processors. Resources prioritize startups and SMEs while remaining open to the wider research community.

### 10. China achieves first Earth–Moon two-way high-speed laser communications: ~100 Mbps downlink (China · Space comms)
**Summary:** Per the CAS Technology and Engineering Center for Space Utilization and CCTV News on Aug 31, China established a two-way laser link over more than 400,000 km in an Earth–Moon laser communications experiment—the country’s first Earth–Moon bidirectional high-speed laser link, marking a step from LEO laser comms into cislunar space. Preliminary rates reach about 1.25 Mbps uplink and about 100 Mbps downlink; an 8K lunar image that takes roughly 4–5 minutes over microwave would take about 12 seconds at 100 Mbps laser. The breakthrough is framed as support for crewed lunar missions, lunar research stations and deep-space exploration.

**Links:**

- [Chinese Academy of Sciences — First Earth–Moon two-way high-speed laser communications](https://www.cas.cn/cm/202608/t20260831_5119358.shtml)

**Commentary:** Cislunar “bandwidth” is starting to sound like the internet—deep-space missions often bottleneck on the link before the lander.
