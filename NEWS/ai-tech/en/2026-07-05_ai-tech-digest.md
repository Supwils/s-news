### 4. Russia Proposes Ministry of Digital Development as Sole **AI** Regulator, Effective **September 1** (Policy / Russia)
**Summary:** Per **ForkLog** on **July 5, 2026**, the Russian government published a proposal to amend **2008** ministry regulations, granting the Ministry of Digital Development, Communications and Mass Media exclusive authority over artificial intelligence. The ministry would set state policy, coordinate technology deployment, establish mandatory requirements for **AI** systems, and approve relevant rules from other agencies — adding roughly **20** new functions covering developer support, data-center development, **AI** on the **GosTech** platform, and workforce training. The decree is under interagency review and slated to take effect **September 1, 2026**.

### 8. **KAIST** Study: **AI Agents** Can Use Up to **136.5×** More Energy Than Conventional Generative **AI** (Research / South Korea)
**Summary:** Per **Digital Today** on **July 5, 2026**, a **KAIST** team led by Professor **Yoo Min-soo** systematically quantified compute and power consumption of **AI agents** in real service environments for the first time. A **70-billion-parameter** commercial **LLM agent** averaged **348.41 watt-hours** per question — **136.5×** higher than conventional generative **AI** Q&A — with response times up to **153.7×** longer. While external tools like search or code execution ran tasks, **GPUs** sat idle without separate computation for up to **54.5%** of total runtime, revealing a new inefficiency where expensive accelerators wait on multi-step agent orchestration. Results were presented in **February** at the **HPCA** conference.
**Links:**

- [Digital Today — AI agents can use up to 136.5 times more energy than conventional AI, KAIST study finds](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/78155/ai-agents-can-use-up-to-1365-times-more-energy-than-conventional-ai-kaist-study)

**Commentary:** Behind the agent hype hides an ignored energy tax — multi-step reasoning burns not just tokens but "GPU idle time," making per-watt inference efficiency the next competitive battleground.
