## II. Policy and Regulation

### 2. U.S. Congress advances Open-Source AI Leadership Act to promote U.S. open weights and flag Chinese-model risks (Policy)

**Summary:** Reporting dated September 6 says the House Energy and Commerce subcommittee advanced H.R. 10152, the Open-Source AI Leadership Act, by voice vote. The bill would direct the Commerce Department to map barriers to adopting U.S. open-weight models, compare U.S. versus Chinese adoption, and within 18 months of enactment publicize risks of Chinese models (censorship, supply-chain, misuse). It would also create a single Commerce contact to promote U.S. open weights to companies, agencies, states, and qualified foreign partners. Sponsor Gabe Evans argued Alibaba, DeepSeek, and Moonshot lead the open-weight lane; the text states it does not authorize Commerce to ban or pull open models. Full committee markup and midterm timing remain uncertain.

**Links:**

- [The Star / SCMP — Congress advances bill on U.S. open-source AI vs China](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/06/congress-advances-new-bill-to-give-us-edge-in-open-source-ai-race-with-china)

### 4. DOJ files Statement of Interest backing LLM training as fair use (Copyright)

**Summary:** Reuters reported that on September 1 the Trump administration filed a Statement of Interest in Manhattan federal court in the New York Times–OpenAI multidistrict copyright fight, arguing that training large language models on copyrighted texts generally constitutes fair use and citing scientific progress, competitiveness, and national security. Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward said the U.S. should not be disadvantaged by a “plainly incorrect understanding of copyright law.” The Times said the administration is siding with “trillion-dollar AI companies.” The filing is advisory, not binding, but is the federal government’s first systemic intervention in this wave of training suits. Commerce Secretary Lutnick also urged G20 peers to embrace fair use while finding ways to protect creators.

### 11. Embodied AI blue book maps a three-stage cost curve: R&D, order-pull, then platforms (China · Industry)

**Summary:** China Economic Net and related outlets covered the September 2 launch of *China Embodied Intelligence Industry Development Report (2026)* by Central University of Finance and Economics, Shenzhen Meili Mofang Robotics, and Social Sciences Academic Press. The blue book argues cost reduction will move through R&D-driven validation (slow declines), order-pulled scale (clearer scenes and BOM convergence), and platformization (shared modules and service operations stabilizing the curve). It says the near-term path is “local generality” in high-value scenes before broader diffusion, and that government tools—guidance funds, compute vouchers, standards, open scenes, and first-of-a-kind procurement—should shift from point subsidies toward supply-chain coordination and public capability platforms.
