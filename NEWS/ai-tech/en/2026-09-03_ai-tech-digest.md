## I. M&A and the Open Ecosystem

### 1. Nvidia seals Hugging Face deal at about $12.93B, pledging the platform stays open (M&A)

**Summary:** On September 3, Nvidia confirmed a definitive agreement signed September 2 to acquire Hugging Face for about $12.93 billion—roughly $11.9 billion to stockholders plus up to about $1 billion in equity retention for joining employees—with closing targeted for the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals. In an official blog post, Jensen Huang said Nvidia will scale the platform and infrastructure while keeping Hugging Face open: developers choose models, frameworks, clouds, and compute, with Nvidia silicon not required and other chip vendors still supported. CNBC cited CEO Clément Delangue saying he approached Huang over the summer. The hub serves more than 18 million developers and hosts about three million models. It is Nvidia’s second-largest deal after the ~$20 billion Groq asset purchase late last year.

## II. Policy and Regulation

### 2. Sanders unveils Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act: permanent ASI ban plus a pause on advanced AI (Policy)

**Summary:** On September 3, Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar announced forthcoming legislation to permanently ban development and deployment of superintelligent AI and to pause advanced AI development until a new cabinet-level federal AI regulator sets safety rules and a model-review process. The bill would also push U.S. policy toward international agreements and export controls to prevent ASI anywhere, with severe penalties framed as a “corporate death penalty” and up to about 20 years’ imprisonment for evasion. The full text is not yet public and passage is uncertain, but it turns frontier labs’ verbal safety pledges into a legislative wager.

### 3. G20 endorses U.S. “Carolina Principles”: light-touch, sector-specific AI rules, no new AI agency (Policy)

**Summary:** Bloomberg and others reported on September 3 that G20 members on September 2 unanimously endorsed the Carolina Principles at a U.S.-hosted tech and innovation ministerial in North Carolina: sector-specific rule-making for AI and emerging tech, avoiding new dedicated AI regulators, closer government–industry vetting, and explicit attention to the costs of delaying breakthrough products. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick said the framework won full backing—including China and Russia—weeks before the Trump–Xi meeting expected in Washington on September 24, where AI is likely on the agenda. EU officials still stressed joint risk preparedness; the U.S. also reiterated no appetite to ease advanced-chip export controls on China.

**Links:**

- [The Star / Bloomberg — US strikes light-touch AI regulation accord with G20 members](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/03/us-strikes-light-touch-ai-regulation-accord-with-g20-members)

**Summary:** On September 2 Google launched Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, claiming stronger reasoning and coding at 3.7 Flash–level speed and introductory pricing (about $0.75 / $3.75 per million input/output tokens through end-2026)—its third Flash drop in six weeks. ### 11. Baidu and Renmin University propose NE-R1: RL-trained on-demand retrieval for NER, accepted at EMNLP 2026 (China / Research)

**Summary:** Baidu and Renmin University (with collaborators) introduced NE-R1, using an R1-style two-stage recipe (multi-task initialization plus end-to-end RL) so a named-entity model first decides whether external search is needed before answering directly or with retrieval. Chinese coverage around September 3 said the 7B variant averaged about +2.52 F1 in-domain across MIT-Movie, OntoNotes, GENIA, and peers, and about +1.18 F1 on cross-domain transfer; the paper was accepted to EMNLP 2026 main conference. The bet is difficulty-aware retrieval instead of always memorizing or always searching.

**Links:**
