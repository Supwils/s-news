## I. Policy & Markets

### 1. China lithium-ion battery consumption tax takes effect: 2% from Sep 1, rising to 4% in 2027 (Policy / China)
**Summary:** China’s Ministry of Finance, General Administration of Customs and State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 20 of 2026 took effect on September 1, 2026: mature battery products including lithium-ion cells face a 2% consumption tax, rising to 4% from September 1, 2027, while sodium-ion, solid-state and other next-gen chemistries remain exempt through end-2028. Concurrent tax-administration rules say OEMs that buy finished packs solely to assemble vehicles—without continuously producing taxable battery products—generally cannot credit the tax. Industry estimates put the per-vehicle hit at hundreds to over a thousand yuan for 60–80 kWh packs, adding pressure on thin-margin BEVs already facing purchase-tax tapering and price wars.

**Commentary:** Lithium batteries are exiting blanket tax holidays as a mature industry—while solid-state and sodium-ion keep a relative policy window.

### 2. U.S. judge preliminarily blocks EPA bid to send four California emission waivers to Congress (Policy / United States)
**Summary:** On September 2, U.S. District Judge Beryl Howell granted a preliminary injunction barring the EPA from reclassifying four Clean Air Act Section 209(b) waivers as Congressional Review Act “rules” and sending them to Congress for repeal; the court ordered restoration of the status quo before the June 12, 2026 announcement and a compliance status report. The waivers cover passenger-vehicle GHG standards, Advanced Clean Cars I, a related reinstatement, and Small Off-Road Engine (SORE) rules. Reuters reported lawmakers had been preparing votes later in September; EPA signaled it may appeal.

## II. Batteries & Charging

### 4. Mercedes-linked ProLogium says Gen 3.5 solid-state cells enter mass production at 381 Wh/kg (Battery / Taiwan–Europe)
**Summary:** CarNewsChina reported on September 6 that ProLogium Technology—partnered with Mercedes-Benz—says its Generation 3.5 Lithium Ceramic Battery (LCB) has entered mass production, quoting 381 Wh/kg gravimetric and 903 Wh/L volumetric density for a 185.4 Ah large-format cell. Energy-density figures were inspected by TÜV Rheinland, and UL Solutions tested the cell under China’s GB/T 43568-2026 all-solid-state methodology. Taoyuan capacity starts near 0.5 GWh, while the Dunkirk, France plant is planned for an initial ~4 GWh phase and up to ~44 GWh design capacity. The report stresses cell specs are not yet proof of cost, yield or pack-level durability for high-volume EV traction batteries.

**Links:**

- [CarNewsChina — Mercedes-backed ProLogium starts 381 Wh/kg solid-state production](https://carnewschina.com/2026/09/06/mercedes-backed-prologium-starts-381-wh-kg-solid-state-production-as-carmakers-watch-from-sidelines/)

**Commentary:** Crossing a “mass production” label matters—but cost and installed vehicle volume remain the real commercialization gates, not a single Wh/kg headline.
