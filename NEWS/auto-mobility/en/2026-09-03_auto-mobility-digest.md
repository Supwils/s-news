## I. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Platforms

### 1. Tesla stages Cybercab event in Austin: wheel-free robotaxi spotlighted, scale and regulation still unclear (Autonomy / US)
**Summary:** Tesla held an invite-only Cybercab event on September 3 at Gigafactory Texas in Austin, teasing “no steering wheel, no pedals.” CNBC reported that about 45 Cybercabs were authorized for driverless operations in Texas as of the prior evening. CNN noted Tesla’s current robotaxi service is mainly Model Y–based across roughly six cities in Texas and Florida, with about 380,000 cumulative unsupervised miles—far below Waymo’s stated ~220 million driverless miles—while Waymo added Denver, San Diego, and Tampa this week to reach about 14 commercial cities. Public booking scale and a proven profit path remain unclear.

### 2. London’s first hailable “self-driving taxis” go live: Uber + Wayve, ~15 cars with safety drivers required (Mobility / UK)
**Summary:** On September 3, UK startup Wayve and Uber launched the UK’s first commercial supervised autonomous ride-hail service in London. Riders requesting UberX, Uber Electric, or Uber Comfort may be matched to Wayve-equipped Ford Mustang Mach-E EVs at no extra fare. Transport for London licensed the vehicles as private hire, but a licensed safety driver must remain in the front seat; fully driverless operation needs separate DVSA approval, widely seen as unlikely this year. Only about 15 vehicles are licensed versus roughly 100,000 interested riders on Uber’s list; Waymo and Baidu are also testing in London.

**Links:**

- [The Guardian — London’s first self-driving taxis for hire hit the streets](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/03/london-first-self-driving-taxis-for-hire-wayve-uber)
- [Wayve — Wayve and Uber Launch First-Ever Autonomous Rides in the UK](https://wayve.ai/press/wayve-uber-launch-autonomous-rides/)

**Commentary:** “Europe’s first” is a licensing and customer-experience milestone, not a driverless breakthrough—fifteen supervised cars open the regulatory door more than they replace drivers.

## II. EVs & Major Automaker Products

### 3. Ford targets ~100,000 first-year Fathom EV truck sales: sub-$30k price aims at non-Tesla record (EV / US)
**Summary:** Electrek and Carscoops, citing the Wall Street Journal, report Ford set an internal goal of about 100,000 first-year sales for its upcoming midsize Fathom electric pickup. The truck is expected to start under about $30,000, with production planned for Q1 2027 at the reworked Louisville plant using a new “assembly tree” process. Outside Tesla, no automaker has sold more than 100,000 units of a single EV model in one U.S. year; meanwhile U.S. EV share was about 5.7% in August and fell sharply year over year. Fathom will also face the refreshed Chevy Bolt and the upcoming Slate truck.
