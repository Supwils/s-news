## I. EVs & Policy

### 1. Canada moves to repeal federal EV sales mandate (Policy / North America)
**Summary:** On August 15, the Canadian federal government published proposed amendments in the Canada Gazette, Part I, to repeal the mandatory zero-emission vehicle sales requirements under the Electric Vehicle Availability Standard (EVAS) introduced in 2023. The original standard called for ZEVs to represent at least about 20% of model-year 2026 light-duty sales, rising to 100% by 2035. The proposal faces an approximately 75-day consultation ending around October 29. Ottawa says it will later introduce stronger, technology-neutral greenhouse gas standards aimed at a path toward roughly 75% EV sales by 2035 and 90% by 2040, but replacement tailpipe rules are not yet finalized.

**Links:**

- [Canadian Auto Dealer — Ottawa moves to repeal EV mandate as auto industry welcomes shift](https://canadianautodealer.ca/2026/08/ottawa-moves-to-repeal-ev-mandate-as-auto-industry-welcomes-shift/)
- [Canada Gazette — Regulations Amending the Passenger Automobile and Light Truck Greenhouse Gas Emission Regulations](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-08-15/html/reg2-eng.html)

**Commentary:** North American policy is shifting from hard sales quotas to more flexible emissions frameworks—giving automakers breathing room while making climate pathways less certain.

### 2. Hyundai warns of a “lose-lose” if Türkiye is shut out of EU “Made in EU” rules (Policy / Europe)
**Summary:** Hyundai Motor Europe President Xavier Martinet told reporters after visiting the Izmit plant that excluding Türkiye from the EU Industrial Accelerator Act’s “Made in EU” framework could raise vehicle costs and disrupt a decades-built supply ecosystem—a lose-lose outcome. Hyundai has started producing the fully electric IONIQ 3 there. Turkish automakers worry that even if Customs Union rules treat Turkish components as equivalent to EU-origin content, vehicles assembled in Türkiye could still be shut out of subsidies, procurement preferences, and fleet-compliance benefits. Negotiations on the final rules remain open.

**Links:**

- [Türkiye Today — Hyundai warns ‘lose-lose’ outcome if Türkiye excluded from ‘Made in EU’](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/business/hyundai-warns-lose-lose-outcome-if-turkiye-excluded-from-made-in-eu-3226261)

**Commentary:** Europe’s local-content rules are redrawing investment maps; whether nearshore plants “count as Europe” will shape Korean and multinational capacity plans.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Platforms

### 3. Nevada approves Tesla, Uber, and Waymo for paid robotaxis in Clark County (Autonomy / U.S.)
**Summary:** On August 20, the Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved three Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permits allowing Tesla, Uber, and Waymo to offer commercial robotaxi service in Clark County, including Las Vegas. Combined caps total up to about 8,000 vehicles over the next 12 months: up to roughly 5,000 for Tesla and 1,000 each for Waymo and Uber. Uber plans to operate via partnerships with Hyundai’s Motional and Amazon’s Zoox. Tesla’s Cybercab chief engineer said 5,000 is a ceiling, not a pledge, and that reaching about 2,500 within a year would be a strong outcome; industry testimony suggests actual deployments will start far below the caps.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — Tesla, Uber, and Waymo all get the OK to operate thousands of robotaxis in Nevada](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/tesla-uber-and-waymo-all-get-the-ok-to-operate-thousands-of-robotaxis-in-nevada/)
- [Engadget — Nevada allows Uber, Tesla and Waymo to start paid robotaxi service](https://www.engadget.com/2241379/nevada-allows-uber-tesla-waymo-paid-robotaxis/)

**Commentary:** One regulatory decision is casting Las Vegas as a multi-player U.S. robotaxi battleground—permit ceilings are high; execution pace is the real constraint.

### 4. Uber launches Baidu’s fully driverless Apollo Go on its Dubai platform (Mobility / Middle East)
**Summary:** On August 20, Uber announced that Baidu’s fully driverless Apollo Go vehicles are available to Uber riders in Dubai, with New Horizon Luxury Transport as fleet operator. Service initially covers parts of Umm Suqeim and Jumeirah; riders booking UberX or Uber Comfort may be matched with a robotaxi, or can select the dedicated “Autonomous” option in the app. Vehicles are sixth-generation RT6 purpose-built electric robotaxis seating up to three passengers with more than 30 sensors. The companies call Dubai the first live market of a multi-year partnership and a launchpad to scale thousands of Apollo Go vehicles across Uber’s global network; Uber frames it as the first public-road instance of its multi-partner AV network.

### 6. Waymo unveils custom 5nm robotaxi ASIC and names key suppliers (Autonomy / Hardware)
**Summary:** On August 20, Waymo published details of the compute stack in its robotaxi trunks, highlighting a purpose-built 5-nanometer ASIC that cleans, fuses, and runs front-end neural networks on raw lidar, radar, and camera data before it reaches the core driving models—claiming over 1,000 TOPS of front-end ML performance. The chip is already in the latest vehicle generation, including Ojai. ## III. Batteries, Charging & Automakers

### 7. Geely advances 500 Wh/kg solid-state battery road tests, eyes 2027 pilots (Battery / China)
**Summary:** Electrek and follow-on coverage on August 20–21 reported that Geely Holding is developing next-generation solid-state cells targeting energy density up to about 500 Wh/kg, with real-world vehicle validation underway in 2026 and pilot applications planned from 2027 across brands including Geely, Zeekr, Lynk & Co, Volvo, Polestar, Lotus, and Smart. Geely claims range comparable to diesel cars—over 1,000 km per charge—and a lifespan target of up to about 1 million km; mass-production timing and cost competitiveness remain undisclosed. CATL, BYD, Toyota and others are also pointing to a 2027-era solid-state pilot window.
