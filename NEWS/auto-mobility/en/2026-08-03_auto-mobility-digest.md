## I. Autonomous Driving & Regulation

### 1. Zoox wins first U.S. commercial exemption for steering-wheel-free purpose-built robotaxis (Autonomy / Policy)
**Summary:** Amazon-owned Zoox received a temporary NHTSA exemption to manufacture and commercially deploy its purpose-built autonomous sedan without a steering wheel or pedals—the first U.S. approval of its kind for paid passenger service. The exemption runs from July 31, 2026 through July 31, 2028 and allows up to about 2,500 vehicles per year; Zoox plans to start charging fares in Las Vegas next month. NHTSA also advanced AV framework updates, streamlined Part 555 exemptions, and launched a roughly $5 million, three-year SAE ITC partnership on unified AV performance standards.

## II. Safety Recalls & Investigations

### 5. Ford recalls ~86,500 Mustang Mach-Es over rear quarter-window trim that may detach (Recall / EV)
**Summary:** InsideEVs and others reported on Aug 3 that Ford is recalling about 86,543 U.S. Mustang Mach-E models from the 2023–2025 model years (built roughly Feb. 17, 2023–Oct. 8, 2024) because rear side quarter-window trim may detach while driving and strike other vehicles. The root cause is a supplier assembly jig in Mexico that shortened install time so trim primer could not cure properly; a 30-second timer was added from September 2024. Ford cites more than 200 related warranty claims and no known crashes or injuries; owner notifications are expected to begin around Sept. 10, 2026.

**Links:**

- [InsideEVs — Ford recalls 86,000 Mustang Mach-E over flying window trim](https://insideevs.com/news/803565/ford-mustang-mach-e-window-trim-recall/)
- [Carscoops — Ford Mach-E trim recall: glue didn’t have time to dry](https://www.carscoops.com/2026/07/ford-mach-e-trim-recall/)

**Commentary:** A “small” trim defect shows how supplier process changes can amplify safety risk on high-volume EVs.

### 6. NHTSA opens preliminary probe into ~1.2 million Tesla Model 3/Y suspension links (Safety / Tesla)
**Summary:** electrive and others reported on Aug 3 that NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation covering about 1.2 million Teslas—2018–2020 Model 3 and 2021–2023 Model Y—after 156 complaints that the front lower lateral link can detach while driving, impairing steering control or leaving the vehicle undriveable. The agency reports no known related crashes, injuries, or fatalities. The scope exceeds Tesla’s smaller 2021 and 2023 lateral-link recalls and may reflect a different root cause. A preliminary evaluation is the first stage and could escalate toward an engineering analysis or recall.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** Alongside software and ADAS scrutiny, hardware suspension defects pull Tesla back into classic component-safety oversight.
