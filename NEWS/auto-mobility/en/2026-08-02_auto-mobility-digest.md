## I. Batteries, Charging & the Grid

### 1. Revoy raises ~$27M Series A for swappable electric truck dollies in US freight (Battery / Heavy-duty)
**Summary:** CleanTechnica reported on Aug 2 that California startup Revoy (formerly SixWheels) remains in focus after Bloomberg disclosed an about $27 million Series A, taking total funding above $41 million, led by Standard Capital. The product is a detachable electric-drive dolly that sits between tractor and trailer, turning existing diesel Class 8 trucks into swappable hybrids without buying a full battery-electric tractor. Revoy aims for sub-five-minute swaps at highway hubs; third-party MVTS testing previously showed roughly 65.2%–69.7% diesel fuel savings. First commercial operations are slated for 2026 on an about 200-mile corridor near Portland, Oregon.

**Links:**

- [CleanTechnica — EV battery swapping coming to the US (Revoy)](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/08/02/ev-battery-swapping-us-fuel-saving-diesel-startup-revoy/)
- [electrive — Revoy raises $27 million for plug-in electric truck dolly](https://www.electrive.com/2026/07/31/revoy-raises-27-million-for-plug-in-electric-truck-dolly/)

**Commentary:** US heavy-duty electrification is bypassing full truck replacement in favor of existing diesel fleets plus operable swap assets.

### 2. California state-funded pilot equips ~120 homes for V2H/V2G with parked EVs (Charging / Policy)
**Summary:** The Cool Down and others followed up on Aug 2 that Bidirectional Energy and Wallbox, with California Energy Commission support, are installing bidirectional chargers at about 120 homes to test vehicle-to-home backup and vehicle-to-grid export. Earlier Canary Media reporting describes typical setups pairing a Kia EV9 with a Wallbox Quasar 2 and rebate support for equipment and install.

### 3. electrive: Grid capacity is the binding bottleneck for European e-truck depot charging (Charging / Europe)

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Platforms

### 4. Kakao Mobility: Gangnam robotaxi tops 1,000 paid rides in just over two months (Autonomy / Korea)
**Summary:** Seoul Economic Daily reported on Aug 2 that Kakao Mobility’s autonomous taxi service in Seoul’s Gangnam district has surpassed 1,000 cumulative rides since switching to paid service in April. With only two vehicles running, that equals roughly 12 rides per day across the fleet, or about six per vehicle. Service runs weekdays from 10 p.m. to 5 a.m., covering Gangnam Station, Apgujeong, Yangjae and nearby areas. The company plans gradual route expansion with Seoul authorities and aims to raise the end-to-end share of its “AI Planner” stack in on-road tests this year.

**Links:**

- [Seoul Economic Daily — Gangnam robotaxi tops 1,000 paid rides](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/08/02/gangnam-robotaxi-tops-1000-rides-two-months-after-going-paid)

**Commentary:** Small-scale nighttime paid ops prove “chargeable” is not “scalable,” but they do move robotaxis from free trials into paid habit formation.

### 5. San Francisco mayor pushes mandatory “major-event readiness” tests for robotaxis (Regulation / US)
**Summary:** Coverage on Aug 2 continued to spotlight Mayor Daniel Lurie’s letter to California’s transportation secretary demanding that AV operators prove emergency performance before large-scale deployment. The proposal centers on four capabilities: rapidly clearing disabled vehicles from active lanes, dynamic rerouting in emergencies, real-time ops data sharing with local agencies, and tested resilience under traffic and demand surges. Triggers include July 4 fireworks gridlock involving Waymo vehicles and NHTSA’s July 8 letter on AVs entering active emergency scenes; Lurie also backs federal emergency-response legislation.

**Links:**

- [News Anyway — Lurie demands mandatory emergency tests for robotaxis](https://www.newsanyway.com/2026/08/02/san-francisco-av-regulations-lurie-demands-mandatory-emergency-tests-for-robotaxis/)
