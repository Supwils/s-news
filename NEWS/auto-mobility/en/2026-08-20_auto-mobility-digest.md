## I. EVs & Policy

### 1. China’s NEV monthly penetration hits 60.4% for the first time; cumulative share passes half (Policy / China)
**Summary:** Citing CAAM data compiled in August 20 media reports, China’s NEV sales reached 1.561 million units in July 2026, up 23.7% year on year, with monthly new-vehicle penetration at 60.4%—the first time above 60%. Cumulative NEV sales for January–July totaled 9.007 million units, and cumulative market share crossed 50% for the first time at 51.2%. Auto exports stayed elevated, with July shipments above 1 million vehicles and NEVs accounting for more than half of exports for a second straight month.

### 2. U.S.–Canada trade talks hit a critical window; auto tariffs remain the main sticking point (Policy / North America)
**Summary:** U.S. President Donald Trump this week paused planned 50% tariffs on roughly $30 billion of Canadian goods for several more days so negotiators can finalize documents, with a new deadline around August 21. Multiple reports say duties on Canadian-built passenger vehicles remain the hardest issue: the latest U.S. offer would cut the Section 232 headline rate from 25% to about 15%, with U.S. content potentially lowering the effective rate to about 7.5%, while Canada is pushing for a lower rate or broader North American content relief.

**Links:**

- [CBC News — Trump pauses 50 per cent tariffs on Canadian goods for 3 days](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/new-trump-tariffs-deadline-9.7311125)
- [Financial Post — Trump, Carney talk trade as negotiators battle over autos](https://financialpost.com/news/economy/trump-carney-talk-trade-negotiators-battle-autos)

**Commentary:** Integrated North American vehicle supply chains are highly tariff-sensitive; a missed deal would quickly hit cross-border vehicle and parts production.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Platforms

### 4. Waymo opens Zeekr-built Ojai robotaxis to all riders in San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Phoenix (AV / U.S.)
**Summary:** On August 20, Alphabet’s Waymo said its next-generation Ojai robotaxi—running Waymo’s sixth-generation Driver and built in China by Zeekr—is now open to all paying riders in San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Phoenix after early-access free trips that began May 28. The company says about 300 Ojais are already in the commercial fleet, with Denver, San Diego, and Las Vegas planned later this year. Reports say Waymo has imported more than 3,200 Zeekr bodies via the Port of Los Angeles since 2024 while paying roughly 127.5% tariffs on Chinese EVs, yet the platform still undercuts the Jaguar I-PACE fleet it is phasing out.

Even so, Tesla’s unsupervised footprint in Austin remains far smaller than Waymo’s local fleet of more than 300 driverless vehicles.
