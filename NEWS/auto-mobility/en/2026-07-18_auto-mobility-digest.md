## I. Policy & Batteries

### 1. China reinstates lithium battery consumption tax: 2% from September, 4% in 2027; sodium-ion and solid-state stay exempt (Policy / Batteries)
**Summary:** China's Ministry of Finance, General Administration of Customs, and State Taxation Administration announced on Jul 17 that mature battery products including lithium-ion cells will face a 2% consumption tax from Sep 1, 2026, rising to 4% from Sep 1, 2027, ending an roughly 11-year exemption dating to 2015. Sodium-ion, solid-state, and fuel cells remain exempt through end-2028, as do perovskite, tandem, and gallium arsenide PV cells. Caixin and CnEVPost coverage on Jul 18 framed the shift as redirecting support from an oversupplied lithium sector toward next-generation chemistries; CPCA secretary-general Cui Dongshu wrote the same day that self-produced cells used in continuous production can avoid or deduct the tax, strongly pushing automakers to build in-house cell capacity.

- [Caixin — China to Reinstate Battery Tax as Support Shifts to Next-Generation Technologies](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-18/china-to-reinstate-battery-tax-as-support-shifts-to-next-generation-technologies-102465532.html)

**Commentary:** Subsidy retreat has reached the cell itself—"tax mature lithium, spare next-gen" is industrial policy by tax code.

### 2. CPCA's Cui: battery tax reform will force automakers to make their own cells (Industry / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost on Jul 18 cited Cui Dongshu arguing that batteries produced for the taxpayer's own continuous production are exempt, while tax already paid on purchased cells used in continuous production can be deducted by quantity used—so OEMs that build and install their own packs can avoid or offset the levy, while those that buy externally will more easily absorb passed-through costs. Cui said the sequence from halved purchase tax and ending vehicle-and-vessel tax exemptions to restoring battery consumption tax formally opens "equal treatment" for ICE and NEVs, while sodium-ion and solid-state exemptions leave a policy window for next-gen positioning.

**Links:**

- [CnEVPost — China's battery tax shift is a strong push for automakers to make their own cells](https://cnevpost.com/2026/07/18/china-battery-tax-shift-push-automakers-make-own-cells/)
- [electrive — China introduces consumption tax on lithium-ion batteries](https://www.electrive.com/2026/07/17/china-introduces-consumption-tax-on-lithium-ion-batteries/)

**Commentary:** Fine print on who pays the tax will reshape bargaining power—OEMs without cell plants lose cost and strategy leverage.

### 3. California MyFirstEV: 13 automakers commit; first-time ZEV buyers get up to $3,500 instant rebate (Policy / US)
**Summary:** Governor Newsom's office announced on Jul 16 that Ford, GM, Honda, Hyundai, Kia, Lucid, Mitsubishi, Nissan, Rivian, Subaru, Tesla, Toyota, and Volvo will join MyFirstEV. ### 5. Lyft and Curb expand to New York City: licensed taxis available in the Lyft app (Mobility / US)

## III. Markets & New Models

### 6. Germany: BEVs become top powertrain for first time in June—about 84,057 units, ~28.4% share (Market / Europe)
**Summary:** Multiple outlets on Jul 18 cited ADAC data showing that in June 2026 Germany registered about 84,057 battery-electric cars, narrowly ahead of hybrids at about 83,315, making BEVs the top powertrain for the first time with roughly 28.4% share. Plug-in hybrids added about 32,212 units (~10.9%), for a combined plug-in share near 39.3%; gasoline and diesel followed at about 60,796 and 33,862. Average new-car CO2 emissions fell about 13.6% year over year. As Europe's largest auto market and the industry's historic home, the milestone was widely read as a landmark in Europe's EV transition.
