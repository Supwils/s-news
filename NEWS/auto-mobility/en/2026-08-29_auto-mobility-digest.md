## I. Policy & Quality Oversight

### 1. China launches one-year vehicle conformity campaign with surprise factory checks, covering ADAS validation (Policy / China)
**Summary:** Economic Observer and Sina Finance reported on August 29 that MIIT, the Ministry of Public Security, the Ministry of Ecology and Environment, and SAMR began a one-year nationwide campaign on road-vehicle production conformity and quality on August 27. Priorities cover production conformity, reliability, durability, and new-technology test validation—including whether combined driver-assistance and automated-driving features were adequately tested, plus data/cybersecurity and log retention. Supporting guidance calls for unannounced “spot checks” straight to plants and test labs. Firms must finish self-reviews by end-December 2026. The drive follows MIIT’s August 28 naming of 2025 NEV conformity cases.

### 2. Teamsters sue California DMV over heavy autonomous-truck rules (Policy / US)
**Summary:** The Los Angeles Times reported on August 29 that Teamsters California sued the California DMV earlier this month to rescind April rules that lifted the ban on autonomous vehicles over 10,001 pounds and set certification steps including safety-driver testing and about 500,000 miles per stage. Representing about 250,000 California members, the union argues the DMV skipped a required standardized regulatory impact assessment for changes exceeding roughly $50 million in first-year economic effects, estimating true first-year impact above about $280 million, and cites Waymo incidents to question safety and job loss. The DMV declined comment on pending litigation; its director had said in April the update strengthens safety and transparency.

**Links:**

- [Los Angeles Times — California regulators rushed decision on driverless trucks, Teamsters say](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2026-08-29/california-regulators-rushed-their-decision-on-driverless-trucks-teamsters-lawsuit-says)
- [Transport Topics — Teamsters California sues DMV over autonomous truck rules](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/teamsters-california-sues)

**Commentary:** The US heavy-truck AV fight has shifted from “can it drive” to “did regulators lawfully price jobs and safety.”

## II. Autonomy & Driver Assistance

### 3. Tesla briefs Australia/NZ HW3 owners on FSD Supervised v14 Lite progress (Autonomy)
**Summary:** The Driven reported on August 29 that Tesla emailed Hardware 3 (AI3) owners in Australia and New Zealand saying FSD (Supervised) v14 Lite is rolling out in North America and South Korea, with active development underway for Australia and New Zealand. ## IV. Charging Economics & Supply Chain

### 8. New Hampshire North Country Superchargers face Oct. shutdown as power bills swamp revenue (Charging / US)
**Summary:** NHPR on August 29, republishing the Concord Monitor, reported that four Tesla Superchargers on Main Street in Gorham (opened in May at about $407,000) are slated to shut on October 25 after electricity costs crushed economics. Through mid-August, net revenue was about $15,900 at roughly $0.69/kWh while the power bill topped about $32,500; July’s ~$13,200 bill included ~$10,700 in delivery charges, with demand charges adding further pain. More than 1,200 vehicles charged, nearly half non-Teslas. New Hampshire’s ~$17.2 million NEVI round just closed applications, and the owner asks who will put up 20% match if sites lose money when switched on.

**Links:**

- [NHPR — Shutdown of North Country station exemplifies complexities of public EV charging](https://www.nhpr.org/environment/2026-08-29/ev-charging-nh-north-country-station-exemplifies-complexities)

**Commentary:** Rural US fast charging often fails not for lack of plugs but because delivery and demand charges turn private sites into loss leaders.

### 10. AirPro and Revv merge into an end-to-end aftermarket ADAS diagnostics and workflow platform (M&A / Aftermarket)
