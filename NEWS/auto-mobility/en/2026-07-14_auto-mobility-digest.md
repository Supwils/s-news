### 1. China's NEV fleet reaches 48.97 million; 2030 target of 30% of total fleet enters countdown (Market / China)
**Summary:** CnEVPost reported on July 14, 2026 that Ministry of Public Security data shows China's car fleet stood at 371 million at end-June 2026, of which 48.97 million were new energy vehicles (13.19% of the total, up 2.92 percentage points year-on-year). ### 2. California signs MyFirstEV program: $3,500 instant rebate for first-time buyers; Rivian/Lucid get price-cap exemption (Policy / US)
**Summary:** California Governor Gavin Newsom signed SB 168 on July 13, 2026, creating the MyFirstEV program offering first-time EV buyers a $3,500 instant point-of-sale discount on new vehicles ($1,750 on used), backed by $135.5 million in state funding matched dollar-for-dollar by participating automakers — roughly $270 million in total savings. New vehicles are capped at $50,000 MSRP, but California-headquartered EV-only automakers are exempt from the cap: Rivian (from ~$58,000) and Lucid (from ~$71,000) qualify in full, while Tesla — which moved its headquarters to Austin, Texas in 2021 — qualifies only on sub-$50,000 Model 3 and Model Y configurations. The program is the centerpiece of a $600 million zero-emission transportation package, filling the policy vacuum left after federal EV tax credits ended in September 2025.

### 4. US Congress proposes national EV registration fee of $130–150/year to offset lost federal gas-tax revenue (Policy / US)
**Summary:** Marketplace reported on July 7, 2026 that a bipartisan transportation funding bill in Congress includes a proposal for an annual national registration fee on electric and hybrid vehicles, initially around $130/year and potentially rising to $150, to compensate for declining federal gas-tax revenue as EV adoption grows. Forty-two states plus Washington, D.C. already charge separate EV fees ranging from roughly $50 to $300/year; if enacted at the federal level, it would be the first nationwide registration fee on personal vehicles. Brookings fellow Adie Tomer noted that gas taxes are spread across each fill-up while registration fees hit all at once, potentially adding psychological friction for consumers considering electrification; a similar proposal failed to pass in 2025 and is back under discussion in 2026.

**Links:**

- [Marketplace — Congress eyes a national EV registration fee](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/07/07/congress-eyes-a-national-ev-registration-fee)

**Commentary:** After federal EV subsidies exit, "registration fees to backfill tax revenue" is the new battleground — state rebates and federal surcharges may coexist, rewriting the US total-cost-of-ownership model for EVs.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 5. NHTSA moves to drop mandatory steering wheels for robotaxis, following June brake-pedal repeal (Regulation / US)
**Summary:** Carscoops and TT News reported in July 2026 that NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison told CNBC that requiring steering wheels on vehicles "designed never to be driven by a human operator" makes no sense, and the agency "absolutely" will consider ending the requirement — following a June 25 rulemaking launch to remove the mandatory manual brake pedal for purpose-built autonomous vehicles. If both reforms are implemented, purpose-built robotaxis such as Tesla's Cybercab and Amazon's Zoox could reach the road in their original wheel-free, pedal-free designs. In parallel, Morrison sent a July 8 letter to all US autonomous driving developers demanding fixes for emergency-scene failures by month-end, advancing deregulation on hardware and tighter scrutiny on software safety simultaneously.

**Links:**

- [Carscoops — The Feds Might Let Robotaxis Skip The Steering Wheel](https://www.carscoops.com/2026/07/nhtsa-steering-wheel-rule/)
- [TT News — NHTSA considers ending steering wheel mandate for robotaxis](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/nhtsa-steering-wheel-robotaxi)

**Commentary:** NHTSA is systematically dismantling FMVSS provisions written for the human-driver era — steering wheels and pedals exiting clears the federal path for Cybercab-class robotaxis, but emergency-scene software gaps remain the biggest variable.

### 7. New Jersey bill S1677 would allow robotaxi testing but requires camera plus two independent sensing modalities (Regulation / US)
**Summary:** CleanTechnica reported on July 13, 2026 that New Jersey Senators Andrew Zwicker and Gordon Johnson's bill S1677 would allow manufacturers to test fully autonomous vehicles in the state, but requires crash-avoidance systems including a camera and two independent sensing modalities capable of detecting obstacles if the camera fails (radar or lidar not mandated — the language is technology-neutral). The bill also requires coordination with state police and the attorney general, liability insurance of $5 million or more, at least 50,000 crash-free supervised test miles before state authorization, and a pilot period of no less than three years. Tesla has criticized the multi-sensor requirement; Zwicker responded that "at this point, I don't think the evidence is sufficient that a single sensor with software can handle situations that humans can."

**Links:**

- [CleanTechnica — New Jersey Welcomes Robotaxis, But Only If They Have Three Sensor Technologies](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/07/13/new-jersey-welcomes-robotaxis-but-only-if-they-have-three-sensor-technologies/)

**Commentary:** Federal deregulation on steering wheels meets state-level sensor redundancy requirements — US robotaxi regulation is forming a two-tier game of "federal minus hardware, states plus redundancy," structurally challenging vision-only approaches at the state-law level.
