### 3. U.S. NHTSA public comment closes today on pedal-free robotaxi brake rule (Policy / United States)
**Summary:** NHTSA has proposed amending FMVSS No. 135 (light-vehicle brake systems) so vehicles designed solely for automated driving systems can comply without traditional foot brake pedals, while keeping stopping-distance performance requirements; the public comment window closes Jul 27, 2026. If finalized, the rule would create a clearer federal certification path for pedal- and wheel-free robotaxis and weaken the roughly 2,500-vehicle annual production cap tied to today’s exemption regime—relevant for Tesla Cybercab, Amazon’s Zoox, and peers.

**Links:**

- [TechTimes — NHTSA Brake Rule Would End Robotaxi Production Cap: Public Comment Closes Tomorrow](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321624/20260726/nhtsa-brake-rule-would-end-robotaxi-production-cap-public-comment-closes-tomorrow.htm)
- [U.S. DOT — Modernization of FMVSS No. 135 To Accommodate ADS-Equipped Vehicles](https://www.transportation.gov/regulations/federal-register-documents/2026-12981)

**Commentary:** Closing comments is not a final rule—but the direction from exemption carve-outs toward an in-standard path for driverless hardware is clear.

### 4. CaoCao starts onboard-operator-free robotaxi road tests in Hangzhou Binjiang (Autonomous Driving / China)
**Summary:** Geely-incubated ride-hailing platform CaoCao said on Jul 27 it has begun public-road robotaxi testing without an in-vehicle safety operator in Hangzhou’s Binjiang District. ## V. Supply Chain & Hydrogen

### 11. Toyota signs deal for one-third of Cellcentric with Daimler Truck and Volvo (Hydrogen / Global)
**Summary:** Toyota has signed a binding agreement to acquire about a 33% stake in fuel-cell joint venture Cellcentric, becoming an equal partner with Daimler Truck and Volvo Group pending regulatory approval; completion is expected around end-2026 or early 2027. Cellcentric will remain independent, employing more than 560 people with roughly 700 patents, and continue supplying fuel-cell systems for heavy road and off-road vehicles, coaches, rail, marine, stationary power, and machinery. Analysts note batteries already dominate passenger cars and most trucks, so the alliance looks more like cost-sharing to keep hydrogen viable for the hardest-to-electrify heavy-duty use cases.
