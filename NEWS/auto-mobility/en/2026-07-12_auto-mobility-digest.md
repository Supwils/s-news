### 2. China's first national solid-state battery standard GB/T 43568-2026 takes effect July 1: below 5% liquid to qualify (Policy / China)
**Summary:** The Electric Viking, Battery-Tech Network, and others reported that China's national standard GB/T 43568-2026 — "Solid-State Battery for Electric Vehicle, Part 1: Terms and Classification" — took effect on July 1, 2026, establishing the first nationwide classification framework. Batteries with 5%–20% liquid electrolyte are classified as hybrid systems; only cells below 5% liquid content and with a vacuum-drying weight-loss rate of no more than 0.5% qualify as all-solid-state. Ambiguous labels such as "semi-solid-state" are abolished. This is the first installment of a four-part series covering performance, safety, and lifespan; CATL, BYD, and other leading battery makers will reassess line investments and equipment choices accordingly.

### 3. Two mandatory EV safety national standards take effect July 1: no fire/explosion after thermal runaway, physical disconnect required (Policy / China)
**Summary:** News18a reported on June 30, 2026 that "Electric Vehicle Safety Requirements" (GB18384—2025) and "Safety Requirements for Traction Battery of Electric Vehicle" (GB38031—2025) became mandatory on July 1, 2026. New rules require that after a single cell thermal runaway, the pack must not catch fire or explode, with an observation period of at least two hours and an alarm within five minutes. Bottom-impact and post-fast-charge-cycle safety tests are added; vehicles must have physical high-voltage disconnect capability, with a physical "one-button power-off" in an easily accessible driver position. MIIT surveys suggest compliance may add RMB 3,000–5,000 per battery pack and raise overall battery system costs by 15%–20%, with a one-year transition allowing older non-compliant models to clear inventory.

### 4. UN adopts first global fully automated driving regulatory framework; ~90 existing vehicle rules amended (Regulation / Global)
**Summary:** UN News reported on June 24, 2026 that the UNECE World Forum for Harmonization of Vehicle Regulations (WP.29) formally adopted the world's first regulatory framework for fully automated driving systems (ADS), requiring manufacturers to implement audited lifecycle safety management systems, demonstrate through a safety-case approach that systems pose "no unreasonable risk," and achieve dynamic driving performance matching or exceeding a competent human driver. Roughly 90 existing UN vehicle regulations were amended in parallel to ensure vehicles without steering wheels or pedals remain covered by established safety standards. The framework is expected to enter into force roughly one month after adoption, providing a mutual-recognition basis for robotaxis such as Waymo and Zoox designed without human controls.

**Links:**

**Commentary:** A decade into AV "testing mode," the world now has a mutually recognized L4 approval framework — national adoption speed will determine whether robotaxis move from city pilots to scaled commercial deployment.
