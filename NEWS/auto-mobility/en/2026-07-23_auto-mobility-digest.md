### 2. Ford–Geely Spain JV: Shared Valencia Capacity, Faster European Localization (Strategy / Europe)
**Summary:** On Jul 23, Ford and Geely Auto announced a manufacturing joint venture in Valencia, Spain, to share Ford’s plant and build multi-energy vehicles for Europe. Pending regulatory approvals, the JV is expected to begin operations in H1 2027, with the first new vehicle targeted for 2028; ownership would be about 66% Ford and 34% Geely. The plant’s annual capacity is about 500,000 units. It will continue Kuga production and plans a 2028 Bronco-family compact SUV, a new Ford-led crossover co-developed with Geely, and two Geely NEVs (Geely’s first European production plant). Geely’s H1 overseas sales were about 474,000 units, up roughly 158% YoY.

### 6. China Gradually Restarts Robotaxi Licences After Safety Review (Autonomy / China)
**Summary:** On Jul 23, multiple outlets citing Bloomberg reported that China has resumed issuing new robotaxi permits after freezing approvals in April following a large-scale Baidu Apollo Go stoppage in Wuhan. After an industry-wide safety review concluded in late June, authorities are restoring licences city by city rather than all at once. Momenta said on Jul 21 it received intelligent connected vehicle road-test approval in Shenzhen; Shenzhen has also revised rules to lower entry barriers and explore city-wide robotaxi operation. Apollo Go vehicles have reappeared on Wuhan streets since early July, some still with safety drivers.

## III. Ride-Hailing & Regulation

### 7. Federal Judge Issues Preliminary Injunction Against NYC Driver Deactivation Law (Mobility / US)
**Summary:** On Jul 22, U.S. District Judge Gregory Woods in Manhattan granted Uber and Lyft a preliminary injunction blocking New York City’s Local Law 52 from taking effect as planned on Jul 28. The ordinance would generally require 14 days’ notice and an independent appeals process before deactivating drivers, covering more than 80,000 for-hire drivers. The court found the law mainly benefits a narrow group of drivers while severely impairing the companies’ contracts and ability to police passenger safety. Uber and Lyft said the ruling affirms that driver fairness and rider safety must go together.

**Links:**

- [Insurance Journal — Uber, Lyft win court block on NYC driver deactivation law](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2026/07/23/878649.htm)
- [The City Reporter — Judge blocks NYC app driver deactivation protection law](https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2026/07/22/uber-lyft-driver-deactivation-law-court-injunction/)

**Commentary:** U.S. mobility regulation is still tugged between labor protections and platform safety discretion—this round, the courts leaned toward platforms’ real-time risk controls.

### 8. Massachusetts Utilities Add V2G to ConnectedSolutions’ 800+ MW Flexibility Pool (Charging / US)

### 9. Ford F-150 Can Back Up a Home Panel via ~$1,100 GenerLink Bundle (V2H / US)
**Summary:** On Jul 22, Electrek reported that Ford, with Global Power Products, has made a GenerLink transfer switch compatible with the 240V Pro Power Onboard outlet on certain F-150 Lightning (9.6 kW) and F-150 PowerBoost hybrid (7.2 kW) trucks, enabling a single connection to a home’s main electrical panel for hard-wired loads. The equipment bundle—transfer switch, GenerLok cord, and Ford Vehicle Connector—costs about $1,100. Ford estimates a PowerBoost hybrid can power an average home for up to five days; a standard-range Lightning up to two days and an extended-range Lightning up to three. More than 800 U.S. utilities already approve GenerLink meter-mounted installs.
