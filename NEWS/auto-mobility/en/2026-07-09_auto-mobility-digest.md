## I. Policy & Market Environment

### 1. CAAM: China H1 NEV production and sales both exceed 7 million; June exports top 1 million for first time (Market / China)
**Summary:** Xinhua and CLS reported on July 9, 2026, that the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers released June and H1 data. H1 NEV production and sales reached 7.438 million and 7.446 million units, up 6.7% and 7.3% YoY; BEVs accounted for roughly 67% of sales. In June, NEV production and sales were 1.598 million and 1.643 million units, up 26% and 23.6% YoY, representing 58.5% of new-car sales; the H1 share was 49.6%, with domestic penetration reaching 63.1% in June. Exports surged: June auto exports hit 1.037 million units (+75.1% YoY), the first monthly million-unit milestone; NEV exports were 523,000 (+160% YoY), exceeding ICE exports for the first time. H1 auto exports totaled 5.096 million units, with 2.355 million NEV exports (+120% YoY). CAAM Vice Secretary-General Chen Shihua described H1 as showing "three divergences": weak domestic demand vs. export growth, weak passenger cars vs. commercial growth, and shrinking ICE vs. growing NEV.

### 2. U.S. Senate Commerce Committee to vote July 15 on Connected Vehicle Security Act, potentially banning Chinese connected vehicles permanently (Policy / U.S.)
**Summary:** Quartz reported on July 9, 2026, that Senate Commerce Committee Chair Ted Cruz scheduled a full committee vote for July 15 on the Connected Vehicle Security Act of 2026, introduced by Sens. Bernie Moreno (R-OH) and Elissa Slotkin (D-MI). The bill would prohibit import, manufacture, sale, and resale of connected vehicles, software, and hardware linked to China and other foreign adversaries, and empower the Commerce Department to block high-risk vehicle technologies. Vehicle and software restrictions would take effect in 2027; hardware restrictions in 2030. The legislation aims to codify Biden-era rules from early 2025 that blocked Chinese automakers from the U.S. passenger market on national-security grounds, making future policy reversal politically and legally harder; a companion House bill exists. Polestar, owned by China's Geely, was forced to halt U.S. sales after failing to secure Commerce Department authorization under existing rules.

**Links:**

- [Quartz — Senate panel to vote on Chinese vehicle ban bill July 15](https://qz.com/senate-commerce-committee-chinese-vehicle-ban-bill-070926)

**Commentary:** From executive ban to congressional statute, U.S. decoupling from Chinese autos is shifting from emergency tool to institutional guardrail — even third-country assembly routes will face tighter data and supply-chain scrutiny.

### 4. New Jersey advances S1677: driverless commercial vehicles must carry cameras plus two sensor types, effectively barring camera-only Tesla Robotaxi (Policy / U.S.)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on July 9, 2026, that New Jersey lawmakers are advancing bill S1677, requiring fully driverless commercial vehicles to carry cameras plus at least two additional sensing technologies (in practice radar and lidar), with at least 50,000 miles of supervised in-state testing, crash reporting, and official authorization before commercial driverless launch. The mandate would effectively exclude Tesla's camera-only Robotaxi from the nation's most densely populated state while allowing multi-sensor Waymo fleets. The bill targets only fully driverless commercial fleets, not consumer Autopilot or FSD (Supervised). Sponsor Zwicker clarified mass Tesla-owner protest emails were based on misunderstanding; the episode reflects fragmented U.S. AV regulation — California demands detailed safety reports; Texas, Arizona, and Georgia allow self-certification with minimal oversight.

**Links:**

- [Electrek — New bill would ban Tesla's camera-only Robotaxi, not Waymo](https://electrek.co/2026/07/09/new-jersey-bill-tesla-camera-robotaxi/)

**Commentary:** New Jersey shifts regulatory focus from behavioral compliance to hardware configuration — the autonomy stack debate is becoming a state-level admission threshold, with vision-only facing its biggest population-market risk.

### 5. Belgium's AIdoptation begins Europe's first public L4 motorway test at 130 km/h (Autonomous Driving / Europe)
**Summary:** VRT NWS reported on July 9, 2026, that AIdoptation, a tech firm in Sint-Truiden, Limburg, received permission to test an L4 autonomous Maserati on roughly 100 km of the E313 and E314 motorways. Unlike most systems built for low-speed urban driving, this system targets full-speed motorway autonomy at 130 km/h, where drivers theoretically need not watch the road or hold the wheel; a professional driver remains ready to intervene during tests. The company develops autonomy software only and seeks partnerships with automakers. Engineer Louis Jacobs said high-speed vehicle dynamics differ sharply from urban low-speed scenarios, making this Europe's first public-motorway validation of "driver can do other tasks" L4 capability.
