## II. Policy & Regulation

### 3. India’s PM E-DRIVE Traction-Motor Localisation Rules Take Effect for E-Buses and E-Trucks (Policy / India)
**Summary:** From Sep 1, 2026, stricter domestic manufacturing requirements for traction motors and motor-controller systems used in electric buses and electric trucks under India’s PM E-DRIVE scheme take effect. Makers seeking scheme incentives must locally complete key steps such as magnet fitment and rotor/stator, shaft and bearing assembly. The Society of Indian Automobile Manufacturers had asked to push the deadline from Sep 1 to Apr 1, 2027, citing China’s rare-earth magnet export controls and supply-chain disruption; as the date arrived, no approval of that latest extension request was announced. The roughly ₹1.09 trillion programme ties incentives to phased local content, so compliance now directly affects commercial EV output and subsidy eligibility.

**Commentary:** Emerging-market electrification is moving from “subsidies for volume” to hard local-value-chain rules—rare-earth magnet supply is the binding constraint on policy delivery.

### 4. U.S. Transportation Secretary Signals Sharply Lower Fuel-Economy Standards, Toward ~34.5 mpg by 2031 (Policy / U.S.)
**Summary:** Reuters and follow-on reports say Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy stated on Aug 31 in Michigan that the Trump administration will soon announce significantly lower corporate average fuel economy (CAFE) standards, reversing the prior push for higher efficiency and electrification. NHTSA’s December proposal would require a fleetwide average of about 34.5 mpg by 2031, down from roughly 50.4 mpg under the Biden-era path; the final text was not yet released, but automakers expect something close. Congress had already ended civil penalties for shortfalls and consumer EV tax credits; a CAFE rollback would further ease regulatory pressure to prioritize battery-electric mix.

### 5. Maharashtra Aggregator Licence Deadline Arrives: Uber, Rapido Among Applicants (Mobility / India)
**Summary:** The Times of India reported on Sep 1 that ride-hailing platforms operating in Maharashtra faced a same-day deadline to apply for licences under the state’s new motor vehicle aggregator rules; Uber, Rapido and others have applied to continue operations, alongside newer cooperative entrants. The framework caps surge pricing at about 1.5 times the base fare, requires drivers to receive at least about 80% of the fare, and tightens verification, training, insurance and multilingual grievance systems. Officials in Pune warned platforms without permanent licences could face restrictions from Sep 2; some operators still object to per-app vehicle caps, with hearings scheduled.
