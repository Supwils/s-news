## I. Policy & Regulation

### 1. China drafts Road Traffic Safety Law chapter on AVs; automakers may face liability in full autonomy mode (Policy / Autonomous Driving)
**Summary:** Automotive World and the South China Morning Post reported on August 25 that China's National People's Congress Standing Committee began a first review of amendments to the Road Traffic Safety Law that would add a dedicated chapter on autonomous vehicles. Xinhua said the draft would clarify definitions of autonomous driving versus driver assistance; analysts noted that when a legally recognized autonomous system is active, automakers could bear primary responsibility for accidents and traffic violations, while responsibility reverts to the driver if the system is switched off. Automotive World cited industry figures that Level 2 ADAS already appears on about 70.5% of new cars sold in China so far in 2026, while true higher-level AVs cleared for public roads remain scarce. A separate mandatory Level 3/4 safety standard (GB 44721—2026) announced in August is due to take effect in July 2027, pairing product entry rules with liability allocation.

**Links:**

- [Automotive World — China proposes liability rules for autonomous vehicles](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/china-proposes-liability-rules-for-autonomous-vehicles/)
- [South China Morning Post — China’s new traffic safety law set to clarify liability for fully automated cars](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-evs/article/3365164/chinas-new-traffic-safety-law-set-clarify-liability-fully-automated-cars)

**Commentary:** Rewriting "who pressed the pedal" as "who owns the algorithm" is the institutional missing piece before robotaxi scale—clear liability is what makes commercialization investable.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxi

### 2. Tesla confirms Sept. 3 Austin Cybercab event; purpose-built robotaxi to join existing unsupervised fleet (Autonomous Driving / U.S.)
**Summary:** Electrek reported on August 24 that Tesla confirmed an invite-only “Exclusive Access: Cybercab” event in Austin, Texas, on September 3 for top Robotaxi rider-sweepstakes winners, with a public livestream. The substance is adding the two-seat, no-steering-wheel Cybercab to Tesla’s Robotaxi app network that has already run driverless Model Y rides in Austin for more than a year. The Cybercab uses an ~48 kWh pack, ~3,113 lb curb weight, and about 165 Wh/mi—among Tesla’s most efficient EVs—but runs the same FSD software stack as Model Y robotaxis. Independent tracking still puts Tesla’s active unsupervised fleet at roughly 20–30 vehicles, versus Waymo’s scale of about 3,000 driverless cars and far higher paid trip volume.

### 3. Nevada approves paid robotaxi permits for Tesla, Waymo, and Uber in Las Vegas; combined ceiling ~7,000–8,000 vehicles (Mobility Platforms / U.S.)
**Summary:** TechCrunch and follow-on coverage reported that the Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved commercial robotaxi permits on August 20 for Tesla, Waymo, and an Uber subsidiary in Clark County (including Las Vegas): Tesla up to 5,000 vehicles in year one, Waymo and Uber 1,000 each; with Zoox’s preexisting ~100-vehicle permit, media tallies put the county-wide 12-month ceiling near 7,000–8,000. Tesla’s Cybercab chief engineer called 5,000 a ceiling and said a more realistic internal target is around 2,500. Uber plans to operate via Motional and Zoox partners and argued for hybrid human–AV networks; local taxi operators warned of market oversaturation and congestion in the “Golden Triangle.” Companies still must clear inspections, insurance, and rate filings before charging fares.

### 6. Einride plans to deploy 500 Tesla Semis—largest publicly disclosed Semi order to date (Commercial EV / Freight)
**Summary:** Swedish freight-tech company Einride said on August 18 it will deploy 500 Tesla Semi trucks across North America in phases over 24 months starting September 2026, serving Amazon and other customers on corridors in California, Texas, New Jersey, Illinois, and Georgia, managed on Einride’s Saga AI platform. Einride called it the world’s largest Tesla Semi deployment of its kind, taking its deployed electric fleet from roughly 250 to about 750 trucks; the purchase is fully financed by third parties. ### 8. Ford’s U.S. EV sales fall ~57% in H1 as lineup shrinks toward Mach-E (Market / U.S.)
**Summary:** Industry coverage of Ford’s second-quarter sales disclosure showed U.S. EV deliveries of just 16,606 units in the first half of 2026, down about 57.4% year-over-year—fewer than Mach-E alone sold in the year-earlier half—while Q2 EV volumes were still down roughly 40.7%. Ford linked broader volume declines partly to Escape and Lincoln Corsair phase-outs and rental-fleet cuts, but the EV contraction tracks the post–September 30, 2025 federal purchase-credit cliff. Ford is investing about US$5 billion to retool Louisville for a ~US$30,000 midsize electric pickup on its Universal EV platform starting in 2027; its CFO has previously flagged multi-billion-dollar EV losses in 2026, with profitability not expected until around 2029.
