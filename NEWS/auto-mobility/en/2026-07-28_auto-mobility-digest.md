## I. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 1. Baidu Apollo Go and Lyft Freenow begin robotaxi road testing in London (AV / Europe)
**Summary:** On July 28, Baidu’s Apollo Go and Lyft-owned Freenow said they have started road testing sixth-generation RT6 autonomous vehicles in London’s borough of Brent, initially with onboard safety operators across mixed urban and suburban routes. Public ride-hailing is targeted for 2027, subject to regulatory approval; vehicles will run on the Freenow platform alongside taxis and private-hire cars. Waymo is already testing in London, and Uber with Wayve plans a safety-operator service this year, making the city a key European robotaxi battleground.

### 2. Hong Kong Airport Island starts world’s first fully driverless public-road trial in a right-hand-drive system (AV / China)
**Summary:** Hong Kong’s Transport Department granted Baidu Apollo Go the city’s first fully driverless trial permit on July 23, with L4 testing without an onboard safety operator beginning July 27 on Airport Island under remote monitoring—described as the first fully driverless public-road trial in a right-hand-drive, left-hand-traffic system. Baidu said the program can inform deployment across more than 70 right-hand-drive markets; the same day’s London launch also cited Hong Kong RHD experience as a stepping stone.

## III. Automakers & Supply Chain

### 6. Tesla sues Texas supplier, saying locked tooling threatens Cybertruck builds (Supply chain / US)
**Summary:** Per Bloomberg Law and Electrek reports on July 28, Tesla filed suit on July 23 in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas against Angstrom Automotive Group, seeking emergency access to a Troy, Texas plant to retrieve Tesla-owned tooling. The complaint says Angstrom gave July 13 closure notice then refused handoff; about 700 finished parts due to ship July 17 never left; rebuilding tooling would take five to six months; and on-hand stock could run out in days, risking thousands of customer-committed Cybertrucks. Tesla says Angstrom offered to keep running for about $250,000 per week on top of open POs.

## IV. Batteries & Charging

### 9. Dallas places fourth Beam Global order for off-grid solar EV chargers (Charging / US)
