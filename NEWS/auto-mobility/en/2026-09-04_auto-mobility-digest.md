## I. Autonomous Driving & Regulation

### 1. NHTSA opens audit of ~1,000 Cybercabs: wheel-/pedal-free self-certification in focus (Autonomy / US)
**Summary:** After Tesla began commercial robotaxi rides with its two-seat, steering-wheel- and pedal-free Cybercab in Austin, Texas, on September 3, the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened Open Audit Query AQ26002 covering an estimated 1,000 Cybercabs. The agency is examining the process and technical data Tesla used to claim compliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, including whether Tesla treated certain standards as inapplicable because the vehicle lacks conventional manual controls. Reuters syndication and state records cited about 420 autonomous vehicles registered in Texas as of Friday morning, including roughly 45 Cybercabs; TechCrunch and Electrek noted the audit arrived almost in lockstep with commercial deployment.

### 2. France begins on-road Tesla FSD tests to verify data ahead of EU vote (Autonomy / Europe)
**Summary:** RFI and ETAuto reported on September 4 that French transport authorities have started public-road tests with two Tesla vehicles equipped with Full Self-Driving (FSD) Supervised, aiming to verify Dutch and Tesla safety data and assess performance on French roads and urban layouts. Results are expected by mid- to late September to inform a possible EU Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles vote around October 6; approval would need at least 15 of 27 member states representing at least about 65% of the EU population. The Netherlands granted provisional approval in April, followed by Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, and Lithuania, while large markets such as Germany and France have remained cautious.

## II. EVs & Policy

### 4. China battery consumption tax takes effect: 2% on lithium cells, sodium/solid-state still exempt (Policy / China)
**Summary:** From September 1, 2026, lithium primary and lithium-ion batteries face a 2% consumption tax, rising to 4% on September 1, 2027; sodium-ion, solid-state, and fuel-cell batteries remain exempt through December 31, 2028. CarNewsChina and Chinese financial media estimate roughly several hundred yuan of added pack cost at the 2% rate for a ~60 kWh vehicle. The levy sits alongside the shift from full purchase-tax exemption to a half-rate cut and the planned 2027 withdrawal of preferential vehicle-and-vessel tax treatment—part of a broader NEV subsidy phase-down. Analysts generally expect fierce price competition to force OEMs to absorb much of the cost rather than pass it fully to buyers.

**Links:**

- [CarNewsChina — China is gradually phasing out subsidies for new energy vehicles](https://carnewschina.com/2026/09/04/china-is-gradually-phasing-out-sudsidies-for-new-energy-vehicles/)
- [Sina Finance — Power-battery consumption tax lands; will NEVs get more expensive?](https://finance.sina.com.cn/wm/2026-09-03/doc-iniqpqxy6979467.shtml)

**Commentary:** Taxing mature lithium while sparing next-gen chemistries is how Beijing re-prices today’s capacity against tomorrow’s tech bets.

### 5. U.S. judge issues preliminary injunction blocking EPA bid to send California emissions waivers to Congress (Policy / US)
**Summary:** U.S. District Judge Beryl Howell around September 2 granted California a preliminary injunction requiring the EPA to restore the status quo that treated Clean Air Act motor-vehicle emissions waivers as orders, not rules subject to congressional disapproval. Reuters and Inside Climate News report the Trump administration sought to reclassify waivers—including greenhouse-gas and 2035 zero-emission pathway authorities—and send them to the Republican-led Congress. California argues the move would worsen air quality and jeopardize federal highway-funding compliance. A final merits ruling is still ahead, but the injunction blocks the short-term executive–Congress path to void California’s standards.

**Links:**

- [Reuters — US judge bars EPA effort to send California vehicle emissions rules to Congress](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-judge-bars-epa-effort-send-california-vehicle-emissions-rules-congress-2026-09-03/)

**Commentary:** The U.S. EV policy fight has moved from subsidy tables to courtrooms—how long California’s waiver holds will shape multi-state ZEV timelines.

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 6. Alpitronic unveils HYC1000: up to ~1 MW total power, dynamically shared across eight outlets (Charging / Europe)
**Summary:** Italian charger maker Alpitronic showcased its HYC1000 distributed DC fast-charging system at Berlin’s ICNC26, with up to about 1 MW total output from a central power cabinet feeding as many as eight DC outlets, allocating power in ~62.5 kW steps. In a live demo, a new Mercedes-AMG GT 4-Door Coupé drew more than about 600 kW (display readings around 612–620 kW). The system uses eight second-generation silicon-carbide power stacks and offers MCS/CCS2 dispenser options aimed at highway hubs, commercial fleets, and destination charging.

**Links:**

## IV. Automaker Strategy & M&A

### 7. Volkswagen board approves turnaround plan: another ~50,000 cuts, ~100,000 total (OEM / Europe)
**Summary:** Volkswagen’s supervisory board approved its Future Plan / Zukunftsplan 2030, adding roughly 50,000 further global job cuts—including management roles—on top of about 50,000 already planned, for a combined ~100,000. The group also aims to halve its model portfolio by about 2035 and is assessing alternative uses for German plants in Emden, Zwickau, Hanover, and Neckarsulm. CNBC reported on September 4 that shares jumped on the news; management cited tariffs (about €2.9 billion in 2025 tariff costs), China competition, and capacity mismatch. CEO Oliver Blume said the company will invest a “three-figure billion” euro sum in coming years to strengthen brands.
