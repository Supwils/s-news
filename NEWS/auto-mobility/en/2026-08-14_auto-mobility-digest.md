## I. EVs & Policy

### 1. UK opens ZEV mandate consultation; 2030 pure-EV share could fall from 80% (Policy / UK)
**Summary:** The UK Department for Transport on Aug 14 launched a consultation on the Zero Emission Vehicle (ZEV) mandate (VETS), seeking views on the 2027–2035 annual sales trajectory, compliance flexibilities, and the pathway to ending sales of new petrol and diesel cars by 2030. It closes at 11:59 p.m. on Oct 23. Current rules require 33% of new-car sales to be zero-emission in 2026, rising to 80% by 2030. BBC reported that, after pressure from carmakers, ministers are considering cutting the 2030 pure-EV share to as low as 50% with hybrids filling the rest, or keeping 80% with flexibility stretching to 2034. Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander said the end goal is unchanged but industry must be brought along. Officials said July was the strongest UK new-car market since 2019, with more than one in four new cars electric (+45% YoY) and over 2 million EVs on UK roads.

- [BBC — Electric vehicle sales targets could be cut after pressure from car makers](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c1l1yj0zd9no)

**Commentary:** London is keeping the 2030 petrol-and-diesel sales ban on paper while putting the annual hard targets up for negotiation—Europe's EV policy is shifting from linear tightening to a bargained transition.

### 2. CAAM: China's July NEV share tops 60% for the first time; Jan–Jul cumulative share exceeds 50% (Market / China)
**Summary:** The China Association of Automobile Manufacturers disclosed on Aug 12, with follow-up coverage on Aug 14, that nationwide automaker sales in July were 2.584 million vehicles, roughly flat year over year. NEVs accounted for 1.561 million units sold (1.576 million produced), or 60.4% of new-vehicle sales—the first month above 60%. Domestic NEV sales were 1.008 million, down 2.8% year over year, but weaker ICE demand lifted the domestic NEV share to 65.4%; industry overseas sales rose 81.3% year over year and filled the domestic gap. January–July NEV output and sales reached 9.014 million and 9.007 million (+9.5% / +9.6%), with a 51.2% cumulative share, the first time above 50%.

**Links:**

- [Caixin — China's NEV share of new-car sales tops 60% in July](https://www.caixin.com/2026-08-14/102474040.html)
- [EV Look — July NEV sales 1.561 million, 60.4% of total](https://www.evlook.com/sales-58775.html)

**Commentary:** NEVs are now the default in China, but flat absolute volumes and export-led growth show a record penetration rate can coexist with a cool domestic market.

## II. Batteries & Charging

### 3. GM exits Samsung SDI Indiana venture and restarts Ohio Ultium cell production (Battery / US)
**Summary:** The Detroit Free Press reported Aug 14 that General Motors made two US cell-capacity moves in the week of Aug 10: it sold its roughly 50% stake in the Indiana joint venture with Samsung SDI, leaving Samsung to run the plant independently and prioritize energy-storage (ESS) cells; and it is restarting the Ultium Cells plant in Warren, Ohio, with LG Energy Solution after about seven months idle. Ultium operations VP Tom Gallagher confirmed the restart to Reuters on Aug 12. The Ohio plant stopped cell production in January 2026 amid weaker demand, with about 1,330 workers laid off; roughly 1,400 employees are expected back to make large-format NCMA pouch cells used in most GM EVs. GM and LG's Tennessee plant has already shifted toward ESS batteries.

**Commentary:** US cell capacity is being rightsized: EV lines restart where demand supports them, and surplus capacity is being rebadged for storage rather than left idle.

### 5. Nio opens first fifth-generation swap stations; China network passes 4,000 sites (Energy / China)
**Summary:** electrive reported Aug 14 that Nio opened its first fifth-generation battery-swap stations in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Suzhou, Hefei, Chengdu, and Quanzhou; the Qiaoxiang Gymnasium site in Quanzhou is also Nio's 4,000th swap station in China. The new stations let Firefly models use the swap network for the first time and allow all three group brands to share the same infrastructure. Nio said it completed its 120-millionth swap on Aug 7 (it passed 100 million in early February, adding 20 million in about six months). As of Aug 7 it operated 4,006 swap stations and 5,160 charging stations with 29,801 charge points in China, covering more than 550 cities, including 1,049 highway swap sites. European swap expansion has slowed, with no near-term additions of proprietary swap or charging stations there.

**Links:**

### 10. Tesla receives a Nevada robotaxi permit, initially capped at 10 vehicles on the Strip corridor (Mobility / US)
**Summary:** Teslarati reported that the Nevada Transportation Authority issued Tesla Robotaxi, LLC AVNC Permit 002 (Docket 26-05015). Tesla asked in June to run up to 5,000 vehicles in Clark County within a year; the interim order initially caps the fleet at 10 fully autonomous vehicles inside a Las Vegas Strip geofence, with any area or fleet expansion requiring prior NTA approval. Other limits include no operations on roads posted above 45 mph, no pickups within a quarter mile of Harry Reid International Airport without separate authorization, visible "Robotaxi" markings, and "appropriate human supervision." Tesla has said it will not meaningfully scale robotaxi fleets until FSD v15, expected in late 2026 or early 2027.

**Commentary:** The permit shows Tesla can enter the regulatory door, but a 10-car geofence means Nevada still treats robotaxi as a pilot, not an open replacement for ride-hail.
