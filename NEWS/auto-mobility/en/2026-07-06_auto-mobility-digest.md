## I. Policy & Market Environment

### 1. California Launches $135M First-Time EV Incentive — $3,500 Off New Cars, Rivian/Lucid Price-Cap Exemption (Policy / US)
**Summary:** CleanTechnica and InsideEVs reported on July 6, 2026 that after the federal $7,500 EV tax credit expired in September 2025, Governor Gavin Newsom and the legislature finalized $135 million in state funding in the 2026–27 budget, to be administered by CARB through 2031. The program targets first-time zero-emission vehicle buyers in California, offering roughly $3,500 total on new EVs and $1,750 on used ones, split evenly between the state and participating automakers, applied as an instant dealership discount rather than a tax-season credit. New vehicles are capped at $50,000 MSRP and used at $25,000, but California-headquartered Rivian (Irvine) and Lucid (Bay Area) are exempt from price caps; Tesla, now headquartered in Texas, is not. CARB is signing grant agreements with automakers; showroom availability is expected within weeks once rules are finalized.

**Links:**

- [CleanTechnica — California Introducing New EV Incentives, With Boost For Rivian & Lucid But Not Tesla](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/07/06/california-introducing-new-ev-incentives-with-boost-for-rivian-lucid-but-not-tesla/)
- [InsideEVs — California's New EV Incentive Is Almost Here—With Special Benefits For Rivian And Lucid](https://insideevs.com/news/800208/california-ev-credit-rivian-lucid/)

**Commentary:** With federal subsidies gone, California is rebuilding demand support through first-buyer, point-of-sale rebates plus a hometown-OEM carve-out — the state that accounts for roughly one-third of US EV sales is shifting from tax credits to cash-on-the-hood.

### 2. China Ends Vehicle Vessel Tax Breaks for PHEVs/EREVs from 2027; BEV Passenger Cars Remain Exempt (Policy / China)
**Summary:** Automotive World and BitAuto reported around July 6, 2026 that China's Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration, and MIIT jointly announced that from January 1, 2027, halved vessel tax rates for energy-saving vehicles will end, and exemptions for BEV commercial vehicles, plug-in hybrids (including range-extended), and fuel-cell commercial vehicles will be removed; both newly acquired and previously acquired vehicles in those categories must pay under the Vehicle and Vessel Tax Law. BEV and fuel-cell passenger cars remain outside the tax scope because the levy is based on engine displacement. Analysts note that with NEV penetration already past a tipping point, policy is moving from broad hybrid support toward "equal treatment of ICE and electrified" while further tilting toward pure electric; annual ownership costs for PHEV/EREV users will rise, though per-vehicle amounts are modest relative to purchase price.

**Links:**

- [Automotive World — China ends annual tax break for some NEVs, spares BEVs](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/china-ends-annual-tax-break-for-some-nevs-spares-bevs/)
- [BitAuto — New-energy vehicles to pay vessel tax from next year](https://www.bitauto.com/article/1003111275132/)

**Commentary:** After purchase-tax step-downs, vessel tax becomes the second lever to unwind hybrid favoritism — Beijing is drawing a fiscal line that pushes PHEVs toward ICE-like treatment while keeping BEVs exempt.

### 3. BNEF Slashes US 2030 Plug-In Penetration Forecast to 17% as Federal Support Retreats (Market / US)
**Summary:** InsideEVs and Auto123 reported in July 2026 that BloombergNEF's 2026 Electric Vehicle Outlook cut US short- and long-term forecasts for the second consecutive year: plug-in share of new sales by 2030 fell from roughly 47.5% to about 17%. The report cites "full withdrawal of federal regulatory support for electrification" as the biggest factor, including Congress ending the $7,500 credit early in 2025, gutting fuel-economy rules, and the Senate blocking California's stricter emissions waiver — shelving the state's 2035 100% ZEV target (California and allied states are litigating). BNEF also notes US EV prices remain roughly 25% above ICE, the highest gap among regions studied; long-term cost declines still support growth, but US-Europe-Asia penetration gaps will widen around 2026.

**Links:**

- [InsideEVs — How America's Policy Whiplash Derailed EV Growth](https://insideevs.com/news/800503/us-ev-sales-2030-bnef/)
- [Auto123 — U.S. EV adoption rate forecasts are way down](https://www.auto123.com/en/news/ev-adoption-2030-forecast-plummets/74031/)

**Commentary:** When federal carrots are pulled up by the roots, canceling models and cutting North American electrification investment becomes rational — the US EV narrative is shifting from policy-driven catch-up to market-paced climb.

### 9. NHTSA Proposes Removing Manual Brake Pedal Requirement for ADS-Only Vehicles; Comments Due July 27 (Regulation / US)
**Summary:** Repairer Driven News and Car and Driver reported July 6, 2026 that NHTSA's June 26 NPRM would amend FMVSS No. 135: vehicles designed exclusively for automated driving systems (ADS) would no longer need foot-operated service brakes or hand/foot parking brakes, with braking activated by onboard systems instead — **without changing** stopping-distance performance requirements; ADS vehicles retaining manual controls must still meet existing rules. Administrator Jonathan Morrison said the move removes pointless barriers under Secretary Duffy's AV framework while strengthening core safety. NHTSA also formally withdrew January's AV STEP voluntary reporting-for-exemption NPRM. Public comment closes July 27, 2026.

**Links:**

- [Repairer Driven News — NHTSA proposes removing requirement for manual brakes in autonomous vehicles](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/07/06/nhtsa-proposes-removing-requirement-for-manual-brakes-in-autonomous-vehicles/)
- [Car and Driver — Feds Want to Drop Requirement for Autonomous Cars to Have a Brake Pedal](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a71746274/federal-standards-drop-brake-pedal-requirement-driverless-cars/)

**Commentary:** Federal certification for pedal-less L4 vehicles is shifting from case-by-case exemptions to rewriting the standard itself — the compliance cost structure for Cybercab-class designs is being recalculated.
