## I. Autonomous Driving & Regulation

### 1. Cybercab scrutiny deepens: self-certification vs. exemption caps (AV / United States)
**Summary:** Xinhua reported that NHTSA has opened a review of Tesla’s Cybercab federal safety certification, focusing on the process and technical data used to claim compliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS). The two-seater lacks permanently attached steering wheels, pedals, and mirrors; the agency will examine how far Tesla relied on judgments that certain standards do not apply. A September 5 Reuters-sourced analysis in The Hindu notes that the formal exemption pathway caps annual deployments at about 2,500 vehicles, while Tesla engineering chief Lars Moravy previously said Cybercab would not be subject to that cap. Amazon’s Zoox earlier tried self-certification, withdrew, and later secured an exemption. Texas records list about 420 Tesla autonomous vehicles, including roughly 45 Cybercabs.

### 2. Tesla FSD Supervised v14.3.9: can intervene during manual driving to avoid crashes (Software / United States)
**Summary:** Tesla’s AI team said on September 4 that FSD Supervised v14.3.9 is starting to roll out with a new active safety set: the system can activate on the driver’s behalf when an imminent collision is detected and Automatic Emergency Braking (AEB) may not be enough, and may also engage if heavy distraction or an accidental FSD disengagement is detected. Until now, FSD mainly ran when users turned it on; this release keeps part of the same software stack ready in the background during manual driving. Electrek and owner-tech outlets note Tesla has not said whether the feature can be disabled or how it chooses steering avoidance over braking alone.

**Links:**

- [Electrek — Tesla Full Self-Driving can now take over manual driving to avoid a collision](https://electrek.co/2026/09/04/tesla-fsd-active-safety-collision-avoidance/)
- [Not a Tesla App — Tesla FSD 14.3.9 Coming Soon; Will Include New 'Automatic Collision Evasion' Feature](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/4648/tesla-fsd-1439-adds-automatic-collision-evasion-for-manual-driving)

**Commentary:** Turning Level 2 software into an emergency takeover boosts the safety story—and instantly raises liability questions if a false-positive steer goes wrong.

### 3. Changan launches Tianzhu Navigation; execs say ADAS vendors may shrink to five or six by 2028 (ADAS / China)
**Summary:** Changan on September 4 unveiled its in-house “Tianzhu Navigation” ADAS stack (Pro/Max/Ultra/Ultimate), with lidar across the lineup and a one-stage end-to-end architecture. Ultra is cited at about 560 TOPS with a 256-line lidar; Ultimate peaks near 1,000 TOPS. First model Qiyuan Q06 opened pre-orders from about CNY 147,900; Changan said orders topped 11,079 units within two hours, with Ultra versions about 53% of the mix. On September 5, vice president He Gang told media the intelligent-driving supplier field will finish a first consolidation around 2028, leaving perhaps five or six competitive players—“we and Yinwang will remain”—and put cumulative Tianzhu investment above CNY 60 billion with a roughly 7,500-person R&D team.

## II. Charging & Freight Corridors

### 4. BC2BC West Coast corridor: 20 truck chargers and 3 hydrogen stations planned (Charging / North America)
**Summary:** Charged EVs reported on September 4 that California, British Columbia, Oregon, Washington, and Baja California (Mexico) are advancing the British Columbia to Baja California ZEV Corridor (BC2BC) along the I-5 freight spine. The initial buildout targets about 20 electric-truck charging sites and three hydrogen stations; roughly 10,000 trucks use the route daily. Volvo Group North America, Southern California Edison, Tesla, EV Realty and others joined a Port of Long Beach launch event, with CalSTA, CALSTART/Drive to Zero, and UC Davis ITS coordinating planning and technical standards across the five jurisdictions.

**Links:**

- [Charged EVs — Canada, US and Mexico cooperate to launch charging network for electric trucks on the West Coast](https://chargedevs.com/newswire/canada-us-and-mexico-cooperate-to-launch-charging-network-for-electric-trucks-on-the-west-coast/)

**Commentary:** While robotaxis dominate headlines, cross-border freight corridors are the infrastructure that can pull e-trucks from pilots onto trunk logistics.

### 5. India heavy industries ministry pushes OEM-led highway charging after ~50% stations fail (Policy / India)
**Summary:** The Times of India reported that Heavy Industries Secretary Kamran Rizvi told SIAM’s annual session nearly half of mapped public chargers were non-functional. The ministry has identified about 60 priority electrification corridors and shared the list with the industry body, suggesting OEMs divide stretches and deploy—and keep—chargers working, aiming to “saturate” key highways within two to three years. The pitch comes as purchase subsidies fade and New Delhi wants industry to carry more ecosystem build-out; a roughly INR 20 billion charging corpus could be extended to automakers, with bus charging depots also planned across about 160 cities.

**Links:**

- [The Times of India — Govt eyes auto cos-led model to fix EV charging on highways](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/govt-eyes-auto-cos-led-model-to-fix-ev-charging-on-highways/articleshow/133748456.cms)

**Commentary:** Subsidy retreat meets uptime accountability—“sell the car, own the charger” is how emerging markets try to turn installed plugs into usable ones.

## III. M&A and Globalization

### 6. Tata’s Sept 5 Iveco filing: Exor locks 27%; 80% acceptance becomes the make-or-break (M&A / Commercial vehicles)
**Summary:** The Hindu Business Line reported that Tata Motors’ early September 5 disclosure maps the shareholder math for its about €3.82 billion offer for Iveco Group (roughly €14.10 per share). Regulatory clearances and financing are in place; the tender runs September 7–October 26. Largest shareholder Exor has irrevocably committed about 27.06%. If October 16 “Back-End Resolutions” pass, the minimum acceptance threshold can fall from 95% to 80%, unlocking an Asset Sale and Liquidation path to full operational control and delisting. Combined, the companies would sell more than about 590,000 vehicles a year on roughly €21 billion of revenue.
