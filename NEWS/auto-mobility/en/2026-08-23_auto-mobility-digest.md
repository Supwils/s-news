## I. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxi

### 1. Tesla locks in Sept. 3 Austin Cybercab event as wheel-less robotaxi nears production debut (Autonomy)
**Summary:** Multiple outlets reported on August 23 that Tesla has confirmed an invite-only Cybercab launch event in Austin, Texas, on September 3, with a sweepstakes for public tickets. The gold two-seater, unveiled in October 2024, is Tesla’s first production vehicle without a steering wheel or pedals; Musk previously said production would begin before 2027 with a sub-$30,000 target for private buyers. The Information reported that staff have been told the rollout will start with employee rides on public roads before the vehicles join Austin’s existing Robotaxi fleet. Current service still relies mainly on Model Y vehicles and has expanded across Texas, Florida, and California cities, though some state rules still require a human supervisor ready to take over.

### 2. All tracked Tesla Robotaxi rides in Austin unsupervised for two weeks, paving the way for Cybercab (Autonomy)
**Summary:** The Verge reported that crowdsourced Robotaxi Tracker logged 170 Tesla Robotaxi trips in Austin over the past two weeks—all unsupervised—across 54 distinct vehicles; the site’s creator also saw unsupervised activity rise in Dallas and Houston. Earlier this summer the unsupervised footprint had shrunk to roughly 28 active vehicles across six markets, meaning Austin alone now approaches the prior multi-market total. The shift coincides with Cybercab preparations: a vehicle with no manual controls depends on operating without an in-car safety monitor. Waymo still runs more than 300 driverless vehicles in Austin and about 4,000 across roughly 10 U.S. cities, leaving Tesla well behind on fleet scale.

**Links:**

### 3. Nevada clears Tesla, Waymo, and Uber for commercial robotaxis in Clark County, caps near 7,000–8,000 vehicles (Policy / Mobility)
**Summary:** On August 20, the Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved permits allowing Tesla, Waymo, and Uber subsidiary Aviari to offer paid driverless rides in Clark County, including Las Vegas. Tesla’s first-year ceiling is up to 5,000 vehicles; Waymo and Uber/Aviari are each capped at 1,000. TechCrunch put the combined deployment ceiling near 8,000. Tesla’s Cybercab chief engineer said 5,000 is a ceiling, not a commitment, and that reaching about 2,500 within a year would be a strong outcome. Uber plans to operate via partners such as Motional and Zoox; Zoox already holds a separate permit for up to 100 vehicles and began paid service locally in August. Companies still need rate filings, inspections, and insurance before launch; airport service requires separate aviation approval.

## II. EVs & Policy

### 4. After China’s record recall, GB 48001-2026 hardens door-handle rules; Tesla also OTA-fixes ~2.74M driver-monitoring cars (Policy / Safety)
**Summary:** Following the August 21 filings in which nine automakers recalled a combined ~4.27 million vehicles over hard-to-identify interior emergency mechanical releases, weekend coverage focused on mandatory standard GB 48001-2026 on door-handle safety: new type approvals from January 1, 2027, and in-production models by January 1, 2029, must provide clear mechanical release and high-contrast markings. Tesla’s door-handle recall covers ~2.98 million vehicles, remedied mainly with warning labels plus an OTA post-crash window-drop strategy starting September 25; a separate immediate OTA adds cabin-camera monitoring for ~2.74 million China-built Model 3/Y units with insufficient attention monitoring for assisted driving. CNBC and others noted Xiaomi, Leapmotor, and Xpeng are also executing among their largest recalls on record for the same class of issue.

**Links:**

### 5. With federal EV credits gone, U.S. state rebates become the main battleground; California’s MyFirstEV rolls out by brand (Policy / U.S.)
**Summary:** An August 21 Auto Wire roundup noted that federal new, used, and commercial clean-vehicle credits ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025, pushing buyers toward state and utility incentives. California’s MyFirstEV launched August 3 with roughly $135 million each from the state and automakers (~$270 million total), offering about $3,500 off at point of sale for eligible first-time buyers; a CARB August 7 bulletin said only Hyundai, Lucid, and Tesla were live then, with Ford, Rivian, Chevrolet, and Kia due later in August and Toyota, Lexus, Honda, and Subaru around September. Rhode Island’s DRIVE EV rose in January 2026 to as much as ~$3,000 on a new BEV; Colorado’s 2026 base credit falls to ~$750, while an extra ~$2,500 for vehicles under $35,000 MSRP remains, steering demand toward cheaper EVs.

## III. Batteries & Supply Chain

### 6. Samsung SDI buys out GM’s Indiana battery JV stake as the plant pivots to energy-storage cells (Battery / Supply chain)
**Summary:** Samsung SDI has acquired GM’s ~49.99% stake in the SynergyCells joint venture, taking full ownership of the still-under-construction New Carlisle, Indiana, battery plant—Samsung SDI’s first wholly owned battery factory in North America. The project had been framed as a ~$3.5 billion investment with ~27 GWh first-phase capacity aimed at GM EVs; both sides cited weaker-than-expected EV demand. Samsung SDI plans to prioritize stationary energy-storage (ESS) cells initially, while a new joint-development agreement keeps the pair collaborating on high-energy-density, fast-charging prismatic cells for possible future EVs. In parallel, GM and LG Energy Solution restarted EV cell production at the Ohio Ultium plant after a ~seven-month halt, bringing about 1,400 workers back—contrasting with Tennessee’s ESS pivot and Indiana’s exit from near-term automotive cell ownership.

### 7. Geely targets 500 Wh/kg solid-state cells with 2027 pilot deployment across group brands (Battery)
**Summary:** Electrek and InsideEVs reported in late August that Geely Holding is targeting solid-state packs at up to ~500 Wh/kg—roughly double the density of its current “Golden Brick” liquid cells—claiming diesel-comparable range above 1,000 km per charge and a lifespan goal near 1 million km. Real-world validation is underway in 2026, with pilot deployment across Geely, Zeekr, Lynk & Co, Volvo, Polestar, Lotus, Smart, and other group brands planned for 2027. CATL, BYD, and Toyota have also pointed to 2027-era solid-state pilots; “pilot” is not mass delivery, and cost, yield, and large-format manufacturability remain industry bottlenecks.

## V. Charging Infrastructure

### 10. Walmart’s owned 400 kW fast-charging network hits 100 U.S. stores across 20 states (Charging)
**Summary:** Electrek reported that Walmart opened its 100th company-owned DC fast-charging site at a Monument, Colorado, Supercenter, bringing the network to 100 stores across about 20 states. The milestone site has eight plugs (four CCS1 and four NACS) at up to 400 kW, using ABB E-mobility A400 and Alpitronic HYC400 hardware; most EVs still cannot accept the full 400 kW. Software is built in-house by Walmart Global Tech, feeding Apple Maps, Google Maps, PlugShare, and others, with session start and payment inside the Walmart app and discounts for Walmart+ members. The retailer has previously talked about chargers at thousands of Walmart and Sam’s Club locations by 2030, leveraging store density to become one of the largest U.S. fast-charging networks.
