### 1. Global June EV registrations hit 2 million: Europe up 31% to a record, China and US both weaken (Market / Global)
**Summary:** Reuters, via The Straits Times, reported on July 10, 2026, that Benchmark Mineral Intelligence data showed roughly 2 million battery-electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles were registered globally in June, up 7% year over year and 11% month over month — a fourth consecutive month of growth. First-half volume reached about 9.6 million units, up 2%. Europe registered roughly 530,000 units, up 31% year over year and 28% month over month, setting a June record; France, Denmark, Spain, and Portugal all posted new monthly highs. BMI said Europe remains the main engine of global EV growth. China fell 11% to about 1 million units, while North America dropped 13% to about 130,000 following the expiration of US federal EV tax credits. Affordable models including the Volkswagen ID.Polo, Cupra Raval, and Skoda Epiq began deliveries across Europe in June, while higher fuel prices linked to Middle East conflict further boosted switching demand.

### 3. NHTSA weighs ending steering-wheel mandate for robotaxis; chief says cars never driven by humans need no manual controls (Policy / US)
**Summary:** Bloomberg, via The Star, reported on July 10, 2026, that NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison said in a July 9 CNBC interview the agency will "absolutely" consider ending requirements that driverless cars include steering wheels. Morrison argued it makes no sense to mandate manual controls on vehicles designed never to be driven by humans. The statement follows NHTSA's June update to federal safety standards removing the mandate for physical brake pedals on fully autonomous vehicles — part of the Trump administration's push to modernize AV rules and clear design obstacles for purpose-built robotaxis such as Tesla's steering-wheel-free Cybercab. Tesla has begun producing Cybercabs in recent months but has not deployed them broadly; Waymo and Zoox are also developing dedicated driverless vehicle designs. Morrison gave no timeline for steering-wheel rule changes.

**Links:**

- [The Star — US to explore allowing driverless cars without steering wheels](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/07/10/us-to-explore-allowing-driverless-cars-without-steering-wheels)
- [Transport Topics — NHTSA considers ending steering wheel mandate for robotaxis](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/nhtsa-steering-wheel-robotaxi)

**Commentary:** From brake pedals to steering wheels, federal regulators are shifting from a human-driving assumption to a machine-native design framework — but safety validation and emergency-scene performance remain hard gates to commercialization.

## II. Autonomous Driving & Mobility

### 4. NHTSA issues call to action: AV firms must immediately fix interference with first-responder scenes (Regulation / US)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on July 8, 2026, that NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison sent a directive letter to autonomous-vehicle developers stating that a clear pattern of driverless AVs interfering with first responders is unacceptable — including entering active emergency scenes, blocking ambulances and fire trucks, and failing to recognize lights, flares, smoke, and traffic cones. Morrison said emergency scenes are not rare edge cases and that inability to detect and respond appropriately represents a functional insufficiency; developers must submit solutions by the end of July. The letter arrives alongside NHTSA's FMVSS modernization push, which proposes removing human-driver-specific equipment such as wipers, sun visors, and defogging systems. Waymo declined to comment.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — Feds demand autonomous vehicle companies stop interfering with first responders](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/feds-demand-autonomous-vehicle-companies-stop-interfering-with-first-responders/)

**Commentary:** Regulators are loosening hardware rules for dedicated AVs while tightening behavioral expectations — the winners will be those that pass both design exemptions and real-world safety tests.
