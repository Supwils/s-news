## I. Policy & Autonomous Driving

### 1. MIIT tightens ADAS “blue lights”: new certified passenger cars must stop fitting them (Policy / ADAS)
**Summary:** On Jul 29, multiple outlets and CnEVPost reported that external blue driver-assistance indicator lights (industry nickname: “little blue lights”) do not comply with mandatory national standard GB 4785-2019 on exterior lamp colors (white/yellow/red/amber), and MIIT has ordered passenger vehicles to stop installing them; automaker sources said the ban for newly certified models took effect Jul 27. Geely and others said they are aware and will comply; treatment of already-sold vehicles remains to be clarified, and MIIT has not yet published a full formal public document. Li Auto, Xpeng, Nio, BYD, Xiaomi and others had widely adopted the marker.

## II. Batteries & Charging

### 4. FAW Hongqi and partners complete 12C ultra-fast battery tests: 10%–70% in ~3 min 41 sec (Battery)
**Summary:** On Jul 29, FAW said Hongqi, with Lishen Battery and FAW’s battery unit, completed 25°C ultra-fast charging tests: peak rate of 12C, 10% to 70% SoC in 3 minutes 41 seconds, and 10% to 97% in 8 minutes 3 seconds. A high-efficiency ultra-fast anode, low-desolvation-energy electrolyte and composite carbon coating cut internal resistance about 15% versus similar cells, paired with intelligent liquid cooling (pack temperature delta within 3°C). Production vehicle timing is undisclosed—another move in China’s flash-charging race.

### 5. Gelderland launches ~12,000-point public smart-charging pilot with ~10% fee rebates (Charging / Europe)
**Summary:** On Jul 29, Dutch DSO Liander with Vattenfall, Allego, ANWB, Athlon and Deftpower launched a public smart-charging pilot in Gelderland covering about 12,000 chargers through January 2027. Drivers set departure time in an app so charging can shift to quieter grid periods; successful flexible portions earn an average ~10% session rebate. Since July 2026 Dutch grid operators no longer get automatic priority for new connections in congested areas, making software flexibility essential.

**Links:**
