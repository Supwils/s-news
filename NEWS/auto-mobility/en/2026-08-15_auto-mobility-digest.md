## I. Autonomous Driving & Mobility Platforms

### 1. Waymo wins California approval to charge for driverless rides in 18 counties as scale and glitches collide (autonomous driving / US)
**Summary:** Electrek reports the California Public Utilities Commission has cleared Waymo’s largest robotaxi expansion yet: 18 counties from Sonoma to San Diego, paid fully driverless service across the Bay Area and Los Angeles, plus new markets in Sacramento and San Diego. Waymo said the rollout will be gradual and guided by its safety framework; Advice Letter No. 4 was filed in January and had been suspended for review through September 25. The Star, in an August 15 dispatch, said the fleet has grown from about 700 vehicles early last year to nearly 4,000 across 15 metro areas, with about 500,000 paid trips a week. Waymo has issued three federal software recalls since December; stalled, drained vehicles still snarled San Francisco’s July 4 fireworks. NHTSA administrator Jonathan Morrison wrote on July 8 that AVs interfering with police, fire and EMS showed “a clear pattern.”

### 2. California lets heavy-duty AVs test on public roads; Aurora and Kodiak permitted, Kodiak already rolling (autonomous driving / US)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on August 14 that the California DMV has issued public-road testing permits to Aurora Innovation and Kodiak AI, and that Kodiak has started with a handful of trucks around its Mountain View office. The permits sit under rules adopted April 28 that lifted a ban on testing driverless vehicles over 10,000 pounds. A human safety operator must remain behind the wheel, and operations on roads posted 25 mph or below are barred except on a “direct route” between destinations. Both firms are California-based but had tested and commercialized mainly in Texas (Aurora’s Dallas–Houston and other lanes from May 2025; Kodiak’s Permian driverless work from January 2025, later Dallas–Houston). Kodiak said it received a drivered testing permit on August 13, the first step on a drivered-test to driver-out-test to driver-out-deployment path.

- [TechCrunch — Self-driving trucks are officially testing on California highways](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/self-driving-trucks-are-officially-testing-on-california-highways/)
- [Kodiak AI — Kodiak Receives California DMV Autonomous Truck Testing Permit](https://kodiak.ai/news/kodiak-california-dmv-autonomous-truck-testing-permit)

**Commentary:** Autonomous freight can finally train on home-state pavement, but a safety driver in the seat means unmanned highway hauling is still a later permit.

### 3. Uber Japan partners with Hinomaru Kotsu for a Tokyo robotaxi pilot aimed at late 2026 (mobility / Japan)
**Summary:** Uber said on August 13 that Uber Japan signed an operational partnership with taxi operator Hinomaru Kotsu to run day-to-day fleet work for a Tokyo AV pilot: depots, cleaning, maintenance, inspections, charging and uptime, with rides matched in the Uber app. The project rests on a March 12, 2026 memorandum among Uber, the UK’s Wayve and Nissan, using Nissan LEAF vehicles with Wayve’s AI Driver, targeting a late-2026 start. Japanese law requires licensed taxi companies to operate passenger transport; experienced Hinomaru drivers will ride as safety operators at first, with fully driverless service subject to later approval.

## II. EVs & Policy

### 4. Porsche leaves the Volkswagen Group EU CO₂ pool and open-pools with XPENG for 2026–2027 (policy / Europe)
**Summary:** electrive and CleanTechnica, citing European Commission documents, report Porsche has withdrawn from Volkswagen Group’s EU new-car CO₂ pool and will form an open pool with Chinese EV maker XPENG for 2026 and 2027. Carmakers may average fleet emissions across brands via pooling; an official EU filing is dated August 5, 2026. Volkswagen Group holds about a 5% stake in XPENG. Analysis says Porsche’s BEV volumes in Western Europe are down nearly 30% year on year in 2026, with BEVs about 30% of regional mix versus almost 40% a year earlier. Carscoops notes the VW Group’s 2025 European average of about 100 g/km missed a 93.6 g/km target, with fines of €95 per gram per vehicle. XPENG delivered about 19,000 EVs in Western Europe in the first half, up about 126%. Pooling fees were not disclosed.

**Links:**

## III. Batteries & Charging

### 8. GM says every 2027 EV will ship with native NACS and plug straight into Tesla Superchargers (charging / US)
**Summary:** General Motors said on August 13, with Electrek following on August 14, that every 2027 Chevrolet, Cadillac and GMC EV will come with a native North American Charging Standard (NACS / SAE J3400) port, so drivers can use compatible Tesla Superchargers and other NACS fast chargers without an adapter. The 2026 Cadillac Optiq and the Chevrolet Bolt arriving in 2026 as a 2027 model already switched; most 2026 GM EVs still use CCS1 and need GM’s roughly $275 NACS DC adapter. 2027 buyers will instead need adapters for CCS1 DC and most J1772 Level 2 (GM lists about $189 and $67). GM said Tesla Supercharger, IONNA, Electrify America and ChargePoint are supported now, with EVgo later, covering nearly 70% of US DC fast chargers. Energy Pass Plug & Charge is live at IONNA and slated to expand to Superchargers.

**Links:**

- [GM News — Every GM electric vehicle with NACS charging](https://news.gm.com/home.detail.html/Pages/topic/us/en/2026/aug/0813-electric-vehicle-nacs-charging.html)
- [Electrek — Every 2027 GM EV will plug directly into Tesla Superchargers](https://electrek.co/2026/08/14/every-2027-gm-ev-will-plug-directly-into-tesla-superchargers/)

**Commentary:** The North American plug war is effectively over; the next hassle is whether the trunk still needs a CCS adapter.
