## I. Policy & Trade

### 1. China phases lithium battery consumption tax back in: 2% from September, 4% a year later (Policy / China)
**Summary:** China’s Ministry of Finance, General Administration of Customs, and State Taxation Administration announced that from Sep 1, 2026, mature batteries including lithium-ion packs will face a 2% consumption tax, rising to the full 4% rate from Sep 1, 2027; sodium-ion, solid-state, fuel cells and certain next-gen PV cells remain exempt through end-2028. Caixin Global follow-ups on Jul 20 framed the move as ending a long EV battery tax break and another step toward equalizing tax treatment with gasoline vehicles, while cost pass-through to car prices remains uncertain.

**Commentary:** After the subsidy era, tax policy is pulling oil and electricity onto the same starting line—the fight over who absorbs battery cost has only begun.

## II. Markets & Pricing

### 3. German study: comparable BEVs ~18% cheaper in real terms (2020–2025); model count quadruples, entry gap remains (Market / Europe)
**Summary:** ICCT and Fraunhofer ISI found that comparable battery-electric cars in Germany became about 18% cheaper in real terms from 2020 to 2025, while ICE prices rose about 2% after inflation; median nominal BEV prices still rose ~42% to about €53,000 as the mix shifted to larger cars. Available BEV models grew from 38 to 159, while ICE options fell from 275 to 194, yet mini/compact BEVs numbered only 19 versus 35 ICE models. Global battery prices fell roughly 35%–37% after inflation, and average range rose about one-third; Germany’s purchase subsidy of up to €6,000 is beginning to reinforce the trend.

**Links:**

- [electrive — Study finds EVs 18% cheaper to own in Germany](https://www.electrive.com/2026/07/20/study-finds-evs-18-cheaper-to-own-in-germany/)
- [MarketScreener / Reuters — Electric cars became cheaper in Germany despite rising average prices](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/electric-cars-became-cheaper-in-germany-despite-rising-average-prices-ce7f51dade8ef626)

**Commentary:** Rising average stickers mask like-for-like price cuts—the real bottleneck is affordable entry models, not technology ceilings.

### 5. D.C. robotaxi bill hearing: Waymo and Uber clash; accessibility gaps draw fire (Mobility / U.S.)
**Summary:** The D.C. Council held hearings on a bill that would authorize fully driverless commercial robotaxis. TechCrunch and TechTimes note the firms still partner in Austin and Atlanta, but their Phoenix collaboration ended Jun 29, and policy interests are diverging.
