## I. Autonomous Driving & Regulation

### 1. Zoox Recalls Software on 105 Robotaxis Over Heavy-Smoke Detection Gap (AV / Safety)
**Summary:** TechCrunch and CNBC reported on Jul 17, 2026 that Amazon-owned Zoox issued a software recall covering its full fleet of 105 robotaxis after the ADS may fail to detect and respond to heavy smoke. Per NHTSA, on Jun 20 an unoccupied Zoox entered an active fire scene obscured by smoke and not cordoned with cones, braked hard while trying to steer away, then stopped; a teleoperator reversed it clear so responders could place cones; no injuries were reported. Zoox has pushed an update that adds heavy-smoke detection to existing emergency-scene logic. The recall decision came around Jul 7, shortly before NHTSA pressed AV operators to prioritize first-responder interference fixes.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — Zoox issues software recall after robotaxi got confused by heavy smoke](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/17/zoox-issues-software-recall-after-a-robotaxi-got-confused-by-heavy-smoke/)
- [CNBC — Amazon's Zoox recalls software after robotaxi drove into heavy smoke](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/17/amazon-zoox-recalls-robotaxi-smoke.html)

**Commentary:** Regulators have elevated first-responder interference from incident noise to an industry red line—software recalls are compliance hygiene before scale.

### 2. San Francisco Mayor Asks State to Tighten Robotaxi Rules After Waymo July 4 Gridlock (Policy / US)
**Summary:** CBS and TechCrunch report that Mayor Daniel Lurie wrote California transportation officials Thursday urging stronger AV operating standards. Triggers include the Jul 4 fireworks traffic jam, when many Waymo vehicles ran out of power and blocked streets, and last December’s blackout, when signals went dark across roughly one-third of the city and AVs stalled in intersections. Lurie seeks four capabilities: promptly clear disabled vehicles from travel lanes, adapt routes and service in real time during emergencies, share live ops data with the city, and prove through testing they can handle major events. Waymo operates in about 11 cities and says it completes more than 500,000 paid rides weekly.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — San Francisco mayor pushes for tougher rules after the Waymo traffic fiasco](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/16/san-francisco-mayor-pushes-for-tougher-rules-after-the-waymo-traffic-fiasco/)
- [CBS San Francisco — San Francisco mayor urges state to tighten robotaxi regulations](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/san-francisco-robotaxi-regulations-waymo-disruptions/)

**Commentary:** As fleets scale, voluntary restraint is no longer enough—cities are treating robotaxis as critical infrastructure, not pilots.

## II. EVs & Automaker Strategy

### 4. Honda to End US Sales of Its Last EV, the Prologue, and Pivot to Hybrids (OEM / US)
**Summary:** The Japan Times and Quartz reported on Jul 17, 2026 that Honda told US dealers the GM-codeveloped, Mexico-built Prologue will cease production and sales after the 2026 model year, with remaining stock expected to clear into early 2027; the US lineup will emphasize hybrids and ICE. Honda’s first-half US sales rose about 2.4%, with hybrids roughly one-third of volume, while Prologue deliveries fell about 40%. The company had already canceled three planned Ohio-built 0 Series BEVs and aims to launch about 15 new hybrids by March 2030, mostly for North America; executives say a future US EV return is not ruled out.

### 7. US Q2 BEV Sales ~247,226: Sequential Rebound, Still Down ~20% YoY (Market / US)
**Summary:** Cox Automotive / Kelley Blue Book data put US BEV sales at about 247,226 in Q2 2026, up roughly 14%–15% from Q1—the strongest quarter since the federal tax credit expired in September 2025—yet still down about 20.5% year over year. EV share was about 5.8%, far below the ~10.6% peak in Q3 2025 when buyers rushed the expiring incentive. Analysts credit new models, state incentives, and charging expansion for a stabilizing floor, while first-half volumes remain well below 2025.
