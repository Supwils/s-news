## I. Policy & Safety

### 1. NHTSA upgrades GM eBoost brake probe to engineering analysis, covering ~1.16M GM/Honda/Acura vehicles (Safety / U.S.)
**Summary:** TechCrunch reported on August 24 that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's Office of Defects Investigation (ODI) has escalated its probe of General Motors' eBoost brake-by-wire system from a preliminary evaluation to an "engineering analysis" (EA26006)—the agency's highest investigation tier and often a step before a mandatory recall. The expanded review covers an estimated 1,164,820 vehicles tied to 745 incidents, 22 crashes or fires, and six injuries, spanning Cadillac Lyriq/Optiq/Celestiq, Chevrolet Blazer EV/Equinox EV/Colorado, GMC Canyon/Acadia, Buick Enclave/Envision, Honda Prologue, Acura ZDX, and the discontinued Cruise Origin. GM had attributed failures to eBoost spindle fractures, but ODI said it kept receiving reports inconsistent with that explanation, including "immediate loss of brake assist" while slowing—cases such as a Lyriq crashing into a storefront and a Blazer EV driver steering into a curb to avoid an intersection. eBoost has rolled out since 2019, replacing the traditional mechanical link between pedal and brakes with an electronic one.

**Links:**

- [TechCrunch — Brake problems in GM EVs draw greater federal scrutiny](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/24/brake-problems-in-gm-evs-draw-greater-federal-scrutiny/)
- [The BRAKE Report — GM eBoost Brake Investigation Expands to 1.16M Vehicles](https://thebrakereport.com/gm-eboost-brake-investigation-nhtsa-ea26006/)

**Commentary:** Once brake feel goes software-defined, redundancy and failure modes must survive independent scrutiny—upgrading to engineering analysis means regulators no longer accept a single-spindle explanation.

### 2. Global media keep following China's record door-handle recall as 2027 mechanical-release rules loom (Policy / China)
**Summary:** BBC continued coverage on August 24 of the aftermath from filings in which nine automakers recalled a combined ~4.3 million vehicles over hard-to-identify interior emergency mechanical releases: Tesla alone is recalling ~2.98 million China-built and imported Model 3/Y/S/X units, to be remedied with warning labels plus an OTA post-crash window-drop strategy; a separate immediate OTA adds cabin-camera monitoring for ~2.74 million China-built Model 3/Y units with insufficient driver-attention monitoring for assisted driving. Xiaomi, Xpeng, Geely, and others are also involved. Reporting cites regulators saying that from January 1, 2027, new cars sold in China must have clear mechanical releases inside and out; NHTSA denied a Model 3 recall petition in July in favor of rulemaking, and UNECE is drafting standards for opening doors when power is cut.

## II. EVs & Markets

### 3. Europe's July BEV registrations rise ~13% YoY; 16-market share hits 25.7% (Market / Europe)
**Summary:** Reuters reported on August 24, citing New Automotive and E-Mobility Europe data, that across 16 markets covering more than 90% of EU and EFTA sales, July battery-electric (BEV) registrations reached ~224,300 units, up ~13% year-on-year and 25.7% of all new sales—Europe's BEV share has stabilized above the "one-in-four" threshold. France hit a record 35% BEV share in July (~44,400 units), with the social-leasing subsidy program cited as a key driver; Germany reached ~29.3% (~78,600 units). Year-to-date through July, Europe has registered nearly 1.5 million BEVs, up ~30% from 2025. Coverage also notes high fuel prices (after February's Iran conflict), more affordable models, and subsidies are driving swaps, but public charging remains a major barrier for apartment dwellers; in the U.S., with federal purchase credits gone, Q2 EV sales were still down more than 20% year-on-year.

### 6. Automotive DRAM lead times exceed 58 weeks; prices up ~180% in three months as AI datacenters outbid automakers (Supply chain / Semiconductors)
**Summary:** GCN reported that the auto industry faces a structural memory shortage driven by AI infrastructure: Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron are shifting capacity toward high-bandwidth memory (HBM), shrinking automotive DRAM/NAND supply. The trio accounts for ~88% of global automotive DRAM; SK Hynix says 2026 output is sold out, and Fusion Worldwide quotes lead times above 58 weeks on new orders. S&P Global put automotive LPDDR4 prices up ~70% YoY by January 2026; UBS estimates ~180% growth over just three months. Premium vehicles with large cockpits and L2+ ADAS can need several times the memory of average cars; GM expects a full-year DRAM-related headwind of ~$1.5–2.0 billion. Tier-one suppliers cannot confirm some 2026 NAND/NOR supply, and the industry broadly expects pricing pressure for at least 12–18 months.

**Links:**

- [GCN — Cars now carrying tens of gigabytes of memory in the dashboard face chip lead times past 58 weeks](https://gcn.com/cars-now-carrying-tens-gigabytes-memory/21059/)

**Commentary:** This is not a pandemic port crisis—it is capacity outbid by AI—so the more screen and ADAS content a car carries, the more fragile its BOM becomes.

## V. Autonomous Driving & Robotaxi

### 10. Beijing Yizhuang grants first commercial robotaxi permits to Baidu Apollo Go and Pony.ai (Autonomy / China)
**Summary:** iEVChina and others reported that from August 21 Beijing issued its first commercial robotaxi licenses to Baidu's Apollo Go and Pony.ai, allowing paid driverless rides within a geofenced zone in the Yizhuang development area—the capital's first truly paid "robotaxi" service. Apollo Go deployed 67 vehicles; Pony.ai's initial fleet is smaller. Vehicles operate without a driver-seat safety monitor but retain a front-seat employee ready to intervene, with a two-passenger limit per trip. Pony.ai had accumulated 500,000+ paid test rides across Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Beijing, and Shanghai; Baidu's Apollo Go claims 20 million+ cumulative rides globally. The permits follow Beijing's gradual loosening of fully driverless testing since 2024, paralleling Nevada's August 20 approval for Tesla, Waymo, and Uber to charge for rides in Las Vegas.
